Module 9 › Chapter 9.6 › Cascading Failures
Definition & why it matters
Cascading failures occur when resource contention in one service causes dependent services to fail. Preventing this preserves availability and reduces outage scope.
Key signs
  • Rising thread or connection pool saturation.
  • Increasing timeout rates and retries.
  • Sudden latency spikes in dependent services.
Common misconception
Retries always help — not true. Unbounded retries amplify resource usage and make cascades worse.
Key Takeaway

Cascading failures are prevented by failing fast and isolating resources to keep one problem from consuming global capacity.

Concrete example — Service A, B, C
Service A calls Service B which becomes slow. Service A continues waiting and consuming threads. Service C calls Service A and now finds threads unavailable. This is the classic propagation sequence.
Scenario: Inventory service times out; order workers exhaust connection pool.
Mistake: Aggressive retries without backoff increases load and prevents recovery.
Good: Fail fast and return cached or default responses while dependency recovers.
Check your design
Which external calls are synchronous, retried heavily, or share a single resource pool? These are immediate candidates for breakers and bulkheads.
