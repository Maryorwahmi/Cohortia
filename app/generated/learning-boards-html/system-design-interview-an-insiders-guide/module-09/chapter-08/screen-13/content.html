Module 9 › Chapter 9.8 › When Not to Use Serverless
Primary constraints
Execution duration limits prevent very long-running tasks; split large jobs into chunks or use orchestration.
Predictable, high-throughput steady loads may be cheaper on dedicated infrastructure.
Strict portability requirements discourage tight use of provider-specific features.
When evaluating a migration, weigh cost savings against refactoring effort and operational complexity introduced by splitting jobs.
Assessment exercise
Example question
A nightly 4-hour batch job: benefits include operational cost savings, but a critical drawback is execution limits requiring refactoring into smaller functions or using an orchestrator.
Reflection
Estimate refactor effort: can the job be partitioned into idempotent chunks? If not, serverless may not be practical.
Decision prompt: create a short matrix listing latency, duration, portability, and cost to compare serverless against alternative architectures.
