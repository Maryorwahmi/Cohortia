Module 09 › Chapter 3 › Two-Phase Commit (2PC)
How 2PC works
  1. Coordinator sends prepare to all participants.
  2. Participants reply ready or abort after ensuring local commitability.
  3. If all ready, coordinator sends commit; otherwise it sends rollback.
Why it fails in practice
  • Blocking: participants hold locks while waiting for coordinator decision.
  • Coordinator failure leaves participants in indeterminate prepared state.
  • Tight coupling and poor scalability for independently deployed services.
Concrete scenario

Imagine three services with separate databases: orders, payments, and inventory. Under 2PC each service must lock rows until the coordinator decides, increasing latency and risking deadlocks during network issues. If the coordinator fails between phases, human or automated recovery is required to resolve prepared participants.

Misconception check
Believing 2PC is a drop-in for transactions across services ignores operational recovery costs and the availability trade-offs it imposes.
Practical note

Prefer asynchronous Saga approaches for microservices unless you control all participants and accept blocking consequences.
