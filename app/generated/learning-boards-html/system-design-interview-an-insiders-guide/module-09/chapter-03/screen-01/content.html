Distributed Transactions and Sagas
Why classic ACID transactions fail in microservices and how Sagas provide eventual consistency

We explore the limits of distributed locking and Two-Phase Commit, then introduce the Saga pattern: a sequence of local transactions plus compensations. You will see choreography and orchestration styles and concrete examples such as checkout and travel booking.

Visual concept
Sequence of local transactions with compensations
Step A
Local transaction
Each service updates its own database using ACID-local operations.
Step B
Publish event
Events trigger the next participant or inform an orchestrator.
Step C
Compensate if needed
If a later step fails, perform compensating transactions to undo prior effects.
Why this matters
Sagas trade synchronous atomicity for resilience and scalability. They let services remain autonomous while enabling business-level consistency through compensations and event-driven flows.
