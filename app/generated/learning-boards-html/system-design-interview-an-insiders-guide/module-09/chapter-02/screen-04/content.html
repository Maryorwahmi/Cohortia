Module 9 › Chapter 9.2 › Why inter-service communication is hard
Definition
Operational cost of distributed communication

When many services communicate, the number of failure modes and the need for consistent policies grows quickly. This creates repeated work and inconsistent behavior if handled in application code.

Key explanation
  • Retries, timeouts, and circuit breakers are cross-cutting and should be consistent across services.
  • Independent implementations lead to different failure modes and debugging complexity.
  • Centralizing these concerns reduces boilerplate and accelerates incident response.
Common mistake
Implementing retry and TLS logic in every service too early, before the system truly needs a mesh, increases complexity without immediate payoff.
Analogy & scenario
Think of network concerns as plumbing. If every room installs its own pressure regulator and leak detector, you end up with inconsistent settings and hard-to-coordinates repairs. A centralized infrastructure plumbing layer gives a single place to adjust pressure across all rooms.
Concrete example
If Service A and Service B use different retry backoffs, A may flood B during recovery. A centralized policy enforces a single backoff and prevents spikes.
Key takeaway
Recognize platform-level networking concerns early to avoid duplication and inconsistent behaviour.
