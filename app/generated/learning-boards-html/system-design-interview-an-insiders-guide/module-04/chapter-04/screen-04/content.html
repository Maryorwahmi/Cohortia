Module 04 › Chapter 4.4 › What is Sharding?
Definition and triggers
Sharding splits rows across independent servers to enable horizontal scale and fault isolation.
When to shard
  • Single node CPU, IO, or storage limits are reached regularly.
  • Backups and maintenance windows cannot complete within acceptable SLAs.
  • Read replicas and caching no longer reduce write contention.
Common misconception
Sharding is not a free performance win—operational costs often outweigh benefits if applied prematurely.
Key Takeaway

Shard when scale demands it and after simpler methods fail, understanding the operational trade-offs.

Concept cards
Benefit: horizontal scale
Add shards to increase capacity and distribute load across servers.
Cost: operational complexity
You must route queries, manage multiple backups, and handle cross-shard operations.
Risk: wrong timing
Premature sharding can increase latency and development time unnecessarily.
Analogy
Sharding is like splitting a warehouse into zones: you reduce congestion but must also coordinate inventory movement across zones.
