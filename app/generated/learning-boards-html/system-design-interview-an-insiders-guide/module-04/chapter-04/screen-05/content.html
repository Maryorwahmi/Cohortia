Module 04 › Chapter 4.4 › Sharding Key
What makes a good sharding key
High cardinality, stability, and alignment with common queries are essential.
Even distribution
Avoid keys where a few values dominate traffic or storage.
Query locality
Most frequent queries should be satisfiable by a single shard to reduce cross-shard requests.
Mutability
If the key changes often, data movement between shards becomes costly and risky.
Examples & contrasts
Good example
tenant_id in a multi-tenant app where queries are tenant-scoped; keeps operations local to a shard.
Bad example
created_at as the key causes all recent writes to target the same shard and creates a hot spot.
Composite key
Combine tenant_id with user_id to preserve locality while improving distribution for large tenants.
