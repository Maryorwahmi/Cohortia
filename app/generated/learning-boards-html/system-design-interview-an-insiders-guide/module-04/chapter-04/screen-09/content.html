Module 04 › Chapter 4.4 › Hot Spots
Why hotspots form
Skewed key distributions, large tenants, and time-based write bursts are common causes.
  • Skewed key values concentrate traffic on one shard.
  • Very large tenants can overwhelm their assigned shard.
  • Sequential keys like timestamps create write-heavy shards.
Mitigation checklist

Consider sub-sharding, directory reassignment, caching, and rate-limiting. Monitor shard metrics to detect drift early.

Mitigation examples
Sub-sharding
Split a hot range into multiple internal partitions to distribute load.
Dedicated shards
Move very large tenants to dedicated shards using a directory to remap their keys.
Buffering and batching
Use write buffers to smooth bursts and reduce peak load on a shard.
Operational note
Rebalancing must be throttled and monitored to avoid overloading destination shards during data movement.
