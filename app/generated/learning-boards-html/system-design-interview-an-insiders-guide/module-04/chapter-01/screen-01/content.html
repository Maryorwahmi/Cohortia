Relational Databases: Foundations and Scaling Trade-offs
A compact tour of ACID, normalization, and the scaling patterns you will meet in system design interviews.
In this lesson
Understand ACID, design normalized schemas, and evaluate scaling strategies like replicas and sharding.
Why it matters
Correctness and transactional integrity are non-negotiable for finance, auth, and inventory systems.
Core topics you will explore
  • ACID transaction guarantees and why they matter.
  • Normalization to eliminate redundancy and anomalies.
  • Operational scaling techniques: vertical scaling, replicas, sharding.
Concrete example
An e-commerce schema: Customers, Orders, Products, Order Items demonstrates keys, joins, and normalization benefits.
Common pitfall
Relying only on vertical scaling delays inevitable complexity when traffic grows beyond single-server limits.
Explore Key Topics
ACID
Transaction guarantees that make data reliable across failures.
Normalization
Organizing tables to reduce redundancy and prevent anomalies.
Scaling
From vertical upgrades to replicas and sharding — trade-offs to consider.
Concept snapshot
Tables, keys, transactions, and scaling strategies form a compact design vocabulary.
Data model
Rows, columns, primary keys, foreign keys, and normalized tables ensure predictable joins and queries.
Scaling pattern
Start vertical for simplicity, add read replicas for scale, consider sharding when dataset and write volume demand it.
When to pick RDBMS
Choose RDBMS for financial systems, user management, and any case where transactional integrity is essential.
Operational cost
Horizontal techniques add complexity; plan for migration and monitoring from day one.
Starter example
Customers, Orders, Products, Order Items — used through the chapter as a running schema example to explore keys, normalization, and scaling choices.
