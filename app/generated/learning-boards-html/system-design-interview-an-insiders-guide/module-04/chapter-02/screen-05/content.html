ACID vs BASE

How transactional guarantees trade off against availability and scale.

ACID (Relational)
  • Strong transactional guarantees across operations.
  • Good for money transfers, inventory reservations, and consistency-critical updates.
  • Tends toward vertical scaling and schema migrations.
Example
A bank transfer must atomically debit one account and credit another, and the system must never see inconsistent balances.
BASE (NoSQL)
  • Prioritizes availability and partition tolerance; accepts eventual consistency.
  • Enables horizontal scaling and flexible schemas.
  • Often used for caching, sessions, and rapidly evolving document data.
Example
A social feed can show slightly stale content to users; availability is preferred over blocking requests during partial failures.
Misconception check
BASE does not mean data is unreliable. It means the system provides different guarantees and often provides ways to tune consistency per operation or to run compensating updates.
Key Takeaway
Pick ACID for correctness-critical domains and BASE for latency and availability at scale, and document the expected inconsistency window.
