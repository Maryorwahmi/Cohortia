Module 08 › Chapter 8.1 › SLAs & Architecture
What an SLA contains
Scope, measurable target (often availability), measurement window, reporting rules, and remedies or credits when missed.
Design implications
Strict SLAs usually require redundancy, failover, and verified recovery procedures. Each requirement increases cost and operational overhead.
Negotiation tip
Align SLA terms with achievable SLOs and document measurement details to avoid disputes about incidents and remedies.
Concrete contrast
A 99.9% SLA allows ~43 minutes of downtime per month; 99.99% allows ~4.3 minutes. These differences lead to dramatically different architecture and runbook investments.
Risk
Missing an SLA can incur credits or penalties and affects customer trust. Design SLAs with clear measurement rules and realistic architecture costs.
Key Takeaway

Treat SLAs as business-level constraints that should be matched by architecture and operational practice, not afterthoughts.
