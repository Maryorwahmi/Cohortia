Module 5 › Chapter 5.4 › Why eviction is necessary
Definition

Cache eviction

Removing items to free space when a cache reaches capacity. The choice of which items to remove is the eviction policy.

Why it matters
  • Directly affects hit ratio and backend load.
  • Poor policies can evict hot items and cause repeated misses.
  • Policy choice should reflect access patterns and re-fetch cost.
Key Takeaway

Eviction is the controllable choice that shapes cache usefulness; measure hit ratio to evaluate it.

Concrete scenario

Imagine a web store caching product pages. If you evict frequently viewed product pages due to an age-only policy, repeat visitors will trigger expensive recomputations. Measuring hits and misses for top products will quickly show the policy’s failure.

Measure
Instrument hit ratio and miss cost per key to quantify impact.
Simulate
Replay traces to see how a policy performs on real workloads.
Misconception: "More memory solves policy issues." Larger caches help but do not replace the need to choose a rule that reflects future use.
