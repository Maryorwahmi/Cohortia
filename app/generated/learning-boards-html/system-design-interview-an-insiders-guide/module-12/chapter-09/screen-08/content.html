Module 12 › Chapter 12.9 › Trade-offs
Comparison Pattern
A three-part rhythm: alternative, pros/cons, deciding factor.
State: Strong vs Eventual Consistency
Strong: simpler semantics but higher write latency and coordination cost. Eventual: lower latency and higher availability but requires careful conflict handling.
Cache vs No Cache
Cache: reduces DB reads and latency but risks staleness and warm-up complexity. No cache: simpler correctness but higher DB cost and potentially worse latency.
Concrete example
If the system requires sub-100ms redirects at high read volume, caching redirects with short TTLs may be justified; acknowledge cache miss storms and propose request coalescing as mitigation.
Common mistake
Picking solutions without naming alternatives hides trade-offs and reduces the interviewer's ability to probe your reasoning.
Key Takeaway

Explicitly weigh alternatives and connect the decision to the requirements; that demonstrates sound judgment.
