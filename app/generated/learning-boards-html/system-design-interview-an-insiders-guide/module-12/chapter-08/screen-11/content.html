# Option A: Relational Database (PostgreSQL)
Pros:
1. Strong consistency and transactional guarantees.
2. Rich query capabilities for joins and complex filters.
3. Familiar operational model for many teams.
Cons:
1. Harder to scale writes and to distribute globally without complex replication.
2. Higher cost for multi-region active-active setups.
3. Denormalization or complex caching may be required for very high read throughput.

# Option B: NoSQL Wide-Column Store (Cassandra)
Pros:
1. High write and read throughput with efficient partitioning.
2. Designed for multi-region replication and eventual consistency.
3. Low per-node latency for append-style workloads.
Cons:
1. Weaker immediate consistency; conflict resolution is application-level.
2. Query patterns must be designed up-front; joins are not native.
3. Operational complexity for provisioning and repair.

# Recommendation and Justification:
Chosen Option: Wide-column store (Cassandra-style)
Justification:
* Read/write throughput and global scale are primary requirements, so a store optimized for those factors scores higher.
* Trade-offs: accept eventual consistency and increased operational complexity.
* Mitigations: use timestamps or vector clocks for ordering, idempotent writes, background reconciliation, and a cache or fan-out-on-write pattern to improve read latency.
Why this choice?
The system's scale and read/write ratios make wide-column stores a natural fit. Justify the trade-off by linking to throughput and SLOs.
Mitigations
  • Idempotent writes and versioned records for ordering.
  • Background reconciliation for eventual consistency.
  • Cache precomputed feeds for fast reads.
Key Takeaway

Match storage design to access patterns and scale; make trade-offs explicit and actionable with mitigation plans.
