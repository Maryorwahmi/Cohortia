Core Concept › What is a Trade-off?
Trade-off — clear definition
An explicit compromise between system properties that is justified by constraints and requirements.
Metaphor
Like tuning a radio: improving one attribute typically hurts another. Architecture choices require similar balancing.
How to frame it
  • Name the competing properties and the constraint that forces a choice.
  • State measurable criteria to evaluate options.
  • Offer mitigations to reduce the downside of your chosen path.
Concrete example
Choose strong consistency for a payments ledger to avoid incorrect balances. That choice can reduce availability during partitions, so you accept downtime in favor of correctness.
Common Misconception
Treating trade-offs as permanent. Many trade-offs are time-dependent; you can change them as requirements and scale evolve.
Key Takeaway

Always tie the trade-off to measurable requirements and propose mitigations for the accepted downside.
