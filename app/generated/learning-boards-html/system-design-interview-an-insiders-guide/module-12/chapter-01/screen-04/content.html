Core Concept

Purpose of the System Design Interview

A definition and practical framing that guides everything you say and draw during the interview.

Definition
A collaborative simulation focused on reasoning, trade-offs, and system-level decisions.
Concrete Example
Designing a social feed: decide personalization, real-time needs, and scale before choosing components.
Common Misconception
Rushing to implementation details without clarifying requirements makes your design brittle and hard to evaluate.
Key Takeaway

Frame the interview as a conversation: surface assumptions, ask questions, and justify trade-offs.

Why it matters

Interviewers assess your ability to produce reasonable, explainable designs under uncertainty. Showing a clear process signals engineering judgment.

Observable behavior
Ask targeted questions and list assumptions aloud.
Bad behavior
Sketching microservices without stating scale or constraints.
Practice prompt
When given a prompt, pause. Say what you know, what you don’t, and list three clarifying questions. This small structure shows control and reduces error.
Try this: "I will assume X unless you tell me otherwise; would you like me to design for X or for Y?"
