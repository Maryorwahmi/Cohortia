Concurrency as a process
Numbered steps show common patterns and mitigations.
Step 1
Detect concurrent operations
Log and tag requests with unique ids and versions to reason about interleavings.
Step 2
Design ownership
Give each service clear ownership of its data to reduce cross-service coordination.
Step 3
Apply idempotency and retries
Make operations safe to retry to handle duplicated or delayed messages.
Common mistake: letting multiple services mutate the same piece of state without a clear coordination protocol.
Concrete example: quota update
Two upload requests increment a storage quota concurrently.
Mitigations
  • Serialize updates using a single owner service or external lock/lease.
  • Use optimistic concurrency with version checks and retries on conflict.
  • Design idempotent increment semantics where repeated messages do not over-count.
Key Takeaway
Concurrency raises ordering and state hazards; ownership and idempotency are your first defenses.
