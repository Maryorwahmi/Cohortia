Distributed Transactions and Concurrency Control
Explore why ACID breaks across services, how Two‑Phase Commit works and blocks, and how Sagas and concurrency controls keep systems available and correct.
In this lesson
ACID recap and distributed complications, 2PC lifecycle, Saga sequence and compensations, locking vs optimistic control.
Why it matters
Choosing the right transaction approach affects availability, latency, and developer complexity in service architectures.
Outcome
You will justify Saga versus 2PC and pick appropriate concurrency controls for high‑scale services.
Explore Key Topics
ACID in one node
Atomicity, Consistency, Isolation, Durability are straightforward within a single database where locks and logs are local.
Two‑Phase Commit
A coordinator asks participants to prepare and then commit; it guarantees atomicity but can block and bottleneck.
Saga pattern
A chain of local transactions with compensations that achieves eventual consistency and higher availability.
Visual Concept
Interactive 16:9 Masterclass
Coordinator
Orchestrates prepare and final decision in 2PC
Participant (DB / Service)
Performs local work, votes commit or abort, holds locks when voting commit.
Saga Step
Local transaction updates its own store and emits an event to continue the saga.
When failures happen
Sagas run compensating transactions; 2PC participants may remain blocked until coordinator recovers.
