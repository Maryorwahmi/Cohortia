Module 3 › Chapter 2.6 › Compensating Transactions
What a compensator is
A compensator is a business action that attempts to undo or reconcile the effects of a previously committed local transaction so that invariants are restored across services.
Design rules
  • Make compensators idempotent so retries are safe.
  • Define what business invariants must be restored, not every low-level change.
  • Document event triggers that cause compensations to run.
Concrete compensator
RefundPayment checks whether a payment was already refunded, then issues a single refund and records a Refunded event. That idempotency prevents duplicate refunds when events are retried.
When full rollback is impossible
Some side effects, like sending an email or an external shipping order, cannot be reversed. Compensators should restore the invariant: mark the order cancelled and refund money rather than trying to retract a sent notification.
Key Takeaway

Build compensations that restore business correctness and make them idempotent to tolerate retries and duplicate events.
