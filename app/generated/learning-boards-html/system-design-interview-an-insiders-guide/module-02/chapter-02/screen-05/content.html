Vertical Scaling
Increase resources on a single server.
When to use: simple apps, early-stage systems, or software that is hard to distribute.
Benefits: fewer distributed concerns, simpler deployment, immediate performance gains.
Limitations: hardware ceilings, downtime risk, single point of failure, cost jumps.
Scenario
Upgrading a VM from 4 cores to 8 cores to handle heavier compute tasks temporarily reduces latency but does not eliminate availability risk.
Analogy & Cost
Like buying a bigger oven: you may serve more food per batch, but if the oven breaks your kitchen stops.
Common mistake
Assuming vertical upgrades remove the need to think about availability or backups. They don't—plan redundancy or migration paths.
Pro tip
Use vertical scaling for quick relief but design the app so you can later split responsibilities across instances if needed.
