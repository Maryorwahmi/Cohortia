Row vs Column

Row stores are great for transactions and many-field updates. Column stores excel for analytics and aggregate scans. Know your dominant workload before choosing.

When to use columnar
  • Frequent aggregations across a few columns.
  • High-cardinality dimensions and large scans.
  • When compression and read efficiency are priorities.
When not to use

Avoid columnar for lots of small point updates or transactional consistency needs; those are OLTP workloads.

Key Takeaway

Pick columnar stores for scan-heavy analytics and row stores for transactional workloads; balance with ETL between systems.

Concrete example

A query that sums sales by region reads only the sales and region columns in a columnar engine, reducing disk reads and making sub-second responses possible even over large datasets.

Misconception

Columnar stores are not a drop-in replacement for transaction databases; often an architecture mixes OLTP and OLAP stores with streaming or batch transfers.
