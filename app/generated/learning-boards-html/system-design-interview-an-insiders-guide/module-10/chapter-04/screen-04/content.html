What the database must provide

Real-time analytics stores must accept heavy writes, return low-latency aggregations, and store time-indexed data efficiently. Each requirement affects schema, indexing, and retention choices.

Key properties
  • Write throughput to keep up with streams and avoid backpressure.
  • Low query latency for interactive dashboards and explorers.
  • Compression and time-partitioning to control storage and speed scans.
Common mistake

Choosing a transactional RDBMS solely because it's familiar, without testing ingestion or aggregate performance.

Key Takeaway

Match technology to your most frequent queries and expected ingestion profile; time should often be a first-class partition key.

Concrete example

If you require "unique users per minute" for the last hour with live ingestion, prioritize write throughput and fast distinct-count aggregations. Systems that support approximate distinct counters and segment-based ingestion scale better than a single transactionally consistent store.

Check your understanding
  • Identify whether your queries are time-windowed, free-text, or heavy aggregations.
  • Ask whether eventual consistency is acceptable for your dashboards.
  • Decide on retention: do you keep raw events or only aggregates long-term?
