Module 10 › Chapter 10.5 › Why aggregation matters
Definition

Aggregation summarizes raw events into counts, sums, averages, or distinct estimates to reduce volume and speed queries.

Concrete example
A stream job increments a per-minute counter for user_click events instead of re-summing millions of rows at query time.
Why it matters
Dashboards read a few aggregated rows instead of scanning raw events, providing interactive latency.
Misconception
Keeping raw events alone is not sufficient for low-latency dashboards; you still need a summarized path.
Key Takeaway

Decide which metrics require per-event precision and which can be safely aggregated to lower compute cost.

Visualization: event funnel
Imagine a funnel where millions of events enter and are summarized into small metric rows:
Raw events: user_click, user_login, purchase — high volume, fine-grained.
Aggregates: clicks_per_minute, unique_users_per_hour — reduced volume for dashboards.
Materialized view: persisted summary table ready for reads.
Use windowing semantics and a strategy for late events to decide how aggregates are updated over time.
