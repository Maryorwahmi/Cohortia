Module 06 › Chapter 6.4 › Kafka as a Distributed Commit Log
Definition
Kafka appends immutable messages to a partitioned log. Consumers read at offsets and may replay history.
Analogy
A ledger where you write entries and everyone can read past pages; you never delete a page immediately after reading.
Concrete Example
A user_events topic stores clicks. Dashboard, analytics, and anomaly detection each consume the same historical feed independently.
Common Misconception
Kafka is not a transient queue where messages disappear upon read; retention and offsets are central concepts.
Key Takeaway

Think of Kafka as durable event storage that also serves as a messaging transport with replay and multiple independent consumers.

Why retention matters
Retention determines how long historical data is available for reprocessing. Set it based on regulatory needs, reprocessing windows, and disk capacity.
Practical note
Because consumers control offsets, they can replay after a bug fix or rebuild state for a downstream system. That capability is often the reason teams choose Kafka.
