Chapter 6.8 › Robust Error Handling
Definition

Retries and DLQs together

Retries attempt to recover transient failures. Dead-letter queues isolate persistent failures for inspection and manual remediation.

Why exponential backoff?
It reduces pressure on a failing dependency by spacing retries and improving the chance of recovery.
Common mistake
Retrying non-transient errors indefinitely will fill logs and waste compute. Use DLQs instead.
Example
Imagine a consumer that writes to a database and the database temporarily rejects connections. The consumer retries three times with delays of 1s, 2s, and 4s. If the database is still unreachable, the message goes to the dead-letter queue. Operators see DLQ entries and inspect payloads for corruption or implement fixes before replaying.
Key Takeaway

Use exponential backoff for transient errors and DLQs to isolate persistent failures so the pipeline stays healthy.

Check your understanding
Which errors should go immediately to DLQ rather than be retried? Think about malformed payloads and validation errors.
