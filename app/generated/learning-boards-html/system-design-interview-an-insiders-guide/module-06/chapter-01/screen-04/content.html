Module 6 › Chapter 6.1 › Definitions
Synchronous Processing

A caller is blocked until the operation completes and returns a result. Simple flow and predictable ordering are strengths.

Analogy: waiting at the counter until your latte is ready.
Misconception: sync does not always mean slower CPU use; it ties up resources during I/O.
When to use: immediate, strongly ordered responses required.
Asynchronous Processing

Start work and return control to the caller. Completion arrives later via callbacks, messages, or polling, enabling responsiveness and better resource reuse.

Analogy: take a number, browse pastries, return when your number is called.
Concrete example: HTTP request returns before a background thumbnail job completes.
Misconception: async isn’t always parallel; it decouples waiting from doing and often helps I/O-bound concurrency.
