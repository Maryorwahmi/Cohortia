Introduction to Asynchronous Processing
Understand when and why to choose non-blocking patterns in distributed systems.
Orientation

You will learn what synchronous and asynchronous processing mean, see practical benefits like responsiveness and fault tolerance, and study patterns to handle trade-offs. The goal is to prepare you to defend architecture choices in interviews and to design systems that scale without blocking critical paths.

Explore Key Topics
Responsiveness
How non-blocking I/O keeps UIs interactive while background work continues.
Scalability
Reuse threads and fan out requests to increase throughput without linear thread growth.
Fault Tolerance
Queueing, retries, and fire-and-forget let systems degrade gracefully instead of failing completely.
Analogy
Coffee shop: waiting at the counter vs. taking a number and coming back when called.
Blocking threads
Synchronous
If the barista must finish one drink per customer before taking the next, wait time grows quickly. In software, blocking threads tie up resources and reduce throughput.
Key Takeaway

Asynchronous processing decouples request initiation from completion. Use it to improve responsiveness and scalability, but design for complexity around state and consistency.
