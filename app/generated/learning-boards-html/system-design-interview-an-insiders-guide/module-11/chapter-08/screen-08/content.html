Module 11 › Chapter 11.8 › Content Delivery Networks
Definition

CDN and edge caching

A distributed network that caches static content at geographically close edge locations to speed up delivery.

• Edge caching reduces round-trip time for users.
• Origin offload lowers storage read costs and protects the backend.
• CDNs may offer DDoS protection and WAF capabilities.
Operational notes
  • Use cache-control headers and TTLs to balance freshness and cache hit rates.
  • Plan invalidation strategies for updated assets to avoid stale content at the edge.
  • Combine CDN with processed versions to reduce egress and improve UX.
Key Takeaway

A CDN is essential for global responsiveness; configure caching headers and processed asset sizes to maximize benefit.
