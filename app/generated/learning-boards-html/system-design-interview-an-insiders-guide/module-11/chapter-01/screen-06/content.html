Definitions & Implications
  • Scalability: ability to add resources to handle more work. Drives sharding and replication choices.
  • Availability: uptime requirements that guide redundancy, failover, and SLA targets.
  • Latency: acceptable response times; affects caching and edge strategies.
  • Durability: guarantees that data persists even after failures; backup and replication policies follow.
  • Consistency: where immediate correctness is required and where eventual consistency is acceptable.
  • Cost-effectiveness: balancing infrastructure cost against performance and availability goals.
Decision Grid
Strong Consistency
Use for auth, billing, profile updates where correctness is essential.
Eventual Consistency
Acceptable for like counts or feed propagation where slight delays are tolerable.
Practical Example
If you require 99.99% uptime and <200ms feed loads, expect a significant cost premium for global replication and edge caching. Decide which markets need full guarantees.
