Module 11 › Chapter 11.5 › Message Queues
Definition
Buffering and Decoupling
Queues persist events so producers and consumers operate independently and reliably.
Buffering: absorbs bursts and protects downstream systems.
Decoupling: producer only needs to enqueue, improving responsiveness.
Reliability: durable persistence prevents data loss during transient failures.
Example
Kafka persists messages to disk; consumer lag indicates when downstream processing is slower than production.
Misconception
Queues do not replace monitoring; they change where you observe backpressure and failure modes.
Operational Callouts
  • Monitor consumer lag
    Lag growth is the first signal of backpressure; auto-scale or rebalance partitions when needed.
  • Tune retention and partitions
    Retention policies and partition counts determine how long spikes can be absorbed and how well load is distributed.
  • Ensure durability
    Verify that the queue persists to disk and that replication settings match your durability needs.
Key Takeaway

A durable queue both shields storage from spikes and enables independent scaling of producers and consumers.
