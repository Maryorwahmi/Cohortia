Layer 2 Vulnerabilities: Why Security Matters

Understanding the Inherent Trust Model of Ethernet

The Ethernet Trust Model

Ethernet devices inherently trust frames based on MAC addresses without extensive validation, creating a blind spot for attackers. This trust is exploited by various Layer 2 attacks.

Common Layer 2 Attacks

  • MAC Address Spoofing: Impersonating a legitimate device.
  • DHCP Starvation/Rogue DHCP: Disrupting IP address assignment.
  • ARP Poisoning: Redirecting traffic via forged ARP replies.
  • MAC Flooding: Overwhelming switch MAC tables.
Key Takeaway

Layer 2's inherent trust makes it vulnerable; dedicated security features are essential to protect data integrity and network availability.

Impact of Layer 2 Attacks

  • Data Integrity: Traffic interception and modification.
  • Availability: Denial of service (DoS) for legitimate users.
  • Confidentiality: Exposure of sensitive data.
  • Network Disruption: Unstable network operations.

Real-World Scenario: MAC Flooding

An attacker floods a switch port with thousands of fake MAC addresses. This can overflow the switch's MAC address table, forcing the switch to behave like a hub and forward all traffic out all ports, exposing sensitive data to the attacker.

Why It Matters

Without proper Layer 2 security, even a well-designed network can be compromised from within, leading to significant business impact and security breaches. Proactive defense is critical.
