Safety Considerations for Quality Profiles

Understand the implications of modifying or disabling rules.

Best Practices for Rule Management

  • •**Understand Implications:** Know what a rule does before disabling.
  • •**Prioritize Security:** Be extra cautious with security-related rules.
  • •**Prefer Lowering Severity:** Instead of disabling, consider reducing severity.
  • •**Regular Review:** Periodically check profiles for relevance and effectiveness.
Key Takeaway

Always exercise extreme caution when modifying or disabling rules in Quality Profiles, especially those impacting security, to avoid creating blind spots in your analysis.

Risk: Creating Security Blind Spots

Disabling a rule, particularly one designed to detect critical security vulnerabilities, can inadvertently create a significant blind spot in your code analysis. This means SonarQube will no longer check for that specific type of vulnerability, potentially allowing insecure code to slip through your pipeline. Always ensure that any disabled rule is genuinely irrelevant to your project or is covered by other compensating controls.

Scenario: Justifying a Rule Deactivation

Consider a rule that flags 'Utility classes should not have public constructors' (S1118). While generally good practice, your team might have a specific framework or design pattern where public constructors for utility classes are intentionally used and managed. In such a case, after careful consideration and team agreement, you might choose to deactivate this specific rule in your custom Quality Profile. However, this decision should be documented and regularly reviewed to ensure it remains valid as your project evolves.
