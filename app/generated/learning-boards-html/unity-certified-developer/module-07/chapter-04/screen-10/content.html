Avoiding runtime allocations and GC hitches
Practical patterns to stop frequent garbage collection pauses.
Problem
Allocating new objects inside Update() or other hot paths creates garbage that triggers the collector and causes frame-time spikes.
Solutions
  • Reuse List and array instances instead of newing them each frame.
  • Use object pools for bullets, effects, and frequently spawned items.
  • Avoid generating temporary strings or LINQ allocations in hot paths.
Example
A pooled bullet system reuses existing objects, reducing Instantiate/Destroy calls and eliminating per-frame allocations from creation and teardown.
Key Takeaway

Eliminate frequent allocations in hot paths. Use pooling and reuse to avoid GC spikes that interrupt gameplay.

Quick anti-patterns
Avoid in Update()
Creating new Lists, strings, or calling ToArray creates allocations every frame.
Prefer pooling
Pre-create reusable objects and toggle their active state instead of Instantiate/Destroy.
Profiler check
Look for scripting allocations per frame and GC spikes in the Profiler to confirm reduced churn.
