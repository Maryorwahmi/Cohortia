Module 6 › Chapter 6.3 › FK vs IK
Forward Kinematics (FK)

Rotate joints sequentially; animator sets each joint transform. Great for authored motion, limited for reactive tasks.

Example: keyframe shoulder, then elbow, then wrist to create a wave animation.
Misconception: FK is not obsolete; it's the basis for many base clips that IK will augment.
Key Takeaway

FK gives you precise authored motion, but not runtime adaptability for reaching targets.

Inverse Kinematics (IK)

Provide a desired end effector position and rotation; the solver computes joint rotations to meet that target. Ideal for runtime interaction and adaptive animation.

Concrete: set the right-hand world position to a moving sphere and the solver bends the shoulder and elbow automatically.
Misconception: IK doesn't always produce perfect results without hints and reasonable targets.
Pro Tip

Blend IK weights and use hints to keep solver output natural and avoid fighting base animation.
