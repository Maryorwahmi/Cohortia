Color Grading
Adjusts white balance, contrast, saturation, and tone mapping to define a scene's palette and emotional feel.
Example: Use HDR mode and ACES tone mapping to preserve highlights and create cinematic contrast.
Misconception: Color Grading fixes poor asset colors — instead, it unifies and stylizes assets that are already reasonably lit.
AO, SSR, Motion Blur
Ambient Occlusion deepens contact shadows. SSR simulates reflections using screen pixels. Motion Blur adds velocity-based blur for movement.
Performance: AO and SSR can be expensive; lower quality and sample counts on constrained hardware.
Use case: SSR is worth enabling where shiny surfaces are central to the scene, like a wet street in a night scene.
Practical tip
Tune Color Grading first because it changes perception of other effects; small exposure changes can reduce need for strong Bloom.
Optimization
Lower AO sample counts, reduce SSR resolution, and selectively apply Motion Blur only to camera or high-velocity objects.
Key Takeaway
Prioritize Color Grading and Bloom for immediate mood improvements; use AO/SSR/Motion Blur sparingly and profile their cost.
