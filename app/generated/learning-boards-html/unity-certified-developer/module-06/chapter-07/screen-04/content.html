Module 7 › Chapter 6.7 › What is Post-Processing?
Formal definition

Post-processing runs after the scene is rendered and modifies the final image using full-screen effects. It changes per-pixel color, brightness, and sampling to achieve visual goals.

Why it matters
It unifies tone and readability, corrects exposure, and provides cinematic control without reauthoring assets.
Concrete example
Add an emissive cube and enable Bloom in a URP profile to make the cube appear to glow at distance.
Misconception
Post-processing is not just decoration; it solves contrast and focus issues and can hide rendering problems if misused.
Visual examples
Before
Raw render without post-processing: flat contrast, less emphasis on light sources, and uniform sharpness across depth.
After
With Bloom and Color Grading: light sources read better, colors support mood, and focus can direct player attention.
Key Takeaway

Treat post-processing as a targeted tool: pick effects that solve a visual problem, test in Play mode, and verify with the Profiler for performance cost.
