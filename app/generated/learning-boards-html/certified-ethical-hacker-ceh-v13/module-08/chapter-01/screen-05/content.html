Stealth Port Scanning
Run these scans in a controlled lab and observe device logs and packet captures to validate detection differences.
Commands (lab only)
nmap -sT <target_IP>
nmap -sS -Pn <target_IP>
nmap -sF -Pn <target_IP>
nmap -sX -Pn <target_IP>
nmap -sN -Pn <target_IP>
The -Pn option skips host discovery so scans focus on port behavior for comparison.
Example
Compare a full connect scan and a SYN scan against a VM with UFW enabled and record ports labeled as open, closed, or filtered.
Misconception
Assuming a stealth scan is invisible. It may still be logged by advanced IDS or by the host's TCP stack behavior.
What to capture
  • Packet flags and sequence of responses per port.
  • Firewall logs showing dropped or accepted packets.
  • IDS alerts for signature matches or unusual traffic patterns.
Check your understanding
If a FIN scan yields no reply on an open port, what does that tell you about the target stack and the stateful firewall behavior?
Key Takeaway

Choose the scan type to match the expected inspection method and always validate with packet captures and logs.
