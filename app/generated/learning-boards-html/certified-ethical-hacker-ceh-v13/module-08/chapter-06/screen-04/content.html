Module 8 › Chapter 8.6 › What are Scanning and Enumeration?
Core definitions
  • Scanning: identify live hosts, open ports, and services to map an attack surface.
  • Enumeration: actively query protocols to list users, shares, records and configuration.
  • Difference: scanning finds candidates; enumeration extracts the specific details you need to act.
Concrete example
Port 445 reported open by a scan. enum4linux returns shares and user names. That output points to weak permissions or default accounts to verify manually.
Key Takeaway

Treat scanning as a discovery step and enumeration as the verification step that provides actionable evidence.

Common misconceptions
  • "If a scanner flags a service, it's exploitable." Scanners suggest possibilities, but manual validation is required.
  • "Filtered means closed." Filtered usually indicates a firewall or IDS is blocking probes, not necessarily a closed service.
  • "Aggressive scans are always fine." Aggressive scans can disrupt services and must be authorized.
Pro tip
Start with SYN or host discovery scans and increase intensity only when within scope and necessary.
