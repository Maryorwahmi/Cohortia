Module 08 › Chapter 8.3 › What is an APT?
Definition

Advanced Persistent Threat

A targeted, long-term intrusion where an adversary gains access and remains undetected to pursue espionage or theft. Persistence and stealth define the threat profile.

Contrast: Opportunistic vs APT
  • Opportunistic: quick, noisy, often financial.
  • APT: targeted, stealthy, long-term objectives.
Common misconception

Long duration does not mean loud activity; APTs minimize noise and mimic normal behavior to evade detection.

Key Takeaway

Defend by increasing visibility into behavior and internal activity, not just blocking known malware.

Concrete example
Imagine a targeted email that delivers a malicious document exploiting a zero-day. The attacker installs a backdoor, waits, and then harvests credentials. Over weeks they quietly access several servers and copy small encrypted archives to cloud storage under a legitimate-looking domain. No single antivirus alert appears, and traffic looks like normal HTTPS calls.
Check your understanding
  • What behavior would make you suspicious about an account that rarely moves data?
  • Which logs might reveal unauthorized scheduled tasks or remote execution?
Analogy

An APT is like a stealthy spy living in a building rather than a burglar breaking in and leaving immediately. The spy learns routines and blends in to steal information over time.
