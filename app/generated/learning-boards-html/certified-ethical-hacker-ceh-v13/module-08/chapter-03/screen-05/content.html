APT Lifecycle — numbered phases

Use these phase markers to guide what to monitor and where to hunt for anomalies.

01 Reconnaissance
02 Initial Compromise
03 Privilege Escalation
04 Lateral Movement
05 Persistence
06 Command & Control
07 Exfiltration
Reconnaissance
OSINT, social media profiling, and targeted data collection inform which users or systems to compromise.
Initial Compromise
Spear-phishing, malicious documents, or zero-day exploits deliver droppers and backdoors on one or more machines.
Privilege Escalation
Attackers obtain higher privileges through credential theft or local exploits to access more resources.
Lateral Movement
Tools that harvest credentials and remote execution techniques let attackers move across the network and reach targets.
Persistence
Backdoors, scheduled tasks, and registry modifications help attackers survive reboots and patches.
Command & Control
Encrypted or blended channels such as DNS tunneling or HTTPS provide covert control and data staging.
Exfiltration
Attackers compress, encrypt, and send small chunks using legitimate channels to avoid DLP systems.
Key Takeaway

Correlate signals across phases—small anomalous actions become significant when chained together into a campaign.
