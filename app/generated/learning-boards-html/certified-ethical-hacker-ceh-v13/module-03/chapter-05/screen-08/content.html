Module 4 › Chapter 3.5 › Rainbow Tables & Salting
How rainbow tables work
They map hashes back to candidate passwords using precomputed tables to speed recovery when salts are absent.
  • Advantages: very fast lookup once you have the table and matching hash format.
  • Limitations: tables grow huge when salts vary or when hash algorithms add complexity.
Practical test
If you can obtain raw hashes from a system, check whether a unique salt is used. Absence of salts increases the risk that precomputed tables will succeed.
Misconception check
Do not assume all stored hashes are protected. Legacy systems and misconfigurations still present opportunities for table-based cracking.
Key Takeaway

Modern salting practices neutralize rainbow tables for most systems; only unsalted or weakly salted hashes remain vulnerable.
