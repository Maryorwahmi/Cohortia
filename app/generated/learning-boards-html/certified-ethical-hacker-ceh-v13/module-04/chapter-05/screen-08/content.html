Module 4 › Chapter 4.5 › Baiting & Quid Pro Quo
Baiting

An attacker leaves enticing physical media or offers a seemingly useful download to make victims connect or run malicious content.

Example
USB drives labeled 'Employee Salaries' left in common areas. Someone plugs one in to view contents and malware executes, granting the attacker access.
Defenses
  • Disable autorun and removable media execution.
  • Use application whitelisting to prevent unauthorized executables.
  • Include media-handling policies in training.
Quid Pro Quo

An explicit exchange where the attacker offers a favor or service in return for access or information.

Example
A caller offers free IT help and asks users to temporarily disable antivirus or provide credentials to 'fix a problem'.
Common mistake

Treating these as low-skill attacks underestimates how persuasive a well-constructed exchange can be.

Key Takeaway

Technical controls like whitelisting and disabling autorun, paired with training, reduce the success of baiting and quid pro quo.
