Module 4 › Chapter 4.5 › Psychological Principles
Core principles
Authority
People obey perceived authority figures; attackers impersonate IT or leadership to escalate compliance.
Urgency / Scarcity
Pressure to act quickly reduces critical checks and increases mistakes.
Fear / Intimidation
Threats narrow focus and prompt rapid compliance to avoid the threatened consequence.
Trust / Liking
People help those they like or trust; attackers build rapport or impersonate familiar services.
Curiosity / Helpfulness
Unexpected items or requests trigger exploration; baiting relies on curiosity to bridge technical defenses.
Reciprocity (Quid Pro Quo)
Offering a favor or help creates obligation; attackers trade help for access or credentials.
Application and testing
Design training scenarios that stress the same principle used by attackers; for example, simulate urgent emails to test response to scarcity, or friendly phone scripts to test authority compliance.
Pro tip
When measuring susceptibility, isolate one principle per simulation to identify which lever is most effective against your users.
Common mistake

Testing with unrealistic or overly technical scripts underestimates real-world susceptibility; keep scenarios believable and contextual.
