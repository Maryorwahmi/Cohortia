Module 4 › Chapter 4.5 › What is Social Engineering?
Definition

Social engineering is the psychological manipulation of people into performing actions or divulging confidential information. It uses natural human behaviors such as trust, helpfulness, curiosity, and fear.

Why it works
Humans follow routines and social rules; attackers exploit those predictable responses rather than technical flaws.
Common misconception
Simple scripts are not harmless; well-researched, context-aware social engineering is highly effective and often overlooked.
Key Takeaway

People are an attack surface. Effective security pairs technical controls with human-focused assessment and training.

Concrete example
Scenario: An attacker calls claiming to be IT support, says a critical update requires the user's password to fix an urgent outage. The attacker uses a friendly tone and references a recent internal memo to seem legitimate. The employee, concerned and wanting to help, provides credentials. This single disclosure defeats password-only protections.
Quick checklist
  • Does the request exploit authority or urgency?
  • Is the requester verifiable through another channel?
  • Could curiosity or helpfulness cause the action?
Reflective question: What routine request in your environment might be abused tomorrow?
