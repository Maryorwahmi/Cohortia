Core Concept › Known-Plaintext Attacks

Definition and why it matters

When an attacker has both plaintext and ciphertext, they can observe how encryption behaves and look for weaknesses such as key reuse or predictable IVs.

Where it appears
Protocols with static headers, predictable messages, or reused keys across files frequently provide known-plaintext opportunities.
Tester actions
Identify repeated encrypted structures, check IV usage, and test whether keys are reused across contexts.
Key Takeaway

Known plaintext is most useful to detect bad implementation or reuse patterns; recommend per-message randomization and key separation.

Supporting explanation

  • Modern ciphers resist simple known-plaintext breaks, but implementation issues can leak information.
  • Look for reused IVs, deterministic encryption, or key reuse across different data classes.
  • Document the pattern, illustrate with small non-production samples, and recommend randomized IVs and strong key management.
Concrete scenario
A backup archive uses the same key and IV for many files. This reuse reveals structural patterns across ciphertexts and suggests a direct remediation: use unique IVs and rotate keys.
