Core Concept › Dictionary Attacks

What a dictionary attack is

A targeted sequence of guesses derived from wordlists and known patterns to prioritize likely secrets instead of trying every possibility.

Wordlist selection
Start with common lists, then add organization-specific or leaked-password samples to reflect realistic user behavior.
Mangling rules
Apply rules that append numbers, swap letters, or change case to increase coverage without exploding the search space.
Key Takeaway

Tailor wordlists to the target to find user-chosen secrets quickly and create focused remediation like banned lists or training.

Supporting explanation

  • Commonly used wordlists can recover many real-world passwords in minutes when applied to captured hashes.
  • Applying mangling rules increases success while remaining efficient compared to blind brute-force.
  • Document list provenance and rules so findings are reproducible and explainable to stakeholders.
Concrete example
Using a wordlist that includes the company name and common variants often recovers reused or company-themed passwords during a corporate engagement.
Common misconception
Assuming dictionary attacks are amateurish; well-built lists and rules are a professional tester's fastest way to find realistic vulnerabilities.
