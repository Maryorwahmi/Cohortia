Common real-world failures

  • Keys stored with broad access or in plain files instead of secure stores.
  • Absence of per-entry salts or weak hashing parameters for stored secrets.
  • Password reuse and weak policies that make dictionary attacks practical.
Key Takeaway

Attack the people and process problems first: improving storage, rotation, and access control typically reduces the most risk.

Remediation checklist

Use per-password salts and slow KDFs
Salts and iterations slow offline attacks and prevent precomputation.
Control key access and rotate keys
Limit who can read key material and rotate keys so exposed keys have limited lifetime.
Harden password policies and technical controls
Banned lists, complexity, and rate-limiting reduce success of offline and online guessing.
Common misconception
Switching ciphers without changing storage or access controls often leaves the system as vulnerable as before.
