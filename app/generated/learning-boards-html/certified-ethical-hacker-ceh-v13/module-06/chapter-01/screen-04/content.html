Core Concept

Evolution of IEEE 802.11 Standards

A compact timeline showing common capability shifts and testing implications.

802.11b/g
2.4 GHz, lower speeds
Testing note: legacy devices and crowded bands often mean predictable traffic patterns and outdated firmware.
802.11a
5 GHz support
Testing note: requires adapters and antennas that support 5 GHz capture.
802.11n
MIMO, wider channels
Testing note: multi-stream traffic may affect handshake timing and capture completeness.
802.11ac
High throughput, 5 GHz centric
Testing note: require adapters with wider channel capture to avoid dropped frames.
802.11ax (Wi‑Fi 6)
Spectral efficiency, 2.4/5/6 GHz
Testing note: new timing and resource unit behaviors need updated analysis tools; hardware support varies.
Example
A legacy 2.4 GHz AP running 802.11g often has devices with weak encryption and firmware that can be probed with older tools.
Common mistake
Assuming newer hardware is secure—misconfiguration can leave modern APs vulnerable despite strong protocols.
Key takeaway
Identify the standard first; let frequency and capability guide your tool and adapter choices.
