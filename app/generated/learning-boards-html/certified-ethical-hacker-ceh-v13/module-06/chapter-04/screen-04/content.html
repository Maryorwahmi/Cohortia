Module 06 › Chapter 6.4 › Evil Twin & Rogue AP
Core Concept
Evil Twin and Rogue AP Explained
Why mimicked networks succeed and how trust is exploited.
Definition
A fake access point broadcasting a legitimate ESSID to intercept client traffic or capture credentials.
How it works (step summary)
  • Attacker creates a fake AP with the same ESSID.
  • Deauth floods force clients off the real AP, prompting reconnection.
  • Client auto-connects; attacker provides DHCP and captive portal to capture credentials.
Common misconception
Encryption alone is not sufficient if the attacker can impersonate servers or present fake login pages; mutual auth and cert validation are required.
Example
A user’s device auto-joins "CompanyWiFi" after a deauth. The attacker’s fake AP supplies an IP and a login page, capturing test credentials in the lab.
Key Takeaway
Protect the authentication channel and train users to verify certificates and unexpected login prompts.

Check your understanding
If a client warns about a certificate, treat it as suspicious. Does the network require server certificate validation? If not, add it.
