Module 06 › Chapter 6.4 › Deauthentication Floods
Process Flow
Deauthentication Floods
Forcing reconnections to increase Evil Twin success rate.
  1. Attacker injects forged deauth frames targeting client or AP MAC addresses.
  2. Client disconnects and starts reconnection logic.
  3. If a fake AP is present and stronger or seen first, client may attach to it.
Defensive checks
  • Enable management frame protection where available.
  • Deploy WIDS/WIPS rules to alert on high deauth rates.
  • Audit AP placement and transmit power to avoid overwhelming legitimate signals.
Key Takeaway
Deauth floods are the common trigger that pushes devices toward Evil Twins; detect them and strengthen management frame protections.
Terminal examples
sudo aireplay-ng --deauth 10 -a <AP_BSSID> wlan0mon

# widespread flood example (tool varies):
sudo mdk3 wlan0mon d -a <AP_BSSID>
The first command targets a specific AP for ten deauth frames. The second shows a typical mdk3-style flood; these tools can create high volume and should only be used in lab environments with permission.
Misconception
Some assume deauths are noisy and easy to spot. Large environments need tuned thresholds; low-rate, targeted attacks can evade naive alerts.
