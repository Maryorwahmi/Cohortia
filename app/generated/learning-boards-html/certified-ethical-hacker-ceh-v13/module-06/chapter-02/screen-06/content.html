Hidden SSID Mechanics
How probe requests and association frames expose SSIDs despite beacon suppression.
Why hiding SSID is used
Administrators sometimes disable SSID broadcast for aesthetics or perceived privacy; however, the name still travels in other management frames.
How it is revealed
  • Client probe requests often contain the hidden ESSID when searching to reconnect.
  • AP probe responses include the ESSID when responding to a named probe from a client.
  • A controlled deauthentication forces reauthentication and reveals the ESSID during association.
Concrete Example
A hidden AP shows <length: 0> in airodump-ng until a client reconnects, at which point the ESSID appears in the capture file.
Misconception Check

Hiding an SSID is not encryption. It only obscures the ESSID in beacons; other frames will expose it.

Practical Steps
Passive Strategy
Listen with airodump-ng until a client probe or association reveals the SSID. This avoids interference.
Authorized Active Strategy
Under authorization, a controlled deauthentication can accelerate revealing the ESSID but may disrupt users.
Key Takeaway

Hidden SSIDs are exposed by normal client and AP behavior; capture patiently or escalate only with permission.
