Passive vs Active Reconnaissance
Understand definitions, trade-offs, and real examples to choose an appropriate approach.
Definition: Passive reconnaissance — listening only, collecting broadcast frames and client activity without transmitting.
Definition: Active reconnaissance — sending probes or deauthentication to elicit frames or force reauthentication.
Key points
  • Passive is stealthier and preserves network operations; it captures beacons, probe requests, and association events.
  • Active gets faster results for hidden SSIDs or handshakes but increases detectability and risk of disruption.
  • Misconception: hiding SSID equals security. In practice, client traffic reveals it.
  • Patience and channel focus often make passive reconnaissance practical and efficient.
Example
Observe a client's probe request for a hidden ESSID; no packets are transmitted by you and the ESSID is revealed in captured frames.
Common Mistake
Using active deauthentication without authorization and disrupting legitimate users.
Visual: Recon Trade-offs
Passive Flow
Start adapter in monitor mode, run airodump-ng, collect beacons and client frames until ESSIDs appear or you have sufficient data.
Active Flow
With authorization, use targeted probes or a deauthentication to force client reauth and quickly reveal hidden SSIDs or capture handshakes.
Key Takeaway

Prefer passive reconnaissance to reduce detection and disruption; escalate to active only with permission and a clear objective.
