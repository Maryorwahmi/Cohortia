Module 6 › Chapter 6.6 › IAM Risks
Definition

Identity and Access Management

IAM controls who can act and what they can do. Look for overly permissive roles, missing MFA, and long-lived credentials.

Checks to run
  • Find wildcard actions or principals in policies
  • Confirm MFA is enforced for console access
  • Locate unused or unrotated access keys
Example
Overly permissive role
A role allowing full storage and compute actions could let an attacker enumerate buckets, download data, and create new instances to persist access. Treat such policies as high priority.
Common misconception
Complex policies are not automatically safe. Complexity can mask broad privileges that enable escalation.
Key Takeaway

Treat IAM findings as high impact: they often enable lateral movement and access to sensitive resources.
