Core Concept
Why WEP is broken
A short IV and IV reuse make the cipher vulnerable to statistical key recovery.
Definition
The Initialization Vector is a small, non-secret value combined with the key; in WEP its limited range causes reuse and repeated keystream fragments.
Analogy
Like reusing a one-time pad many times: repeat usage leaks patterns that let an attacker deduce the key.
Concrete example
Capturing many ARP packets yields repeated plaintext structure; with IVs you can correlate ciphertexts and recover the WEP key statistically.
Key points
  • WEP uses a short IV which frequently repeats in real traffic.
  • IV reuse produces repeated keystream fragments that leak key material.
  • Active injection (ARP replay) speeds IV collection to reach statistical thresholds.
Key Takeaway

Protocol parameters such as IV length matter. A strong cipher can be broken by poor IV design.
