Encapsulation in practice
Expose intent, hide representation.
Why it matters
Encapsulation reduces ripple effects from changes, protects invariants, and makes it safe to refactor internal data without breaking callers.
Checklist
  • Are fields private where possible?
  • Does the class expose actions rather than raw data?
  • Can internal structure change without changing callers?
Concrete example
A ShoppingCart exposes addItem and total. Callers never manipulate the item list directly. If the cart switches to a map of item counts, tests that rely on public behaviors still pass.
Pro tip
Limit getters
Avoid exposing internal fields with getters unless those fields truly represent the class's responsibility rather than its storage.
