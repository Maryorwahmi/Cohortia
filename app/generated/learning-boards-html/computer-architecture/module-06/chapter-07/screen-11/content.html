Module 6 › Chapter 6.7 › Reservation Stations
Definition

Reservation stations & schedulers

Structures that hold decoded micro-ops until their operands are ready and a functional unit is available to execute them.

Per-unit vs Unified
  • Per-unit stations can waste available units if queues are imbalanced.
  • Unified schedulers allow dynamic allocation and higher utilization.
  • Hybrid approaches balance complexity and specialization.
Concrete example

If ALU station entries fill while FP stations are empty, a per-unit design stalls ALU work; a unified scheduler would map ready ALU micro-ops to any free port capable of the operation.

Trade-off

Unified queues require more complex wakeup and arbitration logic, which costs area and design effort.

Key Takeaway

Unified reservation stations are a common modern choice because their flexibility improves functional-unit utilization across diverse workloads.
