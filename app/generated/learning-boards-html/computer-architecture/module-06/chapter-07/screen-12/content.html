Module 6 › Chapter 6.7 › Caches & OoO Interaction
Concept

Cache hierarchy as a supplier

Instruction windows and wide issue need data and instructions from caches. L1 supplies low-latency access; L2 and L3 reduce off-chip misses for wider windows.

Typical sizes
L1I/D: 32–64KB, L2: 256KB–4MB per core, L3: shared 3–96MB
Why it matters
More in-flight loads require more cache capacity and bandwidth; otherwise the instruction window stalls on cache misses.
Example

Apple pairs massive ROBs and wide issue with larger per-core L2 and big shared L3 to keep many outstanding loads from stalling progress.

Misconception check

Larger caches help many workloads but add area and energy. The net benefit depends on workload locality and memory access patterns.

Key Takeaway

Balanced scaling of caches with ROB and issue width is essential; mismatches waste silicon or leave wide cores underutilized.
