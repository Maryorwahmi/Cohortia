Module 6 › Chapter 6.7 › Functional Units & Ports
Concept

Execution ports

Ports are the issue targets that route micro-ops to functional units. Port specialization determines which operations can proceed in parallel.

Port examples
  • Load ports (memory reads)
  • Store ports (write and address calc)
  • ALU/Branch ports (integer arithmetic, branches)
  • Vector/FPU ports (SIMD operations)
Misconception check

Ports do not equate to identical lanes; a port may be limited to specific operations or have lower throughput for certain op types.

Examples from families
Intel: 8 ports with mapped responsibilities: multiple ALU/branch ports, two load ports, and store ports.
AMD: ~10 execution ports—more INT/FPU bandwidth for throughput.
Apple: 14+ ports in some cores to sustain very wide issue.
Analogy & takeaway

Think of ports as elevators to workstations: many elevators help only if they go to useful floors. Evaluate port type distribution as well as count.

Key Takeaway

Execution port counts indicate capacity but functional mapping determines which instruction mixes actually speed up.
