Instruction sequence
SW R1, 0x1000(R0)   # Store 0xABCD to 0x1000
LW R2, 0x1000(R0)   # Load from 0x1000
SW R3, 0x1004(R0)   # Store 0xEF01 to 0x1004
LW R4, 0x1000(R0)   # Load from 0x1000
Below are LSQ snapshots after each instruction issues. Each row shows index, VA, PA, data, ROB, AddrKnown.
After SW R1
1 | 0x1000 | 0x1000 | 0xABCD | ROB12 | yes
After LW R2 (issues)
Load compares to prior stores → finds entry 1 → receives 0xABCD via forwarding.
After SW R3
1 | 0x1000 | 0x1000 | 0xABCD | ROB12 | yes
2 | 0x1004 | 0x1004 | 0xEF01 | ROB14 | yes
After LW R4
Load compares prior stores in order, finds entry 1 again and forwards 0xABCD.
Speculation variant
If LW R4 is issued before SW R3's address is known, the processor may predict no conflict and execute R4 early. If SW R3 later resolves to 0x1004, speculation was correct; if it resolves to 0x1000, R4 must be re-executed and dependent work replayed, costing cycles.
Key Takeaway

Simulating LSQ state makes the forwarding and replay mechanics concrete and shows why order and associative checks are required.
