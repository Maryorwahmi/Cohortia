Capacity and consequences

ROB size defines how many uncommitted instructions can be tracked. More entries allow deeper speculation and higher ILP extraction, especially across long dependency chains.

Benefit
Larger instruction windows can hide latencies and expose parallelism.
Cost
More area, power, complex commit logic, and larger potential flush penalties on mispredicts.
Rule of thumb
Designers weigh ILP gains against power/area and the typical cost of mispredictions for their target workloads.
Practical example
If a 128-entry ROB has a branch in the middle, on average 64 speculative instructions may be flushed, representing many wasted cycles and lost issue bandwidth.
