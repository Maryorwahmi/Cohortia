Reservation Stations and the CDB

Understand how buffers and a broadcast bus coordinate out-of-order completion, and why the CDB commonly limits write-stage throughput.

Est. read: 8–12 minutes
What you'll learn
  • Design and sizing of reservation stations and their fields.
  • CDB structure, arbitration, and the effect on IPC.
  • Hardware techniques to reduce CDB delay and area trade-offs.
Why it matters

The reservation station file and CDB determine how many instructions can be in-flight and how many results can be retired per cycle. That directly affects achievable IPC, area, and power.

Reservation station fields, CDB arbitration, and bandwidth trade-offs
Reservation Stations

Small buffers that hold op, tags, values, and a busy bit. Size scales with the number of stations.

Common Data Bus

Broadcasts result tag and value. Wire length and load make it slow and power hungry.

Bandwidth Choices

Multiple buses, segmentation, and local bypassing trade area and power for throughput.

Visual concept
Reservation Stations (rows)
Rows hold op, tags Qj/Qk, Vj/Vk, address and dest tag.
Common Data Bus
Broadcasts (tag, value, valid) to all stations and the register file.
Arbitration
Only one result per bus per cycle; contention requires a priority scheme.
