Bandwidth example

If FUs can complete six results in a cycle but only two CDBs exist, four results must wait. Over time this queueing reduces effective retirements per cycle, lowering IPC below the theoretical peak.

Quantitative exercise

Estimate peak concurrent completions under a typical mix, then choose M CDBs so that M meets or slightly exceeds that peak to avoid stalls in common cases. Simulate rare bursts separately to judge cost-effectiveness.

Design rule of thumb

Modern high-performance designs often use 2–4 CDBs to balance comparator area and broadcast throughput. Use workload-driven analysis to pick a number between these bounds.
