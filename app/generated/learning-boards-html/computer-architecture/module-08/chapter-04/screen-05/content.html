Module 8 › Chapter 8.4 › DRAM vs SRAM
Comparison table
SRAM (Caches)
  • 6 transistors / bit
  • Access ~1–2 ns
  • Low latency, high power, high cost
  • Used for L1/L2 caches
DRAM (Main memory)
  • 1 transistor + 1 capacitor / bit
  • Access ~50–100 ns
  • High density, low cost, needs refresh
  • Used for system memory
Example: caches use SRAM because they must deliver single-cycle hits; DRAM is used when capacity per dollar matters even though single-access latency is higher.
Refresh and bandwidth cost

DRAM capacitors leak and require periodic refresh. Refresh cycles consume time and can block accesses, reducing effective bandwidth and increasing apparent latency. System designers must budget for refresh when estimating usable GB/s.

Common mistake

Treating DRAM as a simple slower SRAM ignores refresh and bus contention effects that reduce effective throughput.
