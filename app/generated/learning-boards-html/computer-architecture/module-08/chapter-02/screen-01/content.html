Directory-Based Coherence
Point-to-point coherence for large multiprocessors

Directory entries track a block's state, which caches share it, and who owns a writable copy. This lets systems send messages only to relevant caches, avoiding broadcast traffic at scale.

In this lesson
Read/write handling, sharer-list strategies, latency vs bandwidth trade-offs, and distributed directory techniques.
Why it matters
For systems beyond a few dozen cores, broadcast-based snooping overwhelms interconnects; directories let coherence scale to hundreds or thousands of cores when designed carefully.
Explore key topics
Directory Entry
State, sharer bit vector, owner pointer — the per-block phone book.
Sharer Compression
Coarse vectors, limited pointers, and hierarchical designs trade accuracy for storage.
Directory vs Snooping
Point-to-point reduces bandwidth at scale; snooping gives lower latency for small sockets.
