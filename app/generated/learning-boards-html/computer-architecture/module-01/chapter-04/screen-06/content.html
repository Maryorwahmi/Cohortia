Module 1 › Chapter 1.4 › Static Power
P_static = V × I_leakage
Leakage flows even when gates are not switching, and it increases as process technology scales.
  • Power gating: cut power to idle blocks to eliminate leakage; costs: state loss and wake latency.
  • Body biasing: adjust threshold voltage to trade speed for leakage reduction.
  • Multi-threshold: use high-threshold cells on non-critical paths to reduce leakage.
When leakage matters
At advanced nodes leakage can be 30–50% of total power, so ignoring it will break energy budgets and raise cooling needs. Embedded and battery-powered systems must manage leakage aggressively to preserve standby life.
Example trade-off
Power gating a large block saves leakage during idle but costs milliseconds to restore state; good for long idle periods, bad for very short sleeps.
Common mistake
Applying heavy leakage mitigation everywhere increases area and complexity; focus on hotspots and long-idle regions.
