Module 1 › Chapter 1.4 › Power vs Energy
Precise definitions
Understand the mathematical relationship and practical impact on runtime decisions.
Key formulas
P = dE/dt
E = P × t (for constant power)

Example:
100 W × 1 s = 100 J
10 W × 20 s = 200 J
Concrete example
A fast, power-hungry core may finish earlier and sleep, reducing total energy compared with a slower, low-power core that runs longer.
Common misconception
Lowering power always lowers energy. Not necessarily — runtime changes can increase total energy.
When to optimize which
  • Mobile: prioritize energy per task to extend battery life.
  • Datacenter: minimize energy costs and manage peak power for cooling.
  • Desktop: balance peak power and sustained thermal limits.
Key Takeaway
Always evaluate optimizations by their effect on both instantaneous power and total energy for the target workload.
