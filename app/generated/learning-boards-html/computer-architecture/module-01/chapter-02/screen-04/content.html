Module 1 › Chapter 1.2 › Iron Law
Definition and meaning

The Iron Law isolates the three independent contributors to execution time. Each term is measurable and often the target of microarchitectural change.

Equation
Execution Time = Instruction Count × CPI × Clock Cycle Time
Why this helps
It lets you predict the impact of compiler, microarchitecture, and process changes independently.
Common misconception
Clock frequency alone does not guarantee faster execution; CPI and instruction count can negate frequency gains.
Concrete example
# Example calculation
Instruction Count = 10,000,000,000
CPI = 1.3
Clock cycle time = 2.5e-9 seconds (400 MHz is 2.5e-9?)
Execution Time = 10e9 * 1.3 * 2.5e-9 = 32.5 seconds
Note: Use consistent units. Clock cycle time is the reciprocal of frequency. If frequency is quoted, convert to seconds per cycle before multiplying.
Key Takeaway

Treat execution time as a product of three measurable levers — optimize where the largest contributor lies.
