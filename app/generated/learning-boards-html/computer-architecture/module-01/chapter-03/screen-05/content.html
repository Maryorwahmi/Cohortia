Definition Dennard scaling
Shrinking transistors kept power density steady
Mechanism

As dimensions shrink, voltage and current scale so that switching energy per transistor falls and power per area stays similar. That let designers raise frequency without increasing cooling beyond existing solutions.

  • Smaller transistors → lower capacitance → less energy per switch.
  • Voltage reductions multiplied the savings when possible.
  • System cooling and packaging stayed practical while clocks rose.
Concrete example

When process nodes allowed voltage scaling, designers increased clock rates and gained single-thread performance; cache sizes grew with transistor budgets as well.

Misconception

Assuming voltage always scales freely is wrong. At small geometries leakage prevents proportional voltage reductions, breaking Dennard scaling.

Key Takeaway

Dennard scaling explained frequency growth; its end is the technical root of the power wall.
