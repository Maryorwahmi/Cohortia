Why frequency scaling hit a thermal limit
Root causes
  • Leakage current rises at small geometries, increasing static power.
  • Power density grows and creates hot spots on die.
  • Cooling and packaging cannot scale cheaply to match power increases.
Pentium 4 case

Architects pushed to 3.8 GHz with a deep pipeline, but the design consumed high power and heat, prompting a switch to more efficient microarchitectures.

Practical consequence

Design priorities shifted to performance per watt. That caused multicore adoption and a search for architectural specialization.

Misconception

Some assume simply adding more power solves frequency limits; in practice, power-delivery and thermal constraints, plus cost, make that unviable.

Key Takeaway

When frequency scaling stopped, architects prioritized throughput and efficiency over raw clock speed.
