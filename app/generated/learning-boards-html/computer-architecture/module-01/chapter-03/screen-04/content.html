Core Concept Moore's Law
Moore's Law is about transistor count growth
An economic observation that enabled decades of scaling and industry investment.
Formal statement

Transistor count doubled approximately every two years. This drove expectations about capability and cost, guiding investment in fabs and design tools.

Why this is economic
  • Manufacturing improvements and investment reduce cost per transistor over many nodes.
  • Scaling requires large capital expenditure and coordinated industry effort.
  • Transistor growth creates opportunities, not automatic performance gains.
Concrete example
When transistor budgets rose, designers added larger caches or multiple cores instead of simply making one core faster. That choice depends on workload.
Common misconception

People often assume more transistors equal higher single-thread performance. In reality, without matching changes in architecture and power management, extra transistors can remain unused or be allocated to accelerators and cache.

Check your understanding

Given a fixed power budget, would you rather increase core count, cache, or add a small accelerator? Explain briefly for a web server workload.

Key Takeaway

Moore's Law creates capacity; architecture determines how that capacity becomes performance.
