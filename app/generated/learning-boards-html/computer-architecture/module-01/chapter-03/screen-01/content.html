Orientation

This chapter traces a chain: transistor scaling enabled exponential growth; Dennard scaling allowed rising frequencies; when power limits arrived, architects changed strategy toward parallelism, heterogeneity, and accelerators. You'll learn definitions, causes, consequences, and design responses.

Moore's Law
Transistor count doubles ~every two years; an economic observation with huge architectural consequences.
Power Wall
Dennard scaling ended, meaning higher frequency no longer implied the same power density.
Architectural Response
Multicore, heterogeneity, and accelerators became primary levers for performance per watt.
