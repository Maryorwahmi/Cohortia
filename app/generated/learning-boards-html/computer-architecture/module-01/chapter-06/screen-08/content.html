Instruction mix and ILP
Instruction composition and available parallelism determine unit counts and issue width.
Instruction mix (example)
  • Loads/stores: 40%
  • Integer ALU: 30%
  • Floating-point: 20%
  • Branches: 10%
Such a mix suggests balancing memory bandwidth and ALU resources rather than many FP units.
ILP example
An independent multiply-add chain across vector lanes exposes high ILP and benefits wide issue. A linked pointer-chase loop exhibits low ILP and gains little from widening the front-end.
Common mistake

Assuming more issue width always increases performance. If ILP is limited by dependencies, extra width is wasted power and area.

Key Takeaway

Measure instruction mix and ILP first. Provision units and issue width to match observed parallelism and operation types.
