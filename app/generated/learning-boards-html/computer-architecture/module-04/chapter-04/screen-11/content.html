Module 4 › Chapter 4.4 › Performance Impact
Quantifying the benefit
Forwarding removes most RAW stalls; loads remain costly.
Typical numbers
Without forwarding a pipeline may see a CPI near 1.5 from RAW stalls. With forwarding this can drop to about 1.1–1.2 for many workloads.
Effect of load-use pairs
Each load-use pair still costs an extra cycle. If such pairs are frequent, they add significantly to CPI even with forwarding.
Analysis method
Estimate baseline CPI with forwarding, then add the frequency of load-use pairs times one cycle to compute expected CPI for your workload.
Practical example
If forwarding reduces RAW stalls to give CPI = 1.12 and your code has 5% load-use pairs, approximate CPI = 1.12 + 0.05 * 1 = 1.17.
Key takeaway
Forwarding brings large gains, but measuring load-use frequency is essential to predict real CPI for memory-heavy code.
