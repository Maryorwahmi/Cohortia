Module 4 › Chapter 4.4 › Reducing Load-Use Stalls
Strategies and trade-offs
Practical techniques to lower the frequency of load-use penalties.
Compiler scheduling
Insert independent instructions between a load and its use to hide the one-cycle delay. Low cost but depends on available independent work.
Out-of-order execution
Hardware can execute other ready instructions while a load waits, reducing the visible stall count for the program at higher complexity and power cost.
Speculation & value prediction
Speculatively execute dependent instructions or predict the loaded value to proceed; mispredictions require recovery and cost extra cycles.
Decision guide
Choose a mitigation based on workload: compilers for simple gains, out-of-order or speculation for heavy memory-latency workloads where complexity is justified.
Key takeaway
No single method eliminates load-use cost in an in-order pipeline; combine compiler and microarchitectural approaches as appropriate.
