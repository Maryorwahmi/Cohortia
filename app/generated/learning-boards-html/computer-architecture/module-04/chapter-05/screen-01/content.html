Load-Use Hazards and Pipeline Stalls

Understand the timing mismatch that forces a stall, see how the hardware detects it, and learn compiler patterns that avoid the penalty.

Why it matters
In-order pipelines often pay these stalls frequently in pointer-heavy code.
Orientation

You will step through the five-stage timing, implement the simple stall reaction, compute CPI impact using the formula CPI = CPI_base + p × 1, and practice scheduling to hide delays. Examples focus on load followed immediately by a dependent arithmetic instruction.

What you'll practice
  • Detecting load-use pairs
  • Inserting a pipeline bubble
  • Scheduling instructions to avoid stalls

Concept Snapshot
Data ready at end of MEM, dependent needs it at start of EX — timing mismatch.
Stall: freeze IF/ID, insert bubble into EX, forward from MEM/WB in the next cycle.
Quick Example
LW R1, 0(R2)
ADD R3, R1, R4  # dependent: causes 1-cycle stall if scheduled immediately
Compiler can insert an independent instruction between the load and add to avoid the stall.
