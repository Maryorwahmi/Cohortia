Estimating PRF size
A simple minimum formula gives a practical baseline.
Minimum example
With 32 architectural registers and a reorder buffer of 128 entries capable of holding writers, the minimum number of physical registers is 32 + 128 = 160.
Designers often provision more than the minimum to avoid free-list exhaustion during bursts of writer activity, at the cost of added area and power.
Practical trade-off
Extra physical registers reduce stalls but increase PRF complexity. Evaluate workload behavior to choose the best balance.
Ask yourself: does the expected working set of speculative writers justify extra registers, or is banking and architecture tuning a better investment?
Key Takeaway

Use the formula to set a minimum and then weigh performance gains against area and power costs when adding margin.
