Wallace's ILP study
Idealized hardware still finds only modest IPC in real programs.
Ideal conditions tested
Perfect branch prediction, perfect memory disambiguation, and infinite functional units.
Observed result
Average IPC modest even then; realistic conditions reduce IPC to about 2–3 for many workloads.
Interpretation

The ILP wall is a structural limit coming from program dependencies, not only from hardware resources.

Design consequence
Since single-thread ILP is limited, manufacturers shifted to multicore, SMT, and accelerators to increase throughput across workloads rather than widening single-thread issue indefinitely.
Key Takeaway

Program structure fundamentally bounds single-thread IPC even when hardware is ideal.
