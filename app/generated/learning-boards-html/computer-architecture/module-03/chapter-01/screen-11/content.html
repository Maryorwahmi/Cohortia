Numeric comparison

Example: single-cycle clock = 10 ns. Multi-cycle clock = 2 ns and load takes 5 cycles → 10 ns total. An ADD that needs 1 cycle in single-cycle costs 10 ns, but in multi-cycle it may take 4 cycles = 8 ns, which is faster.
Key Takeaway

Compare total instruction times (cycles × clock) and weight by instruction mix to estimate real program speed.

Practical checklist

  • Compute cycles × clock for each instruction type.
  • Estimate instruction mix to compute average CPI.
  • Compare program execution time across designs.
  • Include area/power impacts from resource duplication.
