Module 3 › Chapter 3.4 › x86 vs fixed-length ISA
Variable-length costs
  • Requires length decoding to find instruction boundaries.
  • Often needs parallel decoders and guessing hardware for throughput.
  • Consumes area and power relative to a fixed-length design.
Fixed-length benefits
  • Simple alignment and decode logic.
  • Lower decode power and predictable bandwidth.
  • Less hardware dedicated to instruction boundary detection.
Trade-offs: x86 wins on code density and backward compatibility, while fixed-length designs win on simpler, lower-power front-ends. Evaluate choices based on target power, area, and compatibility goals.
