Role of microcode
Microcode handles instructions that are too complex or rare for direct hardware decoding. The microprogram emits μops that implement the ISA behavior.
String operations (REP MOVSB, REP STOSB) often use microcode to implement repeated memory operations.
Floating-point transcendental functions (FSIN, FCOS) are microcoded due to algorithmic complexity.
System or virtualization instructions may be microcoded to centralize complex handling.
Microcode gives CPU designers a managed place to express complex behaviors; it can also be patched in some designs, which is valuable for maintenance. The trade-off is extra latency when microcode is used, but the alternative is far more complex decode hardware for rare instruction shapes.
Analogy
Think of microcode ROM as a reference library: when a translator encounters an exceptionally complex sentence, they consult a book that lists the translation steps instead of inventing a new rule each time.
Key takeaway
Microcode centralizes complexity and preserves ISA behavior at the cost of front-end latency when used.
