BTFNT rule
Predict backward branches taken, forward branches not taken.
Why it works

Most loops use backward branches and are taken many times; if-statements tend to fall through. This structural bias is what BTFNT exploits.

Misclassification example

Some backward branches are used in state machines and are not taken often. In data-dependent branches, direction is not a reliable signal.

Process visualized
A simple two-step decision for each branch:
Step 1
Compare branch target address with current address.
Step 2
If target < current: predict taken. Else: predict not taken.
Key takeaway
Simple direction checks often yield ~65% accuracy for general workloads, a meaningful gain over naive rules at very low cost.
