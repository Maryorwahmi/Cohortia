Module 9 › Chapter 9.2 › Why BTFNT outperforms
Structural bias
Programs tend to use backward branches for loops and forward branches for conditionals.
Concrete example
A loop that iterates 100 times will have 99 taken back-edges and 1 not-taken exit. BTFNT predicts those 99 correctly.
Edge cases
When backward branches are not loops or when forward branches are biased, BTFNT loses its advantage.
Analysis checklist
  • Count backward vs forward branches in a workload.
  • Measure per-branch accuracy under each static rule.
  • If loops dominate, expect BTFNT to outperform simple rules significantly.
Key takeaway
BTFNT is the best low-cost default for many programs because it uses static control-flow structure to raise accuracy where loops are common.
