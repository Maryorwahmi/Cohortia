Module 9 › Chapter 9.3 › 1-bit Predictor
Mechanism

Single-bit state

Store one bit per BHT entry. Predict directly from the bit and flip it on a wrong outcome.

Example: loop of 100
Starts at 0: mispredict on iteration 1, correct for 2–99, mispredict at 100, then next outer loop mispredicts on first iteration again. Two mispredictions per loop = 98% accuracy.
Pitfall
Short loops suffer severely; two-iteration loops yield two mispredictions every time.
Analogy

Light switch analogy

Think of the bit as a light switch that flips whenever the guess is wrong. A single flip makes subsequent identical behavior predicted incorrectly when patterns repeat at boundaries.

Check your understanding

Why does a single bit cause two mispredictions for a loop exit and re-entry? Answer: it flips on the exit and then predicts the wrong value at the next loop entry.
