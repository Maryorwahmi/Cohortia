Size vs aliasing

Smaller BHTs have more aliasing. Larger BHTs reduce collisions but cost area. Balance is workload-dependent.

Small: 256–1024 entries
Low area, higher aliasing risk.
Large: 4096–16384 entries
Higher area, less aliasing, better accuracy for programs with many branches.

Indexing example

A 1024-entry table commonly uses PC bits 2–11. If two different branches share those bits, they map to the same entry and cause interference.

Design note

Before increasing table size, consider XOR-based indexing or tagging to reduce aliasing with smaller area overhead.
