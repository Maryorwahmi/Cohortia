Variable-length instruction formats and decode

Advantages
Large immediates and complex addressing modes can be encoded directly, improving code density and reducing instruction count.
Useful in legacy-rich ISAs where backward compatibility and compact encodings are priorities.
Costs
  • Decoder must parse instruction length and fields dynamically.
  • Higher power usage and more complex pipeline front end.
  • Harder to fetch multiple instructions in parallel.
Decoder hardware often expands complex instructions into simpler micro-operations for execution.
Design consideration
Is the saved instruction count worth the cost of a more complex decoder? The answer depends on workload and power budget.
