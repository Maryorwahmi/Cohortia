Core Concept
TLB design parameters

Understand each parameter and its trade-off: size, associativity, page-size support, replacement, ASIDs.

Size
More entries increase hit rate but consume area and power; L1 tends to be small and fast.
Associativity
Fully-associative reduces conflicts; set-associative reduces complexity while keeping low conflict rates.
Page size & replacement
Large pages reduce required entries; LRU often used, random simplifies hardware.
ASIDs
Attach process IDs to entries to avoid flushes on context switch and preserve TLB warm state.
Example & takeaway
Example
Design an L1 TLB with 32 entries fully-associative for 1-cycle access and an L2 with 512 entries 4-way for reach. The L1 gives low latency for hot pages; L2 catches most cold misses without a page walk.
Key Takeaway

Choose a tiny, fast L1 to hit the common case and use L2 for reach. Use ASIDs to cut context-switch cost.

Common mistake: making L1 too large increases access time and power; measure workload locality first.
