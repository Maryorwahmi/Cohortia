Core Concept

Blocking vs Non-Blocking

A clear definition, an analogy, and the processor-level reason to prefer non-blocking caches when possible.

Analogy — bridge and toll booths

Blocking: single-lane bridge closes on a miss. Non-blocking: multiple lanes and toll booths let independent traffic pass.

Common misconception

Non-blocking caches do not eliminate memory latency; they allow other memory operations and hits to proceed while misses are resolved.

Key Takeaway

Non-blocking behavior keeps independent instructions progressing and increases throughput in high-ILP workloads.

Visual comparison
Blocking

Single outstanding miss. All later accesses stall until response.

Non-Blocking

Multiple outstanding misses tracked and hits served while misses are pending.

Try this thought experiment
If three independent loads target separate blocks and one misses, how many loads can a non-blocking cache complete while the miss is outstanding? The answer depends on hits and available MSHRs; at minimum, hits to cached blocks can complete immediately.
