Module 11 › Chapter 11.7 › Load Imbalance
Problem & causes
  • Uneven data distribution across workers.
  • Dynamic workloads with varying per-task cost.
  • Communication or I/O delays affecting subsets of workers.
Fixes
Dynamic scheduling, work stealing, better partitioning, and reducing task granularity.
Diagnose with profiling
Collect per-thread timings. Look for long-running threads that determine total runtime. Plot a histogram or timeline of completion times.
Analogy
Think of workers as cooks: if one cook has a huge order, the whole kitchen waits even if others finished their dishes.
Example fix
Change from static block partitioning to dynamic task queue with small chunks; processors pull work as they become free.
Key Takeaway

Measure per-worker times, not just aggregate time; imbalance shows up as a long tail and can be reduced by dynamic scheduling or finer-grained tasks.
