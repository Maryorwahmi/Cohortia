Memory Consistency in Multiprocessors
Understand the ordering problem that arises when multiple processors read and write shared memory, and learn the models and fences designers use to balance correctness and performance.
In this lesson
What the multiprocessor consistency problem is and why ordering across processors matters.
Coming up
Sequential consistency, relaxed models, fences, and Dekker's example.
Hands-on
A small two-thread experiment that detects weak models and measures fence overhead.
Overview
We will move from a formal consistency statement to practical architectures. You will practice reasoning about what outcomes are allowed under each model and where fences are needed to prevent incorrect interleavings.
Concept Map
Shared memory, visibility, program order, global order, fences, and synchronization form the core vocabulary we'll use. Think of processors as post offices that may delay or reorder letters unless explicit certified delivery is used.
Key takeaway
Strong ordering is simple to reason about but costly. Weaker models require programmer discipline via fences and acquire/release to ensure correctness.
