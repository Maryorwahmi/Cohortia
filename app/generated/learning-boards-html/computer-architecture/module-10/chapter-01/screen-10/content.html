Module 10 › Chapter 10.1 › Divergence & Masking
Control-flow divergence
Conditional behavior across elements forces SIMD to mask inactive lanes while executing both paths.
Masking keeps correctness but performs extra operations. The cost scales with how many lanes take the minority path.
Example: elementwise conditional arithmetic becomes two masked vector operations instead of one scalar branch per element.
Strategies to manage divergence
  • Reorder or partition data so lanes are homogeneous.
  • Use masked operations when reorganization is too costly.
  • Measure lane utilization to guide optimization choices.
Misconception: masking always fails. In many cases, masked execution is acceptable and simpler than complex reorganization.
Key Takeaway
Masking preserves correctness with divergence but reduces effective parallelism; balance masking cost against data reorganization costs and measure performance outcomes.
