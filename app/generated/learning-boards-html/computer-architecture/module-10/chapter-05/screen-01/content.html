Vectorization and Auto-Vectorization

How compilers transform loops into SIMD operations and when they are blocked.

Vectorization packs multiple scalar operations into one SIMD instruction. It increases arithmetic throughput when iterations are independent and memory is cooperative.

We will cover the enabling conditions, compiler hints, report diagnostics, real costs such as alignment and remainder handling, and manual intrinsics for cases where auto-vectorization fails.

In this lesson: compile-time conditions, hints, and performance
Why it matters: better throughput on vector-capable hardware
Concept Map
Auto-vectorization
Compiler converts loops to SIMD when safe. Example: summing an array.
Aliasing
Overlapping pointers block reordering unless told otherwise with restrict.
Hints & Pragmas
Use __restrict__, assume_aligned, or pragma omp simd to help the compiler.
Performance
SIMD width, memory bandwidth, alignment, and remainders determine speedup.
