Module 12 › Chapter 12.6 › Pareto-optimal Designs
Pareto explanation

A design is Pareto-optimal if no other design is better in every metric. The Pareto frontier is the set of such designs and represents the best trade-offs among chosen objectives, for example IPC versus power or IPC versus area.

Usage pattern
  • Compute metrics for variants.
  • Plot designs in metric space and identify the non-dominated ridge.
  • Choose a point on the frontier that meets constraints or stakeholder priorities.
Concrete contrast
If A is faster but uses more power than B, and neither dominates the other across metrics, both can sit on the Pareto frontier. Designs not on the frontier are strictly inferior and can be discarded from consideration.
Key Takeaway
Use the frontier to reduce options to the most efficient trade-offs; choose among them based on external constraints.
