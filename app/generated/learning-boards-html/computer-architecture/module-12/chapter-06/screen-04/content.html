Module 12 › Chapter 12.6 › What is Design Space Exploration?
Definition & scope

Design space exploration evaluates combinations of microarchitectural parameters to identify designs that satisfy constraints. The space includes both structural choices and microarchitectural buffer sizes, and interactions among parameters determine where improvements are possible.

Parameter ranges (examples):
  • Pipeline depth: 5–20 stages
  • Issue width: 1–8 instructions per cycle
  • Cache size: 8 KB–64 MB; associativity: 1–32 way
  • ROB size: 40–900 entries; branch predictor: 1K–64K entries
Common misconception
Changing a single parameter always helps. In practice, effects depend on other parameters and workload characteristics.
Why exhaustive search fails

Combinatorial growth means millions of configurations even with modest option counts. Evaluating each configuration with full benchmarks would take impractical time. The goal is to find good regions using sampling and models rather than brute force.

Concrete example
With 10 parameters and 5 choices each, you have 5^10 = 9,765,625 configurations. Sampling or surrogate models typically reduce the number of required full simulations by orders of magnitude.
Key Takeaway
Focus exploration on interacting parameters and use sampling to find candidate designs efficiently.
