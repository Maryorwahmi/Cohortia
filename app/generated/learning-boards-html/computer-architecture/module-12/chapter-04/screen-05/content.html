Module 12 › Chapter 12.4 › Dynamic vs Static Power
Dynamic power
P_dynamic = α × C × V² × f
where α is activity factor, C is capacitance switched, V is supply voltage, f is frequency
Implication: reducing V has a superlinear effect because of V squared; lowering frequency also reduces power directly.
Example: halve V and reduce f proportionally, dynamic power can drop by roughly eight times when both V² and f reduce.
Static (leakage) power
P_static = V × I_leakage
where I_leakage is the aggregate leakage current
Implication: leakage is persistent and becomes a larger share as process sizes shrink; it does not disappear when gates are idle unless blocks are power-gated.
Example: at advanced nodes leakage can be 30–50% of total power, so designers use gating and biasing to reduce it.
Concrete design trade-off
  • Reducing V benefits dynamic power strongly but may reduce frequency margins.
  • Leakage mitigation often requires structural techniques that add area or complexity.
  • Quantify both components for meaningful energy or power optimizations.
