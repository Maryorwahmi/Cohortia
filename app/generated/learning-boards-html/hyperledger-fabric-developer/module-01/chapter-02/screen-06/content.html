Channel concept

A channel is an isolated ledger shared only among a specific subset of organizations. Transactions placed on a channel are visible only to its members and stored in that channel’s ledger.

  • Membership determines which peers maintain and validate the channel ledger.
  • Channels support policy-driven governance for creation and access.
  • Channels are ledger scopes, not simple access tags.
Analogy: private chat group — messages are only visible to members
Concrete scenario
Manufacturer + Supplier channel stores pricing and quality checks; a separate public supply ledger records delivery events visible to all partners.
Decisions & alternatives

If you need ledger-level isolation between parties choose channels. If you need selective field-level privacy inside one channel, consider private data collections which keep sensitive fields private while sharing a common channel ledger.

Key Takeaway

Channels isolate ledger state for specific members, enabling confidential business workflows within a shared network.
