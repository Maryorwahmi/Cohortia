What is a permissioned network?

A network where all participants are identified and authorized. In Fabric, this identification is managed by Certificate Authorities that issue cryptographic certificates to organizations and users.

Authentication
Access control
Revocation
Audit trails
Concrete example
A carrier’s certificate ties every tracking update to a verifiable organization, enabling accountability and simple dispute resolution.
Common misconceptions
  • Permissioned is not the same as ad-hoc privacy; it is identity-based governance.
  • Certificates are not only for login — they enable cryptographic proof attached to transactions.
Why this matters

Identity enables policy enforcement, selective visibility, and compliance reporting — essential for enterprise networks where parties must be accountable.
