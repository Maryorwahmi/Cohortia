Module 1 › Chapter 1.1 › What is DLT?
Core definition

A distributed ledger is a database shared, replicated, and synchronized across multiple participants. Each participant often maintains their own copy and updates are validated across the network.

Analogy
Three friends keep the same notebook; an entry becomes authoritative only after the others agree and copy it.
Common misconception
Multiple copies alone are not enough. Without an agreement process, ledgers can diverge and trust is lost.
Key Takeaway

DLT pairs shared copies with procedures to ensure copies converge to the same authoritative state.

Illustrated example
Scenario: Shared invoice ledger
Supplier posts an invoice. Buyer, supplier, and auditor each store a copy. The network requires signatures or acknowledgement so all three copies reflect the same event and date.
Why it matters
This model minimizes dispute over who recorded what and prevents a single operator from unilaterally altering history.
