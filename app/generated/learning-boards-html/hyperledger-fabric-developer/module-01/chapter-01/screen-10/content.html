Module 1 › Chapter 1.1 › Types of Blockchains
Public (permissionless)
Open to anyone. Strong decentralization and censorship resistance; often lower throughput and public visibility.
Use case example
Open token systems or public registries where transparency is the priority.
Private (permissioned)
Controlled by one organization. Higher privacy and performance but centralized control and trust model.
Use case example
Internal finance systems that must meet strict confidentiality and throughput needs.
Consortium (permissioned)
Managed by multiple organizations. Balances decentralization with governance and access control.
Use case example
Intercompany supply chains or trade finance where partners share verification responsibilities.
Key Takeaway

No single model fits all problems. Match openness, privacy, performance, and governance to your business requirements when choosing a ledger model.
