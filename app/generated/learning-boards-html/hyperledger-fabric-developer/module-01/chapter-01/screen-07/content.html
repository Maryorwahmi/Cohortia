Why decentralize?

Distributing ledger copies reduces single points of failure and censorship risk. It improves the network's ability to detect tampering and maintain availability when some participants fail.

Concrete example
If a central database is corrupted, all trust collapses. With distributed copies, other participants can reject the corrupted state and continue operating.
Trade-off
Distribution increases resilience but requires coordination, bandwidth, and agreement rules that affect performance and complexity.
Architecture snapshot
Consortium model
A small group of organizations each run nodes. They share responsibility for validation while keeping identity and access controls.
Key Takeaway

Decentralization increases resilience and reduces central trust, but you must define governance, validator roles, and dispute resolution for a functioning network.
