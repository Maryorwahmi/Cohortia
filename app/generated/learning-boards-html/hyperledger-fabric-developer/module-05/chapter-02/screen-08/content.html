Lifecycle Step 4

Committing the Chaincode Definition

Activating the chaincode on the channel

04

Collective Activation

After sufficient organizations approve, one organization commits the definition to the channel, making the chaincode active.

peer lifecycle chaincode commit \
  -o orderer.example.com:7050 \
  --channelID mychannel \
  --name myasset \
  --version 1.0 \
  --sequence 1 \
  --init-required \
  --signature-policy "OR ('Org1MSP.peer','Org2MSP.peer')" \
  --peerAddresses peer0.org1.example.com:7051 \
  --peerAddresses peer0.org2.example.com:9051
  • •Requires a majority of organizations (per `_Lifecycle` policy) to have approved.
  • •The chaincode becomes available for invocation by client applications.
  • •You can query committed chaincodes using `peer lifecycle chaincode querycommitted`.
Key Takeaway

Commitment is the final step to activate chaincode on the channel, requiring collective approval from network participants.

Why it matters

This step ensures that the chaincode's deployment is a consensus-driven decision, reflecting the agreement of the participating organizations on the shared business logic.

Common Mistake

The `commit` command will fail if the required number of organizations have not yet approved the chaincode definition, leading to deployment delays.
