Understanding Endorsement Policies

Defining transaction validity and required approvals

What are Endorsement Policies?

  • Rules that specify which organizations/identities must endorse a transaction.
  • A transaction is only valid if it meets its defined endorsement policy.
  • Can be defined at the channel level (default) or custom at the chaincode level.
  • Crucial for maintaining trust and integrity in the network.

Example: Custom Chaincode Policy

For a chaincode named asset_transfer, a policy could be:

OR('Org1MSP.peer', 'Org2MSP.peer')

This means a transaction must be endorsed by a peer from either Org1 or Org2.

Dynamic Policy Scenarios

  • High-Value Transfers: Require endorsement from all participating organizations.
  • Minor Updates: May only need a single signatory or a subset of organizations.
  • Role-Based Approvals: Policies can specify endorsement by specific roles within an organization (e.g., 'auditor' role).
  • Lifecycle: Policies are specified during the chaincode definition approval and commit process.
Key Takeaway

Endorsement policies are flexible rules that dictate transaction validity, ensuring that only approved and verified transactions are recorded on the ledger.
