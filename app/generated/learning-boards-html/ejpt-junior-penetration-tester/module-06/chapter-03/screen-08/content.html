Legal Implications

Understanding Unauthorized Access and Legal Consequences

The felony charges and laws like the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA).

What Constitutes Unauthorized Access?

  • No Explicit Permission: Any access without prior, written consent.
  • Exceeding Scope: Going beyond the boundaries defined in the SOW.
  • Accidental Discovery: Even unintentional access can be problematic if not handled correctly.

The Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA)

  • Federal Law: Primary U.S. law against computer crimes.
  • Felony Charges: Violations can lead to significant fines and imprisonment.
  • Intent vs. Action: Even without malicious intent, unauthorized actions are illegal.
Real-World Impact

A penetration tester who scans a client's subsidiary not listed in the SOW, even if they find a critical flaw, could face CFAA charges for unauthorized access.

Key Takeaway

Unauthorized access is a serious legal offense; always ensure your actions are explicitly covered by a formal agreement to avoid criminal charges.
