Fundamental Principle

Explicit Authorization: The "Get Permission First" Mandate

Why written consent is non-negotiable before any testing begins.

The Authorization Process

  • 1Identify Asset Owner: Determine who has legal authority over the systems.
  • 2Obtain Written Consent: Secure explicit authorization, typically via SOW or contract.
  • 3Verify Scope: Ensure the document clearly defines what can be tested.
  • 4Document Everything: Keep records of all communications and agreements.

Why Written Permission is Crucial

  • Legal Protection: Shields you from unauthorized access charges.
  • Clear Expectations: Establishes what is and isn't allowed.
  • Professionalism: Demonstrates adherence to industry best practices.
Example Scenario

Imagine a client verbally agrees to a test. Without written consent, if an incident occurs, you have no legal defense against claims of unauthorized access.

Key Takeaway

Always secure explicit, written authorization from the asset owner before beginning any penetration testing activities to ensure legality and trust.
