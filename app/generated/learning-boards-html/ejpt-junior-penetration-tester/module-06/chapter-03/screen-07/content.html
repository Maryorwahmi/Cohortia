Critical Warning

The Dangers of Exceeding the Defined Scope

Why even well-intentioned actions outside the SOW can lead to severe consequences.

In-Scope vs. Out-of-Scope

In-Scope

Activities explicitly listed and authorized in the SOW (e.g., testing www.example.com).

Out-of-Scope

Any activities not explicitly authorized (e.g., testing dev.example.com or phishing employees).

Consequences of Going Out of Scope

  • Unauthorized Access: Can be a criminal offense (felony).
  • Contract Breach: Leads to immediate termination and potential lawsuits.
  • Reputational Damage: Destroys trust and professional credibility.
Common Mistake

Assuming that finding a vulnerability outside scope justifies testing it. This is a critical error; always seek explicit permission first.

Key Takeaway

Never go out of scope. Adhere strictly to the SOW to avoid legal and professional repercussions, even if you believe it's for the client's benefit.
