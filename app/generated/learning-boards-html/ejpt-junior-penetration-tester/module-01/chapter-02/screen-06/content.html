The Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA)

Prohibiting unauthorized access in the US

What is the CFAA?

A United States federal law enacted in 1986 that broadly prohibits unauthorized access to protected computers. It's a cornerstone of US cybercrime legislation.

Key Takeaway

Any access to a computer system or network without explicit, written permission can be a violation of the CFAA, regardless of your intentions.

Key Provisions

Unauthorized Access: Accessing a computer without permission.

Exceeding Authorized Access: Using permission for unintended purposes.

Causing Damage: Intentionally or recklessly damaging a computer or data.

Example Violation

Scanning a company's network without explicit written consent, even if no vulnerabilities are found, can violate the CFAA.
