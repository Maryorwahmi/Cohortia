Technical Phase 1

Intelligence Gathering: Passive Reconnaissance

Collecting information without direct interaction with the target.

What is Passive Reconnaissance?

This involves gathering information about a target from publicly available sources without directly engaging with the target's systems. The primary goal is to minimize detection risk.

Why it's Crucial

Passive reconnaissance helps build a comprehensive target profile, including network topology, operating systems, applications, and potential entry points, all while remaining undetected. It's the foundation for more targeted attacks.

Common Mistake

Rushing this phase can lead to missed opportunities and less effective, more detectable active reconnaissance or exploitation attempts. Patience here pays off significantly.

Examples of Passive Techniques

  • Google Dorking: Using advanced search operators (e.g., site:example.com intitle:"admin login") to find specific information.
  • WHOIS Queries: Retrieving domain registration details like owner, contact info, and creation dates.
  • DNS Lookups (nslookup/dig): Discovering DNS records, mail servers, and subdomains.
  • Social Media & Corporate Websites: Identifying employee names, technologies, and organizational structure.
  • Shodan.io: Identifying internet-connected devices and services without direct interaction.
Key Takeaway

Passive reconnaissance is about gathering maximum information with minimum interaction, leveraging public sources to build a detailed target profile covertly.
