Technical Phase 5

Post-Exploitation: Deepening the Foothold

Activities performed after initial access to maintain control and achieve objectives.

Privilege Escalation

Moving from a low-privileged user account to a higher-privileged account (e.g., root on Linux, Administrator on Windows).

Maintaining Access

Installing backdoors, creating new user accounts, or establishing persistent shells to ensure continued access.

Internal Reconnaissance

Mapping the internal network, identifying other vulnerable systems, and understanding trust relationships from the compromised host.

Data Exfiltration & Pivoting

Demonstrating the ability to steal sensitive data and using the compromised system as a launchpad to attack other internal systems.

Why Post-Exploitation Matters

  • Demonstrates full impact of a breach.
  • Reveals lateral movement capabilities.
  • Identifies critical data at risk.
  • Provides insights for defense.

Example: Lateral Movement

After compromising a web server, a tester might use it to scan the internal network for an unpatched database server, demonstrating how an attacker could pivot deeper into the organization.

Key Takeaway

Post-exploitation activities are vital for showcasing the true extent of a security breach and providing actionable intelligence on an attacker's potential reach.
