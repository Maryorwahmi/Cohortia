Reverse vs. Bind Shells

Controlling connection direction for optimal access

Reverse Shell

The target machine initiates a connection *back* to your attacking machine. This is often preferred due to firewall rules.

  • Target connects to attacker.
  • Bypasses outbound firewall restrictions.
  • More reliable in real-world scenarios.
Analogy

Like a phone call initiated from inside a building to an external number.

Bind Shell

The target machine opens a listener port, and your attacking machine connects *to* it. Less common due to firewall restrictions.

  • Attacker connects to target.
  • Often blocked by inbound firewalls.
  • Useful in specific lab environments.
Analogy

Like trying to call into a building where incoming calls are blocked.

Key Takeaway

Choose reverse shells when firewalls are a concern, as they leverage common outbound traffic allowances.
