Vulnerability Assessment vs. Penetration Test

Finding weaknesses versus actively exploiting them.

Vulnerability Assessment (VA)

  • Goal: Identify and catalog as many vulnerabilities as possible.
  • Method: Broad scan, often automated, using tools to find known flaws.
  • Outcome: A report detailing weaknesses, their potential impact, and recommendations.
  • Analogy: Casting a wide net to find all potential holes in a fence.
Key Takeaway

VA focuses on comprehensive discovery, while PT focuses on proving exploitability and impact.

Penetration Test (PT)

  • Goal: Actively exploit identified vulnerabilities to demonstrate impact.
  • Method: Focused, goal-oriented exercise, often manual, mimicking a real attacker.
  • Outcome: Proof of concept for successful breaches, showing business risk.
  • Analogy: Trying to get through a specific hole in the fence to reach a target.

Scenario: New Web Application

A company launches a new web application. They first conduct a VA to find all potential SQL injection, cross-site scripting, and misconfiguration flaws. After fixing these, they hire a penetration tester to specifically try and gain administrative access to the application's backend database, simulating a targeted attack. This shows how both assessments complement each other.
