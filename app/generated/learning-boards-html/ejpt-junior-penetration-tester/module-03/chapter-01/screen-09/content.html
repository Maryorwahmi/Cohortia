Defining the Scope of Your Assessment

Clearly outlining what is in and out of bounds.

What is Scope?

The scope defines the precise boundaries of a security assessment, specifying exactly which systems, networks, applications, and data are included, and equally important, what is explicitly excluded from testing.

Why it Matters
  • Efficiency: Prevents wasting time on irrelevant targets.
  • Safety: Avoids impacting critical systems not meant for testing.
  • Legality: Ensures compliance with agreements and legal boundaries.
  • Clarity: Provides a clear understanding for all stakeholders.
Key Takeaway

A clearly defined scope is fundamental for an effective, ethical, and legally compliant security assessment.

Example: Web Application Scope

A client wants a vulnerability assessment for their public-facing web application. The scope explicitly lists the domain name (e.g., www.example.com), specific subdomains, and the IP addresses of the web servers. It also states that the internal corporate network and employee workstations are strictly out of scope. This prevents the tester from accidentally or intentionally scanning unauthorized assets.

Common Mistake: Ambiguous Scope

One of the most common mistakes is having an ambiguous or poorly defined scope. This can lead to testers inadvertently targeting systems they shouldn't, causing unexpected outages, or even legal repercussions. Always clarify and get written agreement on the exact boundaries before starting any assessment.
