Common Vulnerability Scoring System (CVSS)

Quantifying the severity and impact of vulnerabilities.

What is CVSS?

CVSS is an open industry standard for assessing the severity of computer system security vulnerabilities. It provides a numerical score (0-10) to help organizations prioritize remediation efforts.

Key Factors Considered
  • Attack Vector: How the vulnerability can be exploited (e.g., network, local).
  • Attack Complexity: The difficulty of exploiting the vulnerability.
  • Privileges Required: Level of access an attacker needs.
  • Impact (C, I, A): Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability impact.
Key Takeaway

CVSS scores provide a critical, standardized metric for prioritizing vulnerabilities based on their severity and potential impact.

Example: Critical CVSS Score (9.8)

A vulnerability with a CVSS score of 9.8 (out of 10) is considered critical. This typically means it can be exploited remotely over a network, requires no special privileges or user interaction, and results in a complete loss of confidentiality, integrity, and availability. Such a score immediately flags the vulnerability as a top priority for remediation, often requiring immediate patching or mitigation to prevent widespread exploitation.

CVSS vs. Business Risk

While CVSS provides a technical severity score, it's important to remember that it doesn't directly equate to business risk. A high CVSS vulnerability in a non-critical system might pose less overall business risk than a medium CVSS vulnerability in a mission-critical application. Organizations often combine CVSS with their own internal risk assessments to get a complete picture.
