What is Persistence?

Persistence refers to the techniques used by attackers to maintain access to a compromised system across reboots, after initial connection loss, or even after a user logs off. It ensures that the attacker can regain control at any time.

  • •Goal: Ensure continuous access to the target system.
  • •Importance: Prevents loss of access due to system reboots, network interruptions, or security measures.
  • •Demonstration: Shows the long-term risk of a successful breach in a penetration test.
Common Misconception

Gaining root access doesn't automatically mean persistent access. Without specific persistence mechanisms, a system reboot can wipe out your session and require re-exploitation.

Why it's Critical

Losing access means losing progress. Persistence allows penetration testers to continue their assessment without needing to re-exploit the system, saving time and effort.

Example Scenario

After exploiting a vulnerability and gaining a shell, you might add a new administrative user. This new user account provides a persistent backdoor, allowing you to log in directly even if your initial exploit vector is patched.

Key Takeaway

Persistence is about securing your foothold, ensuring that your access to a compromised system is maintained over time, regardless of system events.
