Subdomain Enumeration: Broadening Your Scope

Many organizations host different applications or services on subdomains (e.g., dev.example.com, api.example.com).

Why Subdomains are Important

  • Different Security Configurations: Subdomains may have weaker security than the main domain.
  • Older/Vulnerable Applications: Often host outdated software or test environments.
  • Expanded Attack Surface: Each subdomain is a potential new entry point.
  • Hidden Functionality: May expose internal tools or APIs not meant for public access.
Key Takeaway

Subdomain enumeration uncovers hidden parts of an organization's web presence, significantly increasing the chances of finding vulnerabilities.

Common Subdomain Examples

  • dev.example.com (Development environment)
  • test.example.com (Testing environment)
  • api.example.com (API endpoints)
  • admin.example.com (Administrative panels)
  • blog.example.com (Separate CMS instance)
  • mail.example.com (Email services)
Common Misconception

If a subdomain isn't linked from the main site, it's not relevant. In reality, these 'hidden' subdomains are often prime targets.

Pro Tip

Always re-run technology fingerprinting and directory enumeration on discovered subdomains, as they may have unique configurations.
