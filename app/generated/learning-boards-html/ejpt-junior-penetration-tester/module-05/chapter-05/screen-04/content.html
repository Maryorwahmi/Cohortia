The Anatomy of a Multi-Stage Attack

Chaining Vulnerabilities for Complex Objectives

What is a Multi-Stage Attack?

A multi-stage attack involves chaining together multiple vulnerabilities, tools, and techniques to achieve a complex objective. Each step relies on the successful execution of the previous one.

Why is it Necessary?

Real-world networks are complex and rarely have a single point of failure that grants full access. Attackers must adapt to new information and pivot through systems.

Concrete Example

Exploiting a web app for initial access, then escalating privileges on the server, and finally pivoting to an internal database server.

Attack Progression
Initial Access
Privilege Escalation
Pivoting & Exfiltration
Key Takeaway

Multi-stage attacks are essential for navigating complex networks, requiring a systematic approach to chain vulnerabilities and achieve objectives.
