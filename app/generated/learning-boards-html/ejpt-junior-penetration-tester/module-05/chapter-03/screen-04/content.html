Core Concept

Beyond Initial Access

Why Pivoting and Lateral Movement are Essential

The Reality of Network Compromise

  • Initial access often lands on a less critical system (e.g., web server, workstation).
  • High-value targets (domain controllers, databases) are usually deeper in the network.
  • Direct access to these targets from your attacker machine is typically blocked.
  • Pivoting and lateral movement bridge this gap, enabling deeper penetration.
Key Takeaway

Achieving true impact in a penetration test requires moving beyond the initial compromise to reach critical internal assets.

The Fortress Analogy

Imagine a fortress with multiple layers of defense. Your initial compromise is like finding a way into the guardhouse. From the outside, you can't see or reach the inner courtyard or the king's chambers. The guardhouse becomes your strategic point to move further inside.
