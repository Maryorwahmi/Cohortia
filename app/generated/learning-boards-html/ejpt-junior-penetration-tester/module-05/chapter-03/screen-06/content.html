Port Forwarding: The Gateway

Redirecting Traffic Through a Pivot Host

How Port Forwarding Works

  • A compromised host (pivot) listens on a specific port.
  • Traffic sent to this port is then relayed to an internal target.
  • Bypasses direct firewall rules and network segmentation.
  • Creates a 'tunnel' for your attack tools.
Key Takeaway

Port forwarding is a versatile technique that uses a compromised system to relay network traffic to otherwise unreachable internal services.

Types of Port Forwarding

Local Port Forwarding (SSH -L)

Forwards a remote port to a local port on your attacker machine, ideal for single service access.

Dynamic Port Forwarding (SSH -D)

Creates a local SOCKS proxy, allowing flexible access to multiple services and hosts through the pivot.
