Module 05 › Chapter 5.1 › Why lighting matters
Definition

Lighting defines how surfaces read: contrast, highlight, and shadow gradients reveal curvature and depth. It sets mood by controlling hardness and color of illumination.

Concrete example
A rim Point Light behind an object creates separation and suggests depth even when the main scene is dim.
Common misconception
More intensity does not equal better visibility. Excessive power can wash out detail and flatten perceived form.
Key takeaway
Choose intensity and softness to preserve surface cues and support the scene's narrative.
Quick study: visual cues
  • Highlight and specular color reveal material properties.
  • Shadow softness indicates source size and distance.
  • Contrast controls perceived depth and drama.
