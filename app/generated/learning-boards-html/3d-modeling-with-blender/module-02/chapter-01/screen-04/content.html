Core Concept

Vertices, edges, faces — precise definitions

Understand how each element contributes to structure and visible geometry.

Vertex
A point in 3D space that anchors edges and faces. Move a vertex to change shape locally.
Edge
Connects two vertices and forms boundaries or creases in the mesh.
Face
A surface made by connected edges — quads are preferred for modeling flow.
Concrete example
On a cube, select a single vertex and drag it. The adjacent edges and faces change shape. That demonstrates how vertex edits propagate through topology. If you subdivide a face, you add vertices and edges which change how the model deforms when animated.
Key Takeaway

Treat vertices as control points, edges as structural lines, and faces as the visible skin that should remain clean for deformation.

Common misconception
Adding many vertices always improves detail. In reality, strategic placement matters more than raw count; unnecessary vertices complicate topology and deformation.
