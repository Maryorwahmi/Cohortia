UV Map explained
How the 3D → 2D mapping controls where each texture pixel lands on your mesh.
• U and V are the axes of texture space, analogous to X and Y in 3D.
• Each UV island corresponds to a connected set of faces from the 3D model.
• UVs give you pixel‑level control for painting and baking workflows.
Example
A crate's face occupies a rectangular UV island so a logo can be placed on that face without distortion.
Key Takeaway

Treat UVs as the mapping plan between your texture pixels and mesh surface; adjust island scale for consistent texel density.

Visual: UV island cards
Island
A connected cluster of faces that will be textured together.
0‑1 space
The square texture region used by the engine; islands should fit within it.
Texel density
The pixel density per surface area; keep it consistent for visual quality.
Stretch
A mismatch between 3D area and UV area that causes distortion.
