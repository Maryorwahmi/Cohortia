Module 4 › Chapter 4.1 › Material vs Texture
Material
A collection of properties and shader logic that define light interaction and final appearance.
  • • Controls reflectivity, roughness, metalness, transparency.
  • • Outputs to Material Output node for rendering.
  • • Often shared across many objects to save resources.
Key Takeaway

Prefer one optimized material per surface type; use textures to give per-pixel variation.

Texture
An image or map that feeds data into a material, such as base color, normals, or roughness variation.
  • • Adds detail without changing material logic.
  • • Can be packed into multiple channels for efficiency.
  • • Drives micro-variation that makes surfaces believable.
Example: a single leather material uses a color texture for grain, a normal map for small bumps, and a roughness map for shiny patches caused by wear.
