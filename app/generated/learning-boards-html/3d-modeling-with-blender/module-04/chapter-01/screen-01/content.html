Introduction to Materials and Shaders
How materials define appearance and why Principled BSDF is the go-to PBR shader.

Materials are the skin of your model — they tell Blender how to color, reflect, and scatter light. The Shader Editor is where you connect textures and shader nodes to the Material Output that renders the surface. This lesson focuses on the fundamental controls that shape most real-world materials.

In this lesson
Create simple PBR materials and learn key Principled BSDF settings.
Coming up
Use the Shader Editor and avoid common mistakes with Metallic and Roughness.
Outcome
Export-friendly, optimized materials suitable for game engines.
Visual overview
Material properties connect to the Principled BSDF node which outputs to Material Output.
Base Color
Primary tint; often desaturated before layering textures.
Metallic
0 for non-metals, 1 for metals. Avoid intermediate values for real materials.
