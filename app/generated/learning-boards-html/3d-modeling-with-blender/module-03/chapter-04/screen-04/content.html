Module 4 › Chapter 3.4 › What are Curves in Blender?
Definition
Curves are mathematical paths defined by control points and interpolation. They store an abstract representation of shape which can then be given volume, converted, or used as motion paths. This abstraction reduces vertex clutter and keeps edits fast.
Key properties
  • Control points and handles determine curvature and continuity.
  • Interpolation ensures smooth segments between points.
  • Curve objects are editable without dense polygon topology.
Analogy
Imagine bending a flexible rod by moving a few pegs; the rod smoothly follows because of the spline math.
Concrete example
Create a Bezier curve and shape a handrail. Add a small circle as a bevel object to give it a circular cross-section, producing a smooth 3D rail with minimal control points.
Common misconception
Curves are not just 2D sketches. When extruded or beveled they become real 3D geometry suitable for export or further mesh editing once converted.
Key takeaway
Use curves for long, smooth details — they keep your scene light and edits manageable. Convert to mesh only when polygon-level control or export requires it.
