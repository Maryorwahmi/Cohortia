Module 3 › Chapter 3.3 › Subdivision Surface and Support Loops
Definition
Smooth large faces while keeping defined edges

Subdivision increases mesh smoothness. Support loops placed close to desired edges prevent them from rounding away.

Concrete example
Add Subdivision to a crate, then insert loop cuts near panel edges to keep the edge sharp while the panel surfaces stay smooth.
Misconception
Adding more subdivisions by itself will not create sharp edges; support geometry is required to hold form.
Key Takeaway

Use support loops sparingly where silhouette matters; elsewhere rely on smooth subdivision to reduce polygon counts.

Support Loop Hierarchy
Soft surface area: fewer loops, more rounding
Edge control: tight loops close to edges to keep silhouette
Practical tip: place a pair of loops around each sharp edge; the closer they are, the crisper the result.
