Module 3 › Chapter 3.2 › Scrape & Fill
Definition

Scrape flattens, Fill restores

Use Scrape to establish planar areas and remove bumps. Use Fill to add material into depressions and re-establish smooth curvature.

Scenario
Hard-surface panel needs planar faces: Scrape to flatten, then Fill to round border radii slightly for realism.
Misconception
Filling won't fix very uneven topology; consider smoothing or retopology first if artifacts appear.
Key Takeaway

Alternate flattening and filling with low-strength passes to preserve even polygon distribution and surface continuity.

Technique Walkthrough
A quick three-step approach when cleaning a cheek or hard-surface faceplate.
  1. Assess topology: Look for stretched polygons or lumps to decide if scrape or smooth first.
  2. Scrape at low strength: Define planes or remove small bumps with controlled strokes.
  3. Fill to rebuild: Add subtle volume, then refine edges with Pinch or Crease as needed.
Example Card
On an armor plate, Scrape to reach a planar midsection, then lightly Fill the border to simulate beveled edges before adding a Crease to define the seam.
