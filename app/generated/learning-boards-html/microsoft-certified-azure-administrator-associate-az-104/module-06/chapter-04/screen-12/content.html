Alerting Strategy

Avoiding Alert Fatigue and Ensuring Redundancy

Designing a resilient and effective alerting system.

Common Alerting Mistakes

  • •Alert Fatigue: Too many non-critical alerts lead to operators ignoring notifications.
  • •Lack of Redundancy: Relying on a single notification channel (e.g., only email) for critical alerts.
  • •Poor Thresholds: Setting thresholds too low (false positives) or too high (missed issues).
Impact

These mistakes can undermine the entire monitoring system, leading to missed incidents and prolonged downtime.

Key Takeaway

A well-designed alerting strategy prioritizes actionable alerts, uses appropriate thresholds, and incorporates redundancy to prevent fatigue and ensure reliable incident response.

Best Practices for Alerting (Part 1)

Focus on Actionable Alerts

Only alert on conditions requiring human or automated response.

Set Appropriate Thresholds

Tune thresholds to minimize false positives and catch real issues early.

Use Severity Levels

Assign appropriate severity (Sev 0-4) to prioritize responses.

Pro-Tip

Regularly review your alert configurations and performance. As your environment evolves, so too should your alerting strategy. What worked last month might generate too much noise today.
