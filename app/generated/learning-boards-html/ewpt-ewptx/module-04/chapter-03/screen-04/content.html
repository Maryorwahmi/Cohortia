Authentication vs. Authorization

Knowing who you are versus what you can do.

Authentication

The process of verifying the identity of a user, system, or process. It answers the question: "Who are you?"

  • Requires credentials (username/password, biometrics, tokens).
  • Confirms identity before granting any access.
  • Example: Logging into an application with your username and password.
Key Takeaway

Authentication is the first gate; it confirms identity. Without it, authorization is meaningless.

Authorization (Access Control)

The process of determining what an authenticated user is permitted to do or access. It answers the question: "What can you do?"

  • Defines permissions, roles, and resource access.
  • Occurs after successful authentication.
  • Example: A regular user can view their profile, but not an admin dashboard.
Common Mistake

Assuming authentication implies authorization. A logged-in user isn't necessarily authorized for all actions.
