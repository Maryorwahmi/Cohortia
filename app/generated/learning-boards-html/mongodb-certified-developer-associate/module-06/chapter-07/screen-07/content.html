Module 6 › Chapter 6.7 › mongorestore & Oplog Replay
Definition

Restore workflow

Restore the dumped BSON data, then apply oplog entries for a consistent point-in-time recovery when available.

Command example
mongorestore --host localhost:27017 --drop /data/backups/my_db_backup_2024-01-01 --oplogReplay --db backup_test_db
Key Takeaway

Always verify restores and prefer --drop to avoid conflicting documents; use oplog replay to achieve PITR consistency.

Checks & Misconceptions
  • Misconception: Restoring without dropping existing collections yields a clean state. In reality, you may end with merged or duplicate data.
  • Check: Confirm oplog.bson exists and covers required operations for PITR before replaying.
  • Advanced: Use --oplogLimit or an extracted oplog to stop replay at a precise timestamp when needed.
Validation steps
  1. After restore, run application-level queries to confirm critical documents exist.
  2. Compare counts and hashes for large collections where possible.
  3. Document the time you restored to and any oplog windows used.
