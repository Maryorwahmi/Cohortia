Monitoring definition and purpose
Monitoring lets you verify that redundancy is effective, that secondaries can replay the oplog, and that failover behavior remains safe under load.
Practical example
During maintenance, check the oplog window so secondaries will not require full resync when they restart.
Common misconception
Small, brief lag spikes are not always harmless; what matters is duration and frequency relative to your oplog window.
Key Takeaway

Monitoring converts a replica set from a theoretical redundancy model into operational certainty — watch optime progression and oplog window regularly.

Quick checklist
  • Run rs.status() on the primary to confirm member states and optimeDate timestamps.
  • Run rs.printReplicationInfo() to read the oplog window and configured size.
  • Compute replication lag for each secondary and compare against acceptable thresholds.
  • Use dashboards for historical trends and set alerts for sustained issues.
Why this matters to developers
Monitoring helps you correlate database lag and backlog with application slow writes or unexpected read results, enabling targeted fixes.
