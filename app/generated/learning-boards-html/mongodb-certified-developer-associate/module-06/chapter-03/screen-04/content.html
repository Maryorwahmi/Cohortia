Core Concept
What triggers an election?
An election starts when the primary becomes unreachable to a majority or when an admin steps it down.
Primary unreachable
Network partition, process crash, or host outage can make the primary appear dead.
Manual stepdown
An administrator forces a primary to step down, immediately prompting an election.
No majority
If a candidate cannot get a majority of votes, the replica set will remain without a primary.
Key Takeaway

Majority reachability is the gatekeeper for elections. Without it, no member can become primary and writes are unavailable.

Example scenario
In a three-member set, if the primary fails and the secondaries can both see each other, one will become primary after a vote. If a network split leaves just one secondary isolated, it cannot elect itself because it lacks majority.
  • Definition: Election — process to choose a primary when needed.
  • Misconception: A single remaining secondary can always win — false without majority.
  • Why it matters: Prevents split-brain and preserves data consistency.
Check your understanding
If your two data centers each host two voting members and one center loses networking, can the other center elect a primary? Think about majority across total voting members.
