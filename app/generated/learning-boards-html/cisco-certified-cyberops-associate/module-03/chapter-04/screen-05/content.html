In-line vs. Out-of-Band Deployment

How IDS and IPS Integrate into Your Network

IDS: Out-of-Band

An IDS is typically deployed passively, receiving a copy of network traffic without being directly in the data path.

  • **Traffic Flow:** Receives mirrored traffic (SPAN port, network tap).
  • **Impact on Network:** No direct impact on network performance or availability.
  • **Risk:** Cannot actively block threats; only alerts.
  • **Example:** Monitoring internal network segments for suspicious activity without affecting user experience.

IPS: In-line

An IPS is deployed actively, directly within the network traffic path, inspecting and enforcing policies in real-time.

  • **Traffic Flow:** All traffic passes through the IPS device.
  • **Impact on Network:** Can introduce latency; misconfiguration risks service outages.
  • **Risk:** Can actively block threats, but also legitimate traffic.
  • **Example:** Placed at the network perimeter to block incoming attacks before they reach internal systems.
Key Takeaway

Out-of-band deployment for IDS ensures no network disruption, while in-line IPS deployment enables active prevention but requires careful management to avoid blocking legitimate traffic.
