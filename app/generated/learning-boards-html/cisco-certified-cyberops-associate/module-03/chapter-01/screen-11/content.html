Analyst's Role

Identifying Deviations from the Norm

Security analysts must develop a keen eye for unusual traffic patterns that deviate from expected protocol behavior. This proactive approach is key to detecting emerging threats.

  • •
    Unexpected Ports: Traffic on a port not typically associated with a service (e.g., FTP on port 80).
  • •
    Unusual Volume: A sudden spike in traffic for a specific protocol or destination.
  • •
    Abnormal Destinations: Internal hosts communicating with suspicious external IPs.
Key Takeaway

Focus on understanding 'normal' behavior for each protocol to effectively spot anomalies that indicate security incidents.

Practical Scenarios

Real-World Anomaly Examples

  • •
    FTP Exfiltration: High outbound TCP port 21 traffic from a server not hosting FTP.
  • •
    ICMP Reconnaissance: Sudden surge of ICMP traffic from an internal host to external destinations.
  • •
    DNS Tunneling: High volume of outbound UDP port 53 traffic from a workstation to external IPs.
Common Mistakes

Pitfalls for New Analysts

New analysts often focus solely on known malicious signatures, neglecting to understand underlying protocol behavior. Attackers frequently use non-standard ports to evade detection, making port-to-service mapping crucial.
