GDPR: Protecting EU Personal Data

The General Data Protection Regulation and its global reach

What is GDPR?

A comprehensive EU law granting individuals rights over their personal data and imposing strict obligations on organizations processing data of EU citizens, globally.

Key Principles
  • Data Minimization: Collect only necessary data.
  • Purpose Limitation: Use data for specified purposes.
  • Accountability: Demonstrate compliance.
  • Individual Rights: Access, rectification, erasure.
CyberOps Role
  • Understand data classification.
  • Ensure proper access controls.
  • Handle data breaches with 72-hour reporting.
Practical Example

Breach of EU Customer Data

A company's marketing database, containing names and email addresses of EU customers, is compromised. The CyberOps team must immediately identify the scope of the breach, secure the vulnerability, and prepare to notify the relevant data protection authority within 72 hours, as mandated by GDPR. This involves detailed documentation of the incident and its impact on personal data.

Key Takeaway

GDPR's broad scope and strict timelines demand meticulous data handling and rapid incident response from CyberOps teams.
