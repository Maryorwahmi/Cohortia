Subnetting: Segmenting Networks for Defense

Limiting Attack Blast Radius Through Network Segmentation

What is Subnetting?

Dividing a larger network into smaller, more manageable subnetworks by borrowing bits from the host portion of an IP address.

Security Benefit: Blast Radius Reduction

Limits the impact of an attack by containing it to a smaller segment, preventing easy lateral movement across the entire network.

CyberOps Role

Analyze IP addresses in logs and network captures to understand network boundaries and potential internal attack paths.

Key Takeaway

Subnetting is a fundamental security practice that enhances network defense by segmenting the network, thereby limiting the scope and impact of potential breaches.

Subnetting for Network Segmentation

Large Network (e.g., 192.168.1.0/24)

All devices in one broadcast domain, higher risk of lateral movement.

Subnet 1 (e.g., 192.168.1.0/26)

Contains specific department or device type. Isolated from other subnets.

Subnet 2 (e.g., 192.168.1.64/26)

Another isolated segment, e.g., for guest Wi-Fi or servers. Requires routing to communicate with Subnet 1.

Common Mistake: Ignoring Internal Traffic

Many breaches involve lateral movement *within* the network. Effective internal segmentation and monitoring are as important as perimeter defense.
