Pull-Up vs Pull-Down
Compare when to use each and why
Pull-Up
Idle = HIGH. Switch connects pin to ground when pressed. Common on LaunchPad built-in switches. Internal pull-up can be enabled in software.
Example: PF4 reads HIGH at rest; press the active-low switch and it reads LOW.
When to use
When board design or signal convention expects active-low inputs, or when you want defined HIGH idle.
Pull-Down
Idle = LOW. Switch connects pin to supply when pressed. Use when external circuitry naturally sources the signal or when active-high makes logic clearer.
Example: External switch on Port E pin 0 with 10 kiloohm pull-down reads LOW until pressed, then HIGH.
When to use
When you need a LOW default or when your wiring sources voltage on press.
Common misconception
Thinking internal pull-ups are strong enough for every case. Use external resistors when you need a precise value or stronger noise immunity.
Key Takeaway
Pick pull-up or pull-down to match your switch wiring and board conventions; use external resistors when you need control over the resistor value.
