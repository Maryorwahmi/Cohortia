Module 2 › Chapter 2.1 › Voltage
Definition

Voltage is electrical potential

Voltage measures potential difference and acts like pressure. It determines how much push is available to move charge when a path and resistance exist.

Concrete examples
AA battery ≈ 1.5 V, LaunchPad GPIO HIGH = 3.3 V, USB power = 5 V. Use these numbers to plan resistor drops and expected component behavior.
Misconception check
High voltage does not automatically mean large current; current depends on the path and resistance present.
Key Takeaway

Voltage is the potential 'push' that can cause current; measure it before wiring to confirm compatibility.

Analogy
Water pressure analogy
Pressure in pipes is like voltage: it exists whether or not water flows. Flow requires an open path and depends on resistance.
Worked example
If a pin outputs 3.3 V and an LED forward voltage is 2.0 V, then 1.3 V must appear across the series resistor. That drives whatever current the resistor allows, computed with Ohm's Law.
Common mistake
Assuming the available current equals the voltage number. Always compute the current using the resistance in the circuit.
