Module 2 › Chapter 2.1 › Resistance
Definition

Resistance is opposition to flow

Resistors control current for predictable behavior. They are crucial when interfacing LEDs, sensors, and microcontroller pins.

Series resistor for LED
Example calculation: For a 3.3 V source and LED forward voltage 2.0 V, to obtain 10 mA use R = (3.3 − 2.0) / 0.010 ≈ 130 Ω. Choose a standard 150 Ω or 220 Ω to be safe.
Why use larger standard values
Picking a slightly larger resistor reduces current and protects pins when exact calculated values are not available as standard resistor sizes.
Common mistake
Connecting LEDs without a resistor lets the LED draw uncontrolled current and risks both LED and microcontroller damage.

Pulls for inputs

Key Takeaway
Resistors make current predictable and protect both the source and the load. Use them intentionally.
