Module 2 › Chapter 2.1 › Current
Definition

Current is charge flow

Current, measured in amperes, tells you how much electric charge passes a point per second. Use the water-in-pipe analogy to keep this idea clear.

Concrete figures
Small LEDs: ~10 mA. Motors: hundreds of mA or more. GPIO pins: about 8 mA safe per pin on the TM4C123G family.
Measure indirectly
If you measure voltage across a resistor, compute current with I = V / R. This is often safer than trying to break the circuit to measure current directly.
Misconception check
Do not assume current is unlimited because voltage is present. Limits come from the path, resistances, and device ratings.
Practical example
If a 220 ohm resistor is connected to a 3.3 V pin, the current is I = 3.3 / 220 ≈ 0.015 A, or 15 mA. That is higher than the 8 mA safe guideline for a single TM4C123G GPIO pin, so you would choose a larger resistor or use a transistor driver to protect the pin.
Key Takeaway
Always compare calculated current with the device's safe current limit before connecting components directly to GPIO pins.
Quick checklist
  • Compute expected current with Ohm's Law.
  • Compare with per-pin and total device limits.
  • Use larger resistors or drivers when necessary.
