Transistor = Switch
Use the water‑gate analogy to see how gate voltage controls flow.

A transistor is controlled by a gate; when the gate voltage is high the transistor conducts, when low it blocks.

Digital design uses these two stable states to represent 1 and 0 reliably across billions of switches.

Misconception: it is not used as a gradual dimmer in digital logic; the design assumes sharp on/off.

Example
A button press drives gate HIGH, allowing current and producing a logical 1 at the input pin.
Common mistake
Assuming intermediate voltages are valid logic states in digital circuits.
Analogy: Water‑gate
Gate input is the lever. Flow maps to current. HIGH opens, LOW closes.
HIGH
Gate voltage present, transistor conducts, logical 1.
LOW
Gate voltage absent, transistor blocks, logical 0.
Key Takeaway

Treat transistors as binary switches to reason about logic and timing reliably.
