D Flip‑Flop and Registers
Edge‑triggered storage is the basis of registers and counters.

The D flip‑flop copies D to Q only on a rising clock edge, then holds Q steady.

Registers are groups of flip‑flops storing multi‑bit values synchronously across the processor clock.

Misconception: flip‑flops do not continuously mirror D; they latch on clock transitions.

Example
Program counter bits are stored in flip‑flops and update together on a clock edge.
Check
Trace D before clock, clock edge, and Q after edge to verify behavior.
Stepwise trace

1) Observe D while clock is low. 2) Clock rises. 3) Q updates to the D value at that moment and holds.

This stepwise view prevents assuming continuous behavior and clarifies timing requirements.

Key Takeaway

Flip‑flops provide synchronous storage; groups of them form the registers your software reads and writes.
