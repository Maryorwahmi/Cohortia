Module 4 › Chapter 4.2 › AI Controller: the AI's brain
Definition
The AI Controller interprets perception, makes decisions, requests movement, and manages state variables separate from the Pawn.
• Place perception bindings and state enums on the Controller for reuse.
• Use controller BeginPlay to initialize patrol routines and timers.
• Keep Pawn focused on visuals: meshes, animation, and collision only.
Concrete example
Create BP_EnemyAIController and assign it in BP_EnemyAI details. Put StartPatrol and perception binding in the controller so different pawns can share the same behavior.
Common mistake
Putting pathfinding and OnTargetPerceptionUpdated logic in the Pawn makes the behavior harder to reuse across different enemy types.
Role diagram (card-first)
Controller
  • Bind AIPerceptionComponent events
  • Manage E_AI_State and patrol index
  • Call AI Move To and Stop Movement
Pawn / Character
  • Static Mesh / Skeletal Mesh
  • Capsule Collision and animation
  • Not responsible for perception bindings
Takeaway
Controllers make logic reusable and testing easier.
Pro tip
Use log prints on controller events to confirm behavior is executed from the controller, not the pawn.
Watch out
Avoid duplicating perception bindings in both controller and pawn; it creates confusing callbacks.
