Module 8 › RTO & RPO
Definitions
RTO — acceptable downtime. RPO — acceptable data loss window.
RTO (Recovery Time Objective)
How long you can be offline before business impact is unacceptable. Drives how quickly you must promote a standby or restore.
RPO (Recovery Point Objective)
How much data loss is tolerable, measured in time. Drives how often WAL is archived or whether synchronous replication is needed.
Misconception
Assuming a single backup plan meets both RTO and RPO. In reality, short RTO needs standbys; short RPO needs continuous WAL or synchronous replication.
Mapping metrics to technology
Choose technologies based on the RTO/RPO you set with stakeholders.
Low RTO
Use physical streaming replication with a hot standby to keep promotion time small.
Low RPO
Enable continuous WAL archiving and consider synchronous replication if zero or near-zero data loss is required.
PITR & Backups
Base backups plus WAL archives provide point-in-time recovery to any earlier moment within your retained WAL history.
Practical check
Run a timed restore in staging: measure how long restore takes and how current the data is; compare results to declared RTO/RPO and adjust architecture or runbooks accordingly.
