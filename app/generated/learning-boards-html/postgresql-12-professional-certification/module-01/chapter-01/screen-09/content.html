Module 1 › Chapter 1.1 › Checkpointer and checkpoints
What checkpointer does
Writes modified pages from shared_buffers to disk and records a consistent checkpoint so recovery can replay WAL only after that point.
Checkpoints group I/O work to avoid constant full-page writes, but they create heavier I/O when they run. Monitor checkpoint duration and frequency to balance performance and recovery objectives.
Diagnostic tip
Inspect log messages related to checkpoints to see their timing and whether they are causing I/O spikes that align with performance issues.
Example trade-off
More frequent checkpoints reduce recovery time because less WAL needs replay, but they increase steady-state disk I/O. Choose frequency based on acceptable recovery SLAs and I/O capacity.
Key Takeaway

Checkpoints are a recovery/performance trade-off; tune them with awareness of disk I/O patterns and recovery goals.
