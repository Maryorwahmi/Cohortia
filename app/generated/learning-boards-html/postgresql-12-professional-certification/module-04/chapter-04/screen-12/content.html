Module 4 › Chapter 4.4 › Inspecting pg_locks
Queries
-- Find waiting locks
SELECT * FROM pg_locks WHERE granted = false;

-- Find locks for 'resources' table
SELECT * FROM pg_locks
 WHERE relation = (SELECT oid FROM pg_class WHERE relname = 'resources');

-- Correlate to activity
SELECT l.pid, a.query, l.locktype, l.mode, l.granted
 FROM pg_locks l
 LEFT JOIN pg_stat_activity a ON a.pid = l.pid
 WHERE l.relation = (SELECT oid FROM pg_class WHERE relname = 'resources');
Interpretation
Look for granted=false to find waiters. Use pid to inspect query text and decide to commit, rollback, or communicate with the client.
Troubleshooting steps
Step 1
Reproduce blocking and run pg_locks to identify waiters and holders.
Step 2
Correlate pids with pg_stat_activity to see the SQL responsible and the transaction age.
Step 3
Decide to commit or rollback the holding session, or fix the application logic to avoid long-held locks.
Key Takeaway

pg_locks reveals who waits and who holds locks; combine it with activity info to act safely and restore throughput.
