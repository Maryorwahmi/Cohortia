Transaction Isolation Levels

Control what each transaction can see, avoid invalid reads, and choose an isolation level that fits your workload.

Orientation

This chapter explains READ COMMITTED, REPEATABLE READ, and SERIALIZABLE in PostgreSQL. It shows read phenomena, practical session examples, and the trade-offs between throughput and strict correctness. Expect session commands and sample transactions to try in a safe environment.

Why this matters

Choosing the right isolation level prevents incorrect aggregates, stale reports, or unexpected transaction rollbacks in production systems.

Visual concept
MVCC snapshots & visibility
A concise visual shows transactions taking snapshots of committed rows. Snapshots control which committed rows are visible, which is why dirty reads are impossible in PostgreSQL. We’ll use this idea repeatedly when discussing each isolation level.

Quick facts
  • PostgreSQL supports three isolation levels; dirty reads are prevented by MVCC.
  • READ COMMITTED is default and maximizes concurrency.
  • SERIALIZABLE offers strict correctness via SSI but can produce rollbacks.
Hands-on preview

You will run two concurrent sessions to demonstrate non-repeatable reads under READ COMMITTED, and repeatable reads and serializable behaviors under stronger levels.

Takeaway

Understanding snapshots and visibility is the key to predicting and preventing surprising read behaviors in concurrent systems.
