Module 5 › Chapter 4.6 › Design Rules
Lock ordering
Pick a stable order for acquiring multiple locks to avoid circular waits and deadlocks.
Short transactions
Perform non-database work outside transactions and commit quickly to free locks and allow VACUUM to progress.
Row-level locks
Prefer row locks over table-level locks for finer concurrency control.
SKIP LOCKED + retry
Use SKIP LOCKED for workers and implement requeue policies for tasks claimed but not completed due to crashes.
Autovacuum tuning
Ensure autovacuum runs frequently enough for busy tables so XIDs are frozen before wraparound risk.
Monitoring
Alert on high xid_age, blocked queries, and many idle-in-transaction sessions to catch problems early.
Key Takeaway

Small, consistent discipline in lock ordering, transaction length, and monitoring prevents most concurrency failures.
