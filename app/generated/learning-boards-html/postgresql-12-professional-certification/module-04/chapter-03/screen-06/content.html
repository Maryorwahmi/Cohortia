Architecture

Write-Ahead Log ensures atomic commits

WAL writes intent before data files change so recovery can replay or roll back work after a crash.

How it works
  1. Transaction writes WAL records describing changes.
  2. On COMMIT, WAL records are flushed to disk and acknowledged.
  3. Data files are updated; after crash recovery, WAL is replayed to finish or undo operations.
Concrete example
A committed order is guaranteed to exist after a crash because WAL records will be replayed during recovery to restore that committed state.
Quick notes
Durability depends on WAL flush semantics. Until WAL is synced, a crash may lose recent commits; after WAL is persisted, commits survive recovery.
-- Conceptual log record example (not actual WAL format)
WAL: INSERT orders id=101 customer_id=1 total_amount=1620.00
WAL: UPDATE products id=1 stock_quantity=9
COMMIT
Key Takeaway

WAL records intent before data changes so PostgreSQL can guarantee atomic commits and durability during recovery.
