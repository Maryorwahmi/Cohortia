Module 6 › Chapter 6.3 › OLTP vs OLAP
OLTP (Online Transaction Processing)
Many small transactions, low latency, high concurrency.
  • Prioritize connection pooling and low per-request latency.
  • Be cautious with per-operation memory like work_mem to avoid exhausting RAM.
  • Ensure WAL and checkpoint settings support many small commits.
Example: e-commerce checkout with thousands of short writes and reads per minute.
OLAP (Online Analytical Processing)
Complex, long-running queries scanning many rows for analytics and reporting.
  • Favor larger shared_buffers and higher work_mem to keep scans and sorts in memory.
  • Tune effective_cache_size so the planner trusts cached data availability.
  • Expect long-running queries and tune checkpointing to reduce excessive I/O during reports.
Example: nightly data warehouse queries aggregating millions of rows.
Checklist to classify workload
Transaction size: small vs large
Concurrency: many short vs few long
Queries: index lookups vs full scans
Key Takeaway
Tune to the workload: settings that help OLAP can harm OLTP if applied without care.
Common mistake
Copying benchmark settings from different workload types without testing.
