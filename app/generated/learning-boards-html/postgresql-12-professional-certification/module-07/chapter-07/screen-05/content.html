Recovery Point Objective (RPO)
Define acceptable data loss and map it to replication choices.
Definition and impact
  • RPO is the maximum tolerable amount of data loss measured in time.
  • Synchronous replication can provide RPO of 0; asynchronous typically has small non-zero RPO.
  • Cross-region replication often uses asynchronous mode to avoid latency penalties.
Concrete example
If your RPO is 5 seconds, a remote standby that lags less than 5 seconds is acceptable; otherwise WAL archives must cover the gap for PITR.
Practical guidance
  • Measure real replication lag under production load, not just in tests.
  • Use synchronous replication for critical local standbys where RPO=0 is required.
  • For remote standbys, accept a small RPO and ensure WAL archival covers edge cases.
Misconception

Believing asynchronous replication always causes large data loss. The real RPO depends on lag and monitoring; with proper infrastructure it can be small.
