Recovery Time Objective (RTO)
Decide how quickly services must be restored and which tools meet the requirement.
Definition and tooling
  • RTO is the maximum acceptable downtime after a failure.
  • Automation tools reduce RTO by promoting standbys and updating routing quickly.
  • Patroni and similar tools are common examples for automated failover in PostgreSQL environments.
Concrete example
With Patroni configured, a standby can be promoted and service redirected in well under 15 minutes, meeting a strict RTO target.
Trade-offs and misconceptions
  • Lowering RTO often requires automation and tighter coupling, which can add complexity.
  • Fast promotion does not replace WAL archives for recovering from logical corruption.
  • Design for both quick recovery and safe data integrity.
Misconception

Assuming a promoted standby always gives you a clean recovery. If corruption replicated, promote plus WAL archival recovery is needed.
