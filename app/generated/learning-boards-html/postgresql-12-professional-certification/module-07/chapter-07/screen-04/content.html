Disaster Recovery vs High Availability
Understand differing goals and why both are necessary.
Definition and contrast
  • High Availability: reduce downtime for localized failures, often via fast failover.
  • Disaster Recovery: restore operations and correct data after large-scale disasters or logical corruption.
  • Standbys support HA; WAL archives and base backups are central to DR.
Example (HA)
Primary fails, standby promoted, application reconnects quickly.
Example (DR)
Primary corrupted by a bad query; restore from base backup and WAL archives to a point before the corruption.
Common misconception

A promoted standby alone cannot recover from logical corruption that has already replicated. Use WAL archives for PITR.

Why it matters
When you design recovery you must choose the right tools for the right failure types. HA keeps services running during hardware faults; DR restores correct data after catastrophic or logical failures. Both approaches should be part of a single plan, because they address different failure modes.
Key takeaway
Key Takeaway

Treat HA and DR as complementary. Use replication for fast failover and WAL archiving plus base backups for recoverability from corruption.
