Definition
Failover vs Switchover
Clear operational differences determine risk and required checks.
Failover (Unplanned)
Triggered by an unexpected primary outage. Goal is fast promotion; data loss possible if standbys lag.
Switchover (Planned)
Planned maintenance or testing. Stop writes, let standbys catch up, and promote to ensure zero data loss.
Common misconception
Treating failovers like switchovers risks data loss. Failovers need extra fencing and validation.
Why it matters
Choosing the right procedure preserves consistency and reduces recovery time.
Analogy
Think of a switchover as an orderly handoff during shift change: you finish work, hand over all paperwork, and the next person continues without loss. A failover is an emergency evacuation: you focus on saving lives and may lose some records along the way.
Quick checklist
  • Is the standby's replay_lsn current?
  • Has the old primary been fenced or confirmed down?
  • Are application connections ready to point to the new primary?
Key Takeaway

Use switchover for planned zero-loss operations. Reserve failover for emergencies and apply fencing and LSN checks before promoting.
