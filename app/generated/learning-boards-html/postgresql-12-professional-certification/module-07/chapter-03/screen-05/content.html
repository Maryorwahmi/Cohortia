Module 7 › Chapter 7.3 › What is Synchronous Replication?
Definition and operational impact

Synchronous replication

When synchronous, the primary waits for at least one standby to confirm WAL records were received and flushed to disk before it returns success to the client. This provides zero data-loss guarantees for acknowledged transactions, but it increases commit latency because of the extra round-trip and remote disk I/O.
Example
A banking ledger where every committed transaction must not be lost, even if the primary crashes immediately after commit.
Misconception
Believing synchronous mode protects you from slow standby effects — a lagging standby will slow commits on the primary.
Key Takeaway

Choose synchronous replication only when your business requires RPO=0, and plan for monitoring, redundancy, and the latency cost.

Operational points
  • Confirm sync state in pg_stat_replication as sync.
  • Ensure application_name on standby matches synchronous_standby_names on primary.
  • Watch network latency and standby I/O capacity — both affect commit times.
Behavioral shorthand
Synchronous: primary waits; Asynchronous: primary continues. That difference defines your RPO and latency profile.
