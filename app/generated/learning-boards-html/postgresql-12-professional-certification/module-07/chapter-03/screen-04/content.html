Module 7 › Chapter 7.3 › What is Asynchronous Replication?
Definition and practical context

Asynchronous replication

When the primary commits, it writes WAL to local disk and immediately reports success to the client. WAL shipping to standbys happens after the commit. This minimizes commit latency but introduces a small window where a committed transaction might not yet exist on a standby.
Example
A web storefront allowing a few seconds of potential rollback after a crash to keep latency low for customers.
Misconception
Assuming asynchronous equals safe: it can lose recent commits if primary fails before shipping WAL.
Key Takeaway

Use asynchronous replication when you prioritize low latency and can tolerate small RPO windows; monitor WAL lag to quantify risk.

What to monitor
  • WAL shipping lag and size of retained WAL on the primary or replication slots.
  • Standby connection health — if streaming stops, lag grows and risk increases.
  • Regular base backups and replication slot maintenance to avoid WAL exhaustion.
