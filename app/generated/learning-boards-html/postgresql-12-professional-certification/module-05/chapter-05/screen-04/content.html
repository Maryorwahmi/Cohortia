Module 5 › Chapter 5.5 › What is a physical base backup?
Definition
A complete copy of PGDATA at a known point; used as the anchor for applying WALs during recovery.
Why it matters
It provides the physical files onto which WAL records are applied for Point-in-Time Recovery.
Common misconception
A base backup alone is sometimes treated as sufficient; without the WAL stream or archived WALs produced during the backup, PITR may not be possible.
Key check
Verify presence of base.backup and WAL files in pg_wal or tar contents after the run.
Analogy
Think of a base backup as taking a photograph of a server’s disk layout at a moment; WALs are the film negatives that record changes after the photo. You need both to recreate the full sequence of states.
Concrete example
pg_basebackup -h localhost -U postgres -D /mnt/pg_backups/base_backup_20210930_120000 -P
This creates a plain-format copy of PGDATA in the destination directory. Inspect the directory for base.backup and pg_wal.
Key Takeaway

Base backups plus preserved WAL segments equal a usable PITR starting point; without WALs the backup is incomplete.
