Module 12 › Chapter 11.10 › Psychological Safety
What it is
An environment where people can speak up, report anomalies, and admit errors without fear of recrimination. It’s the foundation for honest incident investigations and sustained learning.
Practical signs
  • Teams openly share near-misses and root causes.
  • Post-mortems focus on processes and systems, not individuals.
  • People volunteer improvements and training without fear.
Common misconception
Psychological safety is not lack of accountability. It separates learning from blame so teams can correct systems quickly.
Key Takeaway

Safety to report and learn accelerates remediation and prevents repeat incidents.

How to practice this week
  1. At the next incident review, explicitly state the goal: find system fixes, not blame.
  2. Ask for two improvement ideas and assign owners with deadlines.
  3. Share a short write-up of the incident and the agreed actions in a shared doc.
Concrete example
An engineer reports a failed firewall rule; the team traces process gaps that allowed the change and updates CI validation, rather than assigning personal blame.
Reflect: What single ritual could make reporting safer for your team?
