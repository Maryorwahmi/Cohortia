Module 12 › Chapter 12.8 › What is Incident Management?
Definition

Structured response and learning

Incident management covers detection through post-mortem, combining tools, roles, and documentation so teams act reliably during disruptions.

Key properties
  • Repeatable steps that reduce cognitive load.
  • Clear role assignments and communication paths.
  • Actionable post-incident learning and tracked fixes.
Key Takeaway

Organize response so fixing and learning are both fast and reliable.

Analogy: Fire drill

A fire drill prepares people so that when the alarm sounds, everyone moves quickly and safely. Incident management is the operational equivalent: rehearsed roles and documented steps reduce panic and speed restoration.

Concrete example

If a payment service returns 5xx errors, an alert triggers, the incident commander is assigned, and a runbook is followed to check recent deployments and logs, leading to a rollback if needed.

Common misconception

Fixing the problem immediately means all work is done. In fact, the post-mortem is required to prevent recurrence and to create measurable action items.
