The Policy Service Node (PSN) Persona

The Workhorse for AAA Requests

PSN Core Function

  • Handles all Authentication, Authorization, and Accounting (AAA) requests.
  • Communicates with Network Access Devices (NADs) like switches, routers, and WLCs.
  • Uses RADIUS or TACACS+ protocols for communication.
  • Enforces policies defined in ISE in real-time.

Importance of Multiple PSNs

  • High Availability: Ensures continuous service even if one PSN fails.
  • Scalability: Distributes the load of AAA requests across multiple nodes.
  • Performance: Reduces latency by placing PSNs closer to NADs.
  • Resilience: Prevents a single point of failure for network access.

Example: Campus Network Deployment

In a large university campus, multiple PSNs would be deployed across different buildings or network segments. This ensures that students and staff can authenticate quickly and reliably, regardless of their physical location, even during peak usage times.

Key Takeaway

The PSN is the primary engine for policy enforcement, processing all AAA requests. Deploying multiple PSNs is essential for achieving high availability, scalability, and optimal performance in enterprise networks.

Misconception: PSN as a Firewall

While PSNs enforce access, they are not firewalls. They make access decisions based on identity and policy, but the actual packet filtering and traffic inspection are handled by other network devices like firewalls or the NADs themselves.
