IDS Placement

An IDS is typically deployed alongside firewalls to provide an additional layer of visibility, monitoring traffic that has already passed initial defenses.

  • •**Location:** Often behind the perimeter firewall, monitoring internal network segments.
  • •**Purpose:** Detects internal threats or sophisticated external attacks that bypassed the firewall.
  • •**Benefit:** Adds visibility without impacting traffic flow.
Example

An IDS monitors traffic between the DMZ and the internal LAN, looking for suspicious lateral movement or data exfiltration attempts.

IPS Placement

An IPS is often placed directly behind the perimeter firewall, inspecting traffic before it reaches internal networks to actively prevent threats.

  • •**Location:** In-line, typically at the network perimeter or between critical segments.
  • •**Purpose:** Acts as a final line of defense against known exploits and emerging threats.
  • •**Benefit:** Provides real-time, active threat mitigation.
Example

A NIPS is placed directly after the internet-facing firewall to block malicious traffic before it can enter the DMZ or internal network.

Key Takeaway

Strategic placement of IDS and IPS, often in conjunction with firewalls, creates a robust, multi-layered defense-in-depth strategy, maximizing both detection and prevention capabilities.
