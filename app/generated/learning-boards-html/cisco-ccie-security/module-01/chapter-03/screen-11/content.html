Digital Certificates and Certificate Authorities

Binding Identities to Public Keys for Verified Trust

Digital Certificate

An electronic document that cryptographically binds a public key to an identity (e.g., a person, organization, or server). It acts as a digital passport, verifying ownership of a public key.

Key Information

Contains public key, identity information, issuer's digital signature, and validity period.

Certificate Authority (CA)

A trusted third party that issues and manages digital certificates. CAs are central to PKI, vouching for the authenticity of public keys.

Role in Trust

Browsers and operating systems have pre-installed trust in root CAs to validate certificates.

Verification Process (Example: HTTPS)

When connecting to an HTTPS website, your browser receives the server's certificate. It then verifies the certificate's digital signature using the CA's public key from its trust store. If valid, trust is established, and a secure connection can proceed.

Real-world Connection

This process ensures you are communicating with the legitimate website and not an imposter.

Key Takeaway

Digital certificates and CAs are fundamental PKI components, enabling the verification of identities and public keys for secure online interactions.
