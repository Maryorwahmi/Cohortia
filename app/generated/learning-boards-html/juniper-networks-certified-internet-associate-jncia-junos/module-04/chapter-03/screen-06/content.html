Module 4 › Chapter 4.3 › Distance-Vector
How it works

Each router periodically sends its full routing table to directly connected neighbors. Neighbors add their own distance and update their tables.

Routers only know distance (metric) and next-hop, not the full path.
Common issues: slow convergence and count-to-infinity loops.
Practical note

Use distance-vector in small or simple networks where ease of setup matters more than scalability.

Example & Mitigations

RIP periodically exchanges route tables. To mitigate loops, protocols use techniques like split horizon, poison reverse, and limiting maximum hop counts.

Misconception
It is incorrect to assume distance-vector never converges; it does, but often more slowly and with more potential for transient loops than link-state.
Check your understanding
If routes increment their metric slowly after a failure, consider distance-vector behavior rather than a link-state SPF problem.
