Why user accounts and authentication matter
Accounts provide identity; authentication confirms it; classes limit what identities can do.
  • Unique accounts make actions attributable for troubleshooting and audits.
  • Authentication prevents unauthorized access and reduces the chance of accidental misconfiguration.
  • Authorization via user classes enforces least privilege and reduces blast radius of mistakes.
  • Centralized servers reduce administrative overhead and ensure consistent policies across many devices.
Example
Two people sharing one account prevents auditing. Unique accounts link changes to an individual for accountability.
Common mistake
Leaving default or weak passwords or failing to disable inactive accounts.
Key Takeaway

Treat accounts as identities: assign unique users, validate them, and restrict their permissions to what they need.

Operational impact
Better access controls reduce mean time to repair and make security investigations straightforward.
Reflection
When scaling beyond a handful of devices, plan for centralized authentication to avoid inconsistent policies.
