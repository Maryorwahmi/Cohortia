Module 6 › Chapter 6.5 › VPN Definition
Definition & benefits

A VPN creates an encrypted tunnel so private networks can exchange data over public infrastructure. Benefits include confidentiality, integrity, and the ability to centralize resources securely.

Analogy
Postcard vs sealed envelope: encryption hides content; integrity detects tampering.
Concrete example
Nightly backup replication over a site-to-site tunnel ensures backups traverse the internet encrypted end-to-end.
Common mistake
Assuming a VPN is equivalent to a private circuit; a VPN secures packets cryptographically but still uses shared networks underneath.
Why this matters operationally
  • Design: decide which subnets need tunnel protection and which can remain public.
  • Performance: encryption adds CPU cost; choose algorithms with appropriate trade-offs.
  • Security: choose lifetimes and PFS to limit the impact of key compromise.
Key Takeaway

VPNs protect sensitive traffic by encapsulating and encrypting it; correct design and parameter choices are essential for both security and connectivity.
