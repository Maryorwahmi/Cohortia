What is High Availability?

A concise definition and why it matters in production networks.

Definition and analogy

High availability keeps services reachable by designing for failure. Think of redundancy like a spare tire — invisible until needed.

Concrete example
Two physical uplinks in a Link Aggregation Group provide bandwidth aggregation and redundancy so a single link failure doesn’t drop the logical interface.
Common misconception
Hardware alone is not enough: you must ensure software and configuration preserve forwarding and sessions.
Why this matters
  • Reduces business risk from outages and protects time-sensitive applications.
  • Maintains user sessions and prevents costly re-connections for services like VoIP or transactions.
  • Enables planned maintenance with minimal user impact when designed correctly.
Key Takeaway
HA combines redundant parts with the right software and tests to keep services running during failures.
