Preemption behavior
Preemption reassigns mastership immediately to a returning higher-priority router. This reduces time spent on a non-preferred master but can cause churn if the preferred router flaps.
When to enable
When you want the designed topology to be restored quickly after recovery.
When to disable
When you prefer stability and wish to avoid master transitions caused by brief recoveries.
Design checklist
  • Decide on a priority convention and document preferred masters.
  • Choose preempt on for quick restoration or no-preempt for stability.
  • Test failover timing and ARP behavior in lab before production deployment.
Common mistake

Enabling preempt without testing can lead to oscillations if the preferred router is unstable.
