Module 6 › Chapter 6.3 › What is a Security Zone?
Definition
Security zone
A logical grouping of interfaces that share a security posture; used to segment networks and simplify policy writing.
Trust zone
Internal LAN interfaces with higher trust.
Untrust zone
External interfaces connected to the internet.
DMZ zone
Public-facing servers with limited access to internal networks.
Host-inbound rules
Controls what services the SRX itself accepts on an interface in the zone.
Takeaway
Use zones to reduce policy complexity: permit or deny traffic between named zones rather than per interface pair.
Example Scenario
Imagine three interfaces: one connects to the internet, one to the employee LAN, and one to a server rack. Assign them to untrust, trust, and dmz respectively. Now policies reference zones, making rules easier to read and manage.
Analogy
Think of zones as rooms in a building. You control who can move between rooms with doors (policies). You don't need a rule for every pair of people; you control access by room boundaries.
Common misconception
Zones are not routing. Assigning interfaces to a zone does not change routing tables. Zones only affect policy evaluation and host-inbound controls.
