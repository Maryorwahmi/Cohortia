Module 6 › Chapter 6.3 › Implicit Deny vs Explicit Deny
Implicit deny
Traffic that does not match any policy between zones is denied by default. This is a safety-first posture and reduces the risk of unintentional exposure.
Use implicit deny as your baseline and keep policies explicit for the flows you want to allow.
When to use explicit deny
Add explicit denies to document intent, protect specific hosts, or make rule behavior obvious during troubleshooting.
Concrete example
You might add an explicit deny for a compromised IP above your general permit-all rule to ensure that host is always blocked and to make that intention visible in the policy list.
Common mistake
Placing an explicit deny below a broad permit makes it ineffective. Always consider order when adding deny entries.
