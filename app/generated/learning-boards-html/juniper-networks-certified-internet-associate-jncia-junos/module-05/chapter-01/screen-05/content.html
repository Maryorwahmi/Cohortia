Module 05 › Chapter 5.1 › Import vs Export Policies
Import Policy
Filters routes before they are installed into the local RIB.
• Use to reject or normalize routes learned from peers.
• Example: reject routes with a specific community from a neighbor to prevent them entering your RIB.
• Practical tip: use import policies to enforce operational boundaries and reduce unexpected routing entries.
Misconception
Applying an export policy when you meant to stop routes from entering your RIB will not prevent local installation. Direction must match intent.
Export Policy
Filters routes taken from the local RIB before advertising to neighbors.
• Use to limit what a neighbor learns, advertise summaries, or set outgoing attributes.
• Example: advertise only an aggregate prefix to an external peer to hide internal detail.
• Practical tip: export policies help protect peers from unnecessary RIB growth and control what reaches customers or transit.
Key takeaway
Pick import to protect your RIB; pick export to control what peers learn.
