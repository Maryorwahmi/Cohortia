Module 05 › Chapter 5.1 › Why routing policies are necessary
Definition
Routing policy: operator-defined rules that filter, modify, or permit routes beyond protocol defaults.
• Use case: prevent internal prefixes from leaking to external peers by exporting only a summary prefix.
• Use case: prefer one BGP path using set local-preference to influence inbound traffic.
• Misconception: policies are just for security. In truth, they are also for traffic engineering and stability.
Analogy
A package-sorting facility uses labels to accept, reject, or relabel packages; routing policies do the same for routes.
Concrete example
You operate an AS with internal prefixes. When peering with an external BGP neighbor for transit, advertise only a summarized default or specific prefixes to avoid sharing full internal addressing.
Why it matters
Prevents route leaks, reduces table size for peers, and enforces policy-driven traffic flows.
Common mistake
Relying solely on protocol controls and assuming neighbors will not advertise unwanted routes; always use explicit policies for boundaries.
Key takeaway
Policies are essential for precise control—design them to express intent and protect your network.
