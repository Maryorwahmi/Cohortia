Introduction to Routing Policy
How Junos OS shapes which routes the router accepts and advertises

Routing policies test route attributes and apply an action. They are global statements that you attach to import or export directions. Think of them as the router's gatekeepers: they inspect labels and decide accept, reject, or modify.

In this lesson
  • Why policies are necessary for filtering and traffic engineering.
  • Import vs export application points and their practical uses.
  • Policy structure, sequential processing, and common mistakes to avoid.
Explore Key Topics
Import Policies
Control which routes enter the RIB from peers.
Export Policies
Decide which local routes you advertise to neighbors.
Terms & Actions
Match criteria and then statements that accept, reject, or set attributes.
Policy as a sorting facility
Routes arrive with labels; match criteria sort them and actions determine their fate.
Scenario
Only advertise a summarized prefix to an external peer to limit table size and exposure.
Outcome
Prevent unnecessary prefix advertisement and keep peering relationships tidy.
Quick note
Remember the implicit reject: if no term matches, the route is rejected unless you explicitly accept unmatched routes.
Why this chapter matters

Routing policies are where operator intent becomes network behavior. Proper policy design prevents accidental leaks, secures BGP sessions, and enables selective traffic engineering without changing protocol algorithms.
