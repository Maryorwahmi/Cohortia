Module 3 › Chapter 3.3 › traceroute — Path discovery
How traceroute works
Traceroute sends probes with increasing TTL. Each router that decrements TTL to zero replies with a TTL-exceeded message, showing the hop address and timing.
Interpreting hops
  • Complete hop lines show responding routers and RTTs.
  • An asterisk (*) indicates no response — possibly ICMP filtered or overloaded.
  • Stopping at a hop suggests the next device may be down or blocking traffic.
Misconception: traceroute shows every physical device; in reality, some nodes intentionally do not respond to TTL-exceeded messages.
Example output
# traceroute 8.8.8.8
traceroute to 8.8.8.8 (8.8.8.8), 30 hops max
 1  192.168.1.1 (192.168.1.1)  0.643 ms  0.428 ms  0.395 ms
 2  10.0.0.1 (10.0.0.1)  1.234 ms  1.189 ms  1.156 ms
 3  * * *
Scenario card: If the trace stops at hop three with asterisks afterwards, investigate hop three's configuration and forwarding behavior. Check for firewall rules or routing issues on that device.
Key Takeaway

Use traceroute to convert a reachability symptom into a hop-level hypothesis. Asterisks point to non-responding hops that deserve targeted investigation.
