Module 4 › Chapter 3.4 › UV Seams
Definition
UV Seam
An intentional edge cut that allows a 3D surface to be flattened into a 2D shell. Choice of seam affects unfolding, texture continuity, and paintability.
Common placement rules
  • Hide seams in less visible areas or along natural creases.
  • Avoid fragmenting important, highly detailed surfaces into many small shells.
  • Balance number of seams versus distortion tolerance for the object.
Analogy

Seams are like pattern cuts in fabric; placed smartly they let cloth lie flat with minimal visible joins.

Illustrative shell cards
Good seam
Vertical seam hidden on the underside of a cylindrical leg keeps the visible surface continuous and simplifies unfolding.
Too many small shells
Creates painting overhead and packing inefficiency despite reduced local distortion.
Key takeaway
Seams are a design choice: hide them, simplify unfolding, and avoid needless fragmentation.
Common mistake
Cutting every problematic edge produces too many shells and makes painting and packing harder.
