Module 3 › Chapter 3.2 › Roughness
Definition
Roughness and Glossiness
Roughness controls the microsurface scattering that blurs or sharpens reflections. Use roughness maps to vary finish across a surface.
Authoring Tip
If your maps read inverted, check whether the shader expects glossiness or roughness and convert accordingly.
Concrete Example

A worn metal plate uses a roughness map: scratches appear darker or lighter depending on whether they expose polished metal or corrosion.

Common Mistake
Flipping a gloss map into roughness without remapping values will make polished areas look rough and vice versa.
Where to Use
Specular Focus
Use low roughness to draw eye with sharp reflections on edges or logos.
Wear & Tear
Drive roughness with grunge masks to simulate wear, dirt, or faded paint.
Platform Tip
Balance map resolution and texture memory; use tiling maps for large surfaces when possible.
