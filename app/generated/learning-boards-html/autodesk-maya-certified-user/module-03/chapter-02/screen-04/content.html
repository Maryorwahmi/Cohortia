Module 3 › Chapter 3.2 › Reflectivity
Definition
Reflectivity and Specular
Reflectivity measures how much of the environment a surface sends back to the viewer. It defines mirror strength and works with specular color to tint reflections.
Visual Rule
High reflectivity + low roughness = crisp mirror. High reflectivity + high roughness = blurred reflections.
Key Takeaway

Tune reflectivity with roughness and environment to sell metallic or glass materials.

Concrete Example
A polished helmet: set reflected color to bright, high reflectivity on edges, and higher roughness at scratched areas for realistic wear.
Comparison
Low Roughness
Sharp, mirror-like reflections. Use for polished metals and glass.
High Roughness
Scattered reflections and soft highlights. Good for worn or painted surfaces.
Common Mistake
Treating reflectivity as color alone. Reflectivity must be combined with roughness and environment to appear correct.
