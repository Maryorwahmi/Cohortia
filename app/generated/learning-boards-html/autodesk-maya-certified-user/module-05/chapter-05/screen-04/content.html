Core Concept › What is Rigging?
Rigging = controls that drive deformation
A rig is the skeleton and controls that let animators pose and animate without editing raw mesh vertices.
  • Provides hierarchical joint chains that move together.
  • Encodes rotation pivots so deformation follows intended anatomy.
  • Simplifies animator workflows and prepares assets for game engines.
Analogy
Think of a rig as the puppet’s armature: move a single control and many parts respond in a coordinated way.
Example
Place shoulder, elbow, wrist, fingertip joints and rotate the shoulder — the whole arm follows due to parent-child relationships.
Key Takeaway
Rigging lets you control complex deformations through a manageable set of joints and controls.
Common Mistake
Ignoring joint placement — even a good skinning workflow won’t fix a poorly positioned joint.
Check Your Understanding
Which comes first on a new character: place joints or bind skin? (Place joints.)
