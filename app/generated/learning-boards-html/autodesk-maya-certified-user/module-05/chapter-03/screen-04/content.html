Module 5 › Chapter 5.3 › What these principles accomplish
Definition
These principles make motion readable: deformation, preparation, and secondary motion communicate weight and intention.
• Squash & Stretch shows elasticity and impact by deforming an object's silhouette while preserving apparent volume.
• Anticipation cues the audience with a small opposing action so the main motion feels deliberate.
• Follow Through and Overlapping Action stagger motion so parts continue and settle after the main action.
Key Takeaway

Treat these rules as intentional choices: pick amounts and timing that match material and emotion.

Concrete example — compare a heavy vs light ball
Heavy ball
Minimal squash on impact, short anticipation, subtle follow through—feels dense and sluggish.
Light rubber ball
Pronounced stretch on launch, bigger squash at impact, longer rebound and wobble during follow-through.
Common misconception: bigger deformation always improves animation. Instead, deformation must match intent—exaggeration supports communication, not arbitrary distortion.
