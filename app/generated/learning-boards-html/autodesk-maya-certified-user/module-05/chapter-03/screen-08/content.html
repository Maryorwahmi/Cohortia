Module 5 › Chapter 5.3 › Anticipation
Definition & timing
A preparatory motion that readies the audience and adds weight to the main action.
• Anticipation is usually short: 1–4 frames for snappy moves, longer for dramatic build-ups.
• It often moves opposite the main action: a small recoil before a forward thrust or a crouch before a jump.
• It can include deformation (slight squash) to suggest stored energy.
Why it matters

Anticipation prevents actions from feeling sudden and gives the viewer time to process intent.

Examples
Jump
Downward translation plus slight compression 2–4 frames before upward launch.
Punch
Arm pulls slightly back across 1–3 frames before a fast forward thrust.
Misconception: anticipation must be large. Subtle, well-timed cues are often more convincing than long wind-ups.
