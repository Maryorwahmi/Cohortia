Animation Principles in Practice
Squash & Stretch • Anticipation • Follow Through

These three principles let you shape how an audience reads motion. You will see practical Maya techniques for scale keyframes, timing, and offsetting curves so motion reads with weight and personality. Examples include a deformable bouncing ball, a crouch-to-jump, and a ponytail settling after a run.

In this lesson
Understand and apply three principles to create expressive, loopable animations.
Why it matters
Clear animation improves readability and player feedback in games.
What you'll practice
Keyframing Scale and Translate, timing anticipation, offsetting secondary motion.
Outcome
A short bouncing-ball animation demonstrating all three principles.

Explore Key Topics
Squash & Stretch: use scale keyframes to show material and impact, preserving apparent volume.
Anticipation: a small opposing movement before a main action to cue the viewer.
Follow Through: stagger secondary motion so parts continue after the main movement ends.
