Rigging Workflow: The Step-by-Step Roadmap

A disciplined checklist to follow for each character rig.

1
Model Preparation
Clean topology (quads preferred), freeze transforms, and repair non-manifold geometry to avoid skinning artifacts.
2
Skeleton Creation
Place joints at pivots and orient them consistently; this foundation determines rotation behavior later on.
3
Initial Skinning
Use smooth bind to attach geometry to the skeleton; this is the beginning shape for weight refinement.
4
Weight Painting
Iteratively paint skin weights for natural deformations; expect this to be the most time-consuming step.
5
IK / FK Decisions
Choose IK for planted interactions and FK for rotational arcs; plan for blending if both behaviors are needed.
Explanation
  • • A repeatable order prevents overlap of destructive actions like deleting history at the wrong time.
  • • Joint orientation errors are hard to correct later — orient early and consistently.
  • • Weight painting is iterative: test poses and refine rather than trying to perfect at first bind.
  • • IK/FK choices are driven by animator needs; ask what motions are expected before implementing a system.
Concrete example
For a humanoid arm: create shoulder, elbow, wrist joints; smooth bind the arm mesh; paint weights so the elbow folds cleanly; add an IK handle for reach and a pole vector to control the elbow direction.
Key Takeaway

Follow the sequence, test often, and avoid deleting construction history on skinned geometry.
