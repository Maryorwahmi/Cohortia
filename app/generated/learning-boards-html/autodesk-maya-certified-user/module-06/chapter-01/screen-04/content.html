Rigging: the puppet inside your model

Rigging creates joints and control structures that animators use to pose characters without editing mesh topology. It separates animation workflow from sculpting and modeling.

Why it matters
Predictable control reduces animator guesswork and avoids corrective work for every pose.
Concrete example
An arm rig with shoulder, elbow, and wrist joints lets you key a believable reach without sculpting the mesh for each frame.
Common misconception
Rigging is not only technical plumbing. If you focus solely on joints and ignore animator usability, the rig will be hard to animate and require extra corrective shapes or FK/IK switches that complicate workflows.
Key takeaway
Rigging gives animators controlled, repeatable mechanics so performance and deformation are reliable across shots.
