Definition › Placement

Good placement habits

Place joints at rotation centers, work in orthographic views, and position joints slightly inside the mesh for better skin deformation.

Practical check
Rotate the joint through expected motion ranges and inspect mesh. Move joint if deformation looks unnatural.
Symmetry tip
Match left and right placements to avoid asymmetric animation artifacts later on.
Example
For an arm: place shoulder at shoulder pivot, elbow at the hinge center, wrist at the base of the hand. Test raising and bending to confirm natural deformation.
Common mistake
Placing joints on the outer surface can lead to visible collapsing or stretching; offset slightly inward for better skinning behavior.
Key Takeaway

Accurate joint placement minimizes corrective work and produces more natural deformation during animation.
