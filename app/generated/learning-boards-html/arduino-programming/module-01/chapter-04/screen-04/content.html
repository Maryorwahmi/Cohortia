Definition

Water analogy for electricity

Use pressure, flow, and constriction to reason about voltage, current, and resistance in circuits. This helps predict how parts interact without complex math at first.

Voltage (pressure)
Force that pushes electrons; measured in volts.
Current (flow)
Rate of charge movement; measured in amps.
Resistance (constriction)
Limits current for a given voltage; measured in ohms.
Key Takeaway

The analogy helps you estimate behavior: raising pressure or lowering constriction increases flow.

Concrete example
Imagine a 5V source and a narrow resistor. The resistor restricts current; if you reduce resistance the current rises. This intuition prepares you to use Ohm's Law precisely in the next screen.
Common misconception
Voltage is not the same as current. Saying 'more voltage means more current' is incomplete — current depends on resistance as well.
Try this
Before you wire anything, say the analogy out loud: identify pressure, flow, and constriction in the planned circuit. This builds intuitive checks for later calculations.
