Module 1 › Chapter 1.2 › CapEx vs OpEx
Trade capital expense for variable expense
Stop buying and maintaining hardware up front; pay for what you use instead.
On-premises case
  • • Large upfront hardware purchases and capacity planning guesses.
  • • Costs include space, power, cooling, and hardware amortization.
AWS case
  • • Convert CapEx to OpEx with pay-for-use compute and storage.
  • • Use reserved or savings plans for predictable workloads to reduce OpEx.
Example
Five amortized servers become hourly instances. If utilization is low or variable, pay-as-you-go avoids wasted capital. If utilization is constant, reserved pricing can combine both OpEx predictability and lower rates.
Visual comparison
CapEx
Large up-front payment for hardware, depreciated over years, with risk of underuse.
OpEx
Ongoing service charges that align cost with actual consumption and allow faster changes.
Common misconception
Some assume OpEx always costs more. The truth is that OpEx reduces wasted capital when utilization fluctuates; success requires active rightsizing and use of savings mechanisms for steady state workloads.
Key Takeaway

Model utilization when converting CapEx to OpEx to see where pay-as-you-go buys flexibility and when reserved pricing is worthwhile.
