Module 3 › Chapter 3.2 › What Went Wrong?
Scenario A: Insufficient gasLimit
If execution needs more gas than gasLimit, the transaction reverts and all state changes are discarded. You still pay the gas consumed up to the cap. For example, a 70,000 gas function capped at 50,000 will fail and consume 50,000 gas.
Consequence
Failed execution and loss of the gas spent. Mitigate by estimating on a local node and setting a safe margin.
Scenario B: Low gasPrice
A low gasPrice makes your transaction unattractive to miners. It will sit in the mempool until network demand drops or it is replaced by a higher-priced transaction. The transaction is not reverted; it is simply delayed.
Consequence
Long pending time. Mitigate by choosing a competitive gasPrice or observing current network estimates before sending.
Diagnostic checklist
If your transaction failed, check gasUsed and gasLimit. If it is pending, compare your gasPrice against recent included transactions to see if it is below the going rate.
