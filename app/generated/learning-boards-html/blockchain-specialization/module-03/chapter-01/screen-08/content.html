Gas and fee components
Gas unit: a measurement of computational steps.
Gas limit: the maximum gas a transaction is allowed to consume.
Gas price: Ether amount offered per unit of gas.
Total fee: gas used multiplied by gas price, paid in Ether.
Concrete example
If a function costs 50,000 gas and the gas price is set to 20 units of Ether-denominated value per gas, the user pays Ether equivalent of 50,000 times that gas price. Insufficient gas limit causes failure but still consumes gas used so far.
Why gas exists
Gas prevents infinite loops and spam by attaching a cost to computation. It also aligns incentives by paying nodes for executing EVM operations.
Key Takeaway

Think of gas as the meter and Ether as the fuel. Together they make computation accountable and economically constrained.
