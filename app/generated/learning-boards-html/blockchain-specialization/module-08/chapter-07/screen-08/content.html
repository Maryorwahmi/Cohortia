Governance Architecture

DAOs: community decision engines

Token-holder voting, multisig execution, and governance tools together form the governance architecture.

Core components
  • Proposal & Voting: Mechanism for token holders to submit and decide on protocol changes.
  • Execution: On-chain modules, multisigs, or timelocks carry out approved actions.
  • Safeguards: Timelocks and multisigs reduce single-point-of-failure risk.
Concrete Example
A community proposal requests 10,000 tokens for a developer grant. The DAO votes, meets quorum, and the multisig executes the transfer after a three-day timelock.
Common Mistake
Handing upgrade power to a single key without a DAO or multisig creates centralization and risk.
Roles & Tools
Token Holders

Right to propose and vote. Governance power scales with token allocation or delegated votes.

Multisig / Timelock

Execute governance outcomes with multiple signers and enforced delays to allow review.

Snapshot

Off-chain voting tool to gather governance intent with low cost.

Tally

Governance UI to display proposals, results, and help bridge off-chain votes to on-chain execution plans.

Key Takeaway

DAOs align protocol direction with stakeholders, but need multisigs, timelocks and clear roles to remain secure.
