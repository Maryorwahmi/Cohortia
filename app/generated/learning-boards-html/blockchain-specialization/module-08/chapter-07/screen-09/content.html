Upgrade Authorization

Multisigs, timelocks, and safe approvals

Use staged approvals to reduce upgrade risk and enable community oversight.

Approval process (typical)
Proposal passed
DAO voting or off-chain vote records intent to upgrade or perform emergency action.
Multisig signs
Multiple keyholders authorize the transaction according to threshold rules.
Timelock window
A delay gives time for review, alerts, or emergency intervention.
Execution
After the timelock, a signed executor calls the action and the state transitions.
Misconception
Assuming a low-threshold multisig is enough. Collusion or compromised keys still threaten safety; choose thresholds and signers carefully.
Concrete scenario
A DAO approves an implementation swap for a proxy. The multisig has a 4-of-6 threshold. The multisig signs the upgrade transaction and puts it in a 48-hour timelock. During those 48 hours, auditors review the implementation, and if a critical issue is spotted, the community can call for a freeze or prepare rollback steps.
Pro-tip
Rotate signers and publish signer custody policies. Use hardware keys and independent custodians for high-value treasuries.
Check
Decide on timelock durations by risk: more funds and user exposure mean longer windows.
