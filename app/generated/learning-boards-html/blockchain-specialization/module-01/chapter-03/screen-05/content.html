What is a Centralized System?

A focused definition and an example make the risks clear.

Definition
One authoritative server or entity controls the canonical data and enforces rules for all clients.
Example: an online bank where a central database records balances and processes withdrawals. If that server is offline, customers cannot access funds, and if it is compromised, the attacker controls the ledger.
Misconception
Centralized systems are not always insecure in trusted settings, but they do concentrate risk and authority.
Why it matters
  • Fast, centralized decision-making and easier upgrades.
  • Single point of failure makes outages and censorship possible.
  • For critical public services, centralization requires strong safeguards and contingency plans.
Key takeaway
When you see a single database or authoritative service, consider what happens when it fails or acts maliciously.
