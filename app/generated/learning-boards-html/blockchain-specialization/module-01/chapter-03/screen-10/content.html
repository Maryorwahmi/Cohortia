Censorship Resistance & Transparency

Distributed ledgers reduce the risk of unilateral blocking and provide verifiable public histories, while privacy remains a separate design concern.

Censorship resistance
No single authority can prevent transactions network-wide. If validators are independent, transactions propagate despite attempts to block them.
Example: a payment from a user in a restricted region still reaches the broader network if relay nodes outside the censoring jurisdiction participate.
Transparency and auditability
Public ledgers let anyone verify history, improving accountability. This makes fraud harder to conceal and enables independent audits.
Caveat: addresses are pseudonymous; chain analysis can de-anonymize actors unless privacy measures are used.
Why it matters
These properties support trust in systems where no central arbiter is acceptable or available.
Design note
Balance transparency with privacy by limiting sensitive data on-chain or using privacy layers when needed.
Misconception
Transparent does not mean anonymous; plan for privacy separately from ledger transparency.
