Distributed Ledger Technology vs Blockchain

DLT is the umbrella term; blockchain is a clear and widely used subtype with chained blocks and cryptographic links.

DLT (broad)
Any ledger distributed across multiple sites or participants without a central administrator. Data structures may vary, and consensus methods can differ.
Examples: blockchains, DAG-based ledgers, and other replicated ledger models.
Blockchain (specific)
Groups transactions into blocks and links them with cryptographic hashes, forming an ordered, immutable chain and relying on a consensus mechanism to agree on history.
Characteristic: cryptographic chaining of blocks for detectability of retroactive changes.
Why it matters
Knowing the data structure informs expected performance and finality characteristics.
Design note
DAGs may improve throughput but change how ordering and finality are achieved.
Common mistake
Don’t assume every DLT offers the same immutability guarantees as a blockchain’s cryptographic chain.
