What is a Decentralized System?

Multiple authorities share control without a single central server, but authority remains concentrated among a limited set of parties.

Definition
Control is split among multiple, often identifiable authorities that coordinate decisions and share responsibility for the system.
Example: a federation of banks or a consortium of organizations each running its server and agreeing on a shared protocol and data exchange.
Trade-offs and checks
  • Improved redundancy over centralized systems.
  • Faster coordination than fully distributed networks because fewer parties vote.
  • Risk of collusion or unequal power among authorities.
Check
Ask whether the authorities have independent incentives and whether governance covers dispute resolution and upgrades.
