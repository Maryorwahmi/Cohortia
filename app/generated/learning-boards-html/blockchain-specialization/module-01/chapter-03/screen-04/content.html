Centralized vs Decentralized vs Distributed

A direct comparison helps you spot where authority, failure modes, and auditability differ.

Centralized
Single server controls data and decisions. Example: an online bank where one database authoritatively stores balances.
Risk: single point of failure, censorship.
Decentralized
Multiple authorities coordinate. Example: a federation of institutions each running their own server and agreeing on shared rules.
Note: less central risk, but authorities may collude.
Distributed
Many independent nodes hold copies of the ledger and validate transactions. Example: a public blockchain where full nodes replicate data.
Benefit: high resilience and censorship resistance.
Key takeaway
Ask who holds the canonical ledger and how many independent failures would be required to break that canonical state.
Common mistake
Do not assume decentralized equals anonymous; distribution often increases auditability even while preserving pseudonyms.
