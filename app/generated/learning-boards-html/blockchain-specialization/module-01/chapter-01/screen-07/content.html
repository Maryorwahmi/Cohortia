Module 1 › Chapter 1.1 › Centralized vs Distributed
Centralized Ledger
  • Authority controls updates and access
  • Fast single-writer updates
  • Single point of failure and custody risk
Example: a bank’s internal database holding account balances.
Distributed Ledger
  • Many participants hold copies and verify updates
  • Tamper-evident history via cryptographic links
  • Resilient to censorship and single failures
Example: a permissioned ledger where departments run nodes to verify payroll entries.
Comparison checklist
Decide by listing required properties: speed, privacy, verifiability, and resilience.
Common misconception
Distributed does not mean ungoverned. Protocol rules and cryptographic proofs provide order and consistency.
