Module 1 › Chapter 1.1 › Key Characteristics
Distributed

Many nodes maintain copies of the ledger, improving resilience and removing single points of failure. The trade-off is coordination and data visibility choices.

Immutable

Cryptographic links between blocks make past records tamper-evident, enabling auditability but complicating deletion.

Transparent

Ledger contents are visible according to network rules, which supports verification but requires privacy design when needed.

Secure

Hash functions ensure integrity; digital signatures prove authority. Combined with consensus, they protect the ledger from undetected tampering.

Example
A public ledger lets an auditor verify every payment without needing a bank’s internal records, because transactions are visible and linked immutably.
Common misconception
Assuming blockchain means both privacy and immutability by default. Public ledgers trade privacy for transparency unless additional privacy features are designed in.
Key takeaway: treat the four characteristics as design levers. They make strong guarantees but create constraints you must manage.
