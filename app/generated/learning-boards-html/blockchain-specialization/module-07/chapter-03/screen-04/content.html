The transparency problem

Every transaction, balance, and interaction is visible. That visibility can leak business intelligence, personal finances, and supply chain details.

Common privacy harms
  • Competitors infer supplier or payment patterns.
  • Individuals' spending habits become linkable across services.
  • Regulated data may be exposed, limiting adoption.
Real-world scenario
A supply-chain DApp needs to prove a batch came from an authorized supplier without publishing the supplier name or full history. A selective proof solves this by revealing only the claim, not the data.
Definition
Transparency is a strength for auditability; privacy techniques target only the information that must remain secret while preserving verifiability.
Common mistake
Hiding everything is unnecessary and often harms auditability. Prefer targeted proofs that confirm conditions without full disclosure.
Key Takeaway

Design privacy to protect specific attributes, not to erase accountability.
