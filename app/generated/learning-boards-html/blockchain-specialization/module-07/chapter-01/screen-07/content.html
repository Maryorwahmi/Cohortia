Module 7 › Chapter 7.1 › Optimistic Rollups
Mechanism

Batches executed off-chain are posted to L1 with the assumption of correctness; an on-chain challenge period allows fraud proofs to reject invalid batches.

Concrete example
Optimism and Arbitrum use fraud-proof windows to protect L1 from invalid state transitions while enabling cheaper transactions.
UX implication
Withdrawals to L1 are delayed by the challenge window, so design UX flows and liquidity assumptions accordingly.
Misconception
They are not inherently insecure; fraud proofs and incentives maintain integrity, but they add latency for final settlement.
Key Takeaway

Optimistic rollups trade withdrawal speed for low on-chain compute and broad compatibility with existing EVM tooling.

Fraud-proof flow
1. Sequencer publishes batch summary to L1.
2. Challenge period begins; anyone may submit a fraud proof if they detect misbehavior.
3. If fraud proven, state is reverted and dishonest actor is penalized; otherwise batch is finalized.
4. After window expires, withdrawals can proceed back to L1.
