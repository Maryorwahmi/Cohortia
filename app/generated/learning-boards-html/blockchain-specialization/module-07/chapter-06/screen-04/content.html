Public vs Private vs Consortium
Understand membership, visibility, and when to apply each model.
Public blockchain
Permissionless: open participation and full transparency. Suited for public token systems but limited by throughput and cost for enterprise workloads.
Private blockchain
Permissioned and centrally controlled. High performance and strong internal privacy, best for single-organization workflows and confidential data.
Consortium blockchain
Permissioned and governed by a set of organizations. Balances privacy with shared governance—common for industry consortia and trade networks.
Decision Checklist
  • Privacy needs: who should see transactions?
  • Throughput expectations: transaction volume and latency requirements
  • Governance model: central control or shared authority?
Common misconception
Permissioned is insecure. Correct governance and identity management make permissioned networks highly secure for enterprise use.
Example
A group of retailers and suppliers using a consortium ledger to share provenance and recall information while keeping pricing private.
Key Takeaway
Select the network model that aligns with the business requirement tradeoffs: privacy, decentralization, and performance.
