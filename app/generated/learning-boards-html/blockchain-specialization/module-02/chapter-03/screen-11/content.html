Module 2 › Chapter 2.3 › 51% Attack
Capabilities of a majority miner
They can censor transactions, block others from adding blocks, and attempt to double-spend by privately mining a longer chain.
Example
An attacker could reverse their own recent transaction by privately mining and then publishing a longer chain that omits it.
What an attacker cannot do
They cannot create coins out of thin air, steal funds without keys, or retroactively change deeply buried history without enormous ongoing cost.
Economic counterbalance
An attack that destroys network value undermines the attacker's own holdings, creating incentive to remain honest.
Key takeaway
51 percent control is dangerous but costly and constrained; decentralization and incentives limit practical attackers.
