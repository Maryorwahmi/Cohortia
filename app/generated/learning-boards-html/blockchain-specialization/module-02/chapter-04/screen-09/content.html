Module 02 › Chapter 2.4 › Key Derivation
One-way derivation
Private key → public key → address. This asymmetry is the cryptographic basis for ownership and transaction signing.
Example
You share an address to receive funds. Without the private key, no one can spend those funds even though the address is public.
Privacy note
Reusing addresses links transactions on-chain; many wallets avoid reuse by deriving new addresses from the same seed.
Misconception
Publishing an address does not expose your private key, but repeated use reduces privacy.
Concrete check
Look up a public address on a block explorer to observe public transactions, then reflect on how the address alone cannot be used to spend funds without the private key.
Why it matters
This one-way relation is why seed phrases and private keys must remain secret: they are the only proof of spending authority for on-chain funds.
Key Takeaway
You can safely share receiving addresses. Never share private keys or seed phrases, and minimize address reuse to protect privacy.
