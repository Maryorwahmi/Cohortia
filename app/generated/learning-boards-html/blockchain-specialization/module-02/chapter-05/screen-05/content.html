Fork mechanics
Step-by-step: what changes, when, and why forks split chains.
Step 1 — Propose change
Developers propose protocol rule changes or parameter updates to address performance, cost, or features.
Step 2 — Upgrade or not
Nodes decide whether to adopt the update. If all upgrade it's smooth; if some don't, incompatibility may follow.
Step 3 — Chain split
A hard fork that gains sustained minority or majority support results in two separate ledgers and potentially two tokens.
Concrete Example
Bitcoin Cash: a contentious hard fork motivated by differing views on block size and scaling, which resulted in a new chain and token focused on higher on-chain throughput.
Soft Fork vs Hard Fork
  • Soft fork: Backward-compatible change; older nodes accept new blocks.
  • Hard fork: Incompatible change; requires upgrade or results in split.
Misconception
A fork does not guarantee success. Network effect, miners/validators, and developer tooling determine adoption more than technical novelty alone.
