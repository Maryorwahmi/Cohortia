Categories and core mechanisms
Quick comparison to spot differences and evaluation criteria.
Utility Tokens
Provide access within platforms. Example: tokens used to pay fees or unlock services on a DApp ecosystem. Evaluate: real usage and demand.
Stablecoins
Maintain value stability using fiat reserves, crypto collateral, or algorithms. Example: fiat-backed versus overcollateralized models have different trust models and risks.
Privacy Coins
Enhance anonymity using cryptography. Example: ring signatures or zero-knowledge proofs hide participants and amounts; weigh privacy against regulatory considerations.
Governance Tokens
Grant voting rights over protocol changes or treasury spending. Example: tokens that influence upgrades often reflect community power dynamics more than pure democracy.
Platform Tokens
Native tokens for platforms that run smart contracts and DApps. Example: Ether powers execution and pays for gas. Evaluate: throughput, tooling, and developer community.
Concrete Example Comparison
USDC (fiat-collateralized stablecoin) uses reserve backing to peg to the dollar; DAI (crypto-collateralized) uses overcollateralization and smart contracts to maintain the peg, each with different trust and technical risks.
Misconception
Do not assume category implies safety: stablecoins have varied trust models, and governance tokens do not guarantee fair representation.
