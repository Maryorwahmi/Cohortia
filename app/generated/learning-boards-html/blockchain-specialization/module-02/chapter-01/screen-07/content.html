Hierarchy Decentralization in practice

Nodes, roles, and trade-offs

Node roles
Full node
Keeps a complete copy of the blockchain and validates all protocol rules independently.
Lightweight client
Relies on full nodes for data and focuses on transaction creation and signature management.
Miners
Collect transactions, build candidate blocks, and compete to produce a block that other nodes accept.
Trade-offs and misconceptions
Distributing authority makes censorship and single points of failure harder, but it does not automatically ensure equality among participants. Infrastructure costs and economic incentives can centralize certain functions, like mining or hosting of large full nodes.
Concrete example
Even with decentralization, a small number of large miners could, in theory, coordinate actions. The defense is widespread replication and diverse independent node operators who enforce rules.
Key Takeaway

Decentralization shifts trust from institutions to protocol rules and widespread participation, but practical factors influence how decentralized a system remains.
