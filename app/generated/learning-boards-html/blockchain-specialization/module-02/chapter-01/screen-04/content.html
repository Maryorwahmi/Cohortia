Centralized trust vs a distributed ledger

Definition and a simple analogy to see why moving away from single authorities mattered for payments.

Definition

A distributed ledger is a replicated record of transactions held by many participants rather than one central operator. It provides redundancy and independent validation so the ledger continues functioning even if some participants fail or act maliciously.

Concrete example
Bank outage vs replicated nodes
A single data center outage can halt centralized payments. With many nodes, other participants keep the system running and can validate new transactions.
Common misconception: "Decentralized means no rules." In reality, rules are encoded in protocol software and enforced by participants who follow consensus rules.
Key Takeaway

Removing a single trusted verifier reassigns trust to a protocol and many independent actors, improving resilience and reducing censorship risk.

Timeline and catalyst
The 2008 financial crisis highlighted systemic risks in centralized finance and helped create receptivity to alternatives that reduce dependency on central institutions.
Why this matters for designers
  • System design must consider who enforces rules and how failures are tolerated.
  • Decentralization trades operational complexity for resilience and censorship resistance.
  • Understanding the motivation guides choices about openness and privacy.
