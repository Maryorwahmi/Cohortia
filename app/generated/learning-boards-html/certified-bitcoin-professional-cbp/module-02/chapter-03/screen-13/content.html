Module 3 › Chapter 2.3 › Common pitfalls and practices
Pitfall
Top misunderstandings
  • Believing signatures encrypt transaction data — they do not.
  • Thinking a signature is the same as a private key — it is not.
  • Assuming public keys must be secret — they are public for verification.
Operational warning

Never hardcode or expose real private keys. Wallets are designed to sign locally so the private key never leaves secure storage.

Check your understanding

When you see a signed transaction on the network, what has been proven and what remains private? Use that question to test your mental model.

Practical checklist
1. Review transaction details before signing
Confirm amounts, recipients, and change outputs because the signature commits you to them.
2. Keep private keys offline when possible
Key custody preserves the meaning of signatures and non-repudiation.
3. Treat signatures as proof, not secrecy
Signatures show authorization and integrity; they do not hide transaction content.
Key Takeaway

Avoid these misconceptions and use secure key-handling practices so digital signatures retain their cryptographic value.
