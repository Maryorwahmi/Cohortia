Module 3 › Chapter 2.3 › Non-repudiation
Definition
Non-repudiation through digital signatures

A verified signature cryptographically ties a signer to an exact transaction. The network treats that proof as evidence the signer authorized the transaction.

Why it matters

It prevents a sender from later denying they authorized that specific payment, enabling trust without a central authority.

Misconception check

Non-repudiation is cryptographic, not legal. Cryptography shows who had the key, not why they signed or whether the key was stolen later.

Concrete scenario

If Alice’s signature on a transaction verifies, nodes accept that the holder of Alice’s private key authorized that exact arrangement of inputs and outputs at signing time.

Practical guidance
Protect your private key

Keep signing operations on devices you control. If the key is exposed, non-repudiation becomes meaningless.

Check before you sign

Always confirm transaction details before approving a signature, since the signature commits you to those exact details.

Key Takeaway

Non-repudiation ties authorization to key control; operational safeguards around key custody preserve that guarantee.
