Module 3 › Chapter 2.3 › Guarantees & Limits
Three core benefits
Clear statements you can rely on when reasoning about transactions.
Authenticity
Confirmation that the private-key owner authorized this exact transaction hash.
Integrity
Any change to transaction fields invalidates the signature, preventing tampering.
Non-repudiation
Only the private-key holder could have created that valid signature, making denial difficult.
Contrast: What signatures do not do
They do not encrypt transaction contents and they do not expose or equal the private key. The transaction remains visible while the signature proves authorization.
Example scenario
Alice signs a transaction to send funds. The network sees the transaction, verifies the signature, and accepts it for propagation because of authenticity and integrity.
Common mistakes
  • Thinking a signature encrypts the transaction. It does not.
  • Confusing the signature with the private key itself. The signature is derived from the key but is not the key.
Key Takeaway
Digital signatures enable open verification of authorization and integrity while keeping the private key secret.
