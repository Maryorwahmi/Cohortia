Soft fork definition
A backward-compatible change where old nodes accept new blocks, because the new rules are a subset of the old ones.
Example: SegWit
Segregated Witness restructured transaction data to increase effective block capacity. Older nodes accepted SegWit blocks even before upgrading, avoiding an immediate split.
Implications and misconception
Soft forks lower the risk of permanent chain splits but still require community coordination. The misconception is believing they require no planning; in reality they need careful activation to ensure miners and nodes converge.
Key Takeaway

Soft forks keep backward compatibility, enabling phased upgrades with reduced chance of a permanent split.
