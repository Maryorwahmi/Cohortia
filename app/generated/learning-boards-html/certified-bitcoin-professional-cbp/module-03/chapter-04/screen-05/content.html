Module 4 › Chapter 3.4 › Longest Chain Rule
Step-by-step chain selection
  1. Nodes validate incoming blocks and attach them to their local view.
  2. If two blocks exist at the same height, both chains are temporarily valid locally.
  3. Miners continue to work; when one chain gets the next block, it increases cumulative work.
  4. All honest nodes switch to the chain with greater cumulative Proof-of-Work and abandon shorter branches.
Concrete example
A transaction in a block that later gets five confirmations becomes practically irreversible because an attacker must outpace the honest network's total work to replace it.
Visual: cumulative work vs block count
Misconception
'Longest' sometimes misread as highest block number. The correct concept is 'most cumulative work', which protects against miners manipulating timestamps or short bursts of mining power.
Why it matters
Cumulative work ties ledger validity to real-world computational cost, creating economic difficulty for attackers who wish to rewrite history.
