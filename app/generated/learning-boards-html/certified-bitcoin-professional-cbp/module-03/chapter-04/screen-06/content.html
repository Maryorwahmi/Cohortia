Module 4 › Chapter 3.4 › Temporary Forks
Fork scenario
Two blocks found nearly simultaneously create two competing branches. Nodes accept what they receive first, but the community resolves the conflict by extending the branch that gains the next block.
Example
If Block A and Block B appear at the same height, and Block B later gets extended, Block A becomes orphaned and its transactions return to the mempool unless included elsewhere.
Common mistake
Thinking orphan blocks signal censorship or error; they are normal and arise from propagation delays and parallel mining.
Pro tip

Treat single confirmation as tentative. For higher-value transfers wait for multiple confirmations to avoid reversal from orphaned blocks.

Visual: chain diverge → converge
Represent chains as parallel colored bars. When one bar extends, the shorter bar is abandoned and its last block becomes an orphan. Transactions re-enter the mempool until included in the chosen chain.
Why it matters
Understanding orphaning explains why confirmations add security and why short-term divergence is operationally acceptable for Bitcoin.
