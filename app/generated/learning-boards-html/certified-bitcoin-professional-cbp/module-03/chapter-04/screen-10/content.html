Module 4 › Chapter 3.4 › Hard Forks
Hard fork definition
A change that breaks compatibility so non-upgraded nodes reject blocks produced by upgraded nodes, potentially creating separate chains.
Example: Bitcoin Cash
When some participants adopted larger block size rules, they diverged from the original Bitcoin rules and created Bitcoin Cash as a separate ledger.
Consequences and misconception
Hard forks can permanently split a community and create two assets. A common misconception is that hard forks are only technical; they are governance events with economic and social consequences for users and services.
Warning

If you hold coins during a hard fork, be aware of how wallets and exchanges handle duplicated coins and chain replays.
