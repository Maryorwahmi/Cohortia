Module 4 › Chapter 3.4 › Simultaneous Discovery
Step-by-step scenario
  1. Miner A and Miner B produce valid blocks at the same height H.
  2. Nodes receive different blocks first and temporarily disagree on the next block to build.
  3. Miner C extends one branch; that branch now has greater cumulative work.
  4. Nodes switch to the heavier branch; the losing block becomes an orphan.
Concrete example
A payment included in Miner A's block will be provisional until the chain containing it gains more confirmations. If the other branch wins, that payment must be re-included or it returns to the mempool.
Common mistake
Believing that orphaned blocks indicate protocol failure. They are normal and simply represent valid work that was superseded by a heavier chain.
Why it matters

This scenario explains why single confirmations are fragile and why waiting for more confirmations reduces reversal risk.
