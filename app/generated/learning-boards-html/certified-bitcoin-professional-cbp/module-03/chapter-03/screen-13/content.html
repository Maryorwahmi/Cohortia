Security

Costly computation deters rewriting history

An attacker would need to control a majority of hash rate to outpace the honest network, making such an attack expensive and difficult to sustain.

Attack scope
A 51% attacker can double-spend and reorganize recent blocks but cannot change consensus rules or mint arbitrary coins beyond protocol limits.
Defensive cost
High network hash rate makes the needed resources and electricity for an attack economically prohibitive for most actors.
Misconception
Controlling 51% does not let an attacker change protocol rules or steal coins from arbitrary addresses; it allows limited reorganization and selective double-spends.
Why incentives help
Honest miners earn predictable revenue; deviating to attack risks losing that income and requires expensive resources, so incentives align with network security.
Key Takeaway

PoW ties control of the ledger to costly hardware and energy, making large-scale attacks expensive and visible.
