Comparison

Centralized Ledger

Single authority stores and controls the ledger. Users must trust that authority to be honest, secure, and available.

Advantages
Simplicity of governance and faster internal changes under a single administrator.
Risks
Single point of failure, censorship risk, and reliance on the custodian's trustworthiness.
Comparison

Bitcoin's Decentralized Ledger

Thousands of nodes each hold identical copies. Validation is collective, and rules are encoded in the protocol.

Advantages
Censorship resistance, increased resilience, and public verifiability without a central custodian.
Clarification
Decentralized does not mean ruleless: consensus rules and validation ensure correctness across independent nodes.
Example
A bank server outage may stop access for many users. In Bitcoin, other nodes continue validating and serving the ledger, so availability is preserved.
Key Takeaway

Decentralization shifts trust from a single operator to verifiable protocol rules and shared data copies.
