Module 1 › Chapter 1.1 › Centralized vs Decentralized
Centralized
Bank or custodian ledger
  • Authority records transactions and enforces uniqueness.
  • Finality is quick because the ledger is authoritative.
  • Risks: single point of failure, censorship, and privacy exposure.
Example
A bank marks Alice’s account when the first transaction arrives and rejects any conflicting spend.
Decentralized
Distributed consensus among many nodes
  • Transactions are broadcast and grouped; nodes independently verify history.
  • No single authority has control; censorship resistance increases.
  • Requires a reproducible ordering and tamper-evident records.
Contrast
Decentralization trades centralized speed and simplicity for resilience and reduced trusted parties, but needs a reliable agreement protocol to choose a single history.
Key takeaway
Preventing double-spend can be solved by central control or by distributed agreement. Bitcoin’s innovation is a way to achieve that agreement without a single trusted party.
