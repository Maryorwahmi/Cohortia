Scenario
Alice has 10 coins and broadcasts two transactions: one to Bob, one to Carol. How does each system resolve this conflict?
Centralized response
The authority processes the first transaction, updates balances, and rejects the second. Benefits: clear finality. Trade-offs: trust, censorship vulnerability.
Naive P2P response
Different peers may accept different transactions depending on arrival order, producing inconsistency and potential loss for participants who accepted the losing transaction.
Bitcoin’s approach
Group transactions into blocks and reach network agreement through costly proofs so independent nodes converge on a single accepted history.
Reflection prompts
  • Which system better protects privacy and which better protects against censorship?
  • What failures are possible in centralized systems that decentralization mitigates?
  • How does the economic cost of rewriting history affect attacker incentives?
Key takeaway
Analyzing the thought experiment clarifies why a reproducible ordering is essential for honest value exchange in digital systems.
