Module 1 › Chapter 1.1 › Double-Spending
Core Concept
The double-spending problem
Digital copies make uniqueness nontrivial. A ledger must declare which spend is valid.
Thought experiment
Alice sends the same coin to Bob and to Carol. Without an agreed ordering, both recipients can claim the coin. The system needs a single authoritative history to choose the valid spend.
Why it matters
Allowing duplicate spends destroys fungibility and trust, making any digital money worthless in practice.
Common mistake
Assuming copying is harmless: it undermines value and enables fraud unless prevented by a ledger or other control.
Concrete scenario
Scenario: Alice’s two sends
Both Bob and Carol see a valid spent message. Which one is accepted depends on who they trust to declare the winner.
Centralized fix (example)
A bank marks the account balance when a transaction arrives and rejects a second spend. This prevents double-spend but centralizes power and risk.
Key takeaway
Any reliable digital currency must produce a single, agreed history of spends so each unit remains unique and cannot be spent twice.
