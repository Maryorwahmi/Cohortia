Examples and limits
DigiCash
Tried privacy-preserving payments but relied on a central issuer to validate and prevent double-spend.
B-money and Hashcash
Introduced ideas about proofs and economic effort as deterrents, but did not fully solve global ordering without trust.
Common shortcoming
Either the design kept a trusted party or it lacked a deployed coordination mechanism that all participants could use to agree on spends.
Why these matters feed into Bitcoin
Bitcoin recombined useful ideas—public broadcasts, cryptographic proofs, and computational deterrents—into a single protocol that achieves a reproducible ordering without trusting one party.
Practical reflection: a robust digital-cash design must answer both 'how do we stop copying?' and 'how do independent participants accept the same answer?'.
Misconception check: reducing spam or adding cost alone does not prevent double-spend unless it also yields an agreed transaction history.
