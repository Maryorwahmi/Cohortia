Core Concept What a Bitcoin Wallet Actually Is
A wallet stores keys, not bitcoins
Think keys to a safe deposit box, not cash in a wallet.
Definition & implications
  • Private key is a secret number that authorizes spending from addresses linked to it.
  • Public addresses are derived and used to receive funds; balances live on the blockchain.
  • Loss of private keys = irreversible loss of access to funds; theft of keys = unauthorized spending.
Concrete example
You can view an address balance on the public ledger, but only the corresponding private key can sign a transaction to move those funds.
Key Takeaway

Custody of private keys equals custody of bitcoin. Design backups and access control accordingly.

Common mistakes & clarifications
  • Misconception: "The wallet holds my bitcoins." Clarification: the ledger holds them; keys control them.
  • Misconception: "I can recover keys through the network." Clarification: without backups, the network cannot recreate your private key.
Pro tip
Treat seed phrases and hardware devices as high-value assets. Limit their exposure to internet-connected systems.
Check your understanding
If you can explain who holds the private key and how recovery works, you are ready to move on.
