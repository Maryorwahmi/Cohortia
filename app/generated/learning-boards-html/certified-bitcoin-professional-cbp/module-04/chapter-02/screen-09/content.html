Core Idea

What confirmations mean

A confirmation is one block deep. Each extra block reduces the chance of reversal, increasing practical finality.

1 confirmation: transaction in a valid block but small reorg risk exists.
3 confirmations: substantially lower risk suitable for moderate-value transfers.
6 confirmations: widely used as a strong standard for high-value finality.
Merchant example

A merchant may display '1 confirmation' to the buyer quickly, but defer shipping until 3 or 6 confirmations depending on the item's value and fraud risk tolerance.

Practical decision

Set confirmation thresholds based on transaction value and acceptable risk, balancing speed and security.

Key Takeaway

Confirmations are probabilistic guarantees that strengthen as more blocks are added on top of a transaction's block.
