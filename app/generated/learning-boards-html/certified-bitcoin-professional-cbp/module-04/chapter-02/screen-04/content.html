Process

Broadcasting and gossip

Your wallet broadcasts a signed transaction to connected nodes. Those nodes validate and relay it to peers, causing rapid spread across the network without central servers.

Step 1
Wallet broadcasts to a few peers.
Step 2
Nodes validate and relay to their peers.
Step 3
Transaction reaches many nodes and enters mempools.
Common misconception

Broadcasting does not guarantee miner inclusion; it only makes the transaction visible to nodes and miners who might accept it.

Propagation example

Imagine your phone sends a tx, a nearby node relays to five peers, those peers relay further, and within seconds a large portion of the network learns about the transaction. Rapid relays improve the chance miners see it before building a block.

Check your tx

If your tx appears in multiple explorers, propagation succeeded. If not, validation failed or your node did not relay it broadly.

Key takeaway

Broadcast is the necessary first step but not sufficient for confirmation; think of it as announcing intent to the network.
