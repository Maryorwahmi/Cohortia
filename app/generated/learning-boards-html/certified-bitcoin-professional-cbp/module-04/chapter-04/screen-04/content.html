Module 5 › Chapter 4.4 › What is a Full Node?
Definition

Full Node

A full node stores and validates the entire blockchain and enforces all protocol rules without reliance on others.

Responsibilities
  • Download and store every block and transaction.
  • Validate proof-of-work and transaction rules.
  • Relay valid transactions and blocks to peers.
Key Takeaway

A full node gives you independent verification and improved privacy because you do not query third-party servers for blockchain data.

Example Scenario
If you receive a payment and run a full node, your software will check the entire chain to confirm that the coins were not previously spent and that all rules were met before reporting confirmation to you.
Common misconception
People often assume running a wallet equals running a full node. Most wallets are light clients. Running a full node requires explicit setup and resources but removes trust in third-party servers.
Why it matters
Full nodes underpin trustlessness: when many individuals run full nodes, the network resists invalid chains and censorship because verification happens at the edges.
