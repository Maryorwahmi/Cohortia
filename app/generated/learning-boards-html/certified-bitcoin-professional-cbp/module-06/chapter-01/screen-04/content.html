Module 6 › Chapter 6.1 › Bitcoin vs Traditional Finance
Traditional Finance
Centralized issuance and policy tools, intermediated payments, regulated access and identity requirements.
Policy Tools
Central banks adjust supply and rates to manage inflation and growth.
Intermediaries
Banks and payment processors mediate trust, compliance, and settlement.
Example
Quantitative easing increases reserves to support lending during downturns, affecting inflation and rates.
Bitcoin
Decentralized ledger, fixed supply cap, permissionless participation, and global settlement layer.
Fixed Supply
A capped issuance schedule introduces scarcity similar to other hard assets.
Permissionless Access
Anyone with a wallet and network access can send and receive value.
Misconception
Decentralization does not eliminate regulation or real-world governance; it alters where control resides.
Takeaway
The choice between centralized policy tools and algorithmic supply changes how economies respond to shocks and who controls monetary outcomes.
Question
If a nation prioritizes active monetary policy, what role could Bitcoin realistically play?
