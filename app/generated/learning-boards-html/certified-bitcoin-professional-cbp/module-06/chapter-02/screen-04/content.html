Module 6 › Chapter 6.2 › Patchwork Regulation
Definition

A patchwork means different laws and standards apply in different places, and enforcement targets intermediaries rather than the protocol itself.

Why it matters
Operational rules differ by country, so global services must adapt onboarding, reporting, and data retention accordingly.
Concrete example
An exchange may be required to share sender and recipient data for transfers above a threshold under FATF-aligned rules. That requirement changes how wallets and custodians collect metadata.
Common misconception
Decentralization does not remove legal obligations; instead obligations fall on people and services that interact with the chain.
Practical checklist
  • Map which jurisdictions your users and servers are located in.
  • Identify which entities you operate that are classified as VASPs.
  • Confirm required data collection and retention timelines for each market.
Key Takeaway

Regulation is local and targets service points; design compliance for people and platforms, not the protocol.
