Techniques

How privacy is achieved

  • Ring signatures and stealth addresses mix transactions to hide sender and recipient relationships.
  • Zero-knowledge proofs allow verification of valid transactions without revealing amounts or participants.
  • Selective disclosure methods can optionally reveal details for compliance when built into a protocol.
Practical example
A user makes a payment where cryptography hides the origin and destination so observers cannot trace funds across transactions.
Trade-offs
Privacy increases confidentiality but can complicate compliance. Regulators worry about misuse, while privacy advocates emphasize financial freedom and safety for vulnerable users.
Common misconception
Privacy coins are always anonymous in all contexts; in reality, metadata and network-level information can leak unless the protocol and user practices are robust.
Key Takeaway
Privacy tech must be assessed for cryptographic soundness and real-world auditability; it solves important problems but invites special regulatory attention.
