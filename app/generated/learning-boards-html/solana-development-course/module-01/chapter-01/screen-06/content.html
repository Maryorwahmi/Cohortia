Broadcast to Consensus
1. Broadcast
A transaction is sent to nearby peers and then relayed across the network.
2. Validate
Nodes check the transaction against protocol rules, such as balances and signatures.
3. Agree
Consensus selects which validated transactions become the next block and order them.
Misconception check: decentralization is not absolute. Design choices often balance validator count, performance, and cost. Think about those trade-offs in your solution design.
Concrete scenario
Picture an election log recorded across hundreds of nodes. If one node is compromised, the other nodes still hold the correct ledger and can reject tampered blocks. That redundancy protects the record’s integrity and availability.
Why it matters
Without centralized control, powerful actors cannot unilaterally rewrite the ledger, improving resilience and trust for public records.
Key Takeaway

Decentralization spreads responsibility and reduces single points of failure, but it introduces performance and governance trade-offs you must weigh.
