What happens during conflict
When two valid block proposals appear, nodes temporarily disagree about which chain is correct. Consensus rules decide which branch becomes canonical based on the protocol's selection criteria.
Temporary forks
Short forks are normal due to network latency; protocols resolve them automatically.
Finality
Finality is the point after which a block is considered irreversible under the protocol's rules.
Misconception check: forks are not necessarily attacks. They often reflect normal timing differences; only persistent, large forks suggest malicious activity or severe network splits.
Concrete example
Two validators submit blocks at nearly the same time. Nodes pick the chain that follows the protocol's tie-breaking rule. The loser’s block is orphaned and its transactions may be re-included later. This preserves a single canonical history.
Key Takeaway

Validators produce blocks and consensus resolves conflicts. Short forks are normal; finality rules tell you when a block is accepted as the single truth.
