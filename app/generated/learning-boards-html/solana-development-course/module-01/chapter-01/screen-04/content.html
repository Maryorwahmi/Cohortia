Module 1 › Chapter 1.1 › What is a Blockchain?
Definition

A blockchain is a distributed ledger composed of linked blocks of transactions. Each participant maintains a copy of the ledger, and the network uses rules to agree on new additions.

Analogy
A neighborhood logbook with photocopies held by every family; entries are added only after group agreement.
Why this matters
Removing a single point of control increases resilience and reduces censorship risk, which matters for public, shared records.
Common mistake
Not every replicated database is a blockchain; the cryptographic linking and consensus are what make a chain tamper-evident.
Key Takeaway

Blockchain is a distributed, append-only ledger where shared copies and agreement rules form a trusted history without a central custodian.

Concrete example
Imagine registering attendance at a public event. Each organizer node keeps a copy of entries. When a new attendee record is proposed, nodes validate it and then append it. If someone later tries to change a prior attendance record, the chain of fingerprints will show a mismatch and the network will reject the tampered copy.
