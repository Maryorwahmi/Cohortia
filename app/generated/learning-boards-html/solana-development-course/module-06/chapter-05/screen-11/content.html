Front-running, Sandwich & DoS Mitigations

A mix of atomic design, fast validation, and economic controls reduce these attacks.
Step 1 — Validate Fast

Perform cheap, decisive checks early: signatures, rent-exemption, and input bounds. Fail early to avoid wasted compute and exploitable windows.

Step 2 — Atomicize Operations

Design instructions so a single transaction completes a logical unit. Avoid multi-transaction state transitions that expose intermediate states to bots.

Step 3 — Economic Controls

Use slippage limits, deposits, or fees to make attacks costly. Rate-limit per-account actions with timestamped counters.

Concrete Example

For a swap: require a maximum slippage parameter signed by the user, compute price impact, and fail if the impact exceeds the signed slippage. This ensures a bot cannot slip the trade beyond the user’s expectation.

Monitoring

Track pending transaction spikes, repeated failures, and unusual mempool activity. Alert on sudden fee increases or many rejects.

Key Takeaway

Combine fast validation, atomic design, and economic disincentives to reduce front-running and DoS vectors.
