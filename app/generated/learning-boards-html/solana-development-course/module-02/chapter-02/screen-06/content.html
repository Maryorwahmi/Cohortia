How account key lists enable concurrency
Validators use the account list to find conflicts. Disjoint account sets mean transactions may run in parallel, increasing throughput. That is why listing every account you touch is essential.
Example scenario
Transaction A touches accounts A and B; Transaction B touches C and D. They can execute concurrently because account sets are disjoint.
Mistake to avoid: omitting an account or misdeclaring write permissions. Programs will fail when required writable accounts are listed as read-only.
Account meta flags
  • is_signer: this account must sign the transaction.
  • is_writable: this account may be modified during instruction execution.
Reflection
When building transactions, explicitly consider which accounts must be writable or signers. That precision both secures your program and helps validators parallelize work.
