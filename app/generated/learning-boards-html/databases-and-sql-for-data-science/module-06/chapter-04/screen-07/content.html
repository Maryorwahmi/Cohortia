Comparison

Primary Key vs Unique Index

Primary key: automatically indexed, enforces uniqueness and non-null identity. Unique index: enforces uniqueness but may be nullable and is not necessarily the table identity.
Primary key index
Fast lookup by identity and enforces a single row per key.
Unique index
Ensures column uniqueness, useful for emails, SKUs, or identifiers that must not repeat.
Example
CREATE UNIQUE INDEX uix_products_sku ON products (product_sku); ensures each SKU appears once in the products table.
Practical notes
Unique indexes are ideal where data integrity matters. Primary keys are the canonical identifier and are usually a good target for joins and lookups. Both provide high selectivity for direct lookups, but neither obviates the need for other indexes tailored to query patterns.
Key Takeaway
Use primary and unique indexes to enforce identity and integrity, and they typically provide the fastest lookups for single-row retrievals.
Common pitfall
Relying only on primary keys: if your queries filter by other columns often, those columns need appropriate indexes too.
