Core Concept
Why monitor database performance?
Monitoring gives you the measurements needed to transform vague symptoms into targeted fixes.
Main benefits
  • Detect regressions and anomalies early
  • Pinpoint whether problems are CPU, I/O, memory, or network related
  • Provide evidence for query or infrastructure changes
Common misconception
Treating a single high metric as the root cause; instead, correlate multiple signals before acting.
Key Takeaway

Monitoring provides the evidence you need to find the root cause of slow queries and to prioritize fixes effectively.

Analogy & scenario
Think of monitoring like instrument gauges on a ship: you glance at several indicators before deciding if you need to change course. In practice, start by collecting a regular snapshot of CPU, I/O, memory, network, connections, and average query latency for representative workloads.
Symptom
A daily report now takes 10x longer at 3pm.
Likely causes
Increased concurrency, heavy disk reads from full scans, or larger data during that window.
First action
Capture snapshot of metrics at that time and inspect active queries for long-running operations.
Check your understanding
  • What combination of signals would suggest missing indexes rather than insufficient CPU?
  • How would you prove that a spike is reproducible during peak load?
