Core Concept

Traditional Data Centers

Understanding the Early Architecture

Definition

Early data centers primarily consisting of physical servers, dedicated storage arrays, and basic networking equipment, managed independently.

Key Characteristics

  • Siloed resources (compute, storage, network managed separately).
  • Hardware-centric, requiring physical procurement for scaling.
  • Limited resource utilization and complex scaling challenges.

Example Scenario

A company needing a new application would buy a new server, install it, and manually configure its network and storage, a process taking weeks.

Traditional Data Center Model

Compute (Servers)
Storage (Arrays)
Networking (Switches)

Each component managed independently, leading to silos and inefficiencies.

Manual Configuration & Slow Scaling

Key Takeaway

Traditional data centers were rigid, siloed, and hardware-dependent, struggling with resource underutilization and slow adaptation to business needs.
