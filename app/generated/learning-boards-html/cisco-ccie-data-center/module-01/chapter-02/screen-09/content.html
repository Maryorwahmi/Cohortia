Network Design Shift

Layer 2 vs. Layer 3 Boundaries

The Evolution to an L3-Centric Data Center Design

Key Differences

  • Traditional 3-Tier: L2 domains often spanned multiple Access switches, relying on STP.
  • Spine-Leaf: L2/L3 boundary typically at the Leaf switch. Servers connect via L2, Leaf to Spine is L3.
  • L3-Everywhere: Spine-Leaf uses Layer 3 routing between Leaf and Spine switches.
Advantage

The L3-centric approach, combined with ECMP, eliminates STP, allowing all links to be active and providing faster convergence in case of failures.

Key Takeaway

Modern data centers leverage an L3-centric Spine-Leaf design to enhance scalability, resilience, and bandwidth utilization by moving away from L2 spanning tree domains.
