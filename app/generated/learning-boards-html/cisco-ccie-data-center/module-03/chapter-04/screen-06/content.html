Evolution of SANs

The Challenge of Separate Networks

Simplifying infrastructure with converged networking.

Traditional Data Center

Historically, data centers maintained two distinct physical networks: one for IP traffic (Ethernet) and another for storage traffic (Fibre Channel).

Consequences of Separation

  • Increased Cabling: Double the physical connections.
  • Higher Costs: Separate switches, adapters (NICs and HBAs), and power.
  • Management Complexity: Requires specialized skills for two different network types.
  • More Rack Space: Dual infrastructure consumes more physical space.

Concrete Example

A server needing both network and storage access would require an Ethernet NIC for IP communication and a Fibre Channel HBA for SAN connectivity, each with its own cabling and switch port.

The Drive for Convergence

The operational overhead of managing separate networks spurred the development of technologies to unify IP and storage traffic onto a single physical infrastructure.

Reduce Complexity › Lower Costs › Streamline Management
Key Takeaway

The complexity and cost of separate IP and Fibre Channel networks highlighted the need for converged solutions to simplify data center infrastructure.
