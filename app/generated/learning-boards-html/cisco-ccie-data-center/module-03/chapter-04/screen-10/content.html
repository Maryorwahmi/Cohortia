Types of SAN Zoning: Port vs. WWN

Understanding the flexibility and implications of different zoning approaches.

Port Zoning

  • Definition: Based on the physical port numbers on the Fibre Channel switch.
  • Pros: Simple to configure initially.
  • Cons: Not flexible; requires reconfiguration if devices move to different ports.
  • Use Case: Small, static SANs where physical changes are rare.

WWN Zoning (World Wide Name)

  • Definition: Based on the unique, hardware-embedded World Wide Name (WWN) of the HBA or storage port.
  • Pros: Highly flexible; zoning remains valid even if devices move to different physical ports.
  • Cons: Initial configuration might require looking up WWNs.
  • Use Case: Dynamic, enterprise-scale SANs where flexibility is key.

Flexibility vs. Simplicity

WWN zoning offers superior flexibility for dynamic environments, as it's tied to device identity rather than physical location, making it the preferred method for most enterprise SANs.

Key Takeaway

WWN zoning is generally preferred over port zoning due to its flexibility, as it ties access to unique device identifiers rather than physical switch ports.
