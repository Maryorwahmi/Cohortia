Architectural Differences

Modular vs. Fixed-Form-Factor Switches

Understand the design principles, scalability, and deployment considerations for each Nexus switch architecture.

Modular Switches (e.g., Nexus 7000, 9500)

  • •
    Chassis-Based: Allows insertion of line cards, supervisor engines, power supplies.
  • •
    Flexibility: Scale port density and interface types (10/40/100GbE).
  • •
    Resilience: Hot-swappable components minimize downtime.
  • •
    Use Case: Core, aggregation layers, large-scale deployments.

Fixed-Form-Factor Switches (e.g., Nexus 3000, 9300)

  • •
    Integrated Components: Fixed set of ports and internal hardware.
  • •
    Cost-Effective: Often more economical and power-efficient.
  • •
    Compact: Ideal for space-constrained environments.
  • •
    Use Case: Top-of-rack, aggregation in smaller deployments.
Key Takeaway

Choosing between modular and fixed-form-factor Nexus switches depends on your specific needs for scalability, redundancy, and cost efficiency in the data center.
