Core Concept

The Need for Data Center Interconnect (DCI)

Connecting Dispersed Data Centers for Business Continuity

What is DCI?

DCI refers to the technologies and practices used to seamlessly connect geographically dispersed data centers, enabling them to function as a single, unified network.

Key Business Drivers
  • Disaster Recovery & Business Continuity
  • Workload Mobility (VM migration without IP change)
  • Active-Active Data Center Designs
  • Geographical Distribution & Resilience
Key Takeaway

DCI is essential for modern enterprises to support disaster recovery, business continuity, and workload mobility, but extending Layer 2 networks over a WAN presents significant technical challenges like broadcast storms and MAC flooding.

Technical Challenges
  • Complex Layer 2 extension over WAN
  • MAC address table flooding
  • Broadcast storms & suboptimal traffic forwarding
  • Maintaining Layer 2 adjacency without extending broadcast domain
  • Expensive traditional solutions (dark fiber, VPLS)
Scenario: Active-Active Data Centers

Imagine two data centers, each hosting a web server for an application. DCI allows both servers to be active simultaneously, sharing the same Layer 2 segment, ensuring high availability and load balancing without complex IP re-addressing during failover.
