The KRACK Attack: A WPA2 Weakness

A significant vulnerability discovered in 2017 that affected the WPA2 protocol's 4-way handshake.

  • •Key Reinstallation Attack: Exploited flaws in the WPA2 4-way handshake.
  • •Traffic Decryption: Allowed attackers to reinstall an already-in-use key to decrypt traffic.
  • •Widespread Impact: Affected virtually all Wi-Fi devices implementing WPA2.
Key Takeaway

The KRACK attack demonstrated that even robust security protocols can have vulnerabilities, underscoring the critical importance of continuous updates and patching.

Lesson Learned

Always prioritize security updates for all wireless infrastructure and client devices to protect against known and newly discovered vulnerabilities.

WPA2 4-Way Handshake Flaw
KRACK exploited a vulnerability in the WPA2 4-way handshake, allowing attackers to force key reinstallation and decrypt traffic.
