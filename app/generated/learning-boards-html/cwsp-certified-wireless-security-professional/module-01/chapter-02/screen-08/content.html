Attack Vector: Denial of Service

Deauthentication Attacks: Disrupting Connectivity

Forcing clients offline by exploiting unencrypted and unauthenticated 802.11 deauthentication frames.

Legitimate Client
Access Point (AP)
Attacker
Forged Deauth Frame
Attacker sends forged deauthentication frames, causing clients to disconnect from the AP.
Impact & Secondary Objectives
  • •Immediate DoS: Clients are forced offline, disrupting network access.
  • •WPA/WPA2 Handshake Capture: Forces reconnection, allowing capture for offline cracking.
  • •Exploits 802.11 Flaw: Deauth frames are unencrypted and unauthenticated.
Key Takeaway

Deauthentication attacks are a powerful DoS tool that can also facilitate credential harvesting by forcing WPA/WPA2 handshake captures.

Protocol Weakness

The lack of authentication for deauthentication frames is a fundamental design flaw in the 802.11 standard that attackers readily exploit.
