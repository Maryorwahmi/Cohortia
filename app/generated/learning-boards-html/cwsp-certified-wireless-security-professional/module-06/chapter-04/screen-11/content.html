Deauthentication Attacks

Forcing Reconnects and Disrupting Service

Purpose in Enterprise Environments

  • Facilitate Rogue APs: Force clients to disconnect and reconnect, potentially to an Evil Twin.
  • Denial-of-Service (DoS): Disrupt network service by continuously deauthenticating clients.

How it Works

An attacker sends specially crafted deauthentication packets to clients, which are interpreted as legitimate commands to disconnect from the access point. This can be targeted at specific clients or broadcast to all clients connected to an AP.

`aireplay-ng` Deauthentication Example

The aireplay-ng tool is commonly used to perform deauthentication attacks.

sudo aireplay-ng --deauth 1000 -a 11:22:33:44:55:66 wlan0mon
  • --deauth 1000: Sends 1000 deauthentication packets.
  • -a 11:22:33:44:55:66: Specifies the BSSID (MAC address) of the target AP.
  • wlan0mon: Your wireless interface in monitor mode.
Safety Note

Deauthentication attacks can cause significant network disruption and should only be performed with explicit authorization and a clear understanding of the potential impact.

Key Takeaway

Deauthentication attacks are powerful for forcing client reconnects to facilitate Evil Twin attacks or for causing denial-of-service, but they carry significant ethical and operational risks.
