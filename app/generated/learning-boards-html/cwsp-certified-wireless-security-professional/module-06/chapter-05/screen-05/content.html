Attack Vector

RF Jamming and Denial-of-Service Attacks

Disrupting wireless communication through overwhelming radio signals.

1

Deauthentication Floods

Simpler DoS attacks using tools like aireplay-ng to disconnect clients from an AP.

2

RF Jamming Mechanism

Broadcasting strong radio signals on the target network's frequency to overwhelm legitimate signals, preventing communication.

3

Specialized Hardware

Achieved using Software Defined Radios (SDRs) like HackRF One or USRP, or modified Wi-Fi adapters.

4

Legality & Ethics

Highly effective but illegal and disruptive; strictly out of scope for ethical penetration tests. Controlled deauthentication is used instead.

Impact of RF Jamming

Disruption
No Communication

Wireless Network Unusable

Legitimate devices cannot connect or transmit data.

Example: Airport Jamming

Imagine an attacker using an SDR to jam the Wi-Fi frequencies at an airport. This would prevent passengers from connecting to the internet, disrupt airline operations that rely on wireless communication, and potentially cause significant chaos. This scenario highlights the severe impact and why such attacks are illegal.

Key Takeaway

While RF jamming is a powerful DoS technique, its illegality necessitates ethical testers to simulate DoS conditions through controlled methods like deauthentication floods.
