Penetration Test Reporting

Crafting a Comprehensive Report: Key Sections

Documenting findings for effective communication and remediation.

Executive Summary

High-level overview for management, outlining key findings, overall risk, and critical vulnerabilities in non-technical terms.

Scope and Methodology

Details what was tested, how it was tested, and under what conditions, ensuring transparency and setting expectations.

Detailed Findings

Technical breakdown of each vulnerability, including description, steps to reproduce (PoC), impact, and severity rating.

Key Takeaway

A well-structured report is crucial for translating technical findings into actionable insights for both management and technical teams, driving effective remediation.

Detailed Findings Breakdown

Description

Clear explanation of the vulnerability and its technical details.

Steps to Reproduce (PoC)

Detailed, step-by-step instructions for replicating the vulnerability, often with screenshots.

Impact

Explanation of the potential consequences if the vulnerability is exploited (e.g., data loss, unauthorized access).

Severity Rating

A classification of the vulnerability's severity (e.g., High, Medium, Low) often using CVSS scores.

Misconception: Technical Jargon is Always Best

While technical detail is crucial for engineers, an executive summary filled with jargon will fail to communicate risk to decision-makers. A good report balances technical accuracy with clear, concise, and business-oriented language for different audiences. The goal is understanding, not just information dumping.
