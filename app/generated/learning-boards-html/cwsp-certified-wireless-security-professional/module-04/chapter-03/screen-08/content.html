Module 4›Chapter 4.3›CTS/RTS and Beacon Floods

CTS/RTS Flood

An attacker floods the network with fake Clear-to-Send (CTS) or Request-to-Send (RTS) frames, tricking devices into thinking the channel is busy.

Virtual Jamming

Creates a 'virtual jam' by falsely indicating channel occupancy, preventing legitimate transmissions.

MAC Layer Exploitation

Exploits the 802.11 MAC layer's collision avoidance mechanisms.

Beacon Flood

An attacker broadcasts a massive number of fake beacon frames, often with random or offensive SSIDs, to overwhelm client devices.

Client Overload

Overwhelms client devices, making it difficult to find and connect to legitimate networks.

Confusion & Annoyance

Causes user confusion and can lead to clients connecting to rogue APs.

Key Takeaway

CTS/RTS floods create a 'virtual jam' by manipulating MAC layer frames, while beacon floods overwhelm clients with fake network advertisements, both disrupting wireless service.
