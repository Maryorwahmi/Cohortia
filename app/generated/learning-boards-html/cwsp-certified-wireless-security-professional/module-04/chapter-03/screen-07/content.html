Module 4›Chapter 4.3›Disassociation vs. Authentication Floods

Disassociation Flood

Clients are disassociated from the AP, disrupting service but allowing for potentially quicker re-association than deauthentication.

Disrupts Existing Connections

Continuously sends disassociation frames, forcing clients to drop their connection.

Quicker Re-association

Clients can often re-associate faster than after a deauthentication, but a flood still causes disruption.

Authentication Flood

An attacker repeatedly sends fake authentication requests to an AP, exhausting its resources and preventing legitimate clients from connecting.

Prevents New Connections

Overwhelms the AP with fake requests, filling its client table or consuming CPU cycles.

Resource Exhaustion

The AP's resources are consumed by processing bogus authentication attempts.

Key Takeaway

Disassociation floods disrupt active connections, while authentication floods prevent new connections by overwhelming the AP with fake requests, both leading to service denial.
