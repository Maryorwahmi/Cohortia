Brute-Forcing WPS PINs with Reaver

Executing the reaver command to retrieve WPA/WPA2 passphrases.

Execute the `reaver` Attack

Launch reaver with your monitor interface, target BSSID, and channel. The -vv flag provides verbose output.

sudo reaver -i wlan0mon -b AA:BB:CC:DD:EE:FF -c 6 -vv

Attack Duration

The attack can take several hours to days, depending on the AP's rate limiting, signal strength, and the attacker's hardware. Patience is key for this type of brute-force.

Key Takeaway

Reaver effectively exploits the WPS PIN vulnerability, ultimately revealing the network's WPA/WPA2 passphrase, highlighting the severe risk of enabled WPS.

Example `reaver` Success Output

[+] WPS PIN: '12345670'
[+] WPA PSK: 'MySecurePassword123'
[+] AP SSID: 'MyWPS_AP'

Upon success, Reaver displays the WPS PIN and the WPA/WPA2 PSK.

Verification Step

After Reaver reports success, you can verify the recovered WPA/WPA2 PSK by attempting to connect a client device to the target AP using that passphrase. It should connect successfully.
