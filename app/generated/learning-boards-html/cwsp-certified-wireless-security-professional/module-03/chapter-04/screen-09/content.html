Automated Prevention: Risks and False Positives

Automated WIPS prevention carries inherent risks, primarily false positives that can lead to denial of service for legitimate users.

False Positives

WIPS misidentifies legitimate activity (e.g., a guest AP or a normal client behavior) as a threat.

Denial of Service (DoS)

Automated actions (e.g., port shutdown, client deauthentication) disrupt legitimate network services, impacting business operations.

Example Scenario

A WIPS misidentifies a legitimate, authorized guest AP as a rogue due to a misconfiguration. If configured for aggressive prevention, it might shut down the guest AP's wired port, causing a denial of service for all guest users.

Key Takeaway

Thorough testing, careful configuration, and a phased deployment are essential to mitigate the risks of false positives and ensure automated WIPS prevention enhances, rather than hinders, network operations.
