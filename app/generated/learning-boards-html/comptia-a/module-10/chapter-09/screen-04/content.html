Module 11 › Chapter 10.9 › Principles
Definition & core claim
Resilience engineering builds systems that tolerate failure, recover, and learn — focusing on capabilities rather than impossible prevention.
Anticipation
Identify failure modes and design mitigations before they occur.
Monitoring
Continuous observation of latency, error rates, and resource use to detect anomalies.
Learning & Adaptation
Use post-incident analysis to change designs and enable runtime behavior changes.
Key Takeaway

Design for detection, containment, and recovery. Monitoring without remediation is incomplete.

Concrete example
Imagine a database replica is the single source of truth for inventory. Anticipation finds this risk. Monitoring tracks replication lag and error rates. If lag rises, adaptation can route reads to caches and signal a failover plan. Learning after the event may lead to adding a second replica across an availability zone.
Common misconception
Assuming that more alerts mean better resilience. Too many noisy alerts can hide real issues; focus on meaningful steady-state indicators tied to customer impact.
