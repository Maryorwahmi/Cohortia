Module 6 › Chapter 6.1 › What is Threat Intelligence?
Definition
Threat intelligence is analyzed and contextualized security data.
It moves beyond isolated indicators to explain actors, motivations, methods, and likely impact so teams make informed decisions.
Explanation bullets
  • Processed: normalized and enriched to be comparable across sources.
  • Analyzed: correlated to identify TTPs and campaigns.
  • Contextualized: linked to assets, sectors, and actor motivations.
  • Actionable: tailored output for the intended audience.
Concrete example
A daily feed lists an IP seen scanning your web servers. With processing and analysis you discover the IP is associated with a botnet known for credential stuffing, active at certain hours, and targeting login endpoints. That context becomes a recommended action: enable login rate limiting and add those indicators to monitoring rules during observed activity windows.
Key takeaway
Intelligence informs decisions, not just blocks.
Common mistake
Treating raw IoCs as finished intelligence without enrichment and analysis.
