Module 6 › Chapter 6.7 › Evidence Preservation
Definition
Collect and protect artifacts before they are changed or destroyed
Prioritize volatile data capture: memory dumps, running processes, and open connections. Then collect persistent images and logs.
Volatile: memory dumps, process lists, open network sockets.
Persistent: forensic disk images and log archives.
Documentation: chain of custody, who collected what and when.
Concrete caution
Do not power down a compromised host before capturing volatile memory or process data if you need those artifacts. If immediate action is required to stop active harm, capture what you can and document what you could not.
Common mistake: performing containment that destroys evidence, then trying to reconstruct events from incomplete records.
Key Takeaway: Preserve volatile artifacts before irreversible containment when feasible, and always document trade-offs.
