Enterprise Security Architecture — definition

A strategic discipline for aligning security controls with business objectives, technology, and compliance to create a unified, auditable security posture.

Key elements
  • Business-driven requirements and traceability
  • Reference models, standards, and design patterns
  • Cross-domain consistency across on-prem and cloud
Key takeaway

ESA turns competing security choices into a coherent, measurable plan tied to risk and business value.

Concrete example

A retail company defines a policy: all payment-card data must be encrypted at rest and access requires multi-factor authentication. The architecture record notes the justification: PCI compliance and customer trust. Engineering implements encryption, and operations monitors access logs. Auditors can trace control to business requirement.

Common misconception

Treating ESA as a product list rather than a set of patterns and traceability leads to inconsistent coverage and audit gaps.

Try this
---
Asset: Customer database
Business objective: Maintain confidentiality and availability
Architectural decision: Encryption at rest, role-based access, failover plan
Success metric: Zero unauthorized access events, recovery RTO within SLA
