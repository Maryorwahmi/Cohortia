Core Concept › NGFW Capabilities & Placement
What an NGFW does
Combines deep packet inspection, application awareness, IPS signatures, and threat feeds to enforce context-aware policies.
Deep Packet Inspection — inspect application payloads, not just headers.
Application Awareness — identify and permit specific apps even on common ports.
Integrated IPS — block known exploits when combined with threat intelligence.
Key Takeaway

Treat NGFWs as policy engines: write rules by application and identity, then validate with traffic tests.

Common mistake
Allowing generic HTTPS instead of specifying application-level or user-group constraints increases attack surface.
Placement scenarios
Decide placement by what traffic the NGFW must inspect and which policies it must enforce.
Perimeter
Protects ingress/egress, integrates with threat feeds, enforces enterprise-wide access policies.
Internal segmentation
Enforces least privilege between sensitive segments to limit lateral movement.
Cloud / East-west
Cloud-deployed NGFWs inspect east-west traffic and apply the same context-aware rules.
Design rule sets from business needs and test them under realistic traffic to avoid blocking critical services.
