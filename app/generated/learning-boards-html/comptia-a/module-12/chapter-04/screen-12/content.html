Immutable Storage and Air‑gapped Backups

Protect backups from deletion or encryption by attackers using immutability or physical separation.

Comparison and Use Cases
Immutable storage: set object locks to prevent modification for a retention period. Air‑gapped backups: physically disconnect or store removable media offline. Use immutability for frequent online access with protection. Use air gaps for the most critical long‑term archives that must remain unreachable by network threats.
Benefit

Both approaches preserve the availability of backups even if production systems are compromised.

Operational cost

Air gaps increase restore time and handling overhead; immutability may increase storage cost and requires lifecycle planning.

Checklist
  • Enable object lock or equivalent immutability where available.
  • Maintain at least one air‑gapped copy of the most critical data.
  • Store encryption keys separately from backups.
  • Include immutable and air‑gapped copies in verification tests.
