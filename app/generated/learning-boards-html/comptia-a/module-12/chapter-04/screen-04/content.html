Core Concept RPO & RTO

Why Backups Matter — RPO and RTO

Use recovery objectives to guide backup cadence and technology choices.

Definitions
Recovery Point Objective (RPO): acceptable data loss measured in time. Recovery Time Objective (RTO): acceptable downtime before services are restored.
Example
File server: RPO 24 hours, RTO 8 hours: weekly fulls, daily differentials, off‑site copy, and tested restores meet both targets.
Common Mistake
Assuming a backup is valid without performing a restore test or integrity check.
Key Takeaway
Set RPO/RTO first; then choose backup cadence and storage to meet those objectives.
Tradeoffs & Example
Frequent incremental backups reduce daily storage but make restores longer because you must replay many change sets. Differential backups simplify restores (only the last full and last differential are required) but grow in size between full backups. Replication reduces RPO toward zero but requires additional infrastructure and careful failover planning.
When to use
Low RPO: replication or CDP. Moderate RPO: daily differentials. Low operational cost: incremental.
Check your understanding
If you need a 1‑hour RPO for a database, would daily differentials suffice? No — consider replication or point‑in‑time recovery strategies.
Key Takeaway

Match technology to objectives. Never design backups before defining acceptable loss and downtime.
