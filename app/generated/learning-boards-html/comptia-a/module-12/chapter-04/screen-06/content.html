Process Flow Backup Scheduling

Scheduling Backups and Storage Growth

Set schedules that reflect change frequency, retention, and operational windows.

Step-by-step scheduling
  1. Inventory data and measure change rates and criticality.
  2. Assign RPO and RTO targets per dataset.
  3. Choose backup types and cadence to meet targets.
  4. Define retention and archival rules for older backups.
  5. Automate alerts for job failures and storage thresholds.
Example schedule: weekly fulls on Sunday, daily differentials at 02:00, hourly incrementals for high-change logs; retention: 30 days for daily, 1 year for weekly fulls in cold storage.
Monitoring & Growth Controls
Enable automated pruning and alerts. Use storage tiers to reduce cost: recent backups in fast storage, older backups archived. Consider egress and retrieval cost when using cloud-tier archivals.
Concrete example
A development dataset with low business impact can be retained weekly for 90 days in archival storage, while a production database must keep hourly backups for 48 hours in fast storage and weekly backups archived for compliance.
Pitfall

Not pruning old backups can exhaust capacity and stop future backups. Automate retention.

Mnemonic

Think: Inventory → Objectives → Cadence → Retention → Monitor.
