Definition
Full interruption (Cutover) test
The most realistic validation: primary systems are intentionally shut down and operations shift to recovery systems.
• High risk and high realism; usually performed rarely.
• Requires explicit approvals, a tested rollback plan, and thorough communication controls.
• Misconception: it's the only valid test; in many cases parallel tests with strong simulations suffice.
Safety checklist
  • Pre-approved rollback triggers and owners
  • Confirmed off-site backups and verification
  • Stakeholder notification and low-impact scheduling
  • Monitoring and rapid escalation pathways
Concrete example
An e-commerce firm schedules a full interruption during an off-peak maintenance window. They shift live traffic to their recovery region after confirming last-known-good backups and have a five-hour rollback window if performance or data integrity KPIs fail.
Key Takeaway

Only perform full interruption tests when needed and safe; document approvals and rollback triggers to manage real-world risk.

Pro tip: run a full dress rehearsal with observers and a dry-run checklist days before cutover to verify readiness.
