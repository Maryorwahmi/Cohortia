Process Flow
Design a testing program
Schedule exercises, map objectives to RTO/RPO, and assign accountable owners.
• Schedule: align frequency to risk and compliance—monthly, quarterly, annual cadence.
• Objectives: tie each exercise to a measurable outcome like RTO achievement or notification latency.
• Scenarios: craft realistic incidents that stress people and systems.
• Roles: facilitator, observer, participant, incident commander—list names and backups.
• Resources: test environments, backups, runbooks, and monitoring tools must be pre-reserved.
Example schedule
Quarterly tabletops, monthly simulations for tactical systems, and an annual parallel test for the top three critical applications.
Key Takeaway

A documented schedule with objectives and owners prevents ad-hoc testing and ensures follow-through on corrective actions.

Roles & responsibilities board
Facilitator
Runs the exercise, keeps time, and enforces scope.
Observer
Records evidence, metrics, and issues; provides unbiased notes for the hotwash.
Incident Commander
Makes executive decisions and approves rollbacks if necessary.
Participants
Technical and business staff who execute tasks per the runbook.
Avoid scheduling tests without reserving environments and named observers—doing so creates incomplete, unreliable results.
