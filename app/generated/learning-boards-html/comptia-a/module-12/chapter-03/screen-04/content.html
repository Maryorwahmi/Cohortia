Core Concept

What is a DRP

The tactical runbook that describes restoring IT systems, data, and networks to satisfy recovery objectives.

Definition
Procedures, responsibilities, and resources to restore IT services within RTO/RPO.
Example
A database with RTO=4 hours, RPO=1 hour: backup locations, decrypt/restore steps, and verification commands are documented.
Common misconception
Backups alone are not a DRP; they must be recoverable, automated, and integrated with system and network restores.
Key Takeaway

Treat the DRP as a living, testable runbook that covers restore commands, verification, and access controls.

DRP quick-reference panel
RTO
Time to restore service
RPO
Acceptable data loss window
Recovery artifacts
  • Encrypted backups with stored key locations
  • Restore commands and automation scripts
  • Network failover and DNS procedures
Roles & communications
Who initiates recovery, who approves failover, and how status is communicated to stakeholders.
