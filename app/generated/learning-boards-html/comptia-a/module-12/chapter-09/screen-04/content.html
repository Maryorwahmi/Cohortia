Core Concept
HIPAA: Disaster Recovery & Backups
HIPAA mandates a disaster recovery plan and a data backup plan to restore ePHI while preserving confidentiality and integrity.
  • Backup location: off-site, encrypted, and access-controlled.
  • Restore proof: procedural sign-offs showing successful integrity checks.
  • Retention: align backup retention with record-keeping requirements for patient data.
Key Takeaway

Design backups and restores for evidence and auditability, not only speed.

Concrete scenario
A ransomware event encrypts clinical records. The DRP requires imaging affected servers, restoring from encrypted off-site backups, and recording integrity checks. Legal and privacy teams must be able to show copies of the process.
Example runbook checkpoint
"Confirm backup restore completed and integrity verified by Clinical IT Lead; log time, backup ID, and verifier name."
Common misconception
Some teams assume backups alone are enough — HIPAA requires demonstrable, auditable restoration capability and controls that show confidentiality was preserved during recovery.
