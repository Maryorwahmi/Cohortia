Core Concept Data Classification
Identify and categorize sensitive information
Make classification policy the source of truth for technical controls.
Definition & Purpose

Data classification groups information by sensitivity and regulatory impact to determine handling requirements such as encryption, access, and retention.

Policy Example
Restricted
PII, PHI, PCI: must be encrypted at rest, require MFA, and be auditable.
Common Mistakes
  • Over-classifying everything leads to user friction and policy bypass.
  • Under-classifying critical assets leaves them exposed to attackers and compliance risk.
  • Not mapping labels to enforceable technical controls makes classification ineffective.
Key Takeaway

Classification must be policy-led and mapped to enforcement: labels trigger storage, access, and retention controls.

Classification Grid (with examples)
Public
Press releases, marketing content — no special handling required.
Internal
Internal memos and process docs — access limited to employees.
Confidential
Financial forecasts, contracts — encrypted and logged access.
Restricted
PII, PHI, PCI — strong encryption, MFA, strict retention.
Hands-on Prompt
List three data types in your organization and assign classifications with justification. Use these to drive DLP policies and key decisions about retention.
