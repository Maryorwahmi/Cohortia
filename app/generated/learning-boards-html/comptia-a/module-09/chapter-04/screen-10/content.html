Module 9 › Chapter 9.4 › Code Signing and Artifact Integrity
Process

Sign, store, verify

Signing is typically performed at build time. The CI process creates an artifact, signs it with a protected private key, and publishes both the artifact and its signature. Deployment systems verify the signature against an allowlist of trusted public keys before accepting the artifact into production.
Example
Sign container images and require verification in your orchestration tool so unsigned images are rejected.
Misconception
Signing alone is not sufficient if the signing key is poorly protected. Use vaults and hardware-backed keys for signing operations.
Practical steps
  • Generate keys in a secure vault or hardware module.
  • Restrict signing to authenticated CI jobs with least privilege.
  • Automate verification on deploy and reject untrusted artifacts.
Key Takeaway
Signing increases trust in artifacts, but it must be combined with key protection and deployment verification to be effective.
