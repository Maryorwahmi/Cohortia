DLP: a precise definition
Inspection + policy enforcement to keep sensitive data within organizational control.
What it inspects
Files, emails, web uploads, clipboard data, removable media, and cloud objects.
Actions it enforces
Block, quarantine, encrypt, alert, or allow with audit trail.
Organizational role
Operationalizes classification and supports incident response when rules trigger.
Common misconception
DLP is not a substitute for encryption or IAM. It complements them by enforcing policy on content flows that those controls may not fully cover.
Concrete example
A document classified as "Restricted" containing payroll bank account numbers is detected by fingerprinting and blocked from being uploaded to an unmanaged cloud storage account. The file is quarantined and a notification is sent to HR and security for review.
Key Takeaway
DLP enforces business-driven rules on content. Tie rules to classification and context to reduce friction and increase accuracy.
