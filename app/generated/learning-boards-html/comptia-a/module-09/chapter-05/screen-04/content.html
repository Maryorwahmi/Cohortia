Module 09 › Chapter 9.5 › What is Data Classification?
Definition
Organize data by sensitivity, criticality, and regulation

Classification creates labels that guide which protections to apply and where. It balances risk, cost, and compliance requirements.

Quick criteria
  • Potential harm from disclosure
  • Legal or regulatory requirements
  • Business criticality and availability needs
Analogy
Sorting goods in a factory

Like sorting raw materials by value and fragility, classification determines how vigorously each item is protected and handled.

Common mistake
Treating classification as a checkbox. It must be maintained, reviewed, and enforced.
Concrete example
Quarterly forecasts → Confidential

Labeling forecasts as confidential lets IT apply encryption and restrict sharing to the finance group only.

How labels inform controls
  • Public: focus on integrity and availability
  • Restricted: strong encryption, strict logging, limited regions
Key Takeaway
Classification is the single most important early decision for targeted, cost-effective data protection.
