Module 9›Chapter 9.1›Secure Design Principles
Definition

Architectural guidelines that reduce attack surface

Design principles guide trade-offs and ensure mitigations are feasible and testable across the lifecycle.

Least Privilege
Grant only necessary permissions to users and components to limit blast radius.
Defense in Depth
Use layered controls so one failure does not compromise the entire system.
Secure Defaults
Systems should default to the most restrictive safe configuration.
Separation of Concerns
Separate roles and components to keep failures contained and simpler to test.
Design analogy
Think of a building: least privilege is a keycard for each room; defense in depth is having locked doors, alarms, and guards. Secure defaults close doors by default, and separation of concerns keeps utilities behind separate walls.
Applied example
Place photo storage behind a service that enforces access tokens and encryption rather than letting the main app directly expose storage credentials.
Key Takeaway

Use design principles as required controls to guide architecture, implementation, and testing decisions.
