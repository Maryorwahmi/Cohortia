Module 5 › Chapter 5.4 › OCSP & Stapling
Definition
OCSP and Stapling
OCSP responders provide per-certificate status. Stapling lets servers include a signed OCSP response in the TLS handshake.
Privacy
Stapling prevents clients from contacting the OCSP responder directly, improving privacy.
Performance
Stapling reduces client latency by embedding the signed response in the handshake.
Availability
Servers must reliably fetch responses; OCSP responders must be highly available to support stapling.
Concrete example
Configure your web server to fetch a signed OCSP response from the CA at regular intervals and present that response during the TLS handshake to avoid client-side OCSP queries.
Common mistake
Assuming stapling removes dependency on the OCSP responder. Servers still need the responder to obtain fresh signed responses.
Key Takeaway

Use OCSP for timely revocation and implement stapling on servers to protect privacy and speed TLS, ensuring responder availability.
