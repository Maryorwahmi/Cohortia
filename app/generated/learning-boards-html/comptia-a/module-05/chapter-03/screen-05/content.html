Component Architecture

Certificate Authority (CA) and hierarchy

Why roots are anchors, intermediates provide operational flexibility, and issuance must be constrained by purpose.

Architecture Cards
Root CA
Ultimate trust anchor. Often offline and tightly protected, used only to sign intermediate keys.
Intermediate CA
Delegates issuance for specific purposes, reduces exposure of the root private key.
Issuing CA
Generates end-entity certificates for servers, clients, or code signing with policy checks.
Operational Notes
  • Keep the root CA offline where practical and sign intermediate certificates in controlled processes.
  • Use HSMs and audited access controls for CA private keys to prevent unauthorized signing.
  • Segment intermediates by purpose to reduce the blast radius of a compromise.
Scenario
If an intermediate CA used for TLS is compromised, only certificates issued by that intermediate need revocation and re-issuance if intermediates are purpose-scoped.
