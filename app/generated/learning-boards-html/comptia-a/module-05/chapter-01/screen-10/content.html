Comparison › Hash vs Stretched Hash

Simple hash

Fast to compute and deterministic. Works for integrity checks but dangerous for password storage without salt and stretching.
Example
Compute SHA-256 of a password once with openssl; result is fast, short, and reproducible by an attacker who knows the input.
Security issue
Fast computation enables high-rate brute-force and rainbow-table attacks unless salt and stretching are applied.

Salted & stretched hash

PBKDF2 or bcrypt uses a unique salt and many iterations, producing output that is expensive to compute and contains metadata about parameters.
Example
Using passlib PBKDF2 with 290000 iterations and a random salt produces a stored hash that resists offline cracking attempts.
Practical checklist
  • Use a unique salt per password.
  • Choose an iteration count aligned with policy and hardware.
  • Prefer adaptive algorithms like bcrypt where appropriate.
Key Takeaway

A salted, stretched hash is orders of magnitude harder to attack offline than a simple hash; the work factor is what provides protection.
