Module 5 › Chapter 5.6 › Rotation & Rekeying
Policy elements
Frequency
Define per key class: session keys, transport keys, master keys.
Generation & Distribution
Prefer HSM/KMS-generated keys and wrapped transport to endpoints.
Archival / Destruction
Archive under encryption with limited access; securely destroy when no longer needed.
Practical example
Rotate TLS keys annually, database master keys quarterly, and session keys every connection. Automate these steps through KMS APIs and orchestration to avoid operational gaps.
Staged rollover pattern
Create new keys, deploy them alongside old keys, migrate active encryption to new keys, and finally revoke and destroy the old keys once safe.
Common pitfall
Failing to coordinate rotation across dependent systems causes availability or decryption failures. Test rollovers in staging before production.
Key Takeaway

Rotation reduces exposure, but must be automated, tested, and staged to avoid outages. Archive and destroy retired keys under strict controls.
