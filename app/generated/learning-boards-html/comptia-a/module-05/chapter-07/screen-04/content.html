Module 6 › Chapter 5.7 › Brute-Force
Definition

Brute-force attacks

Exhaustive key or password guessing until a match succeeds. The defensive strategy is to increase per-guess cost and key space so attacks are infeasible.

Countermeasures
  • Use long symmetric keys (e.g., 256-bit) where applicable.
  • Apply key-stretching (PBKDF2, bcrypt, scrypt) for passwords.
  • Enforce rate limits and lockouts to slow guessing.
Key Takeaway

Brute-force is a resource problem; increase resources required per guess and reduce exposure through rate-limiting and entropy checks.

Concrete Example
Password-derived key scenario

A web application derives encryption keys from user passwords without stretching. An attacker who obtains hashed passwords can try fast GPU-based guesses. Applying bcrypt at a high work factor increases each guess runtime, dramatically reducing feasible guesses per hour and protecting accounts even if hashes are stolen.

Check your understanding
  • Are symmetric keys long enough for the expected lifespan?
  • Are passwords processed with a key-stretching algorithm and sufficient work factor?
  • Are rate limits and lockout policies implemented?
