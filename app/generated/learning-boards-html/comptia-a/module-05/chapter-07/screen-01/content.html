Cryptographic Attacks and Countermeasures

Understand attack vectors that target algorithms and implementations, and learn configuration and architectural controls that mitigate risk today and prepare systems for future threats.

Why this matters
Attacks often exploit implementation weaknesses rather than only algorithmic flaws. Hardening both prevents compromise.
How we'll progress
From brute-force to side channels, then collision mathematics, downgrades, and finally cryptographic agility and post-quantum planning.

This concept block groups attack categories with the defensive posture you should adopt: increase key sizes and use key stretching, eliminate implementation leakage, prefer authenticated encryption to prevent chosen-ciphertext exploits, and implement agility and policy to retire compromised primitives quickly. These are practical, testable engineering actions that reduce attack surface and speed response when cryptographic weakness is discovered.
In this lesson: brute-force, side-channel, CPA/CCA, birthday collisions, downgrade attacks, cryptographic agility, and post-quantum planning.
