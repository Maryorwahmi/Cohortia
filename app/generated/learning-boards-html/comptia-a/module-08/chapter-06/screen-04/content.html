Module 08 › Chapter 8.6 › What is Social Engineering?
Social engineering defined
Manipulating people to bypass security
Key idea: Attackers target trust, urgency, curiosity, and authority. They craft believable scenarios so that an otherwise cautious person takes an unsafe action.
Concrete example
A spoofed IT email warning of immediate password expiry that asks an employee to update credentials via a link.
Common misconception
Believing that firewalls alone protect users. Social techniques bypass technical controls by targeting the user.
Key takeaway
Combine verification procedures and training to make human decisions harder to manipulate.
Psychology at play
  • Authority bias: people obey perceived authority.
  • Urgency: time pressure reduces verification.
  • Reciprocity: offers or favors prompt compliance.
Practical verification steps
  • Confirm requests with a known channel (call the help desk number you trust).
  • Look for generic greetings, mismatched domains, or spelling as IoCs.
  • Use multi-factor authentication to reduce credential reuse risk.
