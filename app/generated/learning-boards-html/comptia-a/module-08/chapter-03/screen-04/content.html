Module 8 › Chapter 8.3 › Assessment Types
Comparison

Scan · Pen Test · Red Team

Identify the right tool for the right question: surface known issues, prove exploitability, or emulate an attacker mission.

Vulnerability Scanning
Automated and broad. Finds known CVEs, provides inventory and remediation lists but not proof of exploitability.
Penetration Testing
Manual and focused. Attempts to exploit to demonstrate impact and prioritize fixes based on real risk.
Red Teaming
Goal-oriented and persistent. Simulates an adversary including social or physical vectors to test detection and response.
Practical comparison
Example: Outdated Web Server
  • Scanner: lists version and relevant CVEs.
  • Pen tester: attempts a module to get a shell and proves data access.
  • Red team: uses the shell plus social vectors to reach high-value data.
Common mistake
Assuming a scanned vulnerability is exploitable without proof. This leads to misprioritized fixes.
Why it matters
Proving exploitability shows business impact, which helps managers decide where to invest. Red teaming adds detection testing, which improves defensive posture.
Key Takeaway

Pick the assessment to match the objective: breadth, proven impact, or adversary emulation.
