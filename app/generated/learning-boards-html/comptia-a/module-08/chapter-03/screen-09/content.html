Architecture

Payloads and attacker actions

Understand payload behavior and post-exploitation steps so you can limit impact while demonstrating realistic attacker capabilities.

Payload types
Reverse shells, Meterpreter sessions, or custom code. Choose based on allowed post-exploitation in the RoE.
Post-exploitation goals
Persistence, privilege escalation, information gathering, lateral movement, and pivoting toward objectives.
Scenario card
After a successful shell on a web server, a tester checks for local privilege escalation vectors, looks for credential stores, and tests connecting to internal hosts reachable only from the web server. Each successful step demonstrates increasing impact and reveals defensive gaps.
Common mistake
Treating an initial shell as the end goal instead of exploring potential escalation and lateral opportunities that define real risk.
Pro tip
Document precise evidence at each post-exploitation step so findings translate into actionable remediation that reduces attack paths.
Key Takeaway

The true impact of an exploit is revealed during post-exploitation when attackers escalate privileges and move laterally to reach valuable assets.
