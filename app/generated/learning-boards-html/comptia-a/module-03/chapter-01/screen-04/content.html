Module 3 › Chapter 3.1 › VLANs
Definition and purpose
VLANs create logical Layer 2 segments to group devices and limit broadcast scope.
Concrete example
Place HR desktops and HR servers on a dedicated VLAN so their discovery traffic remains separate from public guest traffic.
Common mistake
Assuming VLANs alone are adequate security. Without ACLs at Layer 3, routed traffic can freely cross segments.
Key Takeaway
Use VLANs for logical grouping and always pair them with ACLs or firewalls for inter-segment control.
Design checklist
  • Assign VLAN IDs and document purpose and IP ranges.
  • Define trunk vs access ports and tagging strategy.
  • Plan ACLs or firewall rules for all inter-VLAN traffic.
  • Reserve an unused native VLAN for trunks to mitigate double tagging.
Why it matters
Clear VLAN design prevents accidental connectivity and simplifies auditing and incident response.
