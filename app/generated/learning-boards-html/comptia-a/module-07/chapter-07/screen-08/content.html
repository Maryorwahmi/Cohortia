Module 7 › Chapter 7.7 › Hashing & Integrity
Definition

Cryptographic Hashing

A hash maps input data to a fixed-length output. For forensic integrity, SHA256 or MD5 are commonly recorded to demonstrate identical copies.

Usage
Compute hash of original drive and of forensic image; both values must match.
Record
Store hashes in chain of custody and in the forensic report with timestamps and tool details.
Examples
sha256sum /media/forensics/disk_image.dd
# OR
md5sum /media/forensics/disk_image.dd
Misconception
A matching hash proves identical data, but does not replace full documentation of handling and tools used during acquisition.
Key Takeaway

Hashes are essential evidence markers. Compute them before and after imaging and include them in the chain of custody record.
