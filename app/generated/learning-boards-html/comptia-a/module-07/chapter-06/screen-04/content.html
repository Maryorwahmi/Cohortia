Module 7 › Chapter 7.6 › DFIR Lifecycle
Process Overview
Six phases form a loop that reduces risk and improves readiness.
Preparation: Team, playbooks, tools, logging, backups, and training.
Identification: Detect and validate incidents using SIEM, EDR, and logs.
Containment: Short-term stop and long-term prevention while keeping evidence intact.
Eradication: Remove malware, patch root causes, and remove persistence mechanisms.
Recovery: Restore from clean backups and verify integrity before returning to service.
Lessons Learned: Post-mortem to update playbooks, controls, and detection rules.
Key Takeaway

A reliable DFIR program balances speed with evidence preservation and feeds improvements back into preparation.

Phase Cards
1 • Preparation
Playbooks, asset inventory, validated tools.
2 • Identification
SIEM, EDR alerts, IoCs and IoAs.
3 • Containment
Short-term vs long-term strategies.
4 • Eradication
Remove root cause and persistence.
5 • Recovery
Restore from trusted backups and verify.
6 • Lessons Learned
Post-mortem and continuous improvement.
Common misconception
Some teams focus only on containment; without preparation and lessons learned, the organization repeats the same failures.
