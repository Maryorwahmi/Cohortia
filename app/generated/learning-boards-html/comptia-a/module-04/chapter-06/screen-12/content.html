Module 4 › Chapter 4.6 › Provider Choices
Mode summary
SSE-S3 (provider-managed)
Provider manages keys entirely. Low burden, limited customer key control.
SSE-KMS (KMS-managed)
Customer creates and manages keys in KMS. Better policies and auditability.
SSE-C (customer-provided)
Keys provided per request by customer; provider does not store the keys persistently.
Client-side encryption
Encryption before upload; customer retains full control of keys and plaintext.
Example: PCI data
PCI often requires the highest key control. Client-side encryption or tightly controlled KMS policies are preferred so the cloud provider cannot access plaintext.
Misconception
Confusing KMS ownership with client-side control. KMS still performs operations on behalf of the customer under provider service boundaries.
Key Takeaway

Document who can decrypt and how keys are managed; choose the mode matching your regulatory requirements.
