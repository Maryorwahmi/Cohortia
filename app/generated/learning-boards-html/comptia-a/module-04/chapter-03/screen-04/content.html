Core Concept
What is a Hypervisor?
Software or firmware that creates and manages virtual machines, controlling access to host resources.
Role and significance
  • Mediates CPU, memory, storage, and network so multiple VMs share hardware safely.
  • Forms the primary trust boundary for all hosted workloads.
  • Requires minimal, well-controlled management interfaces to reduce attack surface.
Example
A Type 1 hypervisor like ESXi runs directly on hardware and presents a smaller attack surface than a hosted hypervisor on a general OS.
Misconception
Securing guest OSes alone protects the platform — incorrect because the hypervisor sits above all guests.
Analogy and takeaway
Think of the hypervisor as the landlord that controls apartment utilities. If the landlord is compromised, every apartment is at risk.
Treat as OS
Apply similar security controls as you would to a host OS: minimal services, strict access, and patching.
Check your understanding
Why is hypervisor compromise higher impact than a single VM compromise? Answer: it can expose or control all VMs on the host.
Key Takeaway

The hypervisor is a central trust anchor; minimize its attack surface and secure management to protect all guests.
