Ransomware: The Digital Hostage Crisis

When Your Data is Held for Ransom

How Ransomware Works

  1. 1.Infection: Malware gains access to a system, often via phishing or exploiting vulnerabilities.
  2. 2.Encryption: Critical files and data are encrypted, making them inaccessible to the user.
  3. 3.Ransom Demand: A message appears, demanding payment (usually cryptocurrency) for the decryption key.
  4. 4.Impact: Data availability is severely compromised, leading to operational disruption and potential financial loss.
Concrete Example

The WannaCry attack in 2017 encrypted data across hundreds of thousands of computers globally, demanding Bitcoin payments and causing widespread disruption to critical services.

The Ransom Dilemma

Pay: Potential data recovery, but no guarantee and encourages future attacks.

Don't Pay: Data loss if no backups, but doesn't fund criminals.

The decision to pay a ransom is complex, with significant risks and ethical considerations.
Key Takeaway

Ransomware encrypts data and demands payment, severely impacting data availability and posing a significant challenge for recovery.
