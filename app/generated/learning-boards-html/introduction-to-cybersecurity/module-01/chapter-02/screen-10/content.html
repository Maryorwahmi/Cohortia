Social Engineering Tactics

Pretexting: Crafting a Believable Story

Inventing Scenarios to Gain Trust and Information

What is Pretexting?

Pretexting is a social engineering tactic where an attacker invents a fabricated scenario (a "pretext") to gain trust and persuade a victim to divulge information or perform an action. It often involves impersonation and active conversation.

  • •Invented Scenario: A false story or identity is created.
  • •Builds Trust: Aims to establish credibility with the victim.
  • •Active Engagement: Often involves phone calls or direct interaction.
Concrete Example

An attacker calls an employee, pretending to be from HR, needing to 'verify' personal details for a new benefits package, then asks for sensitive information.

Key Elements of Pretexting

Impersonation

Information Gathering

Manipulation

Attackers use these elements to create a convincing narrative and extract data.
Key Takeaway

Pretexting relies on a fabricated scenario and active deception to gain trust and manipulate individuals into revealing sensitive information.
