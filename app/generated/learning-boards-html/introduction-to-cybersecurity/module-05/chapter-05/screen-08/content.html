Preserving the Scene: Forensic Readiness

Ensuring Evidence Integrity for Analysis and Accountability

Forensic readiness is the proactive preparation of systems and processes to efficiently collect and preserve digital evidence during an incident.

  • •Log Collection: Systems configured to gather relevant security logs.
  • •System Imaging: Maintaining clean system images and knowing how to image compromised drives.
  • •Volatile Data: Understanding how to collect data like RAM contents without destruction.
  • •Integrity: Ensuring evidence is collected in a manner that maintains its integrity and admissibility.
Key Takeaway

Proactive forensic readiness is essential for effective incident analysis, root cause identification, and potential legal action.

Evidence Preservation Principles

Chain of Custody

Documenting every handler of evidence to prove its integrity.

Non-Alteration

Collecting evidence without making changes to the original source.

Timeliness

Collecting volatile evidence as quickly as possible before it's lost.

These principles guide the proper handling of digital evidence.

Example: Imaging a Compromised Server

Before attempting to clean or rebuild a compromised server, a forensic image of its hard drive is created. This bit-for-bit copy ensures that all data, including deleted files and hidden partitions, is preserved for detailed offline analysis, without risking alteration of the live system.

Misconception

Simply copying files is not enough; a true forensic image captures the entire disk state, including unallocated space.
