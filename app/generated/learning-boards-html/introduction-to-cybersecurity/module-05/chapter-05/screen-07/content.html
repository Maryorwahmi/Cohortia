Core Concept

Containing the Threat: Limiting Damage and Spread

Strategic Approaches to Isolate and Control Incidents

Containment is the phase focused on stopping the incident's spread and minimizing its impact. Strategies vary widely based on the type and scope of the attack.

  • •Isolation: Disconnecting infected systems from the network.
  • •Blocking: Using firewalls or network access controls to stop malicious traffic.
  • •Application Shutdown: Temporarily taking compromised services offline.
  • •Trade-offs: Balancing the need for rapid containment with minimizing business disruption.
Key Takeaway

Effective containment is about swift, decisive action to prevent further harm, while carefully considering the operational impact on the business.

Containment Approaches

Short-Term Containment

Stop immediate damage and prevent spread (e.g., unplugging a server).

Long-Term Containment

Prevent recurrence and ensure stability (e.g., re-architecting a vulnerable service).

Both short-term and long-term strategies are essential for comprehensive incident management.

Example: Malware Outbreak

If a widespread malware outbreak is detected, short-term containment might involve isolating all affected hosts and blocking the malware's command-and-control IP addresses at the perimeter firewall. Long-term containment could involve deploying new endpoint protection software and updating network segmentation policies.

Critical Consideration

Ensure containment actions do not inadvertently destroy critical forensic evidence needed for later analysis.
