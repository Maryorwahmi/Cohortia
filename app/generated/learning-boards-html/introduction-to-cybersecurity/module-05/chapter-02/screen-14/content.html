Key Concepts: Access Control Models

Essential Terms for Managing Permissions

Access Control

The process of regulating who or what is allowed to view, use, or modify resources within a system.

Discretionary Access Control (DAC)

An access control model where the owner of a resource determines who can access it and what permissions they have.

Mandatory Access Control (MAC)

A rigid access control model where access decisions are based on security labels assigned to subjects and objects, enforced by a central authority.

Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)

An access control model where permissions are assigned to roles, and users are assigned to roles, simplifying management and enforcing least privilege.

Summary Insight

Choosing the right access control model is foundational for securing resources, balancing flexibility with strict enforcement based on organizational needs.
