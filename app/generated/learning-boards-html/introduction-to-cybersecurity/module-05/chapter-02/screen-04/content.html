Foundations of Access Control and Identity Management

Regulating Who Can Access What Resources and Under What Conditions

What is Access Control?

Access control is the security discipline that regulates who (or what) is allowed to view, use, or modify resources within a system. It's about enforcing permissions.

  • •Ensures only authorized individuals and processes interact with sensitive data.
  • •Upholds the core principles of cybersecurity: Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability.
  • •Prevents unauthorized users from exploiting legitimate-looking credentials.
Key Takeaway

Access control is fundamental for cybersecurity, acting as a gatekeeper to protect resources and maintain trust in digital systems.

The CIA Triad and Access Control

Confidentiality

Preventing unauthorized disclosure of information.

Integrity

Ensuring data accuracy and preventing unauthorized modification.

Availability

Guaranteeing authorized users can access resources when needed.

Example: Cloud Document Sharing

When you share a document on a cloud drive, you decide who can view, edit, or comment. This is a direct application of access control, where you, as the owner, set the permissions. If you don't set them correctly, sensitive information could be exposed or altered by unintended parties.
