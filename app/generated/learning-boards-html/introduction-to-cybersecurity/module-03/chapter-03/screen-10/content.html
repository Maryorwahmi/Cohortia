False Positives and False Negatives

The Dual-Edged Sword of IDPS Alerts

False Positive

An alert generated by an IDPS for legitimate activity that is mistakenly identified as malicious.

  • •Impact: Leads to 'alert fatigue' among security analysts.
  • •Risk: Real threats can be overlooked due to excessive noise.
Example Scenario

An employee logs in from a new, authorized laptop, but the IDPS flags it as an 'unusual login location' due to a new IP address.

False Negative

An actual attack or malicious activity that goes undetected by an IDPS.

  • •Impact: Attackers successfully bypass detection.
  • •Risk: Leads to undetected breaches and compromises.
Example Scenario

A zero-day exploit is used to gain access to a server, but the IDPS has no signature for it and no anomaly is detected.

Key Takeaway

Minimizing both false positives and false negatives is crucial for an effective IDPS, requiring continuous tuning and vigilance.
