Multi-Factor Authentication: Beyond Passwords

Adding layers of defense to verify user identity.

  • Multiple Factors: Requires two or more distinct pieces of evidence to verify identity.
  • Increased Security: Significantly harder for attackers to gain access even if one factor is compromised.
  • Layered Defense: Adds a crucial second layer of protection beyond traditional passwords.
  • Fundamental Requirement: No longer optional; essential for protecting critical accounts in modern cybersecurity.
Key Takeaway

MFA is a critical security measure that provides a robust second layer of defense, making unauthorized access significantly more difficult.

How MFA Works

Combines different factors for stronger verification.

Factor 1: Password (Something You Know)
Factor 2: Phone Code (Something You Have)
Access Granted

Example: Online Banking

After entering your password, your bank sends a one-time code to your registered phone. You must enter this code to complete the login, proving you have both 'something you know' and 'something you have'.
