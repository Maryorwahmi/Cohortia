Understanding Gas Efficiency in Data Feed Interactions

Distinguishing read-only operations from state-changing costs

Read-Only Operations

Fetching data with latestRoundData() is a view function. It does not directly consume gas for the caller, making simple price queries very efficient.

State-Changing Operations

Any calculations or state modifications *after* reading the price will consume gas. This is where optimization is crucial.

Example Scenario

A lending protocol reads ETH/USD price. If it then calculates a liquidation threshold and executes a liquidation, the *liquidation* incurs gas, not the price read itself.

Misconception Check

It's not the act of querying Chainlink that's expensive, but rather the subsequent complex logic or state updates within your contract that utilize that data.

Key Takeaway

Focus gas optimization efforts on the logic that processes and acts upon the fetched data, not just the data fetching itself.
