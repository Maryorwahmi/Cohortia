Core Concept

TCP Three-Way Handshake: Establishing Connection

The foundational process for reliable communication.

Step 1

SYN (Synchronize)

The client initiates the connection by sending a SYN packet to the server, proposing a connection and its initial sequence number.

Step 2

SYN-ACK (Synchronize-Acknowledgment)

The server responds with a SYN-ACK packet, acknowledging the client's SYN and proposing its own initial sequence number.

Step 3

ACK (Acknowledgment)

The client sends a final ACK packet, acknowledging the server's SYN-ACK and completing the connection establishment.

Wireshark Tip

Filter for tcp.flags.syn == 1 or tcp.flags.ack == 1 to quickly locate handshake packets. The 'Info' column often provides clear labels.

Common Mistake

Overlooking a failed handshake. This often indicates a firewall blocking the connection or a server not listening on the specified port. Always check for the complete handshake first.

Key Takeaway

The three-way handshake is vital for reliable TCP communication. Its successful completion confirms both parties are ready to exchange data, and its failure points to fundamental network or server issues.
