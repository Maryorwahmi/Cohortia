GDPR: Protecting EU Personal Data

The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) is a landmark EU law governing the collection, processing, and storage of personal data for EU residents, with global implications.

Data Minimization

Collect only necessary personal data.

Privacy by Design

Integrate privacy into system architecture.

Data Subject Rights

Rights to access, erasure, and objection.

Breach Notification

Mandatory reporting within 72 hours.

Key Takeaway

GDPR's global reach means cloud customers must ensure CSPs meet obligations, understand data residency, and have robust Data Processing Agreements (DPAs).

Cloud Implications

  • •Data Processing Agreements (DPAs): Essential contracts with CSPs defining data protection responsibilities.
  • •Data Residency: Verifying CSP data center locations to meet geographical storage requirements.
  • •International Data Transfers: Implementing mechanisms like Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) for compliant cross-border data movement.
  • •Privacy by Design/Default: Ensuring cloud services are configured to protect privacy from the outset.

Example Scenario

An EU-based e-commerce company uses a US cloud provider to host its customer database. Even though the company is in the EU, the US cloud provider must adhere to GDPR for any personal data of EU residents it processes. This requires a DPA and careful consideration of data transfer mechanisms.
