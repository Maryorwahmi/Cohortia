Planning and Implementing DLP Policies

From Data Classification to Policy Tuning

1. Define Sensitive Data

Clearly define what constitutes "sensitive data" for your organization, leveraging existing data classification efforts. This is the foundation of any DLP strategy.

2. Create Specific Policies

Develop granular DLP policies tailored to different data types and cloud services. For example, a policy might prevent credit card numbers from being shared externally via SharePoint.

Challenge: False Positives

False positives are common; overly aggressive policies can disrupt legitimate business operations. This necessitates careful tuning and testing to minimize disruption.

3. Tune & Test Rigorously

Policies should be tuned and tested rigorously, often starting in audit-only mode to understand the impact before enforcing blocking actions. This iterative process is crucial.

Key Takeaway

Effective DLP implementation is a continuous cycle of defining, creating, testing, and refining policies, with a strong focus on minimizing false positives and business disruption.
