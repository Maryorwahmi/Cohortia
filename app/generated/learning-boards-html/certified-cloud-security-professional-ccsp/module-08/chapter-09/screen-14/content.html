Module 9 › Chapter 8.9 › Avoiding Pitfalls in Cryptographic Compliance
Common Mistake

"Default Encryption is Enough"

A frequent error is assuming that enabling basic cloud encryption features automatically satisfies all compliance requirements. Different regulations have specific mandates regarding key management, algorithm strength, and validation levels.

Why it Matters

Tailoring Controls to Regulations

It is crucial to understand the specific requirements of applicable compliance frameworks (e.g., FIPS 140-2, GDPR, HIPAA, PCI DSS) and tailor cryptographic controls accordingly, rather than relying on generic settings.

Key Takeaway

Effective cryptographic compliance requires a deep understanding of specific regulatory mandates and a tailored implementation strategy, not just default settings.

Example

PCI DSS vs. SSE-S3

While SSE-S3 might encrypt data in S3, PCI DSS often requires FIPS 140-2 Level 3 validated key management. Simply using SSE-S3 without verifying the underlying key management meets this standard would be a compliance failure.

Consequence of Error

Non-Compliance Risks

Failing to meet specific cryptographic requirements can lead to significant penalties, audit failures, loss of certifications, reputational damage, and potential legal liabilities, especially in regulated industries.
