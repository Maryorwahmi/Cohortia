Module 9 › Chapter 8.9 › Quantum Computing's Impact on Cryptography
Core Concept

Quantum Computing Threat

The advent of powerful quantum computers poses a significant threat to current asymmetric encryption algorithms (like RSA and ECC), which are foundational to secure communications and digital signatures.

Why it Matters

Future-Proofing Cryptography

To counter this threat, researchers are developing Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) algorithms designed to be resistant to quantum attacks, ensuring the long-term security of digital systems.

Key Takeaway

Awareness of quantum computing's cryptographic threat and planning for Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) adoption are essential for future cloud security strategies.

Example

Impact on TLS and PKI

Quantum computers could break the encryption used in TLS (securing web traffic) and compromise digital signatures used in PKI. This would necessitate a transition to PQC algorithms for all secure communication channels.

Common Misconception

Quantum Threat is Immediate

While the threat is real and requires preparation, large-scale, fault-tolerant quantum computers capable of breaking current encryption are not yet widely available. However, 'harvest now, decrypt later' attacks mean sensitive data encrypted today could be vulnerable in the future.
