Core Concept

High Availability vs. Fault Tolerance

Understanding the nuances of continuous operation

High Availability (HA)

  • •Minimizes downtime by having redundant components.
  • •Allows systems to continue operating even if one component fails, often with a brief interruption.
  • •Focuses on quick recovery and minimizing the duration of outages.
  • •Goal: 'Staying up' with acceptable recovery time objectives (RTO).
Key Takeaway

HA aims for minimal downtime with quick recovery, while FT targets zero downtime and data loss through complete, active redundancy.

Fault Tolerance (FT)

  • •Ensures continuous operation without any interruption or data loss.
  • •Achieved through complete redundancy and rapid, seamless failover.
  • •Components are often active-active or hot standby, ready to take over instantly.
  • •Goal: 'Never going down' with near-zero RTO and RPO.
Analogy

HA is like a car with a spare tire; you can recover from a flat, but there's a stop. FT is like a car with run-flat tires; you keep driving without interruption.

Common Misconception

Assuming HA automatically means FT. HA allows for brief outages, while FT aims for absolute continuity.
