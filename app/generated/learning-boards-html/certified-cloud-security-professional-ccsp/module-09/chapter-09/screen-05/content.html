AI/ML Challenges

Data Privacy and Algorithmic Bias

Understanding the risks of AI/ML deployments in the cloud.

Privacy Concerns

AI systems process vast amounts of data, including personal data, for training and inference. This raises critical questions about data provenance and consent.

  • Data Provenance: Tracing the origin and transformations of data.
  • Consent: Ensuring individuals agree to data use, especially for sensitive data.
  • Right to Explanation: Under GDPR, individuals can request explanations for automated decisions.
Key Takeaway

Proactive measures like data governance and bias detection are essential to mitigate AI/ML risks.

Algorithmic Bias

The potential for AI models to perpetuate or amplify existing biases from training data can lead to discriminatory outcomes, triggering legal and ethical challenges.

Example: An AI hiring tool trained on historical data that favored male applicants might inadvertently learn and perpetuate this bias, leading to fewer female candidates being selected, even if qualified.

Misconception

AI is inherently unbiased because it's code. In reality, AI reflects the biases present in its training data and design.
