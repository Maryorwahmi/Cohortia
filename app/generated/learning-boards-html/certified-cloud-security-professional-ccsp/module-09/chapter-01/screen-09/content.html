GDPR's Global Impact

Applying to organizations processing EU residents' data, wherever they are located.

What is GDPR Extraterritoriality?

The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) applies to organizations processing personal data of EU residents, regardless of the organization's physical location.

Key Implications
  • Non-EU cloud providers serving EU citizens must comply.
  • Adds layers of complexity to global compliance strategies.
  • Requires robust data protection measures for EU resident data.
  • Enforces strict rules on consent, data rights, and data transfers.
Scenario: Non-EU Company, EU Data

A U.S.-based e-commerce company uses a cloud provider with data centers in the U.S. to serve customers globally, including those in the EU.

  • GDPR Applies: Even though the company and data centers are in the U.S., processing EU resident data triggers GDPR.
  • Compliance Required: The company must implement GDPR-compliant data handling, consent mechanisms, and data subject rights.
  • Architectural Impact: May require data segmentation, specific encryption, and careful data transfer agreements (e.g., Standard Contractual Clauses).
Misconception Alert

Many believe GDPR only applies to companies within the EU. This is incorrect; its reach extends globally to any entity processing data of EU residents.

Key Takeaway

GDPR's extraterritoriality demands global compliance for any organization handling EU resident data, necessitating proactive architectural and policy adjustments.
