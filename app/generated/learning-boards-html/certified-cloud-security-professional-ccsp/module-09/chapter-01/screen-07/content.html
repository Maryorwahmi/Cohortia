The Principle of Data Sovereignty

Asserting national control over data collected or stored within its borders.

What is Data Sovereignty?

Data sovereignty is the principle that data is subject to the laws and governance structures of the nation in which it is collected or stored. This implies that the originating nation retains legal control over that data.

Key Characteristics
  • Focuses on legal ownership and control, not just physical location.
  • Often tied to the nationality of the data subjects.
  • Can lead to conflicts when data is stored internationally.
  • A nation may claim control over its citizens' data even if it resides elsewhere.
Data Residency vs. Data Sovereignty

Data Residency

Focus: Physical storage location.
Mandate: Data must be *physically present* within a specific geographic border.

Data Sovereignty

Focus: Legal control and governance.
Mandate: Data is *subject to the laws* of the nation where it originates or is collected.

Tension in Cloud

The global nature of cloud services means data can be physically resident in one country (meeting residency) but still subject to the sovereign laws of another (due to data origin or subject nationality), creating legal conflicts.

Key Takeaway

Data sovereignty is about legal control, often conflicting with physical residency, and requires careful consideration of national laws over data regardless of its storage location.
