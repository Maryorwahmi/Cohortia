U.S. CLOUD Act: Global Reach

Compelling U.S. cloud providers to disclose data regardless of storage location.

What is the CLOUD Act?

The U.S. CLOUD Act (Clarifying Lawful Overseas Use of Data Act) allows U.S. law enforcement to compel U.S.-based cloud providers to disclose data.

Key Provisions
  • Applies to U.S.-based cloud providers.
  • Data disclosure can be compelled regardless of physical storage location.
  • Condition: Provider must have 'care, custody, and control' over the data.
  • Aims to streamline access to electronic data for U.S. investigations.
Conflict with Data Sovereignty

The CLOUD Act directly conflicts with the sovereignty principles of other nations, particularly those in the EU, which view such requests as an infringement on their data protection laws (e.g., GDPR).

Scenario: Legal Dilemma

A U.S. cloud provider stores data for a French company in a data center in Paris. A U.S. court issues a CLOUD Act order for this data.

  • U.S. Provider's Obligation: Must comply with the U.S. court order.
  • French/EU Law: Data is subject to GDPR and French data protection laws.
  • Result: The provider faces a direct legal conflict, potentially violating one set of laws by complying with another.
Key Takeaway

The CLOUD Act creates significant legal tension by asserting U.S. jurisdiction over data held by U.S. providers globally, challenging the data sovereignty of other nations.
