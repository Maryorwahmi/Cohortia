Pitch explained

Pitch determines perceived height. Use it to suggest size, material, and urgency. Changes over time (rising/descending) create motion or closure.

High pitch: quick, alerting, small.
Low pitch: heavy, menacing, large.
Pitch glide: rising increases tension, falling resolves.
Practice example
Imagine a weapon hit. If you raise pitch slightly it reads as a sharper, more piercing hit — useful for a small blade. Lowering pitch and adding a longer low tail shifts perception to a heavier bludgeon.
Common mistake
Relying on pitch alone to create 'power'. Power often needs low-frequency energy and a pronounced transient as well as pitch change.
Key Takeaway

Pitch is a fast shorthand for size and urgency. Use it in concert with timbre and dynamics.
