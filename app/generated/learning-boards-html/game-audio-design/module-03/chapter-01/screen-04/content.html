Module 03 › Chapter 3.1 › Why SFX matter
Definition

SFX as information and emotion

Sound effects convey state and shape feeling. They are short, targeted audio events that confirm, warn, or decorate, but crucially they guide player behavior when designed intentionally.

Feedback: confirms actions, prevents ambiguity.
Atmosphere: supports environment and narrative tone.
Emotion: provokes tension, reward, or surprise.
Concrete example
UI click: short, high transient, fast decay. It reduces user uncertainty. Contrast that with an explosion: long duration, heavy low frequencies, slow decay to convey weight and danger.
Key Takeaway

Match sound traits to function: clarity for UI, subtle loops for ambience, impact for combat.

Practice prompt
  1. Pick one sound you hear often in a game and name its purpose.
  2. Describe one sonic change that would alter the player’s reaction.
  3. Test mentally how that change affects clarity and emotion.
Common mistake: making SFX too loud or complex so they mask other important cues. Instead, make the transient clear and keep supporting textures subtle.
