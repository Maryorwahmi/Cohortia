Module 5 › Chapter 5.3 › Why script audio
What scripting adds
  • Precise timing: open, close, or fire sounds in response to events.
  • Stateful control: pause and resume loops without losing position.
  • Expressive changes: change pitch and volume based on gameplay variables.
  • Efficient layering: use PlayOneShot rather than many temporary sources.
Concrete example

When collecting five items quickly, PlayOneShot lets each pickup chime overlap cleanly instead of cutting the previous chime.

Analogy

Think of Inspector-assigned clips as printed sheet music. Scripting is the conductor who decides when and how loudly the orchestra plays.

Misconception

You don't need a separate AudioSource per sound. PlayOneShot often handles overlapping short sounds on a single source.

Key Takeaway

Scripted audio makes sounds reactive, efficient, and easier to tune to gameplay.
