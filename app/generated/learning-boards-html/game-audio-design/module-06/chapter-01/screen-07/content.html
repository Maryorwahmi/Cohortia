Dynamic Control

Understanding Audio Compression

Taming Dynamics for Consistency and Punch

What it Does

Reduces the dynamic range of a sound, making quiet parts louder and loud parts quieter for more consistent volume.

Key Parameters
  • Threshold: Level at which compression begins.
  • Ratio: Amount of compression applied.
  • Attack: How quickly compression engages.
  • Release: How quickly compression disengages.
Common Uses in Games
  • Dialogue consistency
  • Master bus 'glue'
  • Adding punch to SFX
Concrete Example

Applying light compression to a character's dialogue track ensures that both their whispers and shouts are clearly audible, preventing sudden volume spikes or drops that could make lines unintelligible.

Key Takeaway

Compression is vital for controlling dynamics and achieving a cohesive mix, but always use it judiciously to avoid making sounds lifeless.

Common Mistake

Over-compression, which can make sounds flat, artificial, and remove all natural dynamic expression, leading to a fatiguing and uninteresting mix.
