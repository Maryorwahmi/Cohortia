Principle

Nyquist and aliasing

Sample at least twice the highest frequency you care about. Aliasing occurs when you do not, producing mis-mapped frequencies.

If highest content = 18 kHz → min sampling rate = 36 kHz; choose 44.1 or 48 kHz for headroom.
Aliasing sounds like unexpected low-frequency tones or ringing. It is a misrepresentation, not distortion added by effects.
Concrete check
Example: cymbal energy to 18 kHz recorded at 32 kHz may produce aliasing artifacts in the midrange.
Anti-alias filters help but plan sample rates to provide filter roll-off room.
When in doubt, choose a standard rate (48 kHz) to reduce risk of aliasing for typical audio content.
Key Takeaway

Follow Nyquist and allow filter headroom. Prevent aliasing by choosing a sample rate that is safely above your highest content frequency.
