Definition

Vertical re-orchestration

Compose a track as layered parts that can be mixed at runtime so intensity and color change smoothly.

Base: ambient pad holding harmony and tempo.
Dynamic: melody or percussion that adds focus.
Alert: tension strings or drums for high stakes.
Key Takeaway

Vertical layering keeps a single composition adaptive, avoiding jarring track switches.

Analogy & Common misconceptions
Analogy: The base is a room; layers are lighting and furniture that change mood without rebuilding the room.
Misconception: Layers are not independent tracks. They must be written to combine without clashing.
Contrast: Unlike switching whole songs, layering preserves continuity and tempo.
Concrete example
A dungeon base pad plays continuously; when the player nears a puzzle, a delicate piano layer fades in, then tension drums swell if a trap is sprung.
