The Era of Manual Network Management

Understanding Traditional CLI-Driven Processes

Definition: Traditional Network Management

The historical method of configuring network devices manually via command-line interfaces (CLIs), often device by device. This approach provided granular control but was resource-intensive and prone to human error as networks scaled.

Analogy: Crafting by Hand

Imagine building a complex machine by hand, assembling each component individually. This mirrors the meticulous, device-by-device configuration of traditional networking, where every change requires direct manual input.

Key Takeaway

Manual CLI management was foundational but became a bottleneck as networks grew, highlighting the need for scalable solutions.
