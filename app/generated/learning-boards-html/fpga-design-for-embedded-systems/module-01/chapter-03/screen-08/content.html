Module 2 › Chapter 1.3 › Generational Evolution of FPGAs
Timeline
First Gen (1985–1995)
Simple LUT-based logic for prototyping and glue logic.
Second Gen (1995–2005)
Added block RAM, multipliers and PLLs; viable for DSP.
Third Gen (2005–2015)
Embedded hard processors, high-speed transceivers, PCIe — SoC-style FPGAs.
Fourth Gen (2015–present)
AI/ML accelerators, HLS, and advanced packaging for heterogeneous compute.
Why this matters

Knowing the generation helps you choose the right family: DSP-heavy projects favor second-generation features, embedded systems favor third-generation SoC integration, and AI tasks benefit from fourth-generation accelerators and HLS support.

Misconception check
Do not assume newer generation always means better for your use. A low-power IoT node may prefer a small, older-family device optimized for power over a large AI-targeted chip.
Key Takeaway

Match generation attributes to system needs: memory and DSP for signal work, hard CPUs for software-rich systems, and AI features for inference tasks.
