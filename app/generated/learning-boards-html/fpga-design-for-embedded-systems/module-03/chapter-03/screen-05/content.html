Module 4 › Chapter 3.3 › Technology Mapping and the 4-input LUT
Definition

Mapping netlist to device primitives

The mapper assigns functions to available LUT widths and pairs inferred flip-flops with CLB registers on the device.

4-input AND example
Function: Y = A AND B AND C AND D
Result: Single 4-input LUT programmed with truth table where only A=B=C=D=1 produces 1.
Supporting points
  • Mapper groups gates into sub-functions that fit the LUT input limit.
  • When gates share inputs, they may be packed together to save LUTs.
  • Excess distinct inputs force decomposition across LUTs with routing between them.
Common mistake
Assuming logical equivalence implies equal LUT use. Two logically equivalent forms can map very differently.
Example
A 4-input function maps to one LUT. Adding a fifth distinct input requires a second LUT to combine partial results.
