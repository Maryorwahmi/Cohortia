Module 4 › Chapter 3.3 › Splitting Wide Functions Across LUTs
Scenario

5-input AND decomposition

Mapper chooses X = A & B & C & D in one LUT, then Y = X & E in another. Two LUTs and one routing connection are required.

Concrete example

A logical 5-input AND becomes a short cascade of two LUTs. Each LUT stores truth table entries for its four inputs.

Practical notes
  • Mappers attempt to minimize LUT count but cannot exceed device input width.
  • Chaining increases routing delay compared with a single LUT.
  • Consider sharing intermediate signals or using registers to break logic into stages.
Common mistake

Expecting a single LUT for many-input functions; planning must reflect LUT width limits.
