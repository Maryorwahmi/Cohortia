Module 3 › Chapter 3.2 › T and JK Flip-Flops
T Flip-Flop
Toggles output when T=1; holds when T=0. Useful for dividing clock frequency by two when T is tied high.
Concrete: tying T=1 yields a flip every clock, producing a square wave at half the input clock rate.
Use: simple ripple or synchronous counters where toggling is required.
JK Flip-Flop
J and K inputs allow set, reset, hold, and toggle behavior. When both are high you get toggle; other combinations map to set or reset.
Concrete: configure J=K=1 to toggle for counting, or use J/K combinations to implement conditional set/reset logic.
Misconception: JK is not inherently cheaper in modern FPGAs. Synthesis usually targets D flip-flops, so JK logic often becomes additional combinational resources.
Pro Tip

Prefer D flip-flops in LUT-based FPGAs; express toggle or JK behavior with D plus combinational logic for predictable resource use.

Compare implementations
Try both designs for a 3-bit counter: implement one with chained T flip-flops and another with D flip-flops plus XORs. Observe resource use and maximum clock achievable after synthesis.
