Pipelining, Resource Sharing, and Design Optimization
Understand practical trade-offs and apply targeted techniques to meet frequency or area goals.

This lesson explains how inserting registers, reusing hardware, and guiding synthesis with constraints change clock frequency, latency, and area. You will see concrete Verilog examples, a recommended lab exercise, and a decision checklist for real designs.

Optimization is not magic — it is structured trade-off management. We keep functionality identical while changing timing and resource mapping.

Pipelining
Insert registers to shorten combinational paths and raise fmax; expect higher latency.
Resource Sharing
Reuse multipliers/adders via multiplexers when operations don't overlap in time to save area.
Retiming & Constraints
Move registers across logic and use SDC constraints to guide the synthesis engine toward goals.
