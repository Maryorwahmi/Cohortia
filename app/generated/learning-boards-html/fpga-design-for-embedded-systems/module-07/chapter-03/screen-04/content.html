Module 8 › Chapter 7.3 › What is pipelining?
Definition

Pipelining inserts registers along a long combinational path so each clock performs a smaller slice of the computation. This reduces the maximum logic delay per stage and raises the possible clock frequency.

Why it matters
Higher sustained throughput and the ability to meet tighter timing requirements for high-performance datapaths.
Common misconception
Higher clock rate always reduces latency. Not true: pipeline depth increases latency in clock cycles even if cycle time falls.
Concrete example
A 32-bit multiplier as a single-cycle block may run at 20 MHz. Split into four balanced stages, each stage is shorter, allowing a roughly 80 MHz clock. Latency increases from 1 to 4 cycles, but throughput after startup becomes one result per 80 MHz cycle.
Key Takeaway

Balance stage delays and measure both fmax and cycle latency; pipelining is for throughput improvement under higher clock targets.

Check your understanding
If routing delay dominates a single pipeline stage, retiming or stage reallocation may help. Consider moving the register across logic or splitting heavy logic further.
