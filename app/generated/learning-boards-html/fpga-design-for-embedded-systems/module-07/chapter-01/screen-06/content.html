Module 07 › Chapter 7.1 › Flip-Flop Timing
Terms defined
  • T_co: clock-to-Q delay; time until output is stable after the clock edge.
  • T_setup: data must be stable this long before the capture edge.
  • Hold time: data must remain stable after the edge to be captured correctly.
Example numbers commonly used in illustrations: T_co ≈ 0.2 ns, T_setup ≈ 0.1 ns. These are not zero and contribute to the required clock period.
Concrete scenario
If a launching flip-flop has T_co of 0.2 ns and the path through logic takes 6 ns, and the receiving register needs 0.1 ns setup, the required time without skew is 6.3 ns. If you target 10 ns clock period, slack is 3.7 ns.
Common pitfall
Hold violation

Do not assume hold violations can't happen. They can occur with small routing delay or negative skew; inspect hold checks if you see unstable capturing behavior.

Pro tip
When pipelining, add registers where T_logic dominates to reduce stage delay and increase f_max.
Why it matters
Small timing parameters limit top-end clock frequency; they compound with logic delays on each path.
Check
Always include vendor flip-flop timing in STA runs; don't assume idealized zero delays.
