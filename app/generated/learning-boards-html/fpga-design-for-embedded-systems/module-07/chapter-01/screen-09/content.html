Module 07 › Chapter 7.1 › SDC: create_clock
SDC example
create_clock -name {clk} -period 20.000 [get_ports {clk}]
create_clock -name {clk} -period 20.000 -waveform {0.000 10.000} [get_ports {clk}]
set_clock_uncertainty -setup 0.200 [get_clocks {clk}]
Place these lines at the top of your .sdc so the analyzer knows the clock domain before other constraints.
Concrete advice
If the tool cannot resolve the clock source name exactly, it will not analyze that domain. Match port or net names precisely, and prefer explicit waveform and uncertainty when jitter or duty cycle matters.
Key Takeaway

Define clocks and their uncertainty first so other SDC directives are interpreted relative to known domains.

Misconception
Omitting uncertainty makes reports optimistic; always model expected jitter conservatively.
Check
Verify the clock name resolves with get_clocks in the tool before running full timing analysis.
Why it matters
Timing decisions depend on the clock period and uncertainty; these drive whether slack is positive or negative.
