Module 7 › Chapter 7.2 › 2-FF vs 3-FF
Two-stage

Low latency, high reliability

One cycle of added latency. Good for most single-bit signals where extremely low probability of failure is acceptable.

• Minimal hardware and latency cost
• Typical deployment for interrupts, ready signals, and flags
• Probability of unresolved metastability becomes very small after second stage
Three-stage

Higher reliability, extra latency

Two cycles of added latency. Use when MTBF or safety requirements demand an even lower probability of metastability propagating.

• Further reduces already tiny failure probability
• Useful in safety-critical or aerospace applications
• Consider cost: added latency and pipeline complexity
Decision guidance
If your system tolerates an extra cycle of delay and requires maximal robustness, add a third FF. If latency is critical and failure probability targets are met with two FFs, prefer two-stage. Always document the MTBF assumptions and verify with vendor data when required.
