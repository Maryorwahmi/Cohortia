Module 6 › Chapter 6.3 › Why visualization matters
Core Concept
Visualization connects code to hardware
Synthesis transforms HDL. Visualization reveals whether that transformation matches intent and where optimizations will help.
Definition
Visualization means inspecting RTL, mapped netlists, and physical placement to validate and optimize designs.
Concrete example
Finding an inferred latch in RTL from a missing else branch, then fixing HDL to ensure a register is synthesized instead.
Common mistake
Assuming the fitter will fix logic-level coding errors or hide inefficient combinational chains.
Key Takeaway

Always check the RTL netlist early to validate synthesis intent and prevent wasted effort later in the flow.

Quick diagnostic checklist
  • Compile through Analysis & Synthesis and open the RTL Viewer.
  • Confirm registers, multiplexers, and arithmetic units appear as expected.
  • If you see latches or unexpected counters, inspect your conditional branches and clocking statements.
  • Decide whether to refactor code, add default assignments, or add pipeline stages.
Analogy: think of RTL as the skeleton of your design. If the skeleton is wrong the rest of the body cannot be fixed by clothing or surface treatment. Fix the bones first.
