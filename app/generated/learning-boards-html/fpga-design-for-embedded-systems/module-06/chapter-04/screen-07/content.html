Module 7 › Chapter 6.4 › Preparing a Testbench
What to include
  • Clock generation that matches your target clock period.
  • Reset sequence that asserts for several cycles and deasserts away from a clock edge.
  • Initialization of registers or explicit reset inside the design.
  • Stimulus vectors that exercise expected and edge cases.
  • Assertions or $display/$monitor statements for automated checking.
Example
A testbench that asserts reset for five cycles, then shifts a pattern into the sequence detector to verify a detected pulse appears for one clock cycle.
Do and don't
Do: Keep testbenches self‑checking and reproducible so they run unattended in regression suites.
Don't: Rely only on manual waveform drawing for complex verification; it is error‑prone and hard to automate.
Concrete checklist
Add clocks, asserts/resets, stimulus sequences, and print statements. Add internal signals to the waveform before running.
Key Takeaway

A repeatable, self‑checking testbench is the backbone of reliable verification and regression testing.
