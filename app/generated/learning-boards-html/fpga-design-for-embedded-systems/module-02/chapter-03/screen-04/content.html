Module 3 › Chapter 2.3 › Intel FPGA Families
Overview
Intel families by tier
MAX 10
Low-cost, instant-on with embedded flash and ADC. Example device: 10M50DAF484C7G — 50k logic elements, 1,638 Kbits M9K, 144 DSP blocks, 12-bit ADC.
Cyclone V
Low-power, SoC variants with dual-core ARM Cortex-A9 for embedded OS and heterogeneous applications.
Arria 10
Mid-range with hard PCIe Gen3 and DDR4 controllers for video and networking tasks.
Stratix 10 / Agilex
High-performance families with the fastest transceivers, hard ARM cores, and advanced process nodes for AI and data centers.
Why these distinctions matter
  • Cost-sensitive designs map to MAX 10 or Cyclone families.
  • SoC needs or embedded Linux prefer Cyclone V or Arria SoC families with hard processors.
  • High bandwidth and AI workloads push you to Stratix or Agilex for transceivers and DSP scale.
Common misconception
Newer process nodes always win. In reality, toolchain support, on-board peripherals, and cost are often more important for embedded projects.
Key Takeaway

Pick a family based on the dominant project constraint: cost, embedded processor needs, memory bandwidth, or transceiver speed.
