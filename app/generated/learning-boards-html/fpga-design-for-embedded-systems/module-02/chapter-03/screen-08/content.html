I/O Count
Create a table of every external signal you need: sensors, switches, displays, serial ports, external memory, and debug headers. Sum them — that's the I/O pin budget you must meet.
Quick checklist
  • Digital I/O pins required
  • External memory interfaces (DDR, parallel NOR/NAND)
  • Analog front-ends or ADCs on-board
Clocks & Transceivers
List distinct clock domains and confirm the device PLL/MMCM count. For multi-gigabit links, verify transceiver generation and whether the family supports the protocol you need, such as PCIe or 10G Ethernet.
Common pitfall
Assuming general I/O pins can replace transceivers; high-speed serial requires dedicated hardware in many families.
Example scenario
If your design needs PCIe and external DDR4, you will need a family with hard PCIe controllers and DDR memory controllers, like Arria or higher. For sensor-heavy embedded designs without high-speed serial, a MAX 10 or Artix-7 part is often sufficient.
Key Takeaway

Interfaces frequently determine family choice; confirm I/O and transceiver requirements before evaluating logic capacity.
