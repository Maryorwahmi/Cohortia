Five-step selection process
1. Enumerate resources
List LUTs/LEs, BRAM Kbits, DSP blocks, I/O pins, clock domains, and transceivers.
2. Rank constraints
Decide whether cost, power, or bandwidth is the primary constraint.
3. Map to families
Use family profiles to shortlist candidate devices that meet the dominant constraint.
4. Prototype
Use an evaluation board like DE10-Lite to confirm peripheral and toolchain fit.
5. Re-check power and margin
Ensure device fits power envelope and leave headroom for future features.
Quick example
If a design needs 5,000 logic elements, 50 Kbits of memory, 20 multipliers, and 40 I/O pins, choose the most cost-effective device that meets these numbers. The chapter recommends the Intel MAX 10 in this case because it meets the requirements at lower cost than Cyclone V or Artix-7 alternatives.
Key Takeaway

Use a structured workflow to avoid overlooked constraints and to justify your device choice with measurable checks.
