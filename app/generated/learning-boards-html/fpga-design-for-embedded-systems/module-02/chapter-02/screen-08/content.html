Module 2 › Chapter 2.2 › FLASH-based FPGAs
Definition

Embedded FLASH configuration

FLASH cells hold configuration permanently until erased or reprogrammed, enabling immediate operation after power-up and eliminating external configuration memory.

Instant-on: No serial load from external memory is required at power-up.
Lower standby power: FLASH cells do not need refresh current to maintain state.
Reprogram limits: FLASH supports a finite number of erase/program cycles, typically orders of magnitude fewer than SRAM rewrites.
Use-case example
An automotive power controller that must respond immediately after ignition benefits from embedded FLASH configuration because it eliminates a separate flash chip and shortens startup latency.
Misconception
Non-volatile FLASH does not mean you can reprogram it endlessly; plan firmware updates within the erase/program cycle limits.
Key takeaway
Use FLASH-based FPGAs when instant-on, internal storage, and low standby power are critical, understanding the trade-off in reprogramming cycles and density.
