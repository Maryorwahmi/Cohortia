Core Concept

Definitions and core differences

Clear definitions help you reason about trade-offs and pick the right family for your system.

SRAM-based: Volatile memory holds LUTs and routing; bitstream reload required at power-on.
FLASH-based: Embedded non-volatile cells retain configuration; instant-on and lower standby power.
Anti-fuse: One-time programmable, permanent conductive links formed during programming.
Concrete examples
SRAM example: Prototype FPGA that you reprogram daily to test new logic variants.
FLASH example: An embedded control module that must power up instantly without waiting for a bitstream.
Anti-fuse example: A safety-critical flight controller that must resist tampering and radiation-induced reconfiguration.
Common misconception
Non-volatile does not imply unlimited reprogrammability. FLASH may have limited erase cycles, and anti-fuse is permanent.
Key takeaway
Each technology trades reprogrammability, startup time, and resistance to tampering; choose based on system priorities.
