Module 3 › Chapter 3.1 › What is a VLAN?
VLAN — a compact definition

A VLAN is a logical broadcast domain created by tagging frames with a VLAN ID. It groups devices that should share layer 2 connectivity without regard to physical port location.

Key points
  • Logical separation, not encryption or routing by itself.
  • 802.1Q adds a 4-byte tag containing VLAN ID to identify the segment.
  • VLANs reduce broadcast domains and aid traffic management.
Analogy
Think of VLANs as separate rooms in the same building: people in one room hear each other but not those in another room unless a door (router) is opened.
Example
HR VLAN 10
All HR desktops and servers are in VLAN 10. Broadcasts such as ARP requests stay inside VLAN 10. To reach other VLANs, HR uses an IRB gateway IP.
Common misconception
VLANs are not security boundaries by themselves. Without routing policies or ACLs, devices from other VLANs can communicate if routing allows it.
Key Takeaway

Use VLANs to segment broadcast domains; pair them with routing and policy to enforce access control.
