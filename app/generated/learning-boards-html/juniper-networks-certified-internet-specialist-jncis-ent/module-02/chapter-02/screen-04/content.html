Module 2 › Chapter 2.2 › 802.1Q Trunks
Definition & behavior

A trunk carries multiple VLANs via 802.1Q tags. Access ports are single-VLAN and untagged. Native VLAN frames are untagged on Junos by default.

Common misconception

A trunk does not implicitly provide access to all VLANs unless configured that way. Limiting VLANs on trunks reduces unnecessary broadcast traffic and attack surface.

Key takeaway

Plan trunk VLAN membership to match topology and security requirements before enabling broad VLAN access.

Explanation points
  • Allowed VLANs control which VLAN IDs travel over a trunk link.
  • The native VLAN is untagged by default; consider changing it for security.
  • Access ports remain untagged and forward frames into their assigned VLAN.
Analogy

Think of a trunk as a conveyor with multiple colored lanes; each color carries one VLAN's frames and tags keep them separate.
