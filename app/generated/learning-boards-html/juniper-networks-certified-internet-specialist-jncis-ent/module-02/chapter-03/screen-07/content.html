Module 2 › Chapter 2.3 › Port Roles
Role definitions
Root Port: chosen per switch as lowest-cost path to the Root Bridge; forwards toward root.
Designated Port: one per segment; the port that forwards for that link based on lowest cost to root.
Non-Designated (Blocked) Port: prevented from forwarding to break loops; still listens for BPDUs.
Misconception check
People sometimes think blocked ports are broken. In reality they are intentionally disabled for forwarding to keep the topology safe; they are standby paths for failover.
Compact example
Triangle of switches: elected Root on SW1. SW2 and SW3 each choose their Root Port toward SW1. On the SW2–SW3 segment one port will be Designated and the other Non-Designated (blocked), determined by cost and Bridge IDs.
Key Takeaway

Remember: root ports point upward to the Root Bridge; designated ports serve a link; blocked ports break loops while remaining available.
