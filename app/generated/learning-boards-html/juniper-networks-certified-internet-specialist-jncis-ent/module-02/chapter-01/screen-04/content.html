Core Concept MAC Address Learning
How a switch learns MACs
The switch records source MACs and associated ingress ports. The MAC table drives selective forwarding and limits unnecessary traffic.
Process explained
  • Inspect source MAC on every received frame and record port↔MAC mapping.
  • Use table entries to forward frames only to the port where the destination resides.
  • If destination unknown, flood within the broadcast domain until response builds the table.
  • MAC entries age out and are refreshed by traffic from the same device.
Example
A workstation sends ARP after boot. The switch learns the workstation's MAC from that ARP and records its port.
Common mistake
Assuming switches learn destination MACs or that entries never expire. Both lead to incorrect troubleshooting steps.

Key Takeaway

MAC learning is reactive: traffic from a device creates the table entry you then rely on for efficient forwarding.
