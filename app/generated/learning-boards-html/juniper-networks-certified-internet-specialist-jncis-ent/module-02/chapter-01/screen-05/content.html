Process Flow Forwarding and Flooding
Forward or Flood
A simple decision tree determines selective forwarding or broadcast flooding until learning occurs.
  1. Frame arrives; switch reads source MAC and updates table.
  2. Switch looks up destination MAC in the table.
  3. If found, forward to that port only.
  4. If not found, flood to all ports in the same VLAN except ingress.
  5. When destination replies, the switch learns its MAC and future frames are forwarded selectively.
Concrete example
A new printer sends ARP; initial frames flood. Once the printer responds, its MAC is recorded and normal forwarding resumes.
Misconception
Flooding is not always a fault. It is part of correct switch behavior for unknown destinations.

Pro tip

When troubleshooting intermittent loss, check for MAC table flaps or rapid aging that can cause repeated flooding.
