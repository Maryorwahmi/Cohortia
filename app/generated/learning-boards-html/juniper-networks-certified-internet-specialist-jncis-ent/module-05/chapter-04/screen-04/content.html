Module 5 › Chapter 5.4 › What is an OSPF Virtual Link?
Definition

A logical tunnel between ABRs

It extends Area 0 across a standard non-backbone transit area by using router IDs of two ABRs.

Concrete example

Area 2 connects only to Area 1. ABR1 connects Area 0 and Area 1 with router-id 1.1.1.1. ABR2 connects Area 1 and Area 2 with router-id 2.2.2.2. Configure a virtual link between the ABRs using Area 1 as the transit area to make Area 2 reach Area 0.

Misconception check

A virtual link is not a new interface or tunnel; it leverages existing LSAs across a standard transit area and requires the transit area to carry the necessary LSAs.

Key Takeaway

Use virtual links only when physical backbone links are impossible; they add complexity and depend on a stable transit area.

Annotated diagram
Remember that the transit area must be standard; avoid placing the virtual link through stub or NSSA areas because they block required LSAs.
