Introduction to Routing Policy
Control what routes your device accepts, modifies, and advertises using named policy statements.
In this lesson
Learn the policy-options hierarchy, terms with from/then, and the implicit deny that rejects routes not explicitly accepted.
Why it matters
Precise route control prevents accidental route leaks, enforces path selection, and secures route redistribution between protocols.
Policy hierarchy
policy-options → policy-statement → term → from / then
Implicit deny
Any route not explicitly accepted by a term is rejected at the end of the policy.
Explore Key Topics
Policy Statements
Named collections of terms defining routing logic.
Terms & Conditions
Match with from and act with then for each route.
Implicit Deny
Default safety: only explicitly accepted routes pass.
Concept map
Policy-options contains policy-statements. Each statement holds ordered terms. Terms use from to match and then to act. First match wins; otherwise implicit deny.
Quick visual: term flow
A route enters a policy-statement, checks term 1's from. If match and then accept, route is installed. If no match, proceed to term 2. If no terms match, the implicit deny discards the route.
