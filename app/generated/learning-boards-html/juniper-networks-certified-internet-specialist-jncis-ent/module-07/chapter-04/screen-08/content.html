Module 07 › Chapter 7.4 › Policy Chaining
How chaining works
List policies in the desired order. Each policy can accept, reject, or pass the route to the next policy using next-policy. This lets you build modular, testable rule sets.
Pattern: Filter → Tag → Accept
First policy removes bad prefixes, second sets attributes, third finalizes acceptance.
Gotcha
Missing next-policy will stop evaluation; explicit actions are safer than implicit denies.
Key Takeaway

Design small policies and chain them deliberately. Use next-policy only when you want evaluation to continue.

Example callout
Imagine POLICY_A rejects bogons with then reject, POLICY_B sets local preference for customer routes with then next-policy, and POLICY_C accepts remaining customer prefixes. Evaluation follows the listed sequence and stops when a policy issues accept or reject.
Check your understanding
If a route should be accepted by POLICY_C but POLICY_B lacks next-policy, what happens? You will discover the route is either accepted or rejected earlier, so include next-policy when you intend continuation.
