Why multi-area OSPF?
Split your domain to control LSDB size and LSA flooding so the network remains stable under load.
Architecture: hierarchical areas
Each area keeps its own LSDB. Routers see full detail for their area only.
ABRs maintain LSDBs per area and generate Type 3/4 LSAs to represent other areas.
Localizing LSA flooding reduces SPF recalculation across the entire AS.
Example scenario
A campus network moves from one large OSPF area to three areas. After the change, a switch failure in Area A triggers LSAs only inside Area A; ABRs advertise a summary to other areas. This cuts memory and CPU impact on remote routers and speeds core convergence.
Common mistake: creating many tiny areas increases ABR route summarization and management overhead.
Key takeaway: Localize churn
Design tip: Balance area size vs ABR count
Pitfall: Disconnected non-backbone areas
