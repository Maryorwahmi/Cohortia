Core Concept
Why OSPF Authentication Matters
Prevent rogue neighbors and protect the LSDB.
Definition

OSPF authentication verifies the identity of neighbors before adjacency formation, preventing unauthorized routers from participating in LSDB exchanges.

Concrete Example
A misconfigured device advertising a default route could cause traffic to be diverted. Authentication stops that device from forming an adjacency and injecting such routes.
Common Misconception
Plaintext is often mistaken as secure enough for lab, but it exposes the secret on the wire and should be avoided in production.
Why this matters in practice
  • Protects network stability by blocking rogue LSAs.
  • Reduces incident scope when a device is compromised.
  • Enables controlled key rotation without disruption when using key IDs.
Key Takeaway

Enable MD5 authentication with matching key IDs on peers to prevent unauthorized adjacencies and preserve LSDB integrity.
