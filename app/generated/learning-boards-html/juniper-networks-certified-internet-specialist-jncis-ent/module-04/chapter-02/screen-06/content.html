Module 4 › Chapter 4.2 › Point-to-Point
Definition

Point-to-Point Links

P2P links are ideal for two-node connections. They form direct adjacencies, do not run DR/BDR elections, and exchange LSAs unicast.

When to use P2P
Dedicated serial/fiber links, or any link where exactly two OSPF routers share a segment. Use it to avoid DR/BDR overhead.
Misconception
Point-to-point is not limited to legacy serials — it is a configuration choice for any two-ended link to simplify adjacency behavior.
Key Takeaway

Choose P2P when exactly two routers share a physical or logical link to avoid election complexity.

Concrete example
Two routers connected by a dedicated fiber pair set the interface-type to p2p on both ends. They immediately form a full adjacency and exchange LSAs without DR/BDR overhead.
Check your understanding
If one side is p2p and the other is broadcast, will adjacency form? No — the mismatch prevents neighbor formation even when addresses are reachable.
