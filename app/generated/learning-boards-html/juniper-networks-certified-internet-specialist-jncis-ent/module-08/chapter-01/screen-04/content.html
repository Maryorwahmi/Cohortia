Core Concept › What is High Availability?

Definition and goals

High availability minimizes downtime by removing single points of failure and enabling rapid recovery. It combines redundancy with state synchronization and verification.

Fault tolerance: continue operating when parts fail.
Rapid recovery: restore full functionality quickly.
Design patterns: active/standby, active/active, path diversity.
Concrete example
Two Routing Engines: primary handles routing protocols; backup stays hot and synchronizes state so it can take over without rebooting the control plane.
Key Takeaway

Redundancy must be paired with mechanisms that preserve and synchronize state to achieve true HA.

Misconceptions

  • - Having two of the same component does not guarantee non-disruptive failover unless state is synchronized.
  • - HA is more than hardware redundancy; you must plan for synchronization, testing, and verification.
Why this matters

Applications like voice, video, and critical databases are sensitive to even short reconvergence windows. Good HA reduces user impact and business risk.
