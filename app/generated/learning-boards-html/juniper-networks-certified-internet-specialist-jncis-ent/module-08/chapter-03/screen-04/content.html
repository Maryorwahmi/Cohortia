Core Concept › What is Nonstop Forwarding (NSF)?
Definition

NSF allows the PFE to keep forwarding packets using its last-known forwarding table while the Routing Engine is unavailable or switching over. The PFE holds state for a grace period to bridge the transition.

Why this matters

Without NSF, a switchover can cause the PFE to flush forwarding tables and create traffic loss until the new RE and NSR repopulate forwarding state.

Common misconception

NSF is not a routing protocol fix; it only preserves forwarding while NSR and the new RE restore protocol adjacencies.

Annotated example
Scenario

Primary RE fails and backup RE becomes primary. PFEs continue forwarding using cached routes while NSR reestablishes protocol sessions.

Verification
  • Observe continuous traffic during switchover.
  • Confirm routing adjacencies are reestablished by NSR after switchover.
  • Check device logs for graceful-restart messages for protocols.
Key takeaway
NSF reduces or eliminates traffic loss during RE transitions by letting PFEs forward on cached state until control-plane synchronization completes.
