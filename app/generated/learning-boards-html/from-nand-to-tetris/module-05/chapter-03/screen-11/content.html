Module 5 › Chapter 5.3 › Push vs Pop
Push (simpler)
Typical steps: compute source, read value, write to stack, increment SP.
Push often needs five to six instructions and does not require saving a destination address before the operation.
Pop (costly)
Typical steps: compute destination, save it (R13), pop stack into D, write D to saved address.
Pop commonly uses extra instructions to preserve the address, making it more complex and slower than push.
Practical consequence
When optimizing or debugging your translator, expect more assembly lines for pop operations and check that the temporary register is not accidentally reused during the sequence.
