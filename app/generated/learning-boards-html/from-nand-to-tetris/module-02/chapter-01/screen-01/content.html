Binary Number Representation and Two's Complement
Bits, hexadecimal, unsigned ranges, and the two's complement rule that unifies arithmetic.
Why this matters
All arithmetic in hardware depends on how numbers are encoded. Understanding representation explains overflow, sign, and why a single adder does subtraction.
In this lesson
Convert numbers, use hex as a compact view, build two's complement negation, and practice addition and subtraction by hand.
Explore Key Topics
Binary
Bits represent powers of two; positional weight determines value.
Hex
Groups of four bits map to one hex digit for compact display.
Two's Complement
Negation by invert and add one; single zero and unified arithmetic.
Visual concept
Binary row: 1 0 1 1 0 1
Place weights: 2^5 2^4 2^3 2^2 2^1 2^0
Value: 32 + 8 + 4 + 1 = 45
Hex nibble mapping
0000 → 0 1011 → B
16-bit example: 1011010100111100 = B53C
Common pitfall
Don't assume sign-magnitude or one's complement rules; two's complement is the hardware standard with a single zero and consistent addition.
