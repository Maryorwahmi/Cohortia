Half Adders, Full Adders, and the ALU
Construct addition from gates and assemble the Hack ALU with control bits and flags.
In this lesson
Build half and full adders. Chain 16 full adders to make a ripple-carry adder. Implement the Hack ALU and verify its outputs and flags.
Why it matters
Every arithmetic operation in a CPU flows through these components. Understanding them gives you control over correctness and timing when building processors in HDL.

Visual concept
Think of adders as small calculators. Each calculator produces a sum and may pass a carry to its neighbor. The ALU is a control panel that routes two 16-bit inputs through preprocessing, a function stage, and optional post-processing to produce the final output and flags.
Result
A compact ALU can perform many operations using a small set of control bits.
Caveat
Ripple-carry adders are simple but slow due to sequential carry propagation.
Getting ready
Have your HDL editor ready. Start by implementing the half adder, then the full adder, then Add16, and finally the ALU as specified in the project tasks.
