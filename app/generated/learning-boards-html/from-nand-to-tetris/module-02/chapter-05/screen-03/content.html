Practical checks
  • Trace from source flip-flop's clock-to-Q to destination flip-flop's data input and add destination setup time.
  • Compare the sum to your clock period. If the sum exceeds the clock period, the design fails in the worst case.
  • Look for hold violation risks: fast source paths can change before destination holds the old value.
  • For asynchronous inputs, use at least two DFF stages to reduce metastability propagation risk.
Misconception check
Running the clock faster always improves performance. Reality: if the clock period is shorter than required delays, correctness fails. Correctness must come before frequency.
