Two-stage synchronizer
Pattern: asynchronous input → DFF1 → DFF2 → synchronous logic. DFF1 may go metastable; DFF2 samples after one full clock period, reducing the chance metastability persists.
When to use
Any time a signal crosses clock domains or originates from an external, asynchronous source such as a button or network input.
Design notes
  • One-cycle latency is the trade-off for reliability.
  • For single-bit signals use two DFFs; for multi-bit signals use handshake or encoding to avoid correlated metastability.
  • Increase synchronizer depth only when you need an even lower failure probability.
Key Takeaway
Two-stage synchronizers are a practical, low-cost pattern to reduce metastability risk for asynchronous single-bit inputs.
