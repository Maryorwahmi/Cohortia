Module 1 › Chapter 1.6 › Why start with Nand?
Definition & claim
Nand is a universal primitive; building gates from it trains compositional thinking and verification habits.
Concrete transformation
Not(in) = Nand(in, in). This simple wiring turns a two-input Nand into an inverter by tying inputs together. That practice generalizes: show how other gates can be expressed by arranging Nand outputs and inputs.
Analogy
Think of Nand as a single brick shape. Complex walls follow from repeating a few brick patterns.
Misconception check
Industry uses many gate types for efficiency, but the logical universality of Nand remains a key teaching tool.
Visual example
Not from Nand
Tie both inputs of a Nand to the same signal; the Nand's output is then the inverted input. This small insight is the starting point for other constructions.
Why this matters
By forcing construction from one primitive you build mental models and avoid treating gates as prepackaged black boxes.
