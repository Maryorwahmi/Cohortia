Process Flow

Build Not, And, Or from Nand

Stepwise constructions that convert a single primitive into a functionally complete toolkit.

Step 1 — Not
Not(x) = Nand(x, x). Tie inputs together and the output is the inversion.
Step 2 — And
And(x,y) = Not(Nand(x,y)) = Nand(Nand(x,y), Nand(x,y)). Use a second Nand to invert.
Step 3 — Or
Or(x,y)=Not(And(Not(x),Not(y))) = Nand(Nand(x,x), Nand(y,y)). Apply De Morgan then replace Not with Nand wiring.
Practical note

Constructive proofs give explicit wiring that you can translate directly into HDL, but optimizing gate count and propagation delay is a separate engineering step.

Concrete wiring visual
Mnemonic

Think: Nand is And plus a final inversion. Build new gates by adding inversion or by applying De Morgan transformations.

Example walkthrough
  1. To get Not(a): connect a to both inputs of one Nand.
  2. To get And(a,b): compute t = Nand(a,b); then And = Nand(t,t).
  3. To get Or(a,b): compute na = Nand(a,a), nb = Nand(b,b); then Or = Nand(na,nb).
Key Takeaway

Universality is a construction: explicit Nand wiring yields Not, And, and Or, and therefore any Boolean function.
