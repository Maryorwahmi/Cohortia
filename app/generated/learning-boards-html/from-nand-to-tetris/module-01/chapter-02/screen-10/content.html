Module 1 › Chapter 1.2 › From Algebra to Gates: Nand-only Implementation
Conversion pattern
Or(x,y) = ¬(¬x ∧ ¬y)
¬x = Nand(x,x)
So Or(x,y) implemented as:
  t1 = Nand(x,x)   ; produce ¬x
  t2 = Nand(y,y)   ; produce ¬y
  out = Nand(t1,t2) ; yields Or(x,y)
Use De Morgan to push negations to the literals and then replace each Not with a Nand of a single input.
Practical tip
Always try to algebraically simplify first. A simplified expression may eliminate the need for intermediate negations and therefore save Nand gates.
Check your work
After mapping to Nand gates, re-evaluate the truth table for a few rows to confirm functional equivalence to the original expression.
Common mistake

Converting before simplifying often increases gate count; simplify first to reduce the circuit's complexity.
