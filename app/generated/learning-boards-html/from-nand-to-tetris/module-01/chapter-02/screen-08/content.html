Module 1 › Chapter 1.2 › De Morgan's Laws
Laws
¬(x ∧ y) = ¬x ∨ ¬y
¬(x ∨ y) = ¬x ∧ ¬y
These rules distribute negation and flip the connective. They are central when moving between And/Or forms and when implementing circuits with a restricted gate set.
Concrete transformation
Or(x,y) = ¬(¬x ∧ ¬y)
Not(x) = Nand(x,x)  (so ¬x is Nand(x,x))
Combine these to implement Or with three Nand gates: negate inputs with Nand, then Nand the negations and negate the result as needed.
Common mistake

When applying De Morgan, be sure to negate each literal and flip the connective. Missing a negation produces an incorrect function.
