Module 5 › Chapter 5.4 › Why animations and gestures matter
Definition

Motion communicates; gestures enable direct input

Animations reduce cognitive load by showing transitions. Gestures map touch to meaningful actions like drag, swipe, or pinch.

Why it matters
Motion clarifies change and builds trust; gestures let users manipulate content directly.
Misconception
More motion is not always better. Prefer purposeful, short transitions and respect motion sensitivity settings.
Example
Swiping a card off-screen confirms dismissal and removes ambiguity about the result of the gesture.
Key Takeaway

Design motion to clarify state and map gestures to natural touch actions while ensuring performance and accessibility.

Analogy
Think of animations as stage directions and gestures as the audience interaction. The stage guides attention; the audience moves the scene.
Common pitfalls
  • Animating non-animatable properties leading to jank.
  • Not using useNativeDriver for transforms and opacity when possible.
  • Failing to manage Animated.Value offsets after a gesture ends.

Pick a state change and add a 200ms timing animation of opacity or translate. Test on a device to confirm smoothness.
