Strategic Control Selection

Aligning Controls with Risk and Organizational Needs

Selection Process

Key Factors to Consider

  • 1. Effectiveness: Will it genuinely reduce risk?
  • 2. Cost-effectiveness: Is the cost proportionate to the risk addressed?
  • 3. Feasibility: Can it be implemented with current resources and culture?
  • 4. Compliance: Does it meet regulatory or industry standards?
  • 5. Integration: How well does it fit with existing systems?
  • 6. Organizational Impact: Will it disrupt business or burden users?
Key Takeaway

Control selection is a strategic, ongoing process that balances risk reduction with practical organizational considerations.

Process Flow

From Risk to Control

1.

Risk Assessment Results: Identify and prioritize specific risks.

2.

Evaluate Factors: Consider effectiveness, cost, feasibility, compliance, integration, and impact.

3.

Select & Implement: Choose controls that best align with organizational needs.

Scenario Example

A new data encryption control is proposed. It's highly effective, but its high cost and potential for disrupting legacy systems require careful re-evaluation against the risk it mitigates.
