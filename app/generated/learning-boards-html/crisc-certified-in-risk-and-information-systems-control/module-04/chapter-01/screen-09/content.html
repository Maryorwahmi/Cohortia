Risk Mitigation: Reducing Likelihood and Impact

Implementing controls to lower risk to an acceptable level

What is Risk Mitigation?

Risk mitigation focuses on reducing the likelihood or impact of a risk to an acceptable level through the implementation of various controls. It's the most common strategy.

  • Reduces risk, doesn't eliminate it.
  • Involves implementing controls.
  • Aims for an acceptable residual risk.
Example Scenario

To mitigate data breach risk from phishing, a company implements user training, email filtering, multi-factor authentication, and endpoint detection tools.

Types of Mitigation Controls

  • Technical Controls: Firewalls, IDS, encryption, MFA.
  • Administrative Controls: Policies, procedures, security awareness training.
  • Physical Controls: Access badges, surveillance, environmental controls.
  • Proactive Measures: Vulnerability scanning, penetration testing.
Key Takeaway

Risk mitigation is about actively reducing risk through a combination of technical, administrative, and physical controls, aiming for a proportionate and cost-effective approach.
