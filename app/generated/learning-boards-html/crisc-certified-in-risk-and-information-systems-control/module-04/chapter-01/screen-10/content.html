Risk Mitigation: Proportional Controls and Cost-Benefit

Balancing investment with risk reduction

Proportionality and Cost-Effectiveness

Controls must be proportionate to the risk level and cost-effective, meaning the investment in controls should not exceed the potential cost of the risk event.

  • Controls match risk severity.
  • Cost of control vs. potential loss.
  • Aims for optimal resource allocation.
Example Scenario

A small business mitigates the risk of a minor website defacement with basic security plugins, rather than investing in an expensive enterprise-grade WAF, due to proportionality.

Common Pitfalls in Mitigation

Mistakes often occur when balancing cost and risk. Over-investing or under-investing can both lead to inefficient or ineffective risk management.

  • Over-investing in controls for low-impact risks.
  • Under-investing in controls for high-impact risks.
  • Failing to regularly review and update controls.
Key Takeaway

Always perform a cost-benefit analysis to ensure mitigation controls are proportionate and cost-effective, avoiding both over-investment and under-investment in risk reduction.
