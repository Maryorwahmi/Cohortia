Risk Transfer: Shifting Responsibility

Reallocating the financial impact or operational burden of risk

What is Risk Transfer?

Risk transfer involves shifting the financial impact or operational responsibility of a risk to a third party. The risk itself is not eliminated, but its burden is reallocated.

  • Shifts financial or operational burden.
  • Risk accountability often remains.
  • Commonly through insurance or outsourcing.
Example Scenario

A company purchases cyber liability insurance to cover potential costs from data breaches, effectively transferring the financial risk to the insurance provider.

Methods of Risk Transfer

  • Insurance: Cyber liability, business interruption, professional indemnity.
  • Outsourcing: Shifting risky IT functions to specialized third-party providers (e.g., MSSP).
  • Contractual Agreements: Indemnity clauses, service level agreements (SLAs).
Key Takeaway

Risk transfer reallocates the burden of risk to a third party, typically via insurance or outsourcing, but the ultimate accountability often remains with the organization.
