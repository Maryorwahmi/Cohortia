Categorizing Threats with STRIDE and DREAD

Structured Frameworks for Analysis

STRIDE Methodology

Categorizes threats during design phase.

  • Spoofing: Impersonating entities.
  • Tampering: Unauthorized data modification.
  • Repudiation: Denying actions.
  • Information Disclosure: Unauthorized data access.
  • Denial of Service: Preventing legitimate access.
  • Elevation of Privilege: Gaining higher access.

Example: STRIDE in Action

For a login system, 'Spoofing' would involve an attacker faking credentials, while 'Denial of Service' could be a flood of login attempts.

DREAD Methodology

Assigns ratings to prioritize identified threats.

  • Damage: How much harm?
  • Reproducibility: How easy to repeat?
  • Exploitability: How easy to exploit?
  • Affected Users: How many users impacted?
  • Discoverability: How easy to find?
Key Takeaway

STRIDE helps identify the types of threats, while DREAD provides a framework to prioritize them based on their potential impact and ease of exploitation.
