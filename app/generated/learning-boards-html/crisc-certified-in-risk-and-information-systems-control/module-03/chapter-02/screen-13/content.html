Prioritizing Risks

Risks are ranked based on their assessed level and how they align with the organization's risk appetite. This determines which risks require immediate action.

Stakeholder Involvement

  • Ensures decisions reflect strategic direction and values.
  • Promotes buy-in and shared understanding of risk.
  • Prevents arbitrary decisions and inefficient resource allocation.
Key Takeaway

Effective risk evaluation requires clear criteria, defined risk appetite, and active stakeholder engagement for consistent decision-making.

Risk Response Based on Evaluation

Risks Exceeding Appetite

Require immediate attention and treatment (e.g., mitigation, transfer, avoidance). These are high-priority risks.

Risks Within Appetite (High/Medium)

May require monitoring and potential mitigation. These risks are tolerated but watched closely.

Risks Within Appetite (Low)

Generally accepted. No immediate action required, but periodic review is still good practice.

Real-World Connection

A company's decision to invest in advanced cybersecurity tools for a specific threat is directly driven by its risk evaluation, comparing the threat's ALE against the cost of the control and its risk appetite.
