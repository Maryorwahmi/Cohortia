Module 3›Chapter 3.2›Qualitative Analysis

Definition

A subjective, non-numerical approach that prioritizes risks based on their perceived likelihood of occurrence and potential impact. It's about relative severity, not exact figures.

When to Use It

  • When precise data is unavailable or unreliable.
  • For initial, high-level risk assessments.
  • To facilitate rapid prioritization and communication.
  • When a quick overview of risk severity is needed.
Key Takeaway

Qualitative analysis provides a fast, high-level understanding of risks, crucial for initial screening and communication.

Qualitative Assessment Scale

Likelihood: Very Low to Very High

Impact: Negligible to Catastrophic

Result: Low, Medium, or High Risk Rating

Example Scenario: New Software Vulnerability

A newly discovered vulnerability in a critical, widely used software application.

  • Perceived Likelihood: High (exploit code is public)
  • Perceived Impact: Major (could lead to data breach)
  • Qualitative Risk Rating: High Risk
