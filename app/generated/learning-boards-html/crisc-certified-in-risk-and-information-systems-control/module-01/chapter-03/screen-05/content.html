Qualitative Risk Assessment

Subjective, descriptive, and expert-driven

Definition

A subjective and descriptive approach relying on expert judgment to categorize risks using terms like 'High,' 'Medium,' and 'Low' for likelihood and impact.

When to Use It

Ideal for initial assessments, prioritizing many risks, or when precise data is unavailable. It offers a quick overview.

Example Scenario

A new software project needs a rapid risk overview. Experts categorize potential delays as 'Medium Likelihood, High Impact' based on past experience.

Key Takeaway

Qualitative assessment provides a fast, high-level understanding of risks, leveraging expert insights when detailed data is not feasible.

Qualitative vs. Quantitative

Qualitative
  • Subjective
  • Descriptive (High/Medium/Low)
  • Expert Judgment
  • Quick & Easy
Quantitative
  • Objective
  • Numerical (Monetary Values)
  • Data-Driven
  • Precise & Complex
Common Pitfall

Inconsistent qualitative scoring due to lack of clear criteria. Always define 'High', 'Medium', 'Low' across the organization.
