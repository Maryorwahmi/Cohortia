Accountability in Risk Governance

Clear Roles and Responsibilities for Risk Decisions

What is Accountability?

Accountability ensures that specific individuals or groups are held responsible for risk-related decisions, actions, and their outcomes. It clarifies who owns which risks and who is responsible for managing them.

Example

The Board of Directors is accountable for setting the overall risk appetite, while department heads are accountable for managing operational risks within their units.

Why it Matters

Prevents risks from being overlooked, ensures timely action, and fosters a culture of ownership and responsibility.

Key Aspects of Accountability

  • •Clear Assignment: Roles and responsibilities for risk are explicitly defined and communicated.
  • •Decision Ownership: Individuals are responsible for the consequences of their risk-related decisions.
  • •Performance Link: Risk management performance is often linked to individual and team objectives.
  • •Reporting Structure: Clear reporting lines ensure accountability up the organizational hierarchy.
Common Misconception

Accountability is not about blame, but about ownership and responsibility for proactive risk management.

Key Takeaway

Establishing clear accountability ensures that every individual and department understands and fulfills their role in managing organizational risks effectively.
