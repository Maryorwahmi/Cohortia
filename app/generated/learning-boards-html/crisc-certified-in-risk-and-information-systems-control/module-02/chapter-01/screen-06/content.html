Core Principle

Transparency in Risk Governance

Open Communication of Risk Information to Stakeholders

Defining Transparency

Transparency in risk governance means openly sharing relevant risk information with internal and external stakeholders. This includes risk assessments, mitigation strategies, and the outcomes of risk management activities.

Example

A company publicly discloses its cybersecurity risk posture and incident response plan to reassure customers and regulators.

Benefits

Fosters trust, enables informed decision-making, and enhances organizational credibility.

Key Elements of Transparent Communication

  • •Timeliness: Information is shared promptly, especially for emerging risks.
  • •Understandability: Complex risk data is presented clearly and concisely.
  • •Relevance: Focus on information pertinent to the specific stakeholder group.
  • •Accuracy: Ensure all communicated risk information is factual and verified.
Common Pitfall

Withholding or sugarcoating negative risk information, which can erode trust and prevent proactive solutions.

Key Takeaway

Transparency in risk governance builds trust and empowers stakeholders with the information needed for effective oversight and decision-making.
