Core Concepts

Understanding Risk Appetite, Tolerance, and Capacity

Defining the Foundation of Risk Management

Risk Appetite

The broad amount of risk an organization is willing to accept in pursuit of its objectives. It's a strategic, often qualitative, statement.

Example: A startup has a high appetite for innovation risks.

Risk Tolerance

The acceptable deviation from the risk appetite, often expressed in specific, quantitative terms or limits for particular activities.

Example: No single IT project risk exceeding $500,000 loss.

Risk Capacity

The maximum amount of risk an organization can bear without violating laws, regulations, or solvency, or fundamentally threatening its existence.

Example: The absolute upper limit before catastrophic failure.
Analogy Card

Imagine a car: Risk Appetite is the driver's overall comfort with speed. Risk Tolerance is the specific speed limit set for a journey. Risk Capacity is the car's maximum structural limit before it breaks down.

Common Mistake

Confusing risk appetite with risk tolerance, or having an overly vague appetite statement, can lead to exceeding acceptable risk levels.
