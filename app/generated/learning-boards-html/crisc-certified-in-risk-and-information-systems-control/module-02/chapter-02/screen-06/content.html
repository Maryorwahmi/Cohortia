Risk Tolerance: Defining Specific Limits

Acceptable Deviation from Risk Appetite

What is Risk Tolerance?

It's the acceptable deviation from the risk appetite, setting specific limits or boundaries for particular risk categories or activities.

  • Often expressed in quantitative terms.
  • Provides measurable thresholds for operational teams.
  • Ensures consistency in risk-taking.
Key Takeaway

Risk tolerance translates the strategic risk appetite into concrete, measurable limits, enabling effective monitoring and control of specific risks.

Examples in Practice

Cybersecurity: Maximum acceptable downtime of 4 hours for critical systems.

Financial Risk: Single project budget overrun not to exceed 10%.

Compliance: Zero tolerance for violations of GDPR regulations.

Pitfall

Without clear, measurable risk tolerances, the broader risk appetite can be misinterpreted, leading to inconsistent decision-making and potential overexposure to risk.
