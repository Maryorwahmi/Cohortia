DEFINITION

Monitoring: Understanding Known Unknowns

Tracking Predefined System Health Indicators

What is Monitoring?

The practice of collecting predefined data points (metrics, logs, traces) to track system health and performance against known thresholds and expected behaviors.

Why it Matters

Crucial for detecting expected issues, setting alerts for known failure modes (e.g., high CPU, error rates), and ensuring systems operate within defined parameters.

The Car Dashboard Analogy

Gauges for speed, fuel, engine temperature, and warning lights. These tell you if your car is operating within *expected* parameters.

Limitation

Only tells you about issues you *knew* to look for. Doesn't explain *why* or *where* unexpected problems occur.

Key Takeaway

Monitoring is about reacting to known conditions and thresholds, providing a surface-level view of system health.
