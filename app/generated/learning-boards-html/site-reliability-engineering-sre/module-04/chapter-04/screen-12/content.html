Common Mistakes in Incident Automation

Learning from common pitfalls to build robust systems

Mistake 1: Fire-and-Forget Automation

Deploying automated scripts without proper monitoring of the automation itself. If the automation fails silently, the incident might worsen unnoticed.

Risk

Unseen failures, prolonged incidents, or automation exacerbating the problem.

Mistake 2: Automating the Unknown

Automating complex or poorly understood processes. If the underlying system behavior isn't clear, automation can lead to faster, harder-to-debug failures.

Risk

Introducing new, unpredictable failure modes and increasing incident complexity.

Mistake 3: Neglecting Updates

Failing to update runbooks and automated scripts as the system evolves. Outdated automation can become ineffective, irrelevant, or even harmful over time.

Risk

Ineffective responses, increased manual intervention, and a loss of trust in automation.

Key Takeaway

To build reliable incident automation, continuously monitor its performance, only automate well-understood processes, and regularly update all scripts and runbooks.
