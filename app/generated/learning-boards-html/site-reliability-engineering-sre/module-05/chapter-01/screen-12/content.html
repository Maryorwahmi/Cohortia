SLOs vs. SLAs: Internal vs. External Commitments

Understanding the difference and the SRE buffer for reliability

SLO: Internal Target

An SLO is an internal target for an SLI, guiding engineering efforts to meet user expectations. Missing an SLO typically has no legal consequences.

SLA: Formal Contract

An SLA is a formal, often legal, contract between a service provider and a customer, specifying service levels and penalties for non-compliance.

Key Takeaway

SLOs are internal goals for service quality, while SLAs are external, legally binding agreements. SREs use a buffer to ensure SLOs are met well before SLAs are breached.

The SRE Buffer Strategy
SLA Target

99.9% Availability (e.g., 8 hours, 45 minutes of downtime per year).

Internal SLO Target

99.95% Availability (e.g., 4 hours, 22 minutes of downtime per year).

Why the Buffer?

The buffer provides a safety margin, allowing SRE teams to address and resolve issues before they impact the more stringent, contractually binding SLA, avoiding penalties.
