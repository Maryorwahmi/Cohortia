Common Mistakes

Navigating Alerting Anti-Patterns

Strategies to prevent alert fatigue and ensure alert effectiveness.

1. Alerting on Causes, Not Symptoms

Focusing on internal metrics (e.g., high CPU) rather than user-impacting symptoms (e.g., high latency). This creates noise.

2. Too Many Alerts

Bombarding engineers with non-actionable or low-priority notifications, leading to alert fatigue and missed critical alerts.

3. Noisy Alerts

Alerts that fire for transient issues or non-critical events. Use `for` clauses (e.g., `for: 5m`) to ensure persistence.

4. Lack of Context

Alerts without a clear description, severity, or link to a runbook make effective response difficult.

5. No Clear Ownership

If it's unclear who is responsible for an alert, it often gets ignored, leading to prolonged incidents.

Key Takeaway

Proactively address these common pitfalls to build an alerting system that is effective, sustainable, and trusted by your team.
