Mistakes in SLO Implementation

Avoiding common errors when defining and managing SLOs.

Common Mistakes

  • Too Ambitious/Lax: SLOs that are unrealistic or too easy to meet.
  • Lack of Monitoring: Setting SLOs without the ability to measure them accurately.
  • Too Many SLOs: Leads to alert fatigue and diluted focus.
  • Ignoring Critical Journeys: Not prioritizing SLOs for essential user paths.
Key Takeaway

Thoughtful SLO definition, robust monitoring, and clear distinction from SLAs are crucial for success.

SLOs vs. SLAs

  • SLOs (Service Level Objectives): Internal targets for service reliability.
  • SLAs (Service Level Agreements): Formal, often contractual, agreements with customers, potentially with financial penalties.
  • Relationship: SLOs help you meet your SLAs proactively.
Analogy

Think of an SLO as your personal fitness goal (e.g., run a mile in 7 minutes), while an SLA is a contract with a client to deliver a product by a certain date, with penalties if missed.
