Core Concept Channels & Private Data
Channels
A channel is a private ledger between a defined subset of organizations. Transactions on a channel are invisible to organizations outside it.
When to use
  • Separate workflows with distinct member sets.
  • When ledger isolation simplifies governance or compliance.
Private Data Collections (PDCs)
PDCs share confidential payloads with selected peers while a hash stays on the channel ledger for proof of existence.
When to use
  • Most participants see transaction metadata, but only some see details.
  • Reduces ledger duplication while preserving provenance.
Example
Banks share KYC status on a common channel. Full identity documents are stored in a PDC accessible only to originator and approved reviewers. The channel holds a hash to prove the document existed at a specific time.
Endorsement Policy
Controls who signs transactions
Chaincode Scope
Deploy per channel or across channels
Storage Impact
PDCs reduce full ledger size for non-members
