Module 12 › Chapter 12.1 › Start with the business problem
Definition
Channels are a policy boundary that controls which organizations store and validate a ledger.
Why map data flows?
  • It reveals who needs visibility and which transactions can be isolated.
  • It helps prevent over-sharing and avoids creating too many channels needlessly.
  • It informs endorsement policies and ordering capacity planning.
Common mistake
Creating a single channel for convenience that exposes all transactions to every member, causing privacy leaks and performance issues.
Concrete example: supply chain sketch
Separate channels for raw materials, shipping, and regulatory reporting keep ledgers focused and private.
Example channel choices
Raw Material: Manufacturer + Distributor. Shipping: Distributor + Retailer. Regulatory: all three organizations.
Guided checklist
  1. List transactions and who must see them.
  2. Assign minimal membership per transaction set.
  3. Decide whether PDCs are needed for private subsets.
Key Takeaway

Design channels from data flows: smaller membership reduces ledger bloat and improves privacy. Ask who really needs each record.
