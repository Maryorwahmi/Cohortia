Permissioned identity and governance
Control who can join, transact, and enforce policies.
What it is
A network where participants are authorized, identities are managed by certificate authorities, and governance rules determine endorsement and access.
Concrete example
Banks joining a trade finance consortium require KYC, controlled membership, and strict audit trails—permissioned membership enforces those needs.
Common misconception
Permissioned does not mean single operator. Governance can be shared among members but must be explicitly agreed and enforced.
Key distinctions
  • Known participants versus anonymous public nodes.
  • Enforceable endorsement policies and identity-checked transactions.
  • Privacy controls through channels and private data collections.
Key Takeaway

Make identity and governance primary design constraints; they define who can share data and how policies are enforced across the network.
