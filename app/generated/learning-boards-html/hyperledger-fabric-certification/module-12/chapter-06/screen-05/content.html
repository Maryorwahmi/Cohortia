Supply chain: provenance and privacy
Trace items, speed recalls, and protect commercial data.
Step 1: Record minimal provenance
Store batch IDs, timestamps, and status events on the shared ledger so provenance is verifiable by all participants.
Step 2: Keep sensitive fields private
Use private data collections for pricing, commercial contracts, and sensitive certifications that only a subset of members may access.
Step 3: Link off-chain artifacts
Store large documents off-chain and keep content hashes on the ledger for integrity verification.
Concrete example: IBM Food Trust
Food producers and retailers record product journeys so contaminated batches are identified rapidly, reducing waste and protecting consumers.
Common mistake
Putting excessive data on-chain increases costs and complicates privacy. Keep essential, verifiable facts on-chain and link to off-chain storage for details.
Key Takeaway

Design a minimal on-chain representation for provenance and use PDCs to protect confidential relationships and pricing.
