Definition

Decentralized Identity

DIDs let entities control their identifiers and present verifiable claims. Fabric can act as an anchor or verifier while private data stays off-chain.

Core idea
Store credential hashes and revocation anchors on-chain; hold credential content in private collections or off-chain stores.
Governance
Define issuers, verifiers, and revocation processes. Decide which artifacts the regulator can audit.
Misconception
DIDs do not remove Fabric's CAs; they integrate with existing identity management to enable user-controlled claims.
Concrete example
A university issues a verifiable degree. The ledger records a signed hash and revocation status. Employers verify the hash without accessing the full student record.
Design checklist
  • • Choose what is on-chain (hashes, anchors) versus off-chain (credential content).
  • • Define issuers, revocation procedures, and auditor access paths.
  • • Use private data collections when credentials must be shared among a subset of orgs.
Key Takeaway

DIDs and verifiable credentials complement Fabric by enabling portable claims while preserving enterprise governance and auditability.
