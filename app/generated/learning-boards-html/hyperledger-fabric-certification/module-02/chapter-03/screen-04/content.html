Core Concept
Why identity matters
Identity enables authentication, authorization, and auditability in Fabric’s permissioned model.
  • Authentication: Certificates prove who issued a request.
  • Authorization: Chaincode policies and ACLs rely on organizational membership.
  • Non-repudiation: Signed transactions are traceable to an identity.
Concrete example
When appUser1 submits a transaction, peers verify its certificate chains to a trusted CA defined in the channel MSP before endorsing.
Key Takeaway

Treat certificates as passports and keep private keys secure to preserve network trust.

Analogy: Passport office and checkpoints
Passport Office
Certificate Authority issues passports (certificates).
Passport
X.509 cert binds identity to a public key.
Border Check
Peers and orderers verify certificates against MSP rules.
Misconception check: TLS protects transport, but does not replace identity certificates used for signing and authorization. Both may be X.509 but serve different purposes.
