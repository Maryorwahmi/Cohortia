Channels: private transactions on shared infrastructure

A concise orientation to why channels exist, how they create isolated ledgers, and when you would use them in a consortium.

What you will understand
  • How a channel isolates ledger and state from other channels
  • What a channel configuration transaction and genesis block contain
  • How peers join and what can go wrong operationally
Why it matters

Channels let organizations transact privately without running separate Fabric networks. This reduces infrastructure duplication while preserving confidentiality for business-sensitive flows.

Concrete example

A manufacturer and a logistics provider create a dedicated channel to exchange shipment manifests and pricing. Retailers remain excluded and cannot see those transactions.

Common pitfall

Over-segmentation creates operational overhead; under-segmentation risks exposing sensitive data. Channel design must balance privacy with manageability.
