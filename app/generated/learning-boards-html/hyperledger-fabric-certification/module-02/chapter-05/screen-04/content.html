Core Concept

Channel definition and components

A short definition and example to ground the idea for operational scenarios.

Ledger isolation
Each channel has its own blockchain and state database; data committed on one channel is not visible on another.
Chaincode scope
Chaincode must be installed/approved for each channel where its logic will run and maintain state.
Membership
Only member organizations of a channel can see transactions and participate in endorsement and ledger maintenance.
Ordering
Channels reference ordering service endpoints to produce a separate genesis block and block stream for each channel.
Analogy
Think of channels as private rooms inside a shared office building: resources are shared, but conversations in one room are private to attendees.
Key Takeaway

Channels provide ledger-level confidentiality so multiple business networks can coexist on the same Fabric infrastructure.

Example scenario
Manufacturer ↔ Logistics channel

A private channel between manufacturer and logistics provider stores shipping manifests and pricing. Retailers are not members and cannot access this ledger.

Misconception check: channels isolate ledgers and state, not network packets. Anchor peers and gossip settings still matter for discovery between organizations.
Key Takeaway

Channels let selected participants transact privately while allowing shared infrastructure to reduce duplication.
