Why Load Balancing?

Distributes incoming network traffic across multiple resources to ensure high availability and improve application performance by preventing single points of failure and bottlenecks.

OSI Model Layers

A conceptual framework that standardizes network communication into seven layers. Azure load balancers primarily operate at Layer 4 (Transport) or Layer 7 (Application).

Key Takeaway

Understanding the OSI layer at which a load balancer operates is crucial for selecting the right Azure service for your application's traffic management needs.

Real-World Connection

Imagine a popular e-commerce site during a flash sale. Without load balancing, a single server would be overwhelmed, leading to slow responses or crashes. Load balancing ensures all customer requests are handled efficiently.

Layer 4 vs. Layer 7 Load Balancing

Layer 4 (Transport)

Distributes traffic based on IP address and port (TCP/UDP). Simple, high-performance, low-latency.

Layer 7 (Application)

Distributes traffic based on HTTP/HTTPS requests (URL, headers). Offers advanced routing and security features.

Layer 4 operates at the packet level, while Layer 7 understands application-specific data.
