Understanding JIT and JEA Principles

Minimizing Risk with Just-In-Time and Just-Enough-Access

Why JIT and JEA Matter

  • Reduce Attack Surface: Limits the time and scope of elevated privileges, making accounts less vulnerable.
  • Prevent Lateral Movement: Restricts an attacker's ability to move across systems if an account is compromised.
  • Improve Auditability: Provides clear records of when and why elevated access was granted and used.
  • Enforce Least Privilege: Ensures users operate with the minimum necessary permissions at all times.

Just-In-Time (JIT) Access

Permissions are granted only when needed and for a limited, predefined duration. This prevents standing administrative access.

Just-Enough-Access (JEA)

Users receive only the specific permissions required to perform their task, nothing more. This prevents over-privileging.

Key Takeaway

JIT and JEA are foundational security principles that ensure privileged access is temporary and precisely scoped, significantly reducing the risk of compromise.
