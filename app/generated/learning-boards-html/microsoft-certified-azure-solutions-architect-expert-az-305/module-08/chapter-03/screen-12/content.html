Recovery Actions

Executing Failover and Recovery

Understanding the differences between planned and unplanned failover scenarios.

Failover Types and Commit

Planned Failover

Controlled operation, zero data loss, used for migrations or scheduled DR events.

Unplanned Failover

Immediate operation during actual disaster, potential for minimal data loss.

Commit Operation

Finalizes the failover, making recovered VMs the new primary and stopping original replication.

Key Takeaway

Choosing the correct failover type and understanding the commit process are vital for successful and controlled disaster recovery.

Failover Scenario: Planned Migration

A company decides to migrate its production environment from one Azure region to another during a scheduled maintenance window. They initiate a planned failover, ensuring all data is synchronized, then gracefully shut down the source VMs. The replicated VMs are brought online in the new region, and after verification, the failover is committed. This results in a seamless migration with no data loss.

Failover Scenario: Unplanned Disaster

A critical application's primary region experiences an unexpected, widespread outage. The operations team initiates an unplanned failover. ASR quickly brings up the replicated VMs in the secondary region using the latest available recovery point. While there might be a few minutes of data loss, the application is restored rapidly, minimizing business impact.
