Definition

High Availability explained

High availability is achieved when your database recovers from failures with minimal downtime. In Neo4j, that depends on the cluster topology and Raft consensus among cores.

Concrete example
A three-core cluster loses one core; the remaining two elect a leader and continue after a brief election pause.
Common misconception
Adding cores always improves availability. In truth you must balance quorum size and network reliability.
Visual summary
Causal Cluster
Core members hold consensus state via Raft.
Read replicas
Asynchronous copies for scaling reads; eventual consistency applies.
Operational checks
Monitor discovery addresses, network latency, and cluster member health.
Key Takeaway

HA relies on consensus among cores for safe writes; design your cluster, network, and monitoring to maintain quorum under failure.
