Cypher Clause

The DELETE Clause: Relationships First

Removing specific relationships to maintain graph structure

Purpose of DELETE

Primarily used for removing relationships or nodes that have no existing connections.

Graph Integrity Enforcement

Prevents deletion of connected nodes to avoid 'dangling' relationships and maintain a consistent graph state.

Example: Deleting a Relationship

To remove a specific connection, you must explicitly match and delete the relationship variable.

Key Takeaway

Use DELETE when you need to remove relationships or isolated nodes, ensuring graph integrity by design.

// Delete a specific relationship
MATCH (p1:Person {name: 'Alice'})
      -[r:FRIENDS_WITH]->
      (p2:Person {name: 'Bob'})
DELETE r
// Explanation:
MATCH: Finds Alice, Bob, and their specific FRIENDS_WITH relationship.
DELETE r: Removes only the relationship 'r', leaving Alice and Bob nodes intact.

Common Misconception

Trying to DELETE a node with relationships will result in an error, not a cascading deletion. You must handle relationships separately.
