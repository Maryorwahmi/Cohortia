WITH Clause: Complex Example

Filtering and ordering intermediate aggregated results

Query Breakdown

  • •Match Pattern: Identify people and their movies.
  • •Aggregate with WITH: Count movies per person.
  • •Filter with WHERE: Keep only those with > 5 movies.
  • •Return & Order: Present final results sorted.
Example: Actors with > 5 Movies
MATCH (p:Person)-[r:ACTED_IN]->(m:Movie)WITH p, COUNT(r) AS moviesActedInWHERE moviesActedIn > 5RETURN p.name, moviesActedInORDER BY moviesActedIn DESC
This query finds actors with more than 5 movies, returning their name and count, sorted by movie count.
Key Takeaway

The WITH clause enables complex multi-stage queries, allowing you to aggregate and filter intermediate results before continuing with further processing, significantly improving readability.

Pro Tip

Use WITH to introduce new variables that are derived from previous parts of the query, like aggregated counts, before using them in subsequent clauses.
