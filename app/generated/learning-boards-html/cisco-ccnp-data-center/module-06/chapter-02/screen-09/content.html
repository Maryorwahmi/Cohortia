Granular Security with EPGs and Contracts

Defining Communication Rules Between Application Components

End-Point Groups (EPGs)

  • Logical grouping of endpoints (VMs, servers, containers).
  • Endpoints share common policy requirements.
  • Grouped by application role (e.g., 'Web EPG', 'Database EPG').
  • Abstracts away IP addresses and VLANs.

Contracts

  • Granular security policies defining allowed communication between EPGs.
  • Detailed agreements, not just simple allow/deny.
  • Specify protocols, ports, and actions.
  • Enforced even if EPGs are on the same physical switch.
Key Takeaway

EPGs and Contracts are fundamental to ACI's micro-segmentation, enabling precise control over inter-application communication and preventing lateral threat movement.

Example: Web-DB Communication

A contract allows a 'Web App' EPG to communicate with a 'Database' EPG only on TCP port 1433 (SQL Server), blocking all other ports.

Misconception: VLANs vs. EPGs

EPGs are not just VLANs. They are logical groupings that enable policy enforcement independent of network topology, offering far greater granularity.

Why it Matters

This fine-grained control is a powerful defense against lateral movement, significantly enhancing the isolation and security of your application components.
