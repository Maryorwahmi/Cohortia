Timedelta basics
Create durations and apply them to date or datetime objects to compute offsets.
from datetime import datetime, timedelta

start = datetime(2023, 10, 27, 14, 30)
shift = timedelta(days=5, hours=3)
future = start + shift
print(future)  # 2023-11-01 17:30:00
  • Timedelta accepts days, seconds, microseconds, milliseconds, minutes, hours, weeks.
  • It does not accept months or years because their lengths vary.
  • Always inspect the result to ensure you accounted for boundaries and DST if relevant.
Concrete scenario
Calculate a reminder 3 days and 2 hours before December 25, 2023 at 10:30 AM using subtraction of a timedelta.
Common mistake
Trying to subtract two dates to get a datetime — subtracting produces a timedelta, not a datetime; combine operations correctly.
Key Takeaway

Timedelta is your reliable tool for duration arithmetic expressed in fixed units like days and seconds.
