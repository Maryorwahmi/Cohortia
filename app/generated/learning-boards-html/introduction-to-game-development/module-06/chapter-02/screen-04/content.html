Module 6 › Chapter 6.2 › Why audio matters
Core Concept

Sound as feedback and mood

Sound confirms player actions and sets emotional context. Good audio design increases satisfaction and clarity.

Example
A snappy coin jingle plays on collection; it signals success and encourages repeat play.
Misconception
Louder sounds aren’t always clearer. Balance through the mixer to avoid masking important cues.
Why it matters
Intentional audio improves game feel and helps players learn what to expect from actions and environments.
Design Checklist
  • Decide which actions need instant feedback (e.g., pickups, hits).
  • Choose short, low-latency clips for SFX and stream long music tracks.
  • Group audio in an Audio Mixer for easy balancing during playtesting.
  • Use silence and dynamics as design tools when appropriate.
Scenario
Making a tense hallway: remove music, add distant rumble and soft footsteps to raise focus.
Check
Does the sound make the player's next action clearer? If not, simplify or boost that cue.
Key Takeaway

Treat sound as interaction design: use it to signal results and create atmosphere, balanced through mixer groups.
