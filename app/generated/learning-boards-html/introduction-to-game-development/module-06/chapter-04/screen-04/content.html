Module 6 › Chapter 6.4 › Why Playtesting Matters
Definition
Playtesting is observing real players to identify issues and validate design choices.
Concrete example
A tester calls the jump "floaty" — that observation leads to adjusting physics or animation timing.
Common misconception
Testing only at the end is risky. Early tests catch structural problems when fixes are cheapest.
Key Takeaway
Treat playtesting as a continuous feedback loop, not a final checkpoint.
What to watch for
  • Confusing UI or unclear objectives
  • Controls that feel slow, floaty, or unresponsive
  • Unexpected crashes or glitches
  • Moments that are either boring or frustrating
Example scenario
Run a short session where a player attempts the first level without hints. Note their first 60 seconds and any repeated hesitations.
Reflection
After a session, ask: what single change will help a new player succeed on their first attempt?
