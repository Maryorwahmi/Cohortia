Executing Commands Once with the At Utility

Scheduling Future Tasks for Specific Times

Purpose of at

Schedules commands or scripts to run only once at a specified time in the future.

Simple Scheduling

Specify the time, type commands, then press Ctrl+D to save.

Flexible Time Formats

Supports various formats like now + 10 minutes, 10:30 AM tomorrow, or 14:00 2024-12-25.

Key Takeaway

The at command is ideal for one-off tasks, offering a straightforward way to schedule commands for a single future execution.

Terminal Demo: Scheduling with 'at'
$ at now + 5 minutes
at> echo "Meeting reminder!" > /dev/pts/0
at> Ctrl+D
job 1 at 2024-01-15 10:35

$ at 14:00 2024-12-25
at> /home/user/holiday_script.sh
at> Ctrl+D
job 2 at 2024-12-25 14:00
Explanation

This demo shows how to use at to schedule a command for 5 minutes in the future and another for a specific date and time. After typing commands, Ctrl+D saves the job.
