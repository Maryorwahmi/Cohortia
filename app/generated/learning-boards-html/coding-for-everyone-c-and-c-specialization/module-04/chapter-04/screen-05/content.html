Module 5 › Chapter 4.4 › Member vs Non-member
Member overloads
Left operand is the instance; good for operations that conceptually belong to the type.
Signature: ReturnType ClassName::operatorX(Arg) const
Implicit this: easy access to private members and const correctness.
Limitation: conversions on the left operand are not considered.
Takeaway

Use member overloads when the operation is naturally a method on the type and left-side conversions are not needed.

Non-member overloads
Both operands are parameters; required when left operand is not your type or for symmetric behavior.
Signature: ReturnType operatorX(const T1& a, const T2& b)
Use friend to grant access to private members when needed.
Symmetry: implicit conversions apply to both operands equally.
When required
Stream operators (<< and >>) must be non-members because streams own the left side.
