Operator Overloading — Definition
Redefine operator behavior for user types while keeping language rules intact.
Key rule: cannot change operator precedence or arity.
Member vs non-member: choose based on left operand ownership and access.
Safety: prefer returning new objects by value for arithmetic operators.
Key Takeaway

Operators should read naturally and preserve expected behavior; design choices affect safety and clarity.

Analogy
Think of operators as buttons on a calculator: overloading teaches the buttons how to operate on your custom objects without changing what a button fundamentally means.
Concrete example
Adding two ComplexNumber objects should produce a new ComplexNumber whose real and imaginary parts are the sums of the operands' parts.
Common mistake
Changing the meaning of + to do subtraction or have side effects on operands confuses readers and breaks expectations.
