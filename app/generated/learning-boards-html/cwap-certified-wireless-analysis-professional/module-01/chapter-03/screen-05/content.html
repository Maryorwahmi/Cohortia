802.11n (Wi-Fi 4): Introducing MIMO and Channel Bonding

Transforming wireless performance with multiple antennas and wider channels

Multiple-Input, Multiple-Output (MIMO)

Uses multiple antennas at both transmitter and receiver to create multiple independent data streams (spatial streams), significantly boosting throughput and range.

Channel Bonding

Combines two adjacent 20 MHz channels into a single 40 MHz channel, effectively doubling the theoretical bandwidth and data rates.

  • •Operates in both 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz bands.
  • •Theoretical speeds up to 600 Mbps.
  • •Improved reliability and coverage due to spatial diversity.

Example: MIMO in Action

Imagine a highway. Without MIMO, only one car can pass at a time. With MIMO, multiple lanes open up, allowing several cars (data streams) to travel simultaneously, drastically increasing traffic flow (data throughput).

Common Misconception

MIMO doesn't just mean more antennas; it means using those antennas intelligently to create parallel data paths, not just for redundancy or better signal reception.

Key Takeaway

802.11n's introduction of MIMO and channel bonding was a pivotal moment, enabling significantly higher speeds and laying the foundation for future Wi-Fi advancements.
