Core Concept

Tracing the 802.11 Lineage

From early beginnings to the foundational standards

1997
802.11 (1-2 Mbps)
1999
802.11b (2.4 GHz, 11 Mbps)
1999
802.11a (5 GHz, 54 Mbps)
2003
802.11g (2.4 GHz, 54 Mbps)

Early Limitations

Initial standards offered limited speeds and capacity, primarily serving basic connectivity needs.

Frequency Bands

802.11b/g operated in 2.4 GHz, while 802.11a introduced the 5 GHz band, offering higher speeds but shorter range.

Analyst's Insight

Understanding this lineage is crucial for identifying the capabilities and limitations of older hardware in the field.

Key Takeaway

The evolution of 802.11 standards shows a clear progression towards higher speeds and better spectrum utilization, laying the groundwork for modern Wi-Fi.
