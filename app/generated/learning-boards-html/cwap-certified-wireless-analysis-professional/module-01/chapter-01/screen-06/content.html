Wave Characteristics

Understanding Wave Properties

How frequency, wavelength, and amplitude define wireless signals.

Frequency

Number of wave cycles per second, measured in Hertz (Hz). Higher frequency often means higher data capacity but shorter range.

Wavelength

Distance between two consecutive peaks or troughs of a wave. Inversely proportional to frequency.

Amplitude

The strength or intensity of the wave. Higher amplitude means a stronger signal, less prone to noise.

Interconnection: Frequency & Wavelength

As frequency increases, wavelength decreases, assuming a constant speed of light. This relationship dictates how signals interact with obstacles and travel distances.

Impact on Wireless

  • Lower Frequencies: Travel further, penetrate obstacles better.
  • Higher Frequencies: Carry more data, but more susceptible to attenuation and require line-of-sight.
  • Stronger Amplitude: Improves signal reliability and range.
Key Takeaway

Understanding frequency, wavelength, and amplitude is crucial for predicting signal behavior, optimizing network design, and troubleshooting performance issues.
