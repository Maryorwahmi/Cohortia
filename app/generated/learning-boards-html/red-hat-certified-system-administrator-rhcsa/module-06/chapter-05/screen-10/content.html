Module 6 › Chapter 6.5 › Kernel & Journal
What to collect
Collect a dmesg snapshot and the relevant journal entries to find hardware errors or driver failures.
Commands
sudo dmesg -T | tail -n 100
sudo journalctl -b -1 --since "2024-11-01 09:50:00" --until "2024-11-01 10:05:00"
sudo journalctl -u myapp.service --since "10 minutes ago"
  • Use dmesg for direct kernel output with human timestamps (-T).
  • Use journalctl for service logs, historical boots, and full timestamps.
  • Correlate timestamps from both sources to form an incident timeline.
Example output and interpretation
[Fri Nov 1 10:00:01] ata1.00: exception Emask 0x0 SAct 0x0
[Fri Nov 1 10:00:01] ata1.00: failed command: READ FPDMA QUEUED
[Fri Nov 1 10:00:02] EXT4-fs error (device sda1): ext4_internal_error:...
Interpretation: repeated ATA or EXT4 errors point to disk hardware or driver problems rather than an application bug.
Key Takeaway
Always save dmesg and the relevant journal window before performing disruptive actions.
