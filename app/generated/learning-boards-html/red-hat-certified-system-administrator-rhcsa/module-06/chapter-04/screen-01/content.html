Monitoring and Managing System Logs
Understand journald, rsyslog and logrotate so you can collect, query, forward, and retain logs safely.

Why this matters: well-managed logs speed troubleshooting and protect audit trails. You will practice safe steps for persistence, forwarding, and rotation.

In this lesson

  • Querying the binary journal with journalctl
  • Configuring rsyslog rules and safe restarts
  • Creating logrotate policies for retention and compression
Quick orientation
You will move from read-only queries to safe configuration changes, and finish with rotation rules that protect disk space.
System flow
journald collects structured records; rsyslog writes to files and forwards; logrotate trims and compresses old files.
journald
Binary journal, advanced filtering, needs /var/log/journal for persistence.
rsyslog
Traditional syslog daemon, writes text files, forwards to remote servers.
journalctl
Query and follow logs with time, unit, priority, and boot filters.
logrotate
Rotate, compress, and retain text logs to prevent full disks.
