Module 4 › Chapter 4.1 › Specific vs Broad
Why specific is better
  • Preserves visibility of unexpected bugs during development.
  • Allows precise user messages and targeted recovery.
  • Makes logs easier to parse and act on for maintainers.
Best practice: catch expected exceptions and log unexpected ones with full context; re-raise or fail loudly if appropriate.
Common mistakes
  1. Using except Exception: to hide errors without logging.
  2. Silently ignoring exceptions and continuing with invalid state.
  3. Not including contextual information in logs, making debugging slow.
Pro-tip: include variable values, filenames, and the operation name in logs to reduce time to diagnose issues.
