Compaction: merge, reclaim, and optimize

Definition and operational significance for cluster health and read performance.

What compaction does
  • Merges multiple SSTables into fewer files.
  • Removes tombstones and expired data during rewrite.
  • Reduces the number of files reads must scan, lowering read amplification.
  • Consumes I/O and CPU while running; monitor compactionstats for progress.
Common mistake
Forcing frequent full compactions without considering workload can spike I/O and harm availability.
Concrete example
After a large data deletion, tombstones remain. Running a planned manual compaction rewrites SSTables without those tombstones, reclaiming disk space and improving subsequent read latency. Use nodetool compactionstats to confirm completion.
Key Takeaway
Treat compaction as a balancing act: schedule heavy compaction during low traffic and choose a compaction strategy that matches your read/write profile.
