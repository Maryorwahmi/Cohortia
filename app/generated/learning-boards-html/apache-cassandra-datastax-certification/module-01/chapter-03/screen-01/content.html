Introduction to Apache Cassandra
Why Cassandra exists, its design choices, and when to use it.

Cassandra is an open-source, distributed wide-column NoSQL database created to handle massive data across commodity servers. Its peer-to-peer architecture eliminates a single point of failure, and its replication and partitioning enable linear scalability. In practical systems this translates to continuous writes, resilient reads, and the ability to add nodes with predictable performance.

Think of Cassandra as a distributed log of rows where you design tables to match queries, and choose consistency per operation. That combination of modeling and tunable consistency is what makes Cassandra a strong choice for telemetry, time-series, and large-scale user data where availability and write throughput are priorities.
