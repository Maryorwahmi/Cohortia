Module 6 › Chapter 6.4 › Layers
Definition

Performance is the product of OS, disk I/O, network, JVM, and Cassandra configuration. Each layer adds latency or capacity limits that change how your cluster behaves under load.

Disk I/O
Dominant during writes and compactions; SSDs generally perform better than HDDs.
JVM & GC
Heap sizing and collector choice affect pause times and throughput.
Network
Bandwidth and latency matter for replication and streaming.
Cassandra Config
Compaction, memtables, and data model determine on-disk layout and read amplification.
Example Scenario
After a write spike, many small SSTables appear and read latency climbs. The likely cause is compaction and I/O contention. Fixes include reviewing compaction strategy or providing more IOPS, not immediately rewriting queries.
Key Takeaway

Always check I/O and SSTable counts before changing data model or queries; many latency issues originate below the query layer.
