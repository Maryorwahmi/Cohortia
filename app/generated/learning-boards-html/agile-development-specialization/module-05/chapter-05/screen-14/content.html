Hands‑on Activity
Design a Retrospective Action Plan
Work through the provided challenges using Start/Stop/Continue and craft one measurable action per challenge.
Challenge 1: Stakeholder interruptions
Insight (Stop): Direct ad‑hoc interruptions to developers reduce focus. Action: Route stakeholder updates through the Product Owner. Owner: Product Owner. Measure: ad‑hoc interruptions reduced to zero.
Challenge 2: Stories not Done due to late testing
Insight (Start): Testing deferred until the end of sprint. Action: Require test cases in definition of ready; developers write acceptance tests as they code. Owner: Development team. Measure: blocked stories at sprint end drops to zero.
Challenge 3: Lack of product vision clarity
Insight (Continue/Start): Sprint goals exist but broader vision is unclear. Action: Product Owner to dedicate 15 minutes every other sprint planning to restate vision. Owner: Product Owner. Measure: team reports improved clarity at next retrospective.
Implementation checklist
  • Add each action item to the sprint backlog as tasks.
  • Assign clear owners and set measurable targets.
  • Review progress during daily stand-ups and at sprint review.
  • Start the next retrospective by inspecting whether the experiments were executed and what effect they had.
Key Takeaway

Converting retrospective items into backlog tasks with owners and measures is how you close the loop and make continuous improvement real.
