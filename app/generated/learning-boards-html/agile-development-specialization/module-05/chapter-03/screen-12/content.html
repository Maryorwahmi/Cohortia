Turning points into plans
Use velocity as an empirical guide and maintain conservative buffers.

• Compute a rolling average of completed story points across several sprints to reduce variability.

• Select backlog items whose total points fit a conservative velocity for sprint planning and leave capacity for interruptions.

• Re-estimate stories when requirements change and record spikes as follow-up actions to reduce uncertainty.

Key Takeaway

Use velocity-derived forecasts with caution: they guide decisions but do not replace ongoing refinement and transparency about risk.

Example practice
If your 3-sprint rolling average is 20 points, pick 16–18 points for the next sprint to allow for slack and unexpected work.
Misconception
Don’t expect estimates to be static. Revisit them as the team gains knowledge and as acceptance criteria evolve.
Try this
Calculate your team’s 3-sprint rolling velocity and plan the next sprint at 80–90% of that average to maintain a realistic buffer.
