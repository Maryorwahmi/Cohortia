What is relative sizing?
An approach that compares stories to each other instead of estimating fixed hours.
  • • Relative sizing focuses on the relationship between items: smaller, equal, or larger.
  • • Teams pick a baseline story to anchor the scale, typically a trivial item labeled 1 point.
  • • This method acknowledges uncertainty and avoids a false sense of exactness that hours imply.
Analogy
Think of sizing as comparing apples: you say this apple is twice as big as that one rather than measuring grams precisely.
Key Takeaway

Relative comparison reduces false precision and creates shared reference points for consistent team estimates.

Baseline Example
Baseline: "Add a new field to a form without validation" = 1 point.
Story A
Login with email and password
Relative size: larger than baseline; includes security and session management.
Story B
Export users to CSV
Relative size: larger than baseline; consider data volume and performance.
Common Misconception
Teams sometimes convert points to hours. That undermines the purpose: points are team-specific markers of relative effort, not a universal time unit.
Instead, use completed story points over several sprints to build a velocity-based forecast. The conversion emerges empirically from your team’s delivery history, not by decree.
Try this
Pick a 1-point baseline before your next planning session and size three backlog items relative to it to test consistency.
