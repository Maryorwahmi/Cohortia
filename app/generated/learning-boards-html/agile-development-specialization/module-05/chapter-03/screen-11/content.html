Module 5 › Chapter 5.3 › Common Estimation Mistakes
Frequent anti-patterns
A compact checklist of mistakes and fixes.
  • Points as performance: discourage using story points to measure individuals; use throughput and quality metrics instead.
  • Points → hours: avoid imposing direct conversions; velocity should be learned from history.
  • Stale estimates: re-estimate items after significant changes or before committing to a sprint.
  • Anchoring bias: keep estimates private until the reveal to encourage independent thinking.
Correction
Record assumptions, run short spikes for risky stories, and derive velocity from delivered work rather than declared hours.
Example misuse
If management compares teams by points delivered, teams with different baselines will be unfairly ranked. Points are team-relative.
Pro tip
Document the baseline story and major assumptions after each poker session so future reviewers understand the original context of the estimate.
Key Takeaway
Keep estimates as team tools for planning, not as measures of individual speed; re-evaluate as the context evolves.
