Module 5 › Chapter 5.3 › Story Points: What they measure
What a Story Point represents
An aggregate of effort, complexity and uncertainty, relative to the team baseline.

• Effort: how much work needs to be done, including coding, testing, and review.

• Complexity: architectural or domain difficulty, number of moving parts, and integration needs.

• Uncertainty: unknowns that could reveal new work during implementation.

Example
Login feature = 3 points, Payment integration = 13 points. The larger number signals more unknowns and likely need for spikes or architecture discussion.
Key Takeaway

Use story points to surface risk and complexity, then refine with conversation and historical velocity.

Why Fibonacci?
Larger items carry more uncertainty, so gaps increase. The Fibonacci-like scale reflects that non-linearity and encourages splitting big items.
Pitfall to avoid
Do not use story points for individual performance measurement. Points are team-specific and intended for team forecasting, not ranking or pay decisions.
Pro tip
If a story seems too large, split it. Large point values usually hide multiple concerns and increase uncertainty.
