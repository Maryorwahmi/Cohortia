Core Concept
Why metrics matter
Metrics give objective signals that support inspection and adaptation. They should trigger team conversations and experiments rather than be used for blame.
Concrete example
A burn-down that stays above ideal warns the team they may be blocked or overcommitted, prompting earlier problem-solving.
Common misconception
Using velocity to evaluate individuals. Velocity measures team throughput, not personal performance.
Why it matters
Objective data replaces guesswork and creates a shared understanding of progress and risk.
Apply this idea
Keep a short list of questions metrics should answer each sprint: are we on track, what slowed us, and what experiment will reduce that delay?
Analogy
Like a pilot's instruments, metrics are useful when interpreted by a team that understands context and makes small corrective inputs.
Key Takeaway
Key Takeaway

Treat metrics as neutral signals that guide team conversations and experiments toward improvement.
