Comparison
Burn-up vs Burn-down
Burn-up: focus on completed work and scope. Burn-down: focus on remaining work and sprint pacing. Both answer related but distinct questions.
When to use burn-up
Use burn-up for release tracking and conversations about scope changes and forecasts.
Misinterpretation to avoid
Assuming growth in completed work reduces time to finish when scope is also growing.
Concrete scenario
Your release shows completed work rising 10 points per sprint, but stakeholders add 15 points of scope mid-release. The burn-up makes it clear that unless scope is adjusted, the release will not complete sooner despite steady velocity.
Quick guidance
  • Plot total scope as a separate line.
  • Discuss scope changes explicitly in planning and stakeholder meetings.
  • Use burn-up to support scope negotiation and realistic forecasts.
Key Takeaway

Burn-up charts reveal scope changes and completed work so you can manage release expectations explicitly.
