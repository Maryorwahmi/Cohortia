Definition
Sprint & release burn-downs
A burn-down displays remaining work across time. Use it to verify if the team is pacing to complete the committed work or the release scope.
Process steps
  1. Record total remaining work at sprint start.
  2. Update remaining work daily as stories move to done.
  3. Compare actual line to the ideal trend and investigate deviations.
Common mistake
Interpreting a single sprint spike as systemic rather than an opportunity to inspect for a blocker or scope change.
Concrete example
Imagine a sprint that commits 30 story points and shows 10 points remaining halfway through. If the actual line sits above ideal, you might have unexpected technical work or an underestimated story.
Check your understanding
If a sprint burn-down is flat for most of the sprint and drops sharply at the end, what anti-pattern does that suggest? It often points to delayed integration or testing and a mini-waterfall inside the sprint.
Key Takeaway

Burn-downs surface pacing problems quickly; treat deviations as prompts for collaborative inquiry, not punishment.
