Product Backlog: DEEP qualities

DEEP ensures backlog items are ready when needed and that the backlog remains a useful planning tool rather than a backlog of confusion.

Detailed appropriately
Top items have clear acceptance criteria and stakeholder intent; low-priority items remain high-level until they near delivery.
Estimated
Relative estimates let the Product Owner and team forecast capacity and prioritize by value per cost.
Emergent
The backlog evolves with new information, feedback, and changing context.
Prioritized
Ordering by value, risk, and necessity ensures the most important work is tackled first.
Concrete example
For a food-ordering app a top Product Backlog item such as "view menus" should include what screens are needed, performance expectations, and basic acceptance criteria. A distant idea like "loyalty program" can remain a headline with no immediate estimate.
Common Mistake
Skipping regular refinement leads to unclear stories and stalls during Sprint Planning.
Key Takeaway

Keep the backlog DEEP so high-priority work is ready, reducing planning friction and improving delivery predictability.
