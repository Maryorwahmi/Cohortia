Module 2 › Chapter 2.2 › The Sprint
Definition & Properties
A Sprint is a time-box of one month or less during which a Done, usable, and potentially releasable Increment is created.
Fixed duration: once started, the Sprint end date is set.
Consistent length: keeps a predictable rhythm and meaningful velocity.
Contains all events: Planning, Daily, Review, Retrospective happen inside it.
Concrete example
Two-week Sprint: Sprint Planning (max 4 hours), Daily Scrums each day (15 min), Sprint Review (max 2 hours), Sprint Retrospective (max 1.5 hours). At the end, unfinished Product Backlog items return to the Product Backlog.
Why it matters and common mistakes
  • Protects focus and creates a measurable delivery rhythm.
  • Enables predictable inspection points for product and process.
  • Anti-pattern: extending Sprints undermines predictability and learning.
Key Takeaway

A Sprint’s fixed time-box creates a reliable cadence. Keep lengths consistent and avoid extensions to protect predictability.
