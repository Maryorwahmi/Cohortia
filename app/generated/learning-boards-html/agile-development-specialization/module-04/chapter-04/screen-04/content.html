Module 5 › Chapter 4.4 › Estimate vs Commitment
What estimation does
  • Creates shared understanding of complexity and risk.
  • Guides prioritization and sprint planning decisions.
  • Helps uncover hidden assumptions during discussion.
Common misconceptions
Converting story points directly to hours or using estimates to punish teams reduces their value. Estimates are inputs for planning, not performance targets.
Key Takeaway

Keep estimates as relative guidance and use commitments only for sprint goals to preserve flexibility.

Analogy — Map vs Destination
Think of an estimate as a map: it helps you choose a route but the journey may change. A commitment is the destination the team agrees to reach this sprint. Use the map to navigate, not to bind the route.
Estimate
Relative guess of size and risk.
Example: 3-point story for login.
Commitment
Promise to achieve the sprint goal.
Example: Deliver checkout flow by sprint end.
Practical prompt
Before estimating, ask: what unknowns could move this story several point sizes? Naming uncertainties improves the estimate.
