Module 5 › Chapter 4.4 › Velocity in Planning
Compute average velocity
Sum completed points across recent sprints and divide by number of sprints. Use a window that reflects current team circumstances.
Plan sprints
Pull roughly the average velocity worth of points into the next sprint. Factor in known interruptions.
Key Takeaway

Velocity is a team planning signal; present forecasts as estimates, not guarantees, and revisit them often.

Concrete forecasting example
If average velocity is 25 points and backlog is 100 points, expect about four sprints, acknowledging variation and potential scope changes.
Communication tip: share the calculation and explain that variance, team changes, or unexpected work can alter the forecast.
