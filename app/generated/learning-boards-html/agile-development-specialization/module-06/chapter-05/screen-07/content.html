Process

Run a focused retrospective

A 60-minute retrospective can gather data, generate insights, decide actions, and plan follow-up when well facilitated.

Start, Stop, Continue
Gather ideas on what to start, stop, and continue. Good for action-oriented outcomes.
Mad, Sad, Glad
Surface emotional signals that point to systemic problems or wins.
Four L's
Capture Liked, Learned, Lacked, Longed for to broaden insight types.
Key Takeaway

Always close a retro with 2–3 SMART actions, owners, and a plan to review those actions in the next retro.

Facilitation checklist
  • Set the stage with a check-in and safe-space reminder.
  • Gather data using a chosen format and ensure everyone contributes.
  • Generate insights with grouping and simple root-cause work.
  • Decide actions and assign owners with clear timelines.
  • Close with appreciations and next steps for ART-level sharing if needed.
Kaizen example
After a retro, Team Nova commits to a two-week experiment: limit PR reviews to a WIP of 3, pair on review overflow, and measure cycle time. They will report results at the next retro and decide to keep or adjust the change.
Pitfall

Avoid using metrics as punishment. That destroys psychological safety and causes teams to hide issues instead of improving them.
