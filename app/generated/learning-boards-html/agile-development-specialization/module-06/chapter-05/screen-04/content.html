Core Concept

Flow metrics explained

These metrics measure time and rate across your value stream so you can find waiting, not just output.

Lead Time
Total time from request to delivery; measures end-to-end customer experience.
Cycle Time
Time from when active work begins until completion; measures execution speed.
Throughput
Number of items completed per unit time; measures output rate.
Work In Progress (WIP)
Count of items currently being worked on; limiting WIP improves focus and flow.
Key Takeaway
Pick one flow metric to monitor, measure before you change process, then re-measure after small experiments.
Common Mistake
Using velocity as the only signal leads teams to focus on throughput without fixing delays and handoffs.
Concrete example
Team Alpha reviews five finished stories. Average cycle time is 6 days, average lead time is 14 days, and throughput is three stories per week. Analysis shows most waiting occurs before work begins due to long backlog gating and unclear acceptance. The team decides to set WIP limits and run a 2-week experiment to reduce cycle time by swarming on stories.
How to measure quickly
  1. Choose a sample of completed items (e.g., last 10).
  2. Record dates: request, start, done.
  3. Compute lead and cycle times, then calculate average throughput per week.
Reflection

Which delay looks largest for your team: waiting to start, or time spent in active work? A quick sample will reveal the strongest signal.
