Definition

Kaizen — continuous, small improvements

Kaizen emphasizes frequent, incremental changes driven by the people doing the work. Each change should be small, measurable, and reversible if it doesn't help.

Start small
Pick a tiny experiment that addresses a measured problem.
Measure impact
Collect the same metric you used to identify the problem, then compare.
Share what you learn
Publish results in a knowledge base or community of practice so other teams can reuse it.
Key Takeaway

Kaizen scales when experiments are small, measured, and shared. It turns local improvements into organizational learning.

Practical Kaizen example
A team noticed long code-review queues. Retro action: introduce a 1-hour paired review slot each afternoon for two weeks and limit in-progress PRs to three. Measure cycle time for PRs before and after. Result: average PR cycle time dropped by 30%, and the team documented the pattern so other teams could try it.
Sharing patterns
  • Post short experiment reports in a shared knowledge base.
  • Present notable wins at Inspect & Adapt or community demos.
  • Encourage cross-team pairing sessions to spread techniques.
Try This

Identify one small testable change this week, assign an owner, measure the outcome, and publish the result in your team wiki.
