Module 6 › Chapter 6.2 › Lean-Agile Principles
Core Principles
  • Take an economic view: prioritize decisions that improve end-to-end economic outcomes.
  • Apply systems thinking: optimize the whole value stream, not isolated components.
  • Assume variability: preserve options and learn fast through small increments.
  • Build incrementally: short, integrated learning cycles reduce risk and surface integration issues early.
  • Decentralize decision-making: empower teams to speed delivery and reduce handoffs.
Concrete example
Instead of funding a large rebuild, a team delivers a minimal experiment in one PI to validate an onboarding change and measure whether it reduces manual work by the target percent. This preserves options and reduces potential wasted investment.
Misconception
Treating the principles as a checklist risks hollow compliance. They must inform trade-offs, backlog prioritization, and technical choices continuously.
