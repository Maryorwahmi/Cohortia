Module 6 › Chapter 6.4 › What is DevOps?
Definition
DevOps is practices, culture, and tools integrating development and operations to shorten lifecycle and enable continuous delivery with quality.
Concrete example
A commit triggers an automated build, tests run, and a deployable artifact is produced and handed to operations for deployment and monitoring.
Common misconception
DevOps is not only tools. Without culture and shared responsibility, automation can accelerate failures rather than prevent them.
Why it matters
It turns working code into delivered value more predictably, reducing risk and enabling faster user feedback.
Analogy
Imagine development and operations as runners in a relay. DevOps replaces separate runners with a coordinated team practicing handoffs together so the baton crosses the finish line more reliably.
Check your understanding
Name one manual handoff your team does today that could be automated and paired with a monitoring signal. That change is a practical DevOps step.
Key Takeaway

DevOps is people plus automation: shared responsibility and repeatable pipelines turn Agile iterations into reliable releases.
