Why the Agile Manifesto was written
A short timeline of the problem, meeting, and outcome.
Era of heavyweight processes
  • Large upfront requirements and design documentation.
  • Long delivery cycles that delayed feedback.
  • Frequent mismatches with changing customer needs.
The Snowbird gathering
Seventeen practitioners codified a concise set of priorities to guide better delivery and collaboration.
Concrete example: a team buried in 100-page specs misses a market shift; shorter cycles would have revealed the shift earlier.
Result: a compact manifesto
Four values and twelve principles that prioritize people, working software, collaboration, and responding to change.
Misconception
Agile means no planning — actually it favors adaptive planning with shorter horizons and continuous feedback.
Practice example
Replace a 6-month release with 2-week increments and user demos to validate direction earlier.
Key takeaway
Agile is a response to predictable failures of rigid processes; it prioritizes learning and value delivery.
