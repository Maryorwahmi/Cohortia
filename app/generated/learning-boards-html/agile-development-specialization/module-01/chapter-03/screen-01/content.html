Feedback and Continuous Improvement
How early feedback and retrospectives keep teams aligned with customer needs.
In this lesson

Learn sources of feedback, the learning loop, and how to run effective, blame-free retrospectives that create measurable action items.

Why this matters
Early feedback finds wrong assumptions sooner so the team can adapt while change is cheap.
Explore Key Topics
Early Feedback
Gather fast input from users and stakeholders each iteration to validate assumptions.
Retrospectives
Inspect the team’s process at regular intervals and commit to improvements.
Learning Loop
Plan, build, measure, learn, adapt — repeat to make progress predictable and measurable.
Feedback Flow
Example: coffee shop app sprint review → product backlog update

Sprint Review
Stakeholders test working software and give feedback.
Product Owner
Synthesizes feedback and reprioritizes the backlog.
Next Sprint
Actionable items are planned and implemented early.
Concrete example: After a demo, the coffee shop owner asks for a ‘reorder previous’ button. The product owner adds this to the backlog and the team may include it in the next sprint, reducing wasted work and aligning the product with user needs.
