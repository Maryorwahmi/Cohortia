Module 2 › Chapter 1.3 › Common Mistakes
Pitfalls that block improvement
Simple errors can make feedback ineffective.
  • Treating feedback as a checkbox without acting on it
  • Retrospective lists with no owners or measures
  • Blame-focused meetings that silence team members
  • Relying on a single feedback source and missing signals
Corrective practices
Require owners and measures
Every action must have an owner and a simple success metric.
Keep it small
Limit to 1–3 improvements so the team can implement them.
Foster safety
Reframe problems as process issues; the goal is learning, not blame.
Key Takeaway

Correct easy meeting habits to make feedback lead to measurable improvement.
