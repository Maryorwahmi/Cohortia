Module 3 › Chapter 3.1 › Four Principles
Principle 1
Start with what you do now
Observe and visualize the current flow before proposing change. This reduces friction.
Principle 2
Agree to pursue incremental change
Favor small experiments over big rewrites so you can measure impact and learn quickly.
Principle 3
Respect current roles
Use existing knowledge and authority; ask people to participate rather than replace them.
Principle 4
Encourage leadership at all levels
Frontline team members often spot the best improvements; empower them to act and experiment.
Scenario
A team maps its review steps and lets reviewers propose a WIP limit as a small experiment instead of changing roles or tools.
Key Takeaway
Principles set the tone: they favor safe, evidence-based, and inclusive improvement over top-down mandates.
