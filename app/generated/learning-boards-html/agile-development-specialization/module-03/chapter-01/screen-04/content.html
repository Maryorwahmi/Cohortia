Definition

Toyota, JIT, and the lean root of Kanban

Kanban began as a signal-based pull system that prevented overproduction and reduced waste. The same pull idea helps knowledge teams avoid starting more work than they can finish.

Concrete example
A restaurant kitchen switches from starting many plates to finishing a small number before taking new orders, reducing wait time—equivalent to limiting WIP.
Why it matters
Making inventory visible transforms hidden bottlenecks into actionable signals for improvement and predictable delivery.
Common misconception
Kanban is not the absence of process. It reveals the process so you can improve it deliberately.
Analogy
Imagine a conveyor of tasks visible at all times. Each task is a card. Too many cards create queues and wait time. Kanban introduces signals and limits so the conveyor flows smoothly.
Pull signal
Work is pulled only when capacity exists downstream.
Visible inventory
Cards show active items and where they are stuck.
Key Takeaway

Kanban transfers just-in-time thinking to knowledge work: make work visible, limit it, and improve flow.
