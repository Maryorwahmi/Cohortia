Module 3 › Chapter 3.3 › Passive vs Active Scanning
Passive Scan (Listen)
Clients wait for Beacon frames. This conserves power and is predictable, but relies on AP timing and channel overlap.
  • Power efficient because client does not transmit.
  • Depends on AP beacon interval and client channel dwell time.
  • May miss APs if the client doesn't dwell on the right channel.
Active Scan (Probe)
Client sends Probe Request frames and waits for Probe Responses. Faster discovery, useful for hidden SSIDs or quick UX, but uses more power.
  • Directed probes include an SSID. Broadcast probes use a null SSID.
  • APs that match will reply with Probe Response frames similar to Beacons.
  • Active scanning increases transmit activity and may expose the client.
Example capture
You might see a client send a probe request for "MyCorp" and multiple APs reply with probe responses. If no probe responses appear, the client may be on a different channel or the AP may be down.
Common mistake
Disabling SSID broadcast does not prevent active discovery. Devices probing for that SSID will still reveal it to active scanners.
Key Takeaway

Use passive scanning for low-power scenarios and active scanning for speed or to detect hidden SSIDs; examine probe exchanges to troubleshoot discovery failures.
