Process Overview
SIFS
Shortest IFS used for ACK/CTS. Ensures quick responses.
PIFS
Used by PCF, rarely used in practice.
DIFS
Wait for new transmissions under DCF before starting backoff.
EIFS
Longest IFS used after corrupted frames to avoid further collisions.
Backoff and DCF Steps
  1. Sense medium. If idle for required IFS, enter backoff.
  2. Select random backoff in [0, CW]. Decrement only while medium idle.
  3. When backoff hits zero, transmit. Wait for SIFS then ACK.
  4. If no ACK, assume failure, double CW, pick new backoff, and retry.
Concrete Example
Imagine three stations finish DIFS at the same time. Each chooses a random backoff; station with smallest value transmits first. If two choose the same value and collide, their next CWs double, reducing collision probability on subsequent attempts. This stochastic approach keeps the channel usable in dense environments.
Key Takeaway

IFS ordering and randomized backoff are the mechanisms that let many half-duplex stations share a radio channel with reasonable fairness and efficiency.
