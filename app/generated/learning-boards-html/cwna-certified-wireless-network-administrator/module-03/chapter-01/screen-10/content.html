What QoS Control Does
Traffic class, TID, and priority hints
The QoS Control field holds the Traffic Identifier (TID) and other flags that map the MSDU to an access category. Access categories use different AIFS and contention window values so that higher-priority categories like voice get earlier access. This mechanism is the cornerstone for supporting latency-sensitive traffic over a contention-based medium.
Voice / AC_VO
Shortest AIFS, smallest contention window → lower delay.
Best Effort / AC_BE
Standard AIFS and contention window.
Example: VoIP Packet
How a voice packet gets prioritized
A VoIP MSDU marked with a high-priority TID will use a shorter AIFS and smaller contention window. It will therefore begin decrementing its backoff sooner and has a higher chance to transmit before best-effort traffic. Inspecting a capture, QoS frames show a QoS Control field after Sequence Control that indicates the TID and user priority.
Pro Tip
Prioritization improves latency but not guaranteed throughput. Verify AP WMM settings and ensure devices honor QoS tags.
Key Takeaway

QoS Control provides traffic class metadata that changes AIFS and contention behavior to favor latency-sensitive traffic under contention.

Common Mistake

Assuming QoS tags alone guarantee performance. They increase scheduling priority during contention but rely on AP policy and overall network load.
