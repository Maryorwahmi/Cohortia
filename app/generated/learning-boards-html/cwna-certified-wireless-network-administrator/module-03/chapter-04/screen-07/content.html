Module 3 › Chapter 3.4 › Acknowledgment (ACK) Frames
Why ACKs matter
ACKs give immediate feedback and drive retransmission decisions at the MAC layer.
  • • Sent after SIFS so they have priority and return quickly.
  • • Very short: minimal fields to reduce airtime.
  • • If missing, the sender retries based on MAC retry policy.
Key Takeaway
Inspect ACK patterns first when you see retransmits — lost ACKs often point to interference or weak signal at the receiver.
Concrete example
A client sends a unicast data frame. If the AP returns an ACK after SIFS, the client marks the frame delivered. If the ACK is not seen, the client increases a retry counter and retransmits; repeated failures suggest signal or collision issues.
Misconception
Believing ACKs are optional for reliability. For unicast frames, ACKs are the MAC's required confirmation method; higher layers rely on the MAC's retransmit behavior for efficiency.
