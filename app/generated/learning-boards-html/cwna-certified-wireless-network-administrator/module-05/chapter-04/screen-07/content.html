Definition
RSSI: received signal strength

RSSI reports the power of a received Wi‑Fi signal in dBm. It produces heatmaps useful for spotting coverage gaps and validating that target levels for voice or data are met.

Use: detect coverage holes and verify target RSSI thresholds.
Limit: does not include background noise or channel activity.
Cross‑check: always pair with SNR and utilization data.
Quick calculation & example
# Example: interpreting RSSI
# Target for voice: -67 dBm
Measured RSSI at desk: -68 dBm
Action: consider moving AP or increasing power slightly
Key Takeaway

RSSI locates signal strength but not quality. Use it to find gaps, then analyze SNR and utilization.
