Core Concept
What passive surveying is

A passive survey records existing Wi‑Fi signals by listening. It produces heatmaps and channel charts that represent real, on‑the‑air activity to validate predictions and find problems predictions miss.

Key trait: No association — scanning only
Common output: RSSI, SNR, noise floor, channel utilization maps
Misconception: Strong RSSI alone equals good performance
Analogy & example
Think of a passive survey as taking a photograph of the air. The image shows who else is in the room and how noisy it is. For instance, a predictive model might assume no neighbor networks, but a passive snapshot can show nearby APs and high utilization you didn’t expect.
Concrete example
Validated predictive design
After running a passive survey you compare heatmaps to the predictive plan and mark areas where RSSI or SNR fall short, then adjust AP locations or power.
Key Takeaway

Passive data is ground truth for coverage, interference, and rogue device detection.
