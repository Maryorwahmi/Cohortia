Core Metric
SNR: signal relative to noise

SNR shows how much stronger the desired signal is than the background noise. Calculate it by subtracting the noise floor from RSSI and use SNR thresholds to decide if an area is suitable for voice or high‑bandwidth applications.

Rule of thumb: 20–25 dB for general data; 25–30 dB for voice.
Misconception: strong RSSI can still yield poor SNR if noise is high.
Calculation example
# SNR calculation
RSSI = -60 dBm
Noise floor = -90 dBm
SNR = RSSI - Noise floor = -60 - (-90) = 30 dB
Interpretation: excellent for voice and high throughput
Key Takeaway

Always include SNR in your findings. It explains whether RSSI will translate into usable throughput.
