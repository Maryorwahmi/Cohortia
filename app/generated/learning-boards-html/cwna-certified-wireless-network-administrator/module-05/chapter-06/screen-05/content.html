Heatmap types and interpretations

Coverage (RSSI)
Shows where minimum signal thresholds are met. Look for red zones under target and areas of excessive overlap.
SNR
Shows signal quality. Low SNR with good RSSI indicates noise or interference reducing usable throughput.
Capacity
Maps throughput and client density to reveal bottlenecks in high‑demand areas.
Interference
Highlights CCI and ACI so you can refine channel plans or mitigate non‑Wi‑Fi sources.
Practical tip
Always include at least two heatmaps when recommending a change: one showing the symptom and one showing the likely root cause. That makes your recommendation defensible to non‑technical stakeholders.
Example: mixed symptom
If coverage map shows good RSSI but SNR heatmap shows low values in the same area, the problem is noise. Your next step is a spectrum or interference analysis and a recommendation that addresses the interference source rather than adding APs.
Key Takeaway
Match the heatmap to the question you need to answer and present paired visuals to justify each recommendation.
