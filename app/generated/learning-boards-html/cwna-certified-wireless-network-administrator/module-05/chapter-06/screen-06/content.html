Module 5 › Chapter 5.6 › Coverage vs SNR
Coverage (RSSI)
Indicates where signal level meets a threshold. Useful to spot dead zones but not alone sufficient to guarantee performance.
Example: Office area shows -60 dBm across desks — good coverage baseline.
Common mistake
Adding APs when RSSI looks fine can increase co‑channel interference if SNR and channel plan are not considered.
SNR (Signal-to-Noise Ratio)
Measures signal quality. Low SNR reduces throughput regardless of RSSI. Investigate noise sources before adding APs.
Example: Conference room shows -55 dBm RSSI but SNR of 12 dB and throughput collapse under load — likely non‑Wi‑Fi noise or heavy CCI.
Key Takeaway
Always present both coverage and SNR heatmaps and explain the root cause before recommending AP additions.
