Radio Waves — key terms
Frequency, wavelength, amplitude and why each matters for coverage and interference.
Frequency
Cycles per second. Higher frequency = shorter wavelength, less penetration.
Wavelength
Physical distance between peaks; determines interaction with obstacles.
Amplitude
Signal strength; affects effective range and SNR at the receiver.
Practical example
An access point operating at 5 GHz may deliver higher data rates because of wider available channels and less interference, but interior coverage will often be smaller than a 2.4 GHz AP placed in the same location. This often leads to more APs for 5 GHz deployments.
Common misconception
Assuming higher frequency always improves overall performance. It improves peak throughput potential, but not coverage or penetration.
Key Takeaway
Match band choice to goals: choose lower frequency for coverage, higher frequency for capacity when you can add more access points.
Why it matters
  • Predict where interference and weak signal areas will appear.
  • Decide which band and antenna plans suit a space.
  • Understand tradeoffs between throughput and coverage for design and troubleshooting.
