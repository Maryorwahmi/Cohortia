Definition: ESS
Multiple APs (BSSs) connected to the same wired network and sharing an SSID to allow roaming.

Essentials: ESS requires a common SSID across APs and a wired distribution system to carry traffic between APs and the network.

Example: A campus Wi‑Fi network where a client stays on "CampusNet" as it walks between buildings served by different APs.

Misconception: Identical SSIDs do not by themselves ensure fast or seamless roaming — RF planning and controller features often matter.

Design note

Plan overlapping coverage, channel allocation, and backhaul capacity to support roaming and maintain performance.

Visual: Roaming scenario
Client on call
A mobile client hands off between AP1 and AP2 under the same SSID without reauthentication interruptions when design and controller support exist.
Check
If roaming is poor, inspect signal overlap, channel plan, and whether a controller manages handoffs or the clients perform roaming decisions.
Key Takeaway

An ESS enables roaming but requires planning and often centralized coordination for the best user experience.
