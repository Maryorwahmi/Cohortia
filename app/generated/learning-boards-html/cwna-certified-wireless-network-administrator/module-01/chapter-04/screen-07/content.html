Module 2 › Chapter 1.4 › Antenna Gain
Definition

Gain in dBi and dBd

Gain measures pattern focus relative to a reference: dBi to isotropic, dBd to a dipole. Higher numbers mean a more focused beam and more effective range along that beam.

Practical example
An omni antenna of 3 dBi gives modest horizontal focus; a Yagi of 15 dBi pushes energy far in a tight beam — choose based on required range and beamwidth.
Misconception
Gain is not amplification. It redistributes energy; regulatory limits still apply to effective radiated power in a direction.
Interpreting datasheets
  • Look for both dBi and beamwidth; high dBi often means narrow beamwidth.
  • Consider client spread: wide client distribution needs lower dBi or multiple sectors.
  • Remember regulatory power limits when combining radio power and antenna gain.
Key Takeaway

Read gain as a pattern attribute: higher gain narrows the beam and increases effective range in that beam, but does not add transmitter power.
