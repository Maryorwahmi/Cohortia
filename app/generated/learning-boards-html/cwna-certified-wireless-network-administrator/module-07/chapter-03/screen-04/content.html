Module 07 › Chapter 7.3 › Why wireless networks are regulated
Definition and Rationale

Radio transmissions can be intercepted. When those transmissions include personal identifiers, health data, or cardholder data, laws and standards require controls to protect that data.

Concrete Example
A café guest Wi‑Fi that records emails and session times creates personal data. Under GDPR, the operator must justify collection, secure the data, and inform users.
Common Mistake
Assuming open or passwordless networks are safe for non-sensitive traffic. Without controls, even seemingly innocuous data can create obligations.
Key Takeaway
Design the WLAN assuming that any collected identifier may be regulated; apply strong controls by default.
How to think about obligations
  • Identify which data classes your WLAN touches: personal data, PHI, or cardholder data.
  • Map each class to the applicable regulation: GDPR, HIPAA, or PCI DSS.
  • Choose controls (encryption, auth, segmentation, logging) that satisfy the regulation's intent.
Key Takeaway

Regulations convert into measurable network controls you can test and document for audits or incident response.
