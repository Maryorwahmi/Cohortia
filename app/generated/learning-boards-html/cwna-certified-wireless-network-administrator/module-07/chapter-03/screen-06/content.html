Module 07 › Chapter 7.3 › HIPAA and wireless access to PHI
What HIPAA requires in practice

Protect PHI with layered controls: encrypt, authenticate, segment, log, and keep hardware secure.

Strong Encryption
Use WPA3 Enterprise or equivalent enterprise-grade encryption for links carrying PHI.
Robust Authentication
802.1X with RADIUS, and MFA for administrative access to controllers and records.
Auditing
Keep detailed logs of access to PHI and review them regularly for anomalies.
Scenario: Clinic staff tablets
Place staff tablets on an isolated VLAN with strict ACLs to the EHR servers. Require 802.1X authentication, store logs centrally, and limit admin access by role.
Misconception

Thinking guest isolation is sufficient. Guest isolation must be enforced at network and firewall levels; physical and log controls are also required.

Key Takeaway

Treat any WLAN used to access PHI as high-risk and apply multiple layers of defense and evidence collection.
