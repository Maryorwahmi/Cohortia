What is RF exposure?
Radio frequency exposure measures the electromagnetic energy people receive from transmitters. Two key metrics are power density and SAR.
  • Power density: energy per area, relevant for far-field exposure.
  • SAR: absorption rate measured in watts per kilogram, used in guidelines.
Example scenario
A rooftop directional antenna can focus RF energy into a narrow beam. If the beam crosses a courtyard at head height, power density there may exceed safe limits.
Misconception: "All Wi‑Fi is harmless everywhere."
Reality: Device power and antenna gain create varying exposure profiles.
How to evaluate
  1. Check manufacturer exclusion distances and published EIRP.
  2. Estimate whether beams intersect public areas or worker-access zones.
  3. If uncertain, restrict access or lower power and re-evaluate.
Key Takeaway

RF exposure is a measurable safety issue. Use published MPE guidance and exclusion zones to avoid concentrated beams at human height.
