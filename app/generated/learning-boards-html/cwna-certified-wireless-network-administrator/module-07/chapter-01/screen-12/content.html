Consequences of noncompliance
Legal, operational, and interference risks that affect administrators and organizations.
Legal exposure
Regulators may impose fines, require equipment removal, or pursue other enforcement when rules are broken.
Operational harm
Interference and degraded performance from incorrect power or channel choices can disrupt services and increase support costs.
Procurement risk
Non‑certified or misconfigured devices increase the chance of interoperability problems and longer deployment timelines.
Concrete example
An outdoor access point operated above a region's EIRP limit risks enforcement action and can cause harmful interference to other licensed services.
Mitigation checklist
  • Document regional EIRP and DFS requirements.
  • Use certified hardware and verify firmware behavior.
  • Run post‑deployment verification and monitoring for unexpected transmissions.
Key takeaway
Combine rules, standards, certification, and verification to reduce the chance of costly enforcement or service disruption.
