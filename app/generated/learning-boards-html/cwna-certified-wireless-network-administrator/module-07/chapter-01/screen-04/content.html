International Telecommunication Union
The UN specialized agency that coordinates global spectrum and technical recommendations.
Definition
Allocates radio spectrum internationally and issues recommendations that national regulators use as a basis for rules.
Why it matters
  • Shapes which frequency bands are available for unlicensed use.
  • Provides the international framework that national regulators reference.
  • Helps prevent cross‑border interference by coordinating allocations.
Concrete example
ITU recommendations on ISM bands influenced the global adoption of 2.4 gigahertz for Wi‑Fi, enabling global device production and broad interoperability.
Common misconception
Treating ITU recommendations as national law. They guide national regulators but do not directly enforce device rules inside each country.
Key takeaway

Use ITU outputs as the international starting point; always verify how each national regulator implemented those recommendations.

Try this
When planning for multiple countries, list the ITU band names and then collect national rules that reference those bands to compare permitted uses.
