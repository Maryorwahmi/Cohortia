Module 6 › Chapter 6.2 › RSSI
Definition
Received Signal Strength Indicator (RSSI)
A measure of the power level of the received Wi‑Fi signal at the receiver, reported in dBm. Less negative means stronger.
Excellent: -50 to -67 dBm
Weak: -70 dBm and below — expect lower rates
Worked example
Client RSSI: -60 dBm. If noise floor is -95 dBm, SNR = 35 dB — good. If noise floor is -65 dBm, SNR = 5 dB — poor.
Practical Check
Record client RSSI, then measure noise floor with a spectrum analyzer. Compute SNR to know whether the RSSI is meaningful.
Misconception
A single RSSI reading without noise context can mislead you into unnecessary configuration changes.
