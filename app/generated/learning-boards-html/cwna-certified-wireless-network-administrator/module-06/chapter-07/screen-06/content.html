Process

From graphs to frames: a diagnostic sequence

Follow a deliberate sequence to turn visual anomalies into root-cause actions.
  1. Open an I/O graph and scan for throughput or retransmission spikes.
  2. Note timestamps of anomalies and inspect conversation lists for top talkers at those times.
  3. Filter the capture to the anomaly interval and use expert info to identify specific issues.
  4. Correlate with RF measurements or a spot survey taken at the same time if available.
Example

An I/O graph shows periodic retransmission peaks at 10:05 and 10:40. Conversation view highlights a single client generating many small frames. Filter those times and inspect for retry flags, then run a spot survey at the client location to check SNR and interference.

Pro tip

Always compare graph results to baseline metrics to decide if a spike is abnormal or expected for that environment.

Supporting visual
Imagine an I/O graph with a recurring retransmission peak. That visual points to a time window for a focused analysis. Use that window to reduce your capture to a small, inspectable set of frames and identify whether the retransmissions are tied to one client, an AP, or an environmental event.
Key Takeaway

Stats and graphs accelerate where to look and when; they do not replace packet-level inspection and RF validation.
