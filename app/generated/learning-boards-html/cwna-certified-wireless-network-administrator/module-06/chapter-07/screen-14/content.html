Task 1: Wireshark Filter for Retransmissions

Write a display filter that shows retransmitted 802.11 data frames to or from the client MAC 00:11:22:33:44:55. Use the retry flag and the address filter to capture both directions.

Suggested filter
wlan.fc.retry == 1 && wlan.addr == 00:11:22:33:44:55

This filter returns frames marked as retries where the source or destination is the specified client. Use additional constraints like time or BSSID to further narrow results.

Task 2: Baseline Data Analysis

Given the AP data below, identify two significant deviations and explain their likely implications.

AP 24-hour snapshot:
  • Average Channel Utilization: 35% (Baseline: 20%)
  • Average Client RSSI: -60 dBm (Baseline: -62 dBm)
  • Average Client SNR: 28 dB (Baseline: 30 dB)
  • Average Retransmission Rate: 15% (Baseline: 3%)
  • Number of Associated Clients: 45 (Baseline: 20)
Deviation 1
Metric: Retransmission rate increased from 3% to 15%.
Potential Issue: A large increase in retransmissions indicates a serious RF problem or device-level failures causing repeated frame delivery attempts. This often maps to interference, poor SNR, or a hidden node condition that requires a targeted RF survey and packet-level inspection.
Deviation 2
Metric: Number of associated clients increased from 20 to 45 and channel utilization rose from 20% to 35%.
Potential Issue: The AP may be overloaded by client density or heavy client activity, consuming airtime. Alternatively, increased utilization could be caused by new sources of interference. Use a site survey to confirm client distribution and throughput tests to distinguish load from interference.
Key Takeaway

Use filters to find affected frames and baselines to prioritize investigation: high retransmits and sudden client density spikes demand immediate attention.
