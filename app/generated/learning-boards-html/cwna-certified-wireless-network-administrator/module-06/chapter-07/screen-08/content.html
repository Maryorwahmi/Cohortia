Module 7 › Chapter 6.7 › Passive vs Active Survey
Passive Survey

Listen for coverage and interference

Passive surveys capture beacons, signal levels, and noise without associating, revealing AP presence and interference signatures.

  • Identify coverage holes and rogue APs
  • Measure RSSI, noise floor, and channel occupancy
  • Integrate spectrum data to find non‑Wi‑Fi interferers
Active Survey

Associate and validate client experience

Active surveys involve a client that associates and runs throughput and roaming tests to measure real performance.

  • Validate actual data rates and roaming behavior
  • Measure end-to-end throughput and latency
  • Useful for reproducing user-reported slowdowns
Practical workflow
Start with a passive survey to map RF and identify suspect channels or coverage gaps. Where users report issues, run an active survey in the same spots to confirm actual throughput and roaming behavior. If interference is suspected but not visible in passive Wi‑Fi scans, add spectrum analysis to find non‑Wi‑Fi sources.
Key Takeaway

Use passive surveys for RF discovery and active surveys to validate client experience; both together provide a complete troubleshooting picture.
