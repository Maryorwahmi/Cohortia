Definition
TKIP and Michael (MIC)
TKIP features:
  • Per‑packet key mixing to create unique RC4 keys.
  • 48‑bit IV to reduce reuse frequency.
  • Sequence counter to prevent replay attacks.
MIC (Michael)
A stronger message integrity check to detect tampering and block simple injection attacks that were trivial under WEP.
Concrete example
After TKIP, the same plaintext encrypted at two different times produces different ciphertexts because the per‑packet key differs, preventing keystream reuse attacks that broke WEP.
Key Takeaway

TKIP and MIC prevented immediate WEP attacks by fixing IV reuse and integrity, but cryptographic strength depends on the underlying cipher as well.
