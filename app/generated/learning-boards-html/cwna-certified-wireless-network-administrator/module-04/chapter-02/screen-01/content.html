WEP and WPA/WPA2 Personal Security
From broken IVs to AES‑CCMP: evolution of Wi‑Fi personal protection

This lesson shows why WEP failed, how WPA’s TKIP and MIC provided an emergency fix, and how WPA2‑PSK with AES‑CCMP gives robust confidentiality and integrity for home networks — when combined with a strong passphrase and careful key management.

In this lesson
WEP failures, WPA fixes, WPA2 architecture, and PSK best practices
WEP
RC4 + small IV; vulnerable to IV reuse attacks.
WPA (TKIP)
Per‑packet keying and MIC to stop replay and tampering.
WPA2‑PSK
AES‑CCMP provides confidentiality and integrity when PSK is strong.
Explore Key Topics
Why WEP failed
Small 24‑bit IV and static key caused keystream reuse and rapid key recovery tools.
WPA’s bridge
TKIP and MIC added per‑packet keys and integrity while newer hardware adopted AES.
WPA2‑PSK practice
AES‑CCMP is strong, but PSK strength and rotation are operational necessities.
