Module 4 › Chapter 4.5 › What is a WIPS?
WIPS — live definition
A system that monitors RF, detects wireless threats, and can take countermeasures.
Analogy: a security guard for airspace who both watches and can intercede. Unlike an IDS that only reports, a WIPS can act to protect users and the network.
Example
A personal router connected to a switch advertises a corporate SSID; WIPS detects mismatched MACs and can contain clients until the device is removed.
Common misconception
WIPS is not a silver bullet; it reduces risk and provides data for response, but requires tuning and careful authorization for containment.
Key Takeaway

WIPS actively watches the air and can intervene, making it essential for threats that bypass upper-layer protections.

Components and data
Sensors, analyzer/controller, and a central server produce alerts, logs, and packet captures for forensics.
Sensor
Collects RF frames across channels and sends metadata or captures to the server.
Analyzer
Correlates wireless events with wired infrastructure and decides on containment actions.
Logs & captures
Stored evidence for later forensic review and incident response timelines.
Containment
Automated actions such as deauthentication frames or alerts to administrators.
