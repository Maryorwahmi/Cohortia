Common containment actions
Deauthentication, disassociation, and administrator alerts are the primary tools.
  • Deauthentication frames sent to clients attempting to use the rogue AP.
  • Disassociation frames targeting the rogue AP's BSSID to disrupt its operation.
  • Physical remediation: locate and unplug the rogue device or disable the wired port.
Safety & legal note
Containment may disrupt legitimate service and has potential legal implications; always follow authorization and escalation policies.
Operational checklist
A safe containment workflow minimizes collateral damage.
  1. Confirm the rogue AP using two or more independent signals.
  2. Capture evidence: logs, packet capture, and RF fingerprint.
  3. Notify stakeholders and obtain authorization if required.
  4. Execute narrowly targeted containment and monitor effects.
Key Takeaway

Containment is effective but disruptive; require high confidence and follow governance before performing active measures.
