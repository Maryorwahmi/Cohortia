Core Concept

Secure WLAN Design

Design choices that reduce signal bleed, apply logical segmentation, and enforce strong access controls.

Placement & Coverage
Overlap for roaming; avoid strong signals outside the perimeter; use directional antennas where needed.
Segmentation
VLANs and firewalls to isolate guests, IoT, and corporate traffic and limit lateral movement.
Authentication
Use 802.1X or equivalent strong authentication for corporate SSIDs; apply encryption that fits the user type.
Key Takeaway
Design is a preventive layer: plan APs, VLANs, and access policies so a single breach cannot expose the entire network.
Architecture
Example: Employee vs Guest
Employee VLAN 100 with 802.1X and access to internal services. Guest VLAN 10 with captive portal, separate DHCP, and firewall rules allowing only outbound web and DNS.
Common mistake
Using the same DHCP scope for multiple VLANs or relying on a firewall rule alone without true VLAN separation can break isolation.
Why this matters: proper design reduces the number of decisions you must make after a breach and lowers the chance an attacker can move laterally.
