Module 4 › Chapter 4.1 › Jamming
Definition

Intentional or accidental radio interference

Jamming raises noise floor or injects spurious signals so legitimate frames are dropped or cannot be decoded.

Detection signals
  • High noise levels across a channel.
  • Widespread packet loss and failed associations.
  • Rapid deterioration on a channel that affects many APs or clients.
Example response

Move affected APs to alternate non-overlapping channels, increase AP redundancy on different channels, and run a physical sweep to find sources of interference. In enterprise environments, coordinate with facilities to remove offending equipment.

Misconception

Jamming always needs exotic hardware — false; even consumer electronics or misconfigured devices can create harmful interference.

Key Takeaway

Treat sudden widespread connectivity failures as potential interference and check noise, packet loss, and channel behavior.
