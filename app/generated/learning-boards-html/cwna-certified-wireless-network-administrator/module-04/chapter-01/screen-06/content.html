Module 4 › Chapter 4.1 › Rogue APs
Threat

Unauthorized APs bridging to the LAN

A rogue AP connected to the wired network can provide an attacker with direct internal access or be used to bypass monitoring controls.

Detection techniques
  • Scan for unknown BSSIDs and compare to authorized inventory.
  • Correlate MAC addresses to switch port mappings and physical locations.
  • Use WIPS or NAC to alert on unexpected SSIDs or new APs.
Concrete scenario

An employee connects a consumer AP to a wall jack to provide guest coverage. The AP defaults to remote management and uses a simple password. An attacker locates the AP, logs in, and uses its uplink to reach internal systems.

Mitigation checklist
  1. Enforce port security and disable unused switch ports.
  2. Segment guest and user VLANs; limit what a new AP can access.
  3. Audit and remove unauthorized hardware and update policies.
Common mistake

Assuming an AP is safe because it uses the company SSID. Attackers or employees can bridge the corporate SSID or misconfigure devices.
