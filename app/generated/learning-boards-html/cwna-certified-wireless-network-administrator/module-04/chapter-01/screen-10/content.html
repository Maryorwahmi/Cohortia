Module 4 › Chapter 4.1 › Packet Injection
Architecture impact

Injected packets can alter routing, spoof ARP, or carry malicious payloads. The threat increases when a network lacks segmentation or devices accept unauthenticated frames.

Containment strategies
  • Segment wireless clients from sensitive servers and management networks.
  • Use ACLs to restrict which services are reachable from wireless VLANs.
  • Monitor for unusual ARP, DHCP, or IP conflicts that indicate injection.
Example

After establishing a rogue AP, an attacker injects spoofed ARP responses causing traffic to route through the attacker's host. If internal hosts are not segmented, the attacker can capture or manipulate traffic further.

Detection

Alert on sudden ARP table changes, duplicate IPs, or unexpected DHCP offers. WIPS can also detect anomalous frame injection patterns.

Key Takeaway

Active injection increases risk; limit reach with segmentation and detect anomalies at layer 2 and above.
