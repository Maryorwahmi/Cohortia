Module 4 › Chapter 4.1 › Wireless Medium
Definition

Broadcast nature and visibility

Radio signals propagate beyond physical boundaries, making interception and interference possible without physical cable access.

Why this matters
  • Attackers can capture frames from a distance with a capable adapter.
  • Availability can be affected by noise or deliberate jamming.
  • Protocol weaknesses at layer 2 may be exploitable because management frames are often unauthenticated.
Common misconception

Hiding an SSID prevents discovery — false. Tools can still reveal BSSIDs and management frames, so hiding offers little real protection.

Key Takeaway

Assume the air is observable; rely on cryptographic controls and monitoring, not obscurity.

Analogy & example

Analogy: radio is like a public bulletin board. Anyone nearby can read a posted note unless it is sealed or encrypted. Example: an employee logs into a company app over unencrypted Wi‑Fi at a coffee shop. That traffic can be passively captured unless the application or transport encrypts end-to-end.

Practical check

If you see strong signal strength from unknown APs or broadcasts on unexpected channels, treat these as anomalies and investigate using BSSID and configuration details.

Why it matters to you

Understanding the medium guides every control choice: encryption for confidentiality, MICs for integrity, and monitoring/segmentation for availability and containment.
