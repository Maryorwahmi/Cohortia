Module 4 › Chapter 4.3 › EAP-TLS
How it works
Both client and server present certificates; TLS handshake performs mutual validation before granting access.
  • Provides mutual authentication and strong cryptographic assurance.
  • Client certificates prevent credential sharing because each device has a unique certificate.
  • PKI is required to issue, revoke, and renew certificates at scale.
Operational considerations
Plan automated enrollment, certificate templates, revocation checking, and expiry monitoring to avoid service interruption.
Example
Provisioned laptops use client certificates that the RADIUS server validates during the TLS handshake.
Misconception
Believing EAP-TLS is plug-and-play. Without automated PKI workflows, certificate management becomes a major operational burden.
Key Takeaway

EAP-TLS delivers the highest authentication assurance but requires a mature PKI and lifecycle processes for certificates.
