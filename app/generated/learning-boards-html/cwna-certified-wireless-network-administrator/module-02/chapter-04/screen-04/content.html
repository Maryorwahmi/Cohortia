Module 2 › Chapter 2.4 › What is a BSS?
Basic Service Set (BSS)
Group of stations that communicate under one BSSID. Understand identifiers and roles.
BSSID
A unique identifier for the BSS. In infrastructure mode it is usually the AP's MAC address.
SSID
The human readable network name. Multiple BSSs can share the same SSID.
Station roles
Clients and APs participate in a BSS; APs usually coordinate access and connect to a distribution system.
Key Takeaway

Distinguish SSID from BSSID: name versus unique radio identifier used in association and management.

Concrete example
A small office with one AP. Clients see SSID "OfficeWiFi". The AP's MAC is the BSSID that appears in the management table and ties clients to that AP.
Common mistake
Assuming two APs that broadcast the same SSID are a single BSS; each has a unique BSSID and separate radio coverage.
