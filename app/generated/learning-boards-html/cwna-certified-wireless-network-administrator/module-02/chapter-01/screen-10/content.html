Module 2 › Chapter 2.1 › OFDM vs DSSS
DSSS (Direct Sequence Spread Spectrum)
Spreads signal energy across a wider band, offering resilience to narrowband noise and simpler radio design beneficial to range.
Use case
Low-rate links where range and robustness are priorities, such as early 2.4 GHz deployments.
OFDM (Orthogonal Frequency‑Division Multiplexing)
Splits a channel into orthogonal subcarriers for high spectral efficiency and higher achievable data rates, used in a, g, n, ac, and ax.
Use case
High-throughput environments with cleaner spectrum and client radios capable of OFDM.
Example
A video streaming client benefits from OFDM in 5 GHz, while a remote low-rate sensor may be fine on a DSSS link at 2.4 GHz.
Misconception
Newer modulation always best: false. Environmental and client constraints matter more than novelty alone.
Key Takeaway
Match modulation and band to coverage, interference, and client capability, not to a single metric.
