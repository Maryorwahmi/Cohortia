Module 2 › Chapter 2.1 › 802.11a vs 802.11b
802.11a
5 GHz, OFDM
Higher peak rates up to 54 Mbps and less crowded spectrum, but shorter range compared to 2.4 GHz.
When to choose
Indoor deployments where capacity and interference avoidance are priorities and range is acceptable.
802.11b
2.4 GHz, DSSS
Simpler modulation, better range and lower cost, up to 11 Mbps, but prone to interference in a crowded 2.4 GHz band.
When to choose
Legacy compatibility or deployments that prioritize coverage over capacity.
Example
A warehouse with few walls may prefer 5 GHz for higher throughput, while a small office with partitions might rely on 2.4 GHz for coverage.
Misconception
Assuming OFDM always wins. OFDM increases spectral efficiency, but real network results depend on range, interference, and client radios.
Key Takeaway
Select band and modulation based on deployment constraints, not just peak numbers.
