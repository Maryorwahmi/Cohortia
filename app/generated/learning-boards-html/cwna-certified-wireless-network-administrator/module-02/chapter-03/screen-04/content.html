Module 2 › Chapter 2.3 › Collision Detection vs Avoidance
Claim

Wireless radios are typically half‑duplex and cannot reliably detect collisions while transmitting. This makes CSMA/CD impractical for 802.11.

Concrete example
Two clients out of each other's range both transmit to the same AP. Each thinks the medium was clear and both frames collide at the AP, but the clients cannot detect that collision when they were transmitting.
Common misconception
Treating wireless like wired Ethernet and relying on collision detection leads to wrong troubleshooting steps.
Key takeaway
Design MAC strategies that coordinate access and avoid collisions before transmission.
Analogy

Think of a narrow single‑lane bridge where drivers must take turns; you cannot shout and detect a crash while crossing. Instead, you use a traffic light to prevent collisions before cars enter the bridge.

Why it matters

This constraint drives design choices like CSMA/CA, NAV, and control frames such as RTS/CTS, all of which prioritize prevention and coordination.
