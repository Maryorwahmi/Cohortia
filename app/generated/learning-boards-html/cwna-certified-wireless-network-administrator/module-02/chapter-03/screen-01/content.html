802.11 MAC: coordinating a shared radio

Learn how CSMA/CA, NAV, RTS/CTS, and timing intervals work together to avoid collisions and manage airtime.

Interactive 16:9 Masterclass
In this lesson
CSMA/CA steps, NAV, RTS/CTS, DCF vs PCF and MAC overheads.
Why it matters
Explains why PHY rates rarely equal real user throughput and when control frames help or hurt.
Practical outcome
You will be able to interpret captures and tune RTS/CTS and fragmentation thresholds safely.
Quick concept map
PHY transmits bits → MAC schedules access → CSMA/CA avoids collisions → NAV provides virtual silence → RTS/CTS reserves medium for hidden nodes.
Example
A 54 Mbps PHY often yields 20–25 Mbps to applications because of MAC headers, ACKs, interframe spaces, and backoff time.
Visual summary
Cards show how timing, NAV, and control frames coordinate access.
