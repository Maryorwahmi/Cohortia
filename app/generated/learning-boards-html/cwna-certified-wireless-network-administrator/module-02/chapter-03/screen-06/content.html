Module 2 › Chapter 2.3 › Interframe Spaces
Definitions and roles
  • SIFS: Shortest wait for ACK, CTS, and fragments — highest priority.
  • DIFS: Wait before starting a new contention and backoff for data frames.
  • AIFS: QoS-aware interframe space used in 802.11e/WMM to vary priority by traffic class.
Example
A voice packet may use a shorter AIFS so it gains faster channel access than a large file transfer marked best‑effort.
Why timing matters

Small timing differences enforce priority without extra frames. SIFS protects immediate control replies; DIFS ensures new contenders yield to those replies; AIFS lets QoS shape access.

Key takeaway

Interframe spaces are deterministic timers that prioritize control and QoS traffic with minimal overhead.

Misconfiguration risk
Changing AIFS values without measuring can unintentionally starve other traffic classes.
