CSMA/CA steps
  1. Carrier sense: Listen for existing transmissions.
  2. DIFS wait: Ensure the medium is idle for the DIFS period.
  3. Random backoff: Choose a random slot in the contention window and decrement while idle.
  4. Transmit: Send the data frame when backoff reaches zero and channel stays idle.
  5. SIFS + ACK: Receiver sends ACK after SIFS to confirm successful reception.
Example scenario
Three clients finish heavy downloads and start uploads. Their distinct backoff values usually allow only one client to transmit at a time, reducing collisions at the AP.
Pitfall
Ignoring backoff behavior when diagnosing collisions can lead to misattributing collisions to signal issues instead of contention.
Visual timeline
DIFS — random backoff slots countdown — transmission — SIFS — ACK
Note: SIFS is shortest and gives priority to control/ACK frames.
Key Takeaway

Random backoff plus interframe spacing spreads transmit attempts in time, converting simultaneous intent into serialized use of the medium.
