BPSK — QPSK explained
Phase shifts map to bits and determine noise tolerance
BPSK: one bit per symbol using two opposite phases. Very tolerant of noise and simple to implement.
QPSK: two bits per symbol using four phase points. Twice the bits per symbol but less phase margin.
Practical metric: when SNR falls, devices will fall back from QPSK to BPSK to maintain reliability.
Concrete Example

A device in a crowded office may only decode BPSK while a nearby client with clear line-of-sight uses QPSK. The difference explains why two clients at similar RSSI can experience different rates.

Visualizing the trade-off
Think of constellation points: farther apart points are easier to distinguish under noise.
Misconception
Just because a client "supports" QPSK doesn't mean it's using it; PHY adapts based on channel quality.
Check
When diagnosing, correlate modulation rates with SNR and retry counts rather than RSSI alone.
Key Takeaway

Phase modulation offers a clear robustness vs. rate trade-off; use modulation reporting to guide fixes.
