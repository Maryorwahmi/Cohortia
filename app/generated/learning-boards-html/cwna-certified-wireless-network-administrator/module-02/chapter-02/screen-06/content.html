Module 2 › Chapter 2.2 › QAM Families
Bits per symbol
16‑QAM = 4 bits, 64‑QAM = 6 bits, 256‑QAM = 8 bits
16‑QAM encodes 4 bits per symbol and is used where moderate throughput and robustness are needed.
64‑QAM encodes 6 bits and is common in many 802.11 standards for higher rates.
256‑QAM encodes 8 bits and appears in newer standards for peak rates when channel quality is excellent.
Concrete example

An AP capable of 256‑QAM may advertise a high MCS. A client close to the AP with good SNR can use 256‑QAM; one down the hall will usually drop to 64‑QAM or lower to preserve link reliability.

Practical guidance
Design for the modulation you expect clients to use most of the time, not the peak advertised value.
Misconception
Peak modulation equals sustained throughput. In practice, environmental factors reduce usable modulation.
Key Takeaway
Higher QAM offers speed but only when SNR and interference allow; otherwise PHY will adapt to lower QAM automatically.
