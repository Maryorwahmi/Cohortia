Infrastructure (BSS / ESS)
APs mediate client access to the wired network and enable roaming.
How it works
APs broadcast SSIDs, handle associations and encryption, and forward frames to switches and routers on the wired side.
Example
An office with APs on each floor forming an ESS for seamless roaming between floors.
Operational notes
Plan channels and power to reduce co-channel interference. Ensure wired backhaul capacity supports aggregated client traffic.
Key takeaway
Infrastructure is the standard for managed Wi‑Fi: supports centralized security, roaming, and scale with proper planning.
Comparison: autonomous vs controller
Autonomous APs
Standalone devices with local intelligence; good for small deployments but management scales poorly.
Controller-based APs
Lightweight APs rely on a WLC for configuration and RF optimization; ideal for large enterprise deployments.
Misconception check
Not every infrastructure deployment needs a controller. Small networks often use autonomous APs with manual configuration.
