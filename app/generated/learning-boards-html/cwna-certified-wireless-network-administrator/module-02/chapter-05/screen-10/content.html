Repeaters and Extenders
Extend range by receiving and retransmitting wireless signals; expect performance trade-offs.
How they work
The device listens on a radio, receives frames, and retransmits them; many consumer extenders use a single radio for both directions.
Concrete example
An extender placed halfway between a router and a patio improves coverage but reduces available throughput at the patio client.
Common mistake
Treating repeaters as a long-term solution for high-density or high-throughput needs can lead to user complaints and poor performance.
Key takeaway
Use repeaters for short-term coverage fixes; prefer wired APs, dedicated backhaul mesh, or bridges when throughput is important.
Alternatives
Run Ethernet to another AP, use mesh with dedicated backhaul radios, or install a point-to-point bridge for higher-demand scenarios.
Quick decision guide
  • Low cost and low traffic? Extender may be OK.
  • High throughput or many users? Use wired APs or bridges.
