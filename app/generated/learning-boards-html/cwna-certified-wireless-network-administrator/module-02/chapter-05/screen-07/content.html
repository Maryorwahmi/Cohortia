Access Points — Role and Types
APs bridge clients to the wired network and manage wireless functions.
Autonomous AP
Standalone device with full local control of RF, security, and QoS. Good for small deployments.
Controller-based (Lightweight) AP
Thin AP that obtains configuration from a WLC using protocols like CAPWAP, simplifying large deployments.
Deployment decision
Choose autonomous for small, budget-limited sites; choose controller-based for scale, unified security, and automated RF management.
Key takeaway
AP type affects how you manage, secure, and scale your WLAN. Match AP model to operational needs.
Example
A retail chain with many locations might standardize on controller-based APs for consistent policies, whereas a single small office will likely use autonomous APs.
Misconception check
Controller-based APs are not weaker devices; they simply centralize operational intelligence for easier management at scale.
Quick planning prompts
  • How many APs will you manage?
  • Do you need centralized security and roaming?
  • Is a WLC investment justified by scale?
