Module 4 › Chapter 4.1 › Behavioral Patterns
What they do
Behavioral patterns specify how objects exchange responsibilities and control flow so behavior can change independently of callers.
  • • Define role-to-role communication between objects.
  • • Encapsulate algorithms or control policies as separate objects.
  • • Reduce coupling by delegating decisions to dedicated components.
  • Common misconception: unnecessary indirection when behavior never varies.
Concrete analogy
Imagine a restaurant kitchen where different chefs prepare the same dish with different recipes. The waiter requests a dish by name, not by how it is cooked. The recipes are interchangeable strategies; the waiter is the context delegating to a chosen chef.
Key Takeaway

Behavioral patterns make behavior a replaceable part of a system and keep callers stable while algorithms vary.
