Module 4 › Chapter 4.5 › Deadlocks & Starvation
Deadlock
Two or more threads wait for resources that each other holds, producing circular waiting and no progress.
Example: Thread A holds lock 1 and requests lock 2 while Thread B holds lock 2 and requests lock 1. Both wait forever unless one times out.
Avoidance: enforce a consistent global lock acquisition order across your codebase.
Detection: use timeouts and log failed acquisitions for diagnosis.
Starvation
A thread is perpetually denied a resource or CPU time; it never makes progress even though the system can run.
Example: A low-priority worker rarely runs because higher-priority tasks consume CPU continuously, starving that worker.
Mitigation: ensure fairness in queues, avoid unbounded priority inversion, and design for time slicing or quotas.
Pro tip: Keep critical sections minimal and prefer queue-based decoupling so threads rarely need multiple locks at once.
Warning

Ignoring lock order and long critical sections is a common source of deadlocks and hard-to-reproduce hangs.
