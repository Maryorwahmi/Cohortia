Module 8 › Chapter 8.8 › Green Software Engineering
Definition

What is green software?

Software design that intentionally minimizes energy use and carbon emissions across coding, deployment, and operations. It treats efficiency as a first-class quality attribute.

Practical tactics
  • Choose efficient algorithms and avoid unnecessary polling.
  • Use serverless or auto-scale to avoid idle resources.
  • Compress archives and use retention policies to cut storage energy.
  • Prefer data locality to reduce network energy for frequent reads.
Key Takeaway

Measuring energy and data footprint reveals where to focus optimization. Small changes in hot paths compound into meaningful carbon reductions.

Process

Sustainability checklist for architects

A practical walk-through of decisions that reduce runtime energy and storage footprint, from algorithm choice to deployment location.

Measure
Establish baselines: requests per kWh, storage per GB-month, and deployment carbon intensity.
Optimize
Reduce hot-path CPU cycles and memory usage; cache selectively to avoid repeated work.
Deploy smart
Choose regions and times with lower carbon intensity; use green cloud options where available.
Data life-cycle
Archive, compress, and delete when data is no longer valuable. Less storage equals less energy.
Concrete example

A telemetry pipeline that samples at 1% on non-critical metrics and aggregates hourly can cut storage and processing by orders of magnitude while keeping needed signal for analytics.

Common mistake

Assuming cloud equals green. Without measuring usage and optimizing, cloud waste still creates high carbon cost.
