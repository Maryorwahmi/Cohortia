Module 8 › Chapter 8.6 › Monitoring vs Observability
Definition

Monitoring: collection and alerting on known signals. Observability: collecting rich signals so you can ask new questions about system behavior.

Example
A latency spike visible in a metric prompts a trace lookup and related logs to find the external dependency that timed out.
Common mistake
Collecting high cardinality metrics across every dimension without a prioritization plan leads to metric fatigue and slow troubleshooting.
Side-by-side
Monitoring
  • Checks against known thresholds
  • Resource and uptime focus
  • Fast alerting for predictable failures
Observability
  • High-cardinality signals
  • Trace and log correlation
  • Investigate unknown unknowns
Key Takeaway

Use monitoring for fast detection and observability for deep investigation; they are complementary.
