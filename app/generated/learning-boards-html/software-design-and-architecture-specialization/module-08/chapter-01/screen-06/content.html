Module 8 › Chapter 8.1 › Service-Oriented Architecture
Definition

SOA: modular services with shared orchestration

Services expose business capabilities and interact over networks using standardized protocols. Reuse and interoperability were key motivations.

Typical pieces:
  • Enterprise service bus for routing and transformation
  • Central governance and contract management
  • Synchronous and asynchronous integration patterns
Process
1. Identify business capabilities
2. Define service interfaces and contracts
3. Orchestrate via ESB or message flows
Concrete example
Enterprise integration with ESB

Authentication, billing, and reporting implemented as services that communicate through an ESB which transforms messages and enforces governance. While each service is distinct, the ESB becomes a coordination and potential bottleneck.

Pitfall: distributed monolith — services still tightly coupled through shared infrastructure and synchronous calls.
Pro tip: ensure clear data ownership and minimize centralized orchestration to avoid reintroducing coupling.
Key Takeaway

SOA separates concerns but architecture choices like shared ESBs can recreate coupling; design contracts and ownership carefully.
