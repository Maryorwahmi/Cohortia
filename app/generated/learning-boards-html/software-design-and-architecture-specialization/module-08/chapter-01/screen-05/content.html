Module 8 › Chapter 8.1 › Benefits & Limitations
Advantages

When a monolith helps

  • Fast initial development due to single codebase and simple deploys.
  • In-memory calls reduce latency between components.
  • Lower operational overhead at start — fewer services to monitor.
Limitations

Where monoliths struggle

  • Scaling a single hot component forces entire app replication.
  • Tighter coupling increases blast radius for failures.
  • Introducing new languages or databases is difficult.
Concrete scenario
Order processing spike

If only the order module needs 10x capacity, scaling the whole monolith is costly and exposes unrelated components to risk. This is a clear operational signal to consider splitting responsibilities.

Misconception: vertical scaling solves coupling — it often only postpones operational friction and does not reduce coordination costs.
Pro tip: measure build times and deployment frequency. If small changes trigger full deploys and regressions, prioritize decoupling analysis.
Key Takeaway

Watch for scaling hotspots and coordination costs. These are practical indicators that a monolith may outlive its usefulness.
