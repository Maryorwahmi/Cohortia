Module 8 › Chapter 8.1 › Microservices
Definition

Microservices: autonomous, small, independent

Each service owns its data and deployment. Communication uses lightweight APIs or messaging, and teams own the full lifecycle of services they build.

Enablers:
  • Containers and orchestration platforms
  • Service discovery, lightweight APIs, and message queues
  • Automated CI/CD, monitoring, and tracing
Comparison
SOA often centralizes orchestration and governance.
Microservices favor decentralized ownership and independent deployments.
Concrete example
E-commerce split into services

Catalog, cart, recommendations each with own data store and API. Teams can choose storage optimized for their needs and deploy independently when a feature changes.

Trade-off: operational complexity — more services need tracing, logging, and orchestration.
Misconception: microservices are a silver bullet — they solve some problems but create others that must be managed.
Key Takeaway

Microservices enable autonomy and fine-grained scaling but require mature operational practices to succeed.
