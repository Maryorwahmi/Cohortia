Module 8 › Chapter 8.1 › Monolithic Architecture
Definition

Monolith: single deployable unit

All components — UI, business logic, and data access — live together in one artifact and run in one process or tightly integrated set of processes.

Core traits
  • Single codebase and single deployment step
  • In-process calls, low latency for internal interactions
  • Easy local debugging and testing for small teams
Key Takeaway

Monoliths are pragmatic for early-stage projects: simple deployment and debugging with lower operational cost.

Concrete Example
Early e-commerce app

Product catalog, cart, and checkout implemented in one codebase so the small team could ship features fast and deploy as a single artifact.

Why it worked: low ops, direct calls, and simple rollout.
Common misconception

Monoliths aren’t inherently bad or archaic. The right choice depends on team size, expected growth, and operational capacity.

Mnemonic: anchor vs sails — stable but harder to turn quickly at scale.
Check your understanding
When does a monolith make sense? Small team, simple features, low scaling needs.
When is it risky? When releases slow and scaling requires whole-app replication.
Observation: long build times and frequent cross-module changes are red flags.
