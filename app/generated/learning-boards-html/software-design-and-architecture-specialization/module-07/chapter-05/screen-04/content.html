Core Concept

Why document architecture?

Four reasons anchor the decision to record architecture and decide what to keep current.

Communication — explain intent and constraints to teams and stakeholders.
Knowledge repository — preserve rationale and decisions for the long term.
Analysis — enable reviewers to find risks and trade‑offs quickly.
Onboarding — make new engineers productive faster with targeted views.
Common misconception

Code comments and tests are valuable, but they do not provide stakeholder‑focused views that explain architecture choices and trade‑offs across the system.

Key Takeaway

Document to answer stakeholder questions, preserve decisions, and enable analysis — not to mirror every line of code.

Illustrative example: Photo Sharing App
Communication artifact
A high‑level diagram describing feed generation and photo upload flow
It answers: how do services interact to produce a user feed?
Knowledge artifact
ADRs that describe why we chose eventual consistency for feeds
It answers: why was this trade‑off made and what are its consequences?
Analysis artifact
Quality attribute scenarios for scalability and availability
These scenarios help reviewers assess if the architecture meets traffic and failure requirements.
A final prompt: for each artifact you produce, write a one‑line audience statement that answers which stakeholder will use it and what question it answers.
