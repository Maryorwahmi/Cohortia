Module 7 › Chapter 7.3 › Roles
Evaluation Team
Small, impartial group that facilitates, probes, and documents.
The team asks structured questions, translates business drivers into scenarios when needed, and keeps the process focused on measurable outcomes. They avoid making design decisions themselves; instead they identify areas needing follow-up.
Why impartiality matters
An unbiased perspective reduces political defenses, keeps the architecture under productive scrutiny, and helps stakeholders accept findings.
Stakeholders
Product, architecture, operations, security, and users — each supplies different scenarios and measures.
Architect
Describes decisions and rationale. Should avoid defensiveness and focus on how decisions meet business drivers.
Practical tip
Prepare questions and a stakeholder briefing so everyone knows the session goals and expected outcomes.
Common mistake
Letting one voice dominate. Use the evaluation team to surface diverse perspectives.
Example
Operations highlights network partitions that product did not consider, changing scenario priorities.
Key Takeaway
Clear roles and impartial facilitation produce credible, actionable findings.
