Module 7 › Chapter 7.3 › What is ATAM?
Definition
Formal, systematic, stakeholder-centric evaluation method.
ATAM converts vague quality claims into measurable scenarios, assesses how architecture responds, and records prioritized risks and trade-offs. It is repeatable and documented, so results can guide design changes.
  • Formal: defined steps and roles for impartial evaluation.
  • Scenario-driven: concrete testable situations replace opinions.
  • Outcome-oriented: prioritized risks and recommended mitigation.
Why this helps
Prevents late discovery of architectural failures by testing assumptions early with prioritized scenarios.
Common misconception
ATAM is not a blame game. It is collaborative and focused on learning and mitigation.
Concrete example
Claim: 'high availability.' Scenario: partition during peak traffic. Measure: percent data loss and end-to-end latency.
Key Takeaway
ATAM links architecture to business goals by testing measurable scenarios and producing prioritized, actionable findings.
Try this
Write one measurable scenario for a claim your system makes today.
Watchout
Avoid treating vague goals as evidence. Turn them into scenarios before evaluating.
