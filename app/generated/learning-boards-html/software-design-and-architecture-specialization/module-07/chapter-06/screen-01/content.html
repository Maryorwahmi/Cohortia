Documenting Architectural Decisions (ADRs)
Why the decision was made is often more valuable than the decision itself. ADRs make that why explicit.
In this lesson
Learn what an ADR contains, when to write one, and how to keep ADRs short, discoverable, and actionable.
Why this matters
ADRs prevent lost context, reduce rework, and support informed architectural change across team turnover.
Explore Key Topics
Structure
Title, status, context, decision, options, rationale, consequences.
When to write
For decisions that matter, are hard to reverse, or involve trade-offs.
Workflow
Store ADRs in version control and integrate writing into tasks and reviews.
ADR Example
Title: Use PostgreSQL for Primary Data Store
Status: Accepted
Why it helps
Preserves why PostgreSQL was chosen and the expected consequences.
