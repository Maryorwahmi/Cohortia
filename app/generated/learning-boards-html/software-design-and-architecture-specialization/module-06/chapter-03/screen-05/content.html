Producer behaviour

Producers detect changes and create event messages. They must include stable schema fields and not assume who will consume the event. Producers should emit events quickly and avoid blocking business flow on subscribers.

Channel guarantees
  • Durability: persists events so consumers can read later.
  • Ordering: can preserve sequence for a partition or key.
  • Delivery semantics: at-least-once, at-most-once, or exactly-once depending on configuration.
Consumer responsibilities

Consumers must be idempotent, handle retries, and emit compensating events when necessary. They own their downstream consistency and observability concerns.

Concrete example: OrderCreated flow
OrderService publishes OrderCreated to the broker. InventoryService consumes and reserves stock; if reservation fails it emits InventoryReservationFailed. BillingService consumes and attempts charge, emitting PaymentProcessed. NotificationService subscribes to both PaymentProcessed and InventoryReserved to inform the customer.
Common mistake
Assuming ordering across unrelated partitions. If you need ordering per customer or per SKU, explicitly partition by that key and configure ordering guarantees in the channel.
Pro tip

Map events to delivery requirements early: decide which events need ordering, durability, or higher delivery semantics before implementation.
