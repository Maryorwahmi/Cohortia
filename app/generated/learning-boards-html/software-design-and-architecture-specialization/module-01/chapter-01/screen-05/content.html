Module 1 › Chapter 1.1 › Good vs Poor Design
Good design
  • Clear interfaces and responsibilities.
  • Low coupling, high cohesion.
  • Easy to test and replace components.
Poor design
  • Tangled dependencies and duplicated logic.
  • Frequent regressions after changes.
  • Hard to estimate and slow to deliver.
Example scenario
Compare a payment flow split into gateway, validation, and settlement components versus a single combined function. The split version is easier to test per stage and to swap a gateway without touching validation logic.
Common misconception
Tight coupling for 'optimization' is rarely worth the long-term maintenance cost. Optimize later when you can measure a bottleneck.
Key Takeaway

Choose clarity over fragile shortcuts; boundaries let you change parts safely.
