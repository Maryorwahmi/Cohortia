Module 2 › Chapter 1.4 › What is coupling?
Definition

Coupling is how strongly modules depend on one another. Stronger dependencies increase the chance that a change in one module causes many edits elsewhere.

Concrete example
If a logger reads caller internals like fields, updating those fields forces logger changes — that's content coupling.
Misconception
More interfaces are not always better. Introduce abstractions where they reduce change cost or improve testing, not to show extra layers.
Why it matters
Lower coupling leads to easier testing, independent module evolution, and faster, safer refactors.
Key Takeaway

Reduce the amount of information one module must know about another; prefer explicit inputs over hidden access.

Analogy & quick guide
Analogy
Welding parts together forces joint changes; a column with clear joints lets you change parts independently.
Quick guide
Ask: 'If I change this module, which other modules must also change?' Minimize that set.
Check your understanding
Question
Does passing a single int instead of a whole object reduce coupling? Why?
Answer hint
Yes. Passing only needed data limits the surface area of change, aligning with data coupling.
