Modularity and Cohesion
Understand how small, focused modules and high cohesion make systems resilient and easier to evolve.

Modularity decomposes a system into independently developed and replaceable parts. Cohesion measures internal relatedness: high cohesion means each module does one thing well.

Benefits include clearer reasoning, parallel development, better testing, and code reuse. Common pitfalls are utility bags and God objects that combine unrelated responsibilities.

Contrast

A toolbox (high cohesion) contains related tools; a junk drawer (low cohesion) mixes unrelated items.
Quick takeaway
Aim for many small modules, each with a single, clear purpose. That reduces unexpected coupling and simplifies testing.
