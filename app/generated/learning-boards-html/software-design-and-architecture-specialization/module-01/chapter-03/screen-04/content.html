Modularity: definition and benefits
A module encapsulates a specific piece of functionality behind a small, stable interface.

Benefits include understandability: developers reason about a small scope; parallel development: teams work on separate modules; testability: modules can be unit tested in isolation; and reusability: well-designed modules can be reused in other projects.

Misconception: modularity does not mean splitting everything arbitrarily. Too many tiny modules cause integration overhead. Aim for cohesive responsibilities with stable interfaces.

Analogy
Car modules
Engine, brakes, infotainment — each replaces independently.
Why this matters
Lower risk
Changes to one module have minimal impact on others.
Concrete example
A printing mechanism changes. With modularity, only the ReportPrinter module requires change, not the entire report system.
Refactor pattern
Group related functions and data into a module; extract unrelated responsibilities to other modules; provide a minimal public interface.
Key Takeaway

Design modules around stable responsibilities and interfaces so they can be developed and tested independently.
