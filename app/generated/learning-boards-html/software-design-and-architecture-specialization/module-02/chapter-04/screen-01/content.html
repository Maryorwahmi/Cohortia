Dependency Inversion Principle
Decouple high-level policy from low-level detail using abstractions and injection.
Interactive 16:9 Masterclass
What DIP states
Both high- and low-level modules must depend on abstractions; abstractions must not depend on details.
Why it matters
It enables swapping implementations, simpler tests, and reduced changes to business logic when details evolve.
Concrete example
A report generator depends on IDataSource, not a specific MySQL client, so it works with multiple data providers.
Common mistake
Direct instantiation of concrete dependencies inside high-level classes creates tight coupling and brittle code.
