Module 2 › Chapter 2.2 › Tradeoffs
God Object
Signs: one class touches many domains, has many methods, and is the central point for unrelated changes. This creates brittleness and hidden coupling.
Analogy: a manager who performs every specialist role at the company.
Over-fragmentation
Signs: many tiny classes with thin methods, high indirection, and heavy wiring. Debugging requires jumping between many files.
Analogy: splitting a simple paragraph into sentences across many books.
Practical guidance
  • Group by reason to change: use version control and change history as signals.
  • Extract when tests and responsibilities become clearer, not for its own sake.
  • Prefer clear interfaces that reveal intent, making collaborators easy to swap.
