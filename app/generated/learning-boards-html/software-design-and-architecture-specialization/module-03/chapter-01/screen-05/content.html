Hierarchy

Creational · Structural · Behavioral

Match problem intent to a pattern family. Choosing the right family narrows implementation options and avoids misapplied patterns.

Creational

Control object creation and lifecycle.

Structural

Compose types to form larger systems.

Behavioral

Define communication and responsibility.

Concrete Contrast

Singleton (Creational) differs from Adapter (Structural): Singleton controls instance count, Adapter changes interface without altering creation semantics.

Misconception Check

Don’t confuse 'how objects are connected' with 'how objects are created'; they often co-occur but are distinct design concerns.

Why this helps

Taxonomy accelerates pattern selection and clarifies trade-offs when you explain design choices to others.
