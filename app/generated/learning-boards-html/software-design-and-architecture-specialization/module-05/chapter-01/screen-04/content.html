Module 5 › Chapter 5.1 › What is Software Architecture?
Definition

High-level structure and guiding decisions

Software architecture describes components, their external properties, and how they interact. It includes durable decisions — choices that are costly to change later — and the principles that justify them.

  • Defines major components and boundaries.
  • Specifies interactions and constraints between parts.
  • Encodes principles that support evolution and quality goals.
Concrete example
Choosing to split an application into components that own their own data enables independent scaling and clearer ownership.
Common misconception
Treating diagrams as the architecture itself rather than as a representation of intentional decisions and constraints.
Key Takeaway

Architecture is strategic: durable, decision-focused, and essential for meeting system qualities.

Analogy

Blueprint vs interior design

A blueprint places major walls, circulation, and load-bearing structure. Interior design decides finishes, furniture, and colors. Architecture maps to the blueprint: it sets structural constraints that guide many design decisions later.
Reflection
Think of a recent project. Which single architectural decision most limited future changes? That decision reveals the architecture's practical power.
