Module 6 › Chapter 5.6 › SAAM vs ATAM
SAAM
Focuses on modifiability and functional scenario analysis without an explicit trade-off framework.
  • Good for quick change-impact assessments.
  • Less structured around quality attribute prioritization.
When to use
Small systems or early stage checks focused on modifiability.
ATAM
Structured to reveal trade-offs among competing quality attributes and to create a utility tree and risk profile.
  • Useful for systems with many stakeholder priorities.
  • Produces prioritized scenarios, sensitivity points, and risks.
When to use
Complex systems where trade-offs among performance, security, reliability, and cost must be documented and negotiated.
Example
Banking app: ATAM surfaces that full encryption affects transaction speed — a trade-off requiring stakeholder alignment.
Misconception
Thinking SAAM will reveal trade-offs that require explicit prioritization — it will not.
Check
Which method would you choose for a high-stakes system with many stakeholders? Why?
