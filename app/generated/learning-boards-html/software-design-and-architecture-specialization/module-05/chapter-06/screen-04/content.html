Module 6 › Chapter 5.6 › What is Architectural Evaluation?
Definition and purpose

Architectural evaluation assesses a design for its suitability to meet requirements and business goals, focusing on quality attributes and risks.

Why it matters
Finding issues early avoids expensive refactors later and reduces technical debt.
Concrete example
A monolith with a single database may fail a growth scenario of 10x users; the evaluation recommends distribution or caching strategies.
Key Takeaway

Evaluation reveals risks and gives stakeholders a documented basis for trade-off decisions.

Breakdown
  • Inputs: business drivers, requirements, current constraints.
  • Methods: scenario walkthroughs, utility trees, trade-off analysis.
  • Outputs: risks, trade-offs, sensitivity points, recommendations.
Common misconception
Evaluation is not a binary pass/fail. It documents consequences and trade-offs to guide decisions, not to prescribe a single solution.
