Module 1 › Chapter 1.3 › Qualitative vs Quantitative
Qualitative
Fast, descriptive, good for triage when data is limited.
Uses risk matrices and tiers
Less resource-intensive
Subjective, prone to assessor differences
Quantitative
Numeric, allows financial comparisons and cost-benefit analysis.
Requires reliable asset valuation and frequency data
Produces metrics such as SLE, ARO, ALE
Useful where investment decisions need clear ROI
Use case: quick triage for thousands of small risks.
Use case: justify millstone control spend with ALE numbers.
Warning: quantitative inputs must be validated or results mislead.
