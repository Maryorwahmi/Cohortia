Module 1 › Chapter 1.4 › BCP vs DRP
Definition
BCP: Broad operational resilience

BCP keeps people, processes, facilities and technology working so critical functions continue during disruptions. It prioritizes what must remain available.

DRP: Technical recovery

DRP restores servers, applications and data. It defines recovery steps to meet objectives set by the BIA.

Common Misconception
Treating DRP as the whole plan ignores people and process continuity. Conversely, ignoring technical sequence during BCP causes unrealistic expectations.
Analogy
Think of BCP as the building plan and staffing roster; DRP is the server room procedures to restore utilities and systems.
Concrete Example
Call center outage scenario
BCP action: move agents to remote work with scripted call handling and manual order processing to keep service. DRP action: restore the CRM from backups in an alternate data center and verify connectivity and authentication before resuming normal operations.
BCP Step
Activate remote staffing and communications plan to keep customers served.
DRP Step
Fail over CRM to alternate site, restore latest backups, test transactions.
Key Takeaway

Align DRP technical steps to the BCP priorities identified in the BIA so recovery meets business needs.
