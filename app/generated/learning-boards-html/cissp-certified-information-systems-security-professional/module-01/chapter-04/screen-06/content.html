Module 1 › Chapter 1.4 › RTO & RPO
Timeline Example
RPO: 1 hour • RTO: 4 hours
Imagine a database failure at 10:00. With an RPO of one hour you can restore to data as of 9:00; with an RTO of four hours the service must be back by 14:00. The engineering choices — frequent replication and a ready alternate site — follow from these constraints.
RPO controls
Backup frequency, data replication strategy, and transaction logging determine how recent restored data will be.
RTO controls
Process sequence, hardware readiness, and orchestration determine how quickly services return.
Key Takeaway

Set RTO and RPO from business impact analysis then choose replication, backup, and site options that meet them.

Concrete Example
For an e-commerce database: RPO 1 hour implies near‑hourly backups or continuous replication of transactions. RTO 4 hours implies an alternate environment and tested failover playbook that can restore services within that window.
Misconception
Choosing a fast RTO without a matching low RPO is ineffective; data loss can still make the service unusable.
Check your understanding
Map systems to RTO and RPO and verify whether chosen backups and site options can meet both targets.
