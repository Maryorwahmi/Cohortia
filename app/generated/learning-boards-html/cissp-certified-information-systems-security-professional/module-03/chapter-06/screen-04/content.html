Module 3 › Chapter 3.6 › What is an Embedded System?
Definition & Core Traits
Specialized computing units built into larger systems, optimized for a dedicated task.
Common constraints
  • Limited CPU, memory, and power
  • Long operational lifecycles
  • Often physically accessible
  • Lack of robust update mechanisms
Key Takeaway

Treat embedded devices according to their constraints: prioritize lightweight protections and network isolation rather than assuming full IT controls.

Illustrative Example
A battery-powered environmental sensor deployed for remote monitoring. It collects readings and forwards short messages at intervals. Heavy cryptography or frequent reboots are impractical.
Design pattern
Lightweight authentication
Use efficient cryptographic primitives and short-lived session tokens where possible.
Network isolation
Place devices in restricted segments and limit inbound connections to reduce attack surface.
Common mistake
Assuming frequent patching or heavy encryption is always possible; safety and power constraints may preclude them.
