Module 3 › Chapter 3.4 › CRL vs OCSP
CRL (Certificate Revocation List)
A published list of revoked certificate serial numbers, refreshed on a schedule. Clients download or reference the list when validating certificates.
Pros: simple, cacheable, supports offline validation.
Cons: delayed updates, large lists at scale, periodic intervals may leave windows of risk.
OCSP (Online Certificate Status Protocol)
A protocol where clients query an OCSP responder for a single certificate’s revocation status, receiving a signed, immediate answer.
Pros: near real-time, smaller data transfer per check, finer timeliness.
Cons: requires availability of responders and careful client handling to avoid fail-open weaknesses.
Example & Takeaway
If a certificate is compromised, OCSP can communicate revocation immediately. CRLs are acceptable for controlled networks where periodic updates are acceptable. Misconfigured clients that accept certificates when revocation endpoints fail undermine both methods.
