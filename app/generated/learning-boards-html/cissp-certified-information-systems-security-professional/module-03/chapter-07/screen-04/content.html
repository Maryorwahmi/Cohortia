Core Concept

What is physical security?

Physical security protects people, property, and information through controls that deter, detect, delay, and respond to physical threats. It also includes environmental systems that keep hardware operational.

Definition

A combined set of measures—physical, technical, and procedural—designed to prevent unauthorized access and preserve availability.

Concrete Example

An unlocked server closet enables an attacker to remove drives and bypass logical protections, causing data loss and service interruption.

Misconception

Believing a single strong control eliminates risk. In practice, layers reduce the chance of a successful breach.

Key Takeaway

Protect physical access and the environment to keep logical security effective and systems available.

Analogies & Scenario
Analogy

Think of your facility like a safe. The outer safe, interior compartments, and the lock all work together; removing one layer exposes contents.

Scenario

An employee leaves an access badge active after termination. Without prompt revocation, that badge enables unauthorized entry and creates an insider risk.
