Module 7 › Chapter 6.9 › Third-Party Risk
Definition
Third-party risk is the risk introduced by external vendors affecting your confidentiality, integrity, or availability.
Concrete example
A SaaS analytics vendor that stores customer PII could expose that data if their access controls fail, creating regulatory and reputational consequences for your organization.
Common misconception
Assuming a well-known vendor needs no review. Size alone doesn't guarantee current configuration, operational rigor, or absence of exceptions noted in audits.
Key Takeaway

Treat every vendor relationship as a potential risk vector and require evidence proportional to the impact of a compromise.

Assessment mapping
What to map
  • Data types the vendor processes.
  • Systems and interfaces with access.
  • Privileges and remote access pathways.
  • Regulatory exposures tied to the data.
Risk decision flow
  1. Identify asset and impact.
  2. Request evidence proportionate to risk.
  3. Accept, require remediation, or refuse.
  4. Monitor ongoing posture after onboarding.
Common Mistake

Relying only on self-attested questionnaires for high-risk vendors instead of seeking independent reports or on-site validation.
