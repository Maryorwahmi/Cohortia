Module 6 › Chapter 6.5 › Why logging matters
Definition
What comprehensive logging means and why it’s the foundation of monitoring.
Core claims
  • Logs provide evidence to recreate events and confirm impact.
  • Missing or short retention windows destroy your ability to investigate.
  • Context and timestamps are essential for correlation across sources.
Concrete example
A compromised account leaves failed logons, successful logons, and unusual data access entries across system and application logs. Correlating these traces reveals lateral movement and data access timing.
Key Takeaway

Collect logs from all critical hosts and systems, protect integrity, and ensure sufficient retention to enable incident reconstruction.

Annotation
Who
User or process identity from auth or application logs.
What
Action taken: file access, command run, configuration change.
When
Accurate timestamping is critical for correlating events.
Why (context)
Source IP, process metadata, or related alerts add necessary context.
