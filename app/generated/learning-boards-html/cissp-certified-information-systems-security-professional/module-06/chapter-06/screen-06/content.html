Module 6 › Chapter 6.6 › Aggregation
Why aggregate?
  • Reduce event volume and storage costs.
  • Reduce alert fatigue by summarizing repetitive events.
  • Highlight patterns (counts, peaks) without losing signal.
Aggregation pattern
Group events by source IP, target asset, and event type within a short time window; store count and representative timestamps.
Example
100 failed SSH attempts from 192.0.2.5 to host-db1 within 60 seconds are aggregated into one event: type=BruteForce, source=192.0.2.5, target=host-db1, count=100, window=60s.
Pro tip
Keep a sampling of raw events when possible: aggregation with retention of a short raw sample helps forensic reconstruction if needed.
Common mistake
Aggregating too aggressively and removing sequence information required for timeline reconstruction.
Key Takeaway

Use aggregation to highlight signal and reduce noise, but retain samples or raw logs for investigations and compliance.
