Module 4 › Chapter 4.2 › Firewalls
Definition
A device that permits or denies traffic according to rules and inspection depth.
Packet-Filtering
Inspects headers only. Fast but stateless and context-free.
Stateful Inspection
Tracks connection state to allow legitimate return traffic without separate inbound rules.
Application-Layer / NGFW
Inspects payloads, understands protocols, and may integrate IPS and threat intelligence.
Key Takeaway
Select the lightest inspection that satisfies your threat model and avoid overly permissive rules.
Concrete Example
Allow outbound HTTP/HTTPS from internal workstations and rely on stateful tracking for returning responses rather than opening broad inbound rules.
Misconception
Believing a firewall alone secures an application. Firewalls must be paired with application hardening and monitoring; NGFWs help but do not replace layered controls.
Why it matters
Rule errors or too much trust can expose internal resources. Use deny-by-default and only permit necessary flows to minimize risk.
Example rule pattern (conceptual): permit tcp any host 10.0.0.5 eq 443 deny ip any any
