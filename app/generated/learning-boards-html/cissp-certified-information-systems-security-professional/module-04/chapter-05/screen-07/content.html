Module 4 › Chapter 4.5 › Tunneling Modes
Modes explained
Full-tunnel
All traffic routes through the corporate network, enabling inspection, policy enforcement, and centralized logging.
Split-tunnel
Only traffic destined for corporate resources goes through the tunnel. Local internet traffic bypasses the VPN for performance.
Concrete example
Full-tunnel: user browsing a public news site still goes through corporate controls. Split-tunnel: that same browsing leaves outside corporate inspection.
Risk and mitigations
  • Split-tunnel risk: local traffic can be a vector for attacks back into the VPN session.
  • Mitigation: enforce NAC posture checks, DNS filtering, endpoint antivirus, and limit sensitive resource access.
  • Prefer full-tunnel for environments requiring strict policy enforcement and auditability.
Check your understanding
If policy requires inspection of all client internet traffic, which mode must you configure? (Answer: full-tunnel.)
Key Takeaway

Full-tunnel maximizes control; split-tunnel improves performance but increases exposure and needs compensating controls.
