Module 4 › Chapter 4.5 › Multi-Factor Authentication
Why MFA matters

Passwords alone are vulnerable to theft and phishing. Requiring a second factor, like a push approval or hardware token, greatly lowers the chance that a stolen password will result in a breach.

Something you know — password or PIN.
Something you have — token or smartphone push.
Something you are — biometric check.
Concrete example
A remote worker enters credentials, then approves a push notification on an MFA app. The VPN gateway accepts the second factor and establishes the session.
Deployment notes
  • Apply MFA to VPNs, remote desktops, cloud logins, and admin accounts.
  • Protect recovery flows and service accounts to avoid bypass scenarios.
  • Choose a second factor that balances security and usability for your users.
Common mistake
Applying MFA inconsistently and leaving VPN or RDP endpoints protected only by passwords.
Key Takeaway

MFA significantly reduces credential risk; apply it to every remote access vector and protect recovery paths.
