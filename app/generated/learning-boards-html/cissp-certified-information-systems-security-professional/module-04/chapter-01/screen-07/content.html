Module 4 › Chapter 4.1 › DMZ Design
Definition & Purpose

A DMZ isolates public services from the internal network. It acts as a buffer so attackers must pass additional controls before reaching sensitive systems.

Best practices
  • Two‑firewall design or distinct filtering rules per direction.
  • Allow only necessary ports from the internet.
  • Restrict DMZ to internal services with least privilege.
Scenario: SecureWidgets
Place web and email servers in the DMZ. Web server permits HTTP/HTTPS from the internet and MySQL queries only to the internal DB on a specific port from its IP. Management interfaces for DMZ hosts should be accessible only from the management network.
Key takeaway

The DMZ limits exposure of internal assets and requires strict firewall rules and monitoring to be effective.
