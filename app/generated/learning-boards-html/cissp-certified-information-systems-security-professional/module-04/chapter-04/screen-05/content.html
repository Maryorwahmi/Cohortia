Definition
WPA3: What changed
Three practical improvements protect both personal and open Wi‑Fi use cases.
SAE — Stronger handshake
Resists offline dictionary attacks and provides forward secrecy.
PMF — Protect management frames
Prevents spoofing of deauth/disassoc frames used in attacks.
Enhanced Open
Encrypts client traffic on open networks without a password.
Misconception
Upgrading to WPA3 is essential, but WPA3‑Enterprise with 802.1X is still required for strong per‑user authorization in corporate environments.
Comparison: WPA2 vs WPA3
WPA2
  • PSK vulnerable to offline attacks
  • Management frames often unprotected
  • No Enhanced Open
WPA3
  • SAE handshake with forward secrecy
  • PMF for management frame protection
  • Enhanced Open for encrypted open networks
Key Takeaway

WPA3 fixes several practical weaknesses of WPA2, but enterprise environments still require 802.1X for identity control.
