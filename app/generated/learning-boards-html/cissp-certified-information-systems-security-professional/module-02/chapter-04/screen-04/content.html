Core Concept › Retention Policy
What a retention policy must include
  • Data categories and classification (financial, HR, logs, customer, backups).
  • Minimum and maximum retention periods with documented rationale.
  • Storage locations and required security controls during retention.
  • Roles and responsibilities for enforcing retention and disposal.
Common mistake: retaining all data indefinitely 'just in case'—this creates unnecessary liability.
Concrete example
Tax records: keep for a statutory period (example: multiple years) with encrypted archival and restricted access for audits.
Why this matters
Retention choices affect audit readiness and breach impact. Document the reason and controls for each retention rule so audits can verify compliance.
Key Takeaway

Retention is a documented, auditable risk decision: keep what you must and protect it while retained.
