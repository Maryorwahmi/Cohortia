Module 5 › Chapter 5.4 › Model Comparison
Side-by-side cards
DAC
Owner control; flexible; poor enterprise-scale manageability; classic file permissions example.
MAC
Label-based, centrally enforced; strong but administratively heavy; used where owners cannot override policy.
RBAC
Permissions on roles; practical for enterprises; requires role governance to avoid sprawl.
ABAC
Rules built from attributes for fine-grained, context-aware decisions; requires policy engine and attribute governance.
Decision checklist
  • Do you need context-aware rules (time, location)? Consider ABAC.
  • Do you want low admin overhead and easy onboarding? Consider RBAC.
  • Do you require system-enforced labels and strict need-to-know? Consider MAC.
  • Are you in a small, flexible environment? DAC may be acceptable with audits.
Key Takeaway

Combine models when helpful: RBAC for broad assignment, ABAC for context, MAC for high assurance, DAC for owner flexibility where appropriate.
