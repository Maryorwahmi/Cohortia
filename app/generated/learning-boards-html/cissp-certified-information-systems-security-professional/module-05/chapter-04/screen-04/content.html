Core Concept

What authorization is and why models matter

Authorization maps authenticated subjects to allowed actions. The model you pick decides who manages permissions and how scalable enforcement is.

Quick bullets
  • Authorization is separate from authentication; both are required.
  • Models determine decision authority: owner, system, role, or attributes.
  • ACLs are an implementation mechanism, not a model by themselves.
  • Least privilege guides permission allocation and review frequency.
Key Takeaway

Clear separation of responsibility and a repeatable review process are as important as the model you choose.

Concrete example
{
  "resource": "/reports/Q4.csv",
  "acl": [
    {"principal": "group:Finance", "permissions": ["read"]},
    {"principal": "user:auditor1", "permissions": ["read"]}
  ]
}
Common misconception

People often think ACLs alone are a policy. In reality, the model (DAC, RBAC, ABAC) defines how ACLs are generated and maintained; you must pair mechanism with governance.
