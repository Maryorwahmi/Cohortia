Passwords and knowledge factors

Understand attacks, enforcement, and when to avoid knowledge factors alone.

Common attacks
  • Phishing: tricking users into revealing secrets
  • Credential stuffing: reusing breached credentials
  • Brute-force and dictionary attacks
Mitigations
  • Strong passphrases and length over complexity alone
  • Password managers to prevent reuse
  • Rate limits, account lockout, and monitoring for abnormal access
Example & guidance
Example: migrating away from SMS
If your organization suffers repeated credential theft, require an additional possession factor such as an authenticator app or hardware key to stop access even when a password is compromised.
Common misconception
Frequent forced password rotation always improves security. In practice it can lead to predictable or weaker passwords unless a compromise occurred.
Key Takeaway

Treat passwords as one layer; strengthen them with additional factors and operational controls to reduce exposure to phishing and reuse.
