Module 5 › Chapter 5.2 › Biometrics
Definition

What biometrics are

Biometrics use physiological or behavioral traits as authentication factors; design must address privacy, spoofing, and permanence.

Types
Fingerprints, facial recognition, iris scans, voice, and behavioral biometrics like typing cadence or gait.
Privacy & permanence
Biometric templates are sensitive and often irrevocable; store and process them with strong protections or prefer local device matching.
Example and caution
Example: smartphone unlock
On-device fingerprint matching makes unlocking quick and private because templates never leave the device. Centralizing biometric databases increases exposure and regulatory complexity.
Common misconception
Believing biometric alone is sufficient for high-assurance access. Use biometrics with another factor and evaluate false accept/reject balance.
Key Takeaway

Use biometrics to improve user experience and add security, but never as the sole control for the most sensitive systems without layered protections.
