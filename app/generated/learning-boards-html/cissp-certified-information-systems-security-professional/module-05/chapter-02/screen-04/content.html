Module 5 › Chapter 5.2 › Identity Proofing
Definition

Identity proofing explained

Verify real-world attributes and authoritative documents before linking a person to a digital identity. Assurance should match asset sensitivity.

Typical checks
Name, date of birth, government ID validation, employment records, and third-party verification services.
Common misconception
Proofing is optional. In reality, weak proofing enables imposters to create accounts that can later bypass controls.
Concrete example
Employee onboarding
HR verifies ID and links validated documents to the corporate account. For admin-level access, an in-person identity validation and manager approval are required.
When to re-validate
Role changes, extended inactivity, or suspicious account behavior.
Operational tip
Document assurance levels and make recovery processes match the same verification rigor.
Key Takeaway

Choose proofing methods that meet the required assurance for each system; weak proofing undermines all downstream controls.
