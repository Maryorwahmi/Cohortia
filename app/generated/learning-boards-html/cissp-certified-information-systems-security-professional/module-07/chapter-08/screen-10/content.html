Biometrics: strong but operationally complex

Learn when biometrics add value and what policies they require.

Strengths

Hard-to-share identifiers increase assurance for sensitive access.

Privacy

Biometric templates must be protected and retention policies defined.

Fallbacks

Provide secure alternative authentication for failures and accessibility needs.

Concrete example

A lab requires a card plus fingerprint at the mantrap. The fingerprint template is stored encrypted on the controller, and a policy defines retention, revocation, and a secure fallback PIN issued after identity verification.

Misconception

Biometrics eliminate the need for other controls. They should augment, not replace, layers and procedures.

Key Takeaway

When using biometrics, plan for privacy, failover, and ongoing performance measurement.
