Design to stop tailgating

Mantraps, turnstiles, and escort policies are practical defenses against unauthorized entry.

Mantrap

Two interlocking doors enforce one-person entry and prevent piggybacking.

Turnstile

Control flow in lobbies; cheaper than mantraps but less strict.

Security guards

Human verification and response; essential for exceptions and escorted access.

Key Takeaway

Combine physical anti-tailgating features with escort policies and emergency egress plans.

Concrete scenario

Deliveries are logged at reception, a temporary badge is issued tied to the escort’s credential, and the visitor is escorted through the building to the server-room mantrap where identity checks are repeated before entry.

Misconception

Mantraps cause unacceptable delays. Properly sized and staffed mantraps maintain flow while enforcing security for high-risk zones.

Check

Are escort policies documented and are temporary badges time-limited and logged?

Pro tip

Design mantraps with clear emergency overrides and unobstructed egress for safety compliance.
