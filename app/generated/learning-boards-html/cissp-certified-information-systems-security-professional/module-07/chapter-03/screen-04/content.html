Module 7 › Chapter 7.3 › Why logging matters
Definition
Logs are persistent records of events that provide evidence for detection and investigation.
They should be collected with specific investigative questions in mind and protected to preserve their evidentiary value.
Common misconception
Logging everything without a plan creates noise. Prioritize logs that help answer security and compliance questions and tune retention to use resources wisely.
Key Takeaway

Logs must be purposeful and protected: they are only useful when they can be trusted and are relevant to the questions you need to answer.
