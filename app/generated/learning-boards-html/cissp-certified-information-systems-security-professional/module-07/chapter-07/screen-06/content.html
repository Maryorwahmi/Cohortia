Evidence Integrity and Least Intrusive Methods

Preserving the Original State of Digital Evidence

Evidence Integrity

Ensures digital evidence has not been altered or corrupted. Achieved through cryptographic hashing (e.g., SHA-256) at acquisition and periodically thereafter.

Least Intrusive Methods

Forensic investigators must use methods that cause the least possible alteration to the original evidence, often by working with forensic copies (images).

Key Takeaway

Cryptographic hashing provides a verifiable fingerprint for evidence, while working with copies ensures the original remains pristine, both vital for admissibility.

Original Evidence

The live system or original storage device. Must remain untouched.

Highly Volatile

Forensic Copy (Image)

A bit-for-bit duplicate of the original. All analysis is performed on this copy.

Immutable

Cryptographic Hash

A unique digital fingerprint (e.g., SHA-256) calculated for the evidence to verify its integrity.

Verifiable
