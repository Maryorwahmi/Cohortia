Module 7 › Chapter 7.1 › What are Security Operations?
Definition

Security operations defined

Security operations are daily activities — monitoring, triage, response, and recovery — performed by people and supported by tools to protect information assets.

Concrete Example
A SIEM generates an alert for unusual authentication. Analysts validate, escalate, and open an incident for containment and root cause analysis.
Common Misconception
Tools alone equal security. In reality, unattended tools produce noise; human workflows and tuning convert data into actionable security outcomes.
Key Takeaway
Operations make policy and architecture effective by running and improving controls continuously.
Scope

What SecOps covers

  • Real-time threat monitoring and alert triage
  • Vulnerability management and patch coordination
  • Incident response, containment and recovery
  • Continuous improvement and playbook refinement
Scenario Card
If nightly backups are not tested, corrective controls may fail during recovery. Operations must schedule and validate restores as part of the process.
