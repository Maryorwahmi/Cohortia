Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability
Use these principles to choose appropriate protections for data and services.
Confidentiality
Protect data from unauthorized reading using encryption such as TLS and AES.
Integrity
Detect tampering with hashing and message authentication codes.
Availability
Design redundancy, fault tolerance, and DDoS mitigation to keep services running.
Authentication vs Authorization
Authentication asks who you are; authorization asks what you can do. Use certificates or tokens to authenticate, and role-based controls to authorize actions.
Concrete example
A device with a valid certificate (authentication) may be allowed to publish telemetry but not to execute remote firmware install (authorization). Integrity checks on updates ensure tampered firmware is rejected.
Key Takeaway

Apply layered controls: authenticate identity, authorize actions, encrypt data, and verify integrity for updates and messages.

Misconception
Treating authentication as sufficient. Without proper authorization, an authenticated device may still perform harmful actions.
