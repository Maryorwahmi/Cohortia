Key definitions, processor choices, and design trade-offs
Every IoT device you build lives at the device layer as an embedded system. Embedded systems combine a processor, memory, I/O, and software to perform a single purpose reliably for long runs. Your choices about processor type, wireless radios, and sleep strategies will determine whether a device lasts days on a battery or requires mains power. We'll compare microcontrollers, single-board computers, and SoCs with wireless capabilities, and use practical scenarios to practice selecting the right hardware.

Microcontroller
Low power, low cost. Ideal for simple sensing and control.
Single-board computer
Runs Linux, higher power and cost. Good for gateways and heavy processing.
SoC with wireless
Microcontroller plus built-in Wi‑Fi or Bluetooth for battery devices.
Throughout this chapter we will return to these archetypes and test them against trade-offs such as battery life, response time, and unit cost using short practical scenarios you can reason about immediately.
