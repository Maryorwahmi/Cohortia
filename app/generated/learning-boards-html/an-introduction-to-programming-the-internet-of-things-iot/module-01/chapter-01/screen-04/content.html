What counts as IoT?
Devices that sense the physical world, run software to interpret those signals, and communicate results so actions can follow automatically.
Sensors
Temperature, motion, humidity, pressure — the raw inputs that make the physical world visible to software.
Actuators
Relays, motors, LEDs and valves that let devices change the physical world in response to data.
Connectivity
Networks move data from devices to gateways and cloud services, enabling remote control and analytics.
Concrete example — smart thermostat
A temperature sensor reports readings to a microcontroller. The microcontroller applies simple logic or sends data to the cloud for analysis. Based on a schedule or analytic result, a relay switches heating or cooling. The phone app displays the temperature and allows user adjustments.
Common misconception
Only consumer gadgets are IoT. In truth, industrial monitors and farm sensors follow the same pattern: physical sensing, local logic, connectivity, and applications.
Key takeaway
IoT is defined by physical integration and autonomous data flows, not by a device's brand or price.
