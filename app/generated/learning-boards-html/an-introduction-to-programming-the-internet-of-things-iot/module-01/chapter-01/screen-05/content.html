How falling sensor costs changed design
Reduced per-sensor cost lets you add many measurement points and trade calibration for redundancy and software filtering.
1
Scale measurement
Multiple cheap sensors across a field or factory floor give spatial resolution you could not afford with one expensive device.
2
Redundancy and reliability
Overlapping sensors detect faults and improve overall measurement accuracy through averaging and outlier detection.
3
Software compensates for cheap hardware
Filtering, calibration routines, and edge preprocessing can make inexpensive sensors useful for practical decisions.
Concrete scenario — precision agriculture
Cheap soil moisture sensors placed across fields allow automated irrigation controllers to water only where and when needed. The cost per node makes covering large areas feasible and turns irrigation into a data-driven process.
Common misconception
Assuming low cost equals unusable data. In practice, inexpensive sensors combined with calibration and local filtering often deliver actionable measurements.
Key Takeaway

Sensor affordability unlocks widespread instrumentation; design for calibration and local processing when using cheap parts.
