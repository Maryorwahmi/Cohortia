Core Concept I2C fundamentals

Definition

I2C uses SDA and SCL with 7‑bit addresses. The master initiates communications and devices respond when their address matches.

Quick bullets
  • Shared SDA and SCL lines for multiple devices.
  • Addresses identify devices; typical addresses like 0x76 for BME280.
  • Open‑drain outputs need pull‑up resistors (4.7k typical).
  • Good for many low‑pin sensors; slower than SPI.
Key Takeaway

I2C reduces pin count by sharing two wires and using addresses, but it requires pull‑ups and attention to address conflicts.

Practical wiring & analogy

Think of I2C as a two‑lane street where each house has a number. The master speaks the house number, and only that house answers. Long streets or many houses may require stronger streetlights — the pull‑up resistors.

Wiring
SDA → A4, SCL → A5, VCC to 3.3V for BME280, GND to GND. Add 4.7k pull‑ups if needed.
Addresses
Use an I2C scanner sketch to list device addresses and detect conflicts.
Misconception
Internal pull‑ups always suffice. In practice, external resistors are often required for reliability.
Check
If readings are garbled, run the scanner and verify pull‑ups and wiring before changing code.
