Module 2 › Chapter 2.1 › What is an Arduino?
Definition
Open microcontroller platform for hardware prototyping

Designed for low-cost, accessible control of sensors, actuators, and simple communications. Ideal for testing IoT data flows before moving to gateways or cloud integrations.

Analogy
Think of Arduino as the microcontroller's training wheels: it exposes pins and simple APIs so you can focus on behavior instead of OS details.
Concrete example
Use an Arduino Uno to read a temperature sensor and send CSV-formatted readings over USB to a Raspberry Pi for logging.
Key Takeaway

Arduino gives you fast access to hardware and a simple programming model, making it ideal for iterating IoT prototypes.

Platform details
The board exposes digital and analog pins, PWM outputs, timers, and serial ports. Libraries provide sensor drivers and communication helpers so you do not rewrite low-level code.
Strength
Quick hardware access and large community examples.
Constraint
Limited RAM and no full OS — choose data structures carefully.
Why it matters
Because IoT prototypes must be simple, reliable, and resource-aware, the Arduino's trade-offs let you prototype communication and sensor handling quickly.
