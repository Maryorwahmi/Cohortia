Module 6 › Chapter 6.4 › Intellectual Property
What IP covers
Copyright protects original code, art, music, and narrative. Trademarks protect titles and logos. Patents apply rarely to technical inventions.
Copyright — concrete example
Your game's soundtrack and character art are copyrighted when created; you can prevent unauthorized copying or distribution.
Trademark — concrete example
A unique game title or studio logo used in commerce can be trademarked to prevent confusingly similar branding.
Common misconception
Ideas are not protected by copyright; only the way ideas are expressed is protected.
Practical checklist
  • Catalog assets and authorship for code, art, music, narrative.
  • Decide which names/logos should be reserved as trademarks.
  • Keep dated records and source control to demonstrate creation time.
  • Consult counsel before registering or enforcing rights when commercial stakes are high.
Key Takeaway

IP protection is about documenting what you created and choosing registration when the asset has business value.
