Core motivations
Terms designers use to choose mechanics and measure fit.
Challenge & Mastery
Difficult goals, measurable improvement, and visible achievement systems.
Social Interaction
Shared goals, communication, and systems that encourage collaboration or rivalry.
Discovery & Exploration
Secrets, emergent systems, and open worlds that reward curiosity.
Example
An RPG that offers timed raids (Challenge), hidden side-quests (Discovery), guild events (Social), and cosmetic crafting (Expression) reaches multiple motivations without forcing players into one role.
Key Takeaway

Design to satisfy explicit motivations; avoid assuming players share your taste.

Motivation → Mechanic mapping
Use this as a checklist when you propose mechanics for a feature.
Challenge
  • Leaderboards
  • Skill-based objectives
  • Progressive difficulty
Social
  • Shared goals
  • Guild tasks
  • Trading and comms
Discovery
  • Hidden areas
  • Procedural secrets
  • Exploration rewards
Expression & Catharsis
  • Customization
  • Building tools
  • Relaxed, low-stakes modes
Misconception: Adding all mechanics at once equals broader appeal. Reality: unfocused systems dilute experience. Prioritize and tune.
