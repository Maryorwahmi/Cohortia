Module 5 › Chapter 5.4 › When to Pivot vs. When to Iterate
Signs to iterate more
  • You can form clear hypotheses that still haven’t been tested.
  • Incremental edits produce partial improvement.
  • Changes are low cost and quick to test.
Signs to consider a pivot
  • Repeated iterations don’t resolve the core problem.
  • Feedback consistently points to a misfit with the target audience.
  • The cost of further iteration outweighs potential gains.
Practical checklist before pivoting
Confirm you ran controlled experiments, documented outcomes, tried multiple hypotheses, and evaluated timeline and resource costs. If the evidence favors a pivot, plan one clearly and keep the lessons learned from previous iterations.
