Comparison
Good vs Poor
Good Flow
Clear landmark, light contrast, and a trail of small repeated cues reinforce the intended route.
Why it works
Players can recognize patterns and confirm choices without external instructions.
Concrete fix
Add a single distinct object and a line of petals to lead the eye.
Poor Flow
Multiple competing objects, identical rooms, and no repeated cues create ambiguity and wandering.
Why it fails
The player's attention fragments because nothing stands out reliably.
Quick improvement
Remove two distracting objects and emphasize one clear destination with lighting.
When you test, watch a new player for the first minute: if they pause and look around instead of moving toward the goal, flow needs stronger, consistent cues.
