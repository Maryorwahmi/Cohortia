Definition
Lighting as Narrative
Lighting sets mood, directs the eye, and encodes time or emotional context.
• Warm, soft light suggests safety or memory.
• Cool, low-contrast light implies neglect or danger.
• Focused highlights create narrative anchors for discovery.
Key Takeaway

Match color temperature and contrast to the intended mood, and use highlight regions to guide players to narrative clues.

Examples & Tips
Flickering Candle
Creates unease and suggests human presence or recent activity.
Shafts of Dusty Light
Signal long abandonment and reward looking in beam paths for clues.
Sunset Glow
Elicits nostalgia; pair with personal items to create a poignant moment.
Misconception
Over-highlighting a clue removes the joy of discovery and can feel like a tutorial. Keep highlights purposeful and minimal.
