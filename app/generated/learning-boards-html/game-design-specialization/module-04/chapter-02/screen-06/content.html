Clarity and Feedback — Good vs Bad
How to spot problems and concrete fixes
Good: Clear and immediate
  • Large numeric counters with contrast and unit labels.
  • Distinct icons for item classes with text fallback.
  • Concise, contextual labels that avoid jargon.
Example: An ammo display shows "12 / 30" with a red flash when low, and a subtle click when reloaded.
Bad: Ambiguous or silent
  • Small icons with no label that look similar across categories.
  • No response when a button is pressed or when an ability is triggered.
  • Overloaded visual effects that mask important changes.
Common mistake: Removing feedback to simplify visuals, leaving players uncertain if input had effect.
Key Takeaway

Ensure every critical action has clear, appropriate feedback. Clarity reduces cognitive load; feedback confirms outcomes.

Quick fix checklist
  • Can players read this in two seconds?
  • Does an action provide immediate confirmation?
  • Is feedback proportional to game pace?
