Module 2 › Chapter 2.2 › Input Devices and Their Strengths
Definition & Mental Model

An input device sends player intent to the game. Evaluate devices by precision, degrees of freedom, and feedback.

Precision
Mouse offers pixel-level control; important for point-and-click and precise manipulation.
Degrees of freedom
Analog sticks and motion controls allow smooth movement across axes, useful in 3D space.
Feedback
Tactile or audio feedback confirms input; gamepads excel here.
Key Takeaway

Map your game's core actions to the device quality that most supports them, and provide alternatives where possible.

Examples & Comparison
Mouse — Puzzle/RTS
  • High precision cursor control
  • Direct selection and dragging
  • Best for small targets and precise placement
Gamepad — Fighting/Action
  • Analog input for smooth movement
  • Tactile triggers and vibration feedback
  • Good for rhythm and close-combat feel
Keyboard — Simulation/RPG
  • Many discrete bindings and quick shortcuts
  • Good for complex command sets
Touch — Mobile Casual
  • Direct manipulation and gestures
  • Requires larger hitboxes and clear affordances
Common misconception
Assuming one control method fits all players. Always consider optional mappings and alternatives to support different setups and abilities.
