Module 3 › Chapter 2.3 › What is feedback in games?
Core Concept
Feedback: the game's language

Feedback confirms an action, shows consequences, and guides the player's next move. It must be timely and proportional to event importance.

Concrete example
Sword hit: hit spark, enemy grunt, small damage number, subtle screen shake. Together they confirm and reward the action.
Common misconception
Adding many effects will not help if they compete for attention. Prioritize clarity over quantity.
Key Takeaway

Treat feedback as vocabulary: establish consistent cues and reserve the strongest signals for high-impact events.

Design checklist
  • Is the signal immediate? (under 200 ms when possible)
  • Does it reflect magnitude? (small vs large hits)
  • Is it distinct from other signals?
  • Does it map to player expectations?
Quick exercise
Pick an action in your game and list one visual, one auditory, and one haptic cue that together confirm success without overwhelming the player.
