Module 3 › Chapter 3.2 › What is Worldbuilding?
Core definition
Worldbuilding constructs an internally consistent setting whose rules inform narrative and mechanics.
Claim: A single clear rule outperforms vague omnipotence for design.
Example: Frequent earthquakes → engineering guilds, repair quests, and building puzzles.
Misconception: Art alone equals immersion. Reality: consequences and discoverable evidence are required.
Key Takeaway

Design rules and limits first, then layer history and aesthetics to justify them.

Concrete Example
Imagine a coastal world where a poisonous algae blooms every decade. That single environmental rule creates fishing limits, a ritual for safe harvest, a black market for safe food, and quests to find antidotes. Players encounter documents, NPC fears, and architectural solutions that all point back to the algae’s cycle.
Check Your Understanding
Ask: What visible clues would you place for a player to discover the algae cycle?
Pro-tip: Put one clue in environment, one in dialogue, one in optional text.
