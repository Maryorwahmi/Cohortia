Module 3 › Chapter 3.1 › What Is Game Narrative?
Definition
Narrative as systems and story
Narrative combines events, meaning, and the player's ability to influence how those events are experienced. It includes plot sequence, theme, conflict and pacing, but extends into systems that shape outcomes.
Concrete Example
Haunted mansion premise: retrieving an artifact can be a fixed arc, a branching quest, or an emergent crisis born from systems interacting.
Common Misconception
Narrative is not just cutscenes or text dumps; environmental cues and system outcomes can be primary storytelling tools.
Key Takeaway
Design scenes so mechanics and visuals work together to communicate story, not compete with it.
Illustration: Narrative Elements
Plot
A sequence of cause-and-effect events forming beginning, rising action, climax and resolution.
Theme
Underlying message: loss, courage, corruption, or redemption that gives events meaning.
Conflict
The engine of tension: internal, external, or environmental struggles that drive action.
Pacing
Control of rhythm and reveal—balancing tension with calm so players can process events.
Key Takeaway

Adopt a systems-first mindset: ask how mechanics, level design, and player choice reveal plot and theme without forcing exposition.
