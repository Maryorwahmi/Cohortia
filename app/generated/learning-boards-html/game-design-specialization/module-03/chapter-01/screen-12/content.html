Module 3 › Chapter 3.1 › Show, Don't Tell
Principle
Reveal story through the world
Objects, lighting, and layout can answer who, what, and why without explicit text. Good environmental storytelling invites players to assemble the story themselves.
Design Exercise
Create one room and list three observations a player could make; ensure those observations form a coherent narrative thread.
Alignment
Match mechanics to environmental cues so players can act on what they discover; the world should invite interaction, not hide information behind obtuse puzzles.
Example
Ruined Nursery
Toys, a faded lullaby sheet and scorch marks communicate a tragic past more effectively than a long exposition panel.
Why It Matters
Players who discover story through play feel more connected and rewarded for exploration.
Misconception Check
Environmental storytelling complements, not replaces, carefully placed exposition or scripted moments where needed.
