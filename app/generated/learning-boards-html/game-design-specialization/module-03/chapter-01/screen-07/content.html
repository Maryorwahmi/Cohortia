Module 3 › Chapter 3.1 › Branching Narrative
Design Goal
Make choices that change what players remember
Branching can change quests, NPC disposition, access to areas, or endings. The key is making consequences meaningful and visible.
Scope Management
Use converging nodes and lateral consequences to limit the need for duplicate content while preserving player impact.
Prototype Strategy
Sketch decision trees with explicit consequences, then test whether players perceive differences in playtests.
Scenario: Haunted Mansion — Branching
Choice Point 1: Entrance
Option A: Front door triggers alarms and specters. Option B: Back garden leads to a helpful spirit. Each option changes later quests and NPC reactions.
Design Tip
Prioritize branches that alter player resources, relationships or later challenge types—these are memorable and justify the extra cost.
Misconception Check
Branching isn’t only about many endings; it's about meaningful, perceivable differences during play.
