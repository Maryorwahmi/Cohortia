Module 3 › Chapter 3.4 › Structural Patterns
Formal definition

Structural patterns deal with how classes and objects are composed to form larger structures. They help you express relationships that keep changes localized. Prefer composition when you need flexibility.

Analogy
Think of wiring an appliance: adapters match plugs, facades provide a single power strip to control multiple devices.
Why it matters
Easier integration, safer refactoring, and clearer public interfaces for subsystems.
Examples & patterns
Adapter
Translates one interface into another so incompatible components can collaborate.
Facade
Provides a simplified interface over a complex subsystem of cooperating classes.
Loose coupling
Design goal: reduce direct dependencies and make changes safer.
Key Takeaway

Choose composition and clear translation or delegation boundaries to keep your codebase adaptable.
