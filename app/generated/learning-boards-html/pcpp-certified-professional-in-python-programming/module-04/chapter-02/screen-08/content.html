Module 4 › Chapter 4.2 › I/O-bound vs CPU-bound
I/O-bound
Threads excel when tasks spend time waiting, such as sleeping, reading files, or waiting for a network response. During those waits, other threads can run.
Example: the sleep-based sample shows two threads appear to run concurrently because sleep releases the GIL and allows switching.
CPU-bound
For heavy CPU work, threads are limited by the GIL and may not run in parallel on multiple cores. Multiprocessing or native extensions are better options.
Consider the added complexity of inter-process communication before switching approaches.
Decision checklist
  • Is your workload waiting on I/O? Prefer threading.
  • Is it CPU-heavy? Consider multiprocessing or specialized libraries.
