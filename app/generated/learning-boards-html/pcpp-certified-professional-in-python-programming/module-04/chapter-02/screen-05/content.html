Module 4 › Chapter 4.2 › Race condition
Definition

Race condition

A race condition occurs when concurrent threads operate on shared data and the final result depends on timing. Non-atomic operations like increment are vulnerable.

Concrete scenario
Five threads each perform 100,000 increments on a shared counter. Without synchronization, the final total is often less than expected because increments are lost.
Why it matters
  • Data corruption leads to incorrect program state and hard-to-find bugs.
  • Tests may pass sometimes and fail other times due to timing differences.
  • Assume non-atomicity for multi-step operations on shared data.
Common mistake
Relying on the GIL to make multi-step updates safe. The GIL does not guarantee atomicity for read-modify-write sequences.
Analogy
Think of two people writing a total on the same whiteboard at once. If neither waits, they may both read the same subtotal and write back identical new totals, losing one person's addition.
