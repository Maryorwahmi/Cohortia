Module 8 › Chapter 8.1 › TCP vs UDP
TCP — Connection-oriented
  • Establish connection with three-way handshake, then stream bytes.
  • Guarantees delivery, ordering, and retransmission.
  • Higher overhead and potential latency due to retransmits.
Example
HTTP and file transfer rely on TCP to ensure complete, ordered data.
UDP — Connectionless
  • Sends independent datagrams without handshakes or retransmit guarantees.
  • Lower overhead, lower latency, suitable for real-time traffic.
  • Receiver may get packets out of order or not at all.
Example
Live audio, video, and many game updates use UDP to prioritize timeliness.
Choose TCP when correctness matters; choose UDP when speed and low overhead matter.
