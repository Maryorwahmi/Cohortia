Module 9 › Chapter 8.9 › Session Management
How it works
A Session stores cookies and default headers. Use it to login once and perform many requests that share state.
import requests

s = requests.Session()
login_url = "https://example.com/login"
login_payload = {"username": "user", "password": "password"}
response_login = s.post(login_url, data=login_payload)
print(response_login.status_code)

protected = s.get("https://example.com/dashboard")
print(protected.status_code)
print(s.cookies.get_dict())
Notes & best practices
  • Attach shared headers to session.headers to avoid repetition.
  • Confirm cookies with session.cookies.get_dict during debugging.
  • Close sessions if long-lived resources are a concern: session.close().
Common mistake
Expecting cookies to persist across separate requests without a Session leads to authentication failures and redirects to login pages.
Key Takeaway

Use requests.Session to simulate user journeys and keep your client stateful and efficient.
