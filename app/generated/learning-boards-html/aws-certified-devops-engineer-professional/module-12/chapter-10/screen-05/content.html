The process
A repeatable sequence to keep experiments safe and informative.
1
Hypothesize steady-state
Choose measurable SLIs like p99 latency or success rate and state expected behavior.
2
Vary real-world events
Select faults that mimic realistic failures such as instance termination or network latency.
3
Run experiments safely
Start small; if in production use tight stop conditions and notify responders.
4
Automate for repeatability
Make experiments scripts or templates to collect consistent data and enable scheduling.
5
Minimize blast radius
A single instance or small percentage of traffic reduces risk while giving actionable signal.
Key Takeaway

Follow the process to keep experiments safe and their results trustworthy.

Practical checklist
  • • Define the steady-state metric and threshold.
  • • Pick the minimal blast radius and communication plan.
  • • Prepare monitoring and a CloudWatch alarm for stop conditions.
  • • Automate the experiment in FIS or a runbook and test in staging first.
Misconception
Automation without hypothesis is not better than manual chaos. Always tie automated runs to measurable predictions.
Pro Tip

Start with scheduled small experiments to build team confidence, then expand scope as you establish safe practices.
