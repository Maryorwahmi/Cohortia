Cloud waste — definition & examples
Resources that cost money but deliver little or no ongoing value.
Common examples
  • Always-on non-production EC2 instances
  • Unattached EBS volumes and old snapshots
  • Old S3 buckets storing obsolete datasets
  • Idle databases provisioned for brief tests
Why it happens
Teams assume elasticity removes cost concerns, or lack ownership and consistent tagging to trace responsibility.
Concrete scenario
A developer spins up instances for feature tests, forgets to terminate them after a week. Finance receives a larger bill with no way to assign responsibility until tags or resource lists are reconciled.
Key Takeaway

Preventing waste starts with simple checks and ownership: tag resources, run audits, and automate teardown of temporary environments.

Analogy
Cloud waste is like leaving lights on across an office: each light costs money when it's on and nobody uses it.
Check your understanding
If you found an unattached EBS volume from a month ago, what two actions would you take? Tag it with owner and investigate whether it can be deleted or archived.
Common mistake
Assuming small resources add no cost; many small forgotten items compound into significant monthly spend.
