On-Demand, Reserved Instances, and Savings Plans

Comparing flexible, committed, and hybrid pricing models

On-Demand: Max Flexibility

Pay by the second/hour with no commitment. Ideal for unpredictable, short-term workloads, development, and testing. Most expensive per hour.

Reserved Instances (RIs): Deep Discounts

Commit to specific instance type, region, and tenancy for 1 or 3 years for up to 75% discount. Best for stable, predictable workloads. Lacks flexibility if needs change.

Savings Plans: Flexible Commitment

Commit to a consistent compute usage ($/hour) for 1 or 3 years. Applies across EC2, Fargate, Lambda, regardless of instance family/size. Offers similar discounts to RIs with greater flexibility. Highly recommended.

On-Demand: Highest cost, no commitment, maximum flexibility.

Reserved Instances: Significant discounts for 1 or 3-year commitment to specific instance types. Less flexible.

Savings Plans: Similar discounts to RIs, but more flexible as they apply to compute usage across services, not specific instances.

Key Takeaway

Choose On-Demand for unpredictability, RIs for stable, specific needs, and Savings Plans for flexible, broad compute commitments to maximize savings.
