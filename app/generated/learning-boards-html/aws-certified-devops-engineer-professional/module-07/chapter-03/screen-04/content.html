Module 7 › Chapter 7.3 › What is Fault Tolerance?
Core Concept

Fault tolerance definition

Design systems to continue operating correctly even when parts fail, by containing and recovering from errors.

Why it matters

Reduces user impact, lowers incident severity, and makes recovery predictable.

Common misconception
Retries without backoff are often mistaken for fault tolerance but can worsen outages.
Pro-tip
Design for clear failure modes and test them with injected faults.
Analogy

Think of a system as a bridge with controlled detours. If one lane is closed, traffic reroutes rather than stopping the entire flow.

Concrete example

An order service publishes messages to a queue when a downstream inventory service is slow. Orders are accepted and processed later, preventing user-facing timeouts.

Key Takeaway

Fault tolerance focuses on containment, graceful recovery, and preserving correct outcomes under failure.
