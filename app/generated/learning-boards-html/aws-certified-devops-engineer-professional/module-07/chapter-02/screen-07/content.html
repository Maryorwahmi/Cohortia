Architecture
ASG + ALB recovery flow
Recovery sequence
  1. ALB health checks fail for the instance; the ALB stops routing to it.
  2. The ASG marks the instance unhealthy and terminates it (or replaces it), then launches a new instance.
  3. The new instance registers with the target group; after passing health checks, it receives traffic.
Practical notes
Expect a temporary capacity dip; tune deregistration delay and lifecycle hooks to allow graceful shutdowns, and monitor the ASG launch times to ensure capacity returns within your SLA.
Scenario example
ASG desired capacity 3. One AZ reports degraded network; two instances in that AZ fail health checks. The ALB stops routing to them, the ASG launches replacements in healthy AZs, and capacity returns to three. Measure how long the ALB takes to remove targets and how long the ASG takes to launch replacements to understand RTO impact.
Check your understanding
Why is it important to tune health check thresholds and deregistration delays? Consider false positives and in‑flight requests.
Key takeaway

ALB and ASG together maintain availability: ALB keeps traffic on healthy targets while ASG restores capacity automatically; both need correct tuning for smooth recovery.
