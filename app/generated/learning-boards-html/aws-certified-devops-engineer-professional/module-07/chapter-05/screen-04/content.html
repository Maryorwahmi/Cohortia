Definition

Backup and Restore explained

Create recovery points, store them durably, and restore systems when needed. Best for workloads that tolerate longer downtime.

Example: Nightly backups for a daily analytics job; recovery within hours is acceptable.
Why it matters: Lower cost because you don't run redundant systems, you only store recovery points.
Misconception: A snapshot exists so recovery is guaranteed — without tested restores and correct IAM, it may fail.
Process snapshot
Match RTO and RPO to business needs, then choose backup frequency and retention. Add cross-Region copies to protect against regional failure.
Key takeaway
Backup & Restore is cost-effective but requires routine restore validation to ensure recoverability.
