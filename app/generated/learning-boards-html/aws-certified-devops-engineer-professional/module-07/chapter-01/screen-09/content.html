RTO and RPO explained
Quantify recovery targets and map them to design choices.
RTO
Maximum acceptable downtime before service restoration.
RPO
Maximum acceptable data loss measured in time from the failure point.
Mapping to architecture
Low RTO implies warm or hot standby; low RPO implies frequent replication or synchronous writes.
Concrete example
If a financial service requires RTO 30 minutes and RPO 5 minutes, you would implement rapid failover mechanisms and near-real-time replication to avoid more than five minutes of lost transactions.
Misconception
Choosing an aggressive RPO without design changes will not magically reduce data loss; the architecture must support the target through replication frequency or synchronous writes.
Key Takeaway

Set RTO and RPO with stakeholders, then design replication and failover systems that can demonstrably meet those targets through tests.
