Fault Tolerance in practice
Designs that expect no downtime and no data loss even when components fail.
Active-active replication
All replicas serve requests and keep state consistent.
Strong consistency
Synchronous commits or consensus protocols to prevent data loss.
Operational cost
Higher complexity and expense compared with HA; reserve FT for critical workloads.
Example
A payment processing service uses synchronous replication and distributed consensus so that transactions are never lost and services continue processing while individual nodes fail.
Misconception
Assuming FT is always necessary. Many systems can accept short failovers; FT is only justified when zero downtime and zero data loss are mandatory.
Key Takeaway

Choose FT for services that cannot tolerate any downtime or data loss; be prepared for higher latency and cost.
