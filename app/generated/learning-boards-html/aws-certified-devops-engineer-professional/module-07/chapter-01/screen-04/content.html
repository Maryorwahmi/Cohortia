What resilience means
A practical definition and why operations care about it.
Redundancy
Extra capacity so a single failure doesn't interrupt service.
Replication
Copies of data to limit loss and enable recovery to a known point.
Monitoring & Recovery
Detect issues quickly and follow a plan to restore service.
Concrete example
An e-commerce checkout is replicated to multiple AZs and writes orders to a replicated database. If a server fails, another instance takes traffic; if an AZ fails, instances in other AZs continue handling orders. Monitoring alerts on increased latency and triggers an autoscaling policy.
Common misconception
High uptime guarantees don't guarantee no data loss. Uptime measures availability; you must also plan replication frequency and backups to meet RPO.
Key Takeaway

Design resilience to meet both availability and data-loss goals. Ask stakeholders for RTO and RPO, then map patterns to those targets.
