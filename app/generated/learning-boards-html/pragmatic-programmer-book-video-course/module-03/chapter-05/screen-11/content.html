Module 4 › Chapter 3.5 › Concurrency & Parallelism
Definition
Concurrency vs Parallelism
Concurrency overlaps tasks; parallelism does them at the same time on multiple cores.
Concurrency is often best for I/O-bound workloads where waiting can be overlapped.
Parallelism suits CPU-bound workloads and requires true multi-core execution.
Platform constraints, like Python's GIL, influence which approach yields benefit.
Concrete example
Many concurrent network requests are handled efficiently with async I/O, while heavy number-crunching benefits from multiple processes or distributed work.
Pitfall
Introducing threads or processes increases complexity: synchronization bugs, resource contention, and harder debugging.
Key Takeaway

Pick concurrency for I/O overlap and parallelism for CPU-bound tasks; always verify gains and watch complexity.
