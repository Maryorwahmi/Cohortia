Module 4 › Chapter 4.5 › Know Your Editor
Definition

Mastering your editor means intentionally learning features and configuring them so you spend mental effort on design, not mechanics.

Why this matters
  • Reduces repetitive typing and context switches.
  • Surfaces errors early through integrated tools.
  • Makes refactors and navigation safe and fast.
Concrete example
Enable an LSP-driven completion to see correct function signatures as you type, preventing wrong argument ordering.
Common mistake
Accepting default settings and never learning keyboard shortcuts.
Key Takeaway

Pick three editor habits to practice this week and measure a small, observable benefit.

Practical steps
  1. Enable syntax highlighting and an LSP for your language.
  2. Configure format-on-save and a formatter.
  3. Learn rename symbol and go-to-definition shortcuts.
Analogy: a carpenter's hammer — the better you know it, the fewer mistakes you make and the more quickly you accomplish work.
