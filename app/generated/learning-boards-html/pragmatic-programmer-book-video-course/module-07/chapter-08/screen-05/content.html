Module 7 › Chapter 7.8 › Curiosity & Critical Thinking
Definition & Practice

Ask why. Form a hypothesis. Test it.

Curiosity combined with structured testing moves you from intuition to knowledge. That knowledge reduces risk when adopting new tools or changing architecture.

Question Log
Maintain a weekly list of questions and one experiment per week to answer one question empirically.
Hypothesis-driven experiments
Timebox a small prototype that isolates a single variable. Gather data and reflect on the result.
Concrete Example
A team notices occasional latency spikes. Instead of immediately adopting a new caching library, they hypothesize that the spikes are due to a nightly bulk job. They run a targeted experiment disabling the job for a window, log the results, and confirm the job as the cause. The team then designs a safer scheduling strategy and adds monitoring — a measured, effective outcome.
Misconception
Curiosity is not chasing every shiny framework. It’s disciplined testing focused on meaningful assumptions.
Key Takeaway

Disciplined curiosity produces evidence that guides sustainable choices and reduces blind adoption.
