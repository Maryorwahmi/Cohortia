Source Quality & Verification
Prefer primary documentation and validate with experiments.
Trusted sources
Official docs, well-regarded books, and community maintainers are preferred starting points.
Unverified claims
Random blog posts or dated tutorials can be useful but should be treated cautiously.
Quick verification
Write a tiny proof-of-concept or test to confirm behavior and edge cases.
Concrete scenario
A new library claims to simplify concurrency. Instead of adopting blindly, build a small sample that stresses concurrency and observe memory and correctness behavior.
Common misconception
Popular tutorials or many stars do not replace critical testing in your environment.
Key Takeaway
Use primary sources as anchors and confirm with short, targeted experiments.
