Module 6 › Chapter 6.1 › Prioritization & Iteration
Priority matrix
High value, low risk — implement
High value, high risk — prototype first
Low value, low risk — schedule later
Low value, high cost — avoid
Iteration patterns
  • Deliver minimal increments that provide user value.
  • Reserve time for experiments and prototypes to reduce risk.
  • Revisit acceptance criteria after user feedback and adjust priorities.
Misconception
Mistake: Equating iteration with chaos. In fact, iteration demands measured experiments and clear acceptance criteria.
Key Takeaway

Break work into small, testable increments and use feedback to refine requirements rather than perfecting them upfront.
