Module 6 › Chapter 6.4 › Expectation Management
Core Concept

What expectation management is

Explicitly align stakeholder beliefs with what the team can deliver, given constraints.

Definition
Continuous alignment of deliverables, schedule, and scope with stakeholder understanding.
Why it matters
Perception affects satisfaction; timely, honest updates prevent surprises and build trust.
Common mistake
Assuming silence implies agreement or success. Silence breeds assumptions.
Key Takeaway

Make expectation alignment an explicit, scheduled activity — not an afterthought.

Analogy

Expectations are a shared map

If the team and stakeholders don’t agree on the map, they will walk different directions and be surprised when paths diverge.

Concrete example
Stakeholder expects full feature set in four weeks; team proposes a prioritized MVP using MoSCoW to agree a deliverable for that timeframe.
Misconception
Optimism is not the same as realism. Over-promising erodes trust faster than conservative, accurate communication.
Reflection prompt: When a stakeholder requests "all features quickly," how would you reframe that conversation to create a measurable objective?
