Module 6 › Chapter 6.4 › Transparency
Definition

Transparency in reporting

Openly surface status, risks, and decision options so stakeholders can act.

What to include
Current status, impact, root cause (brief), and two or three mitigation options.
Audience tailoring
Technical details for engineers; concise impact and decision needed for sponsors.
Timing
Communicate problems as soon as they are understood, not after they escalate.
Key Takeaway

Transparency with options preserves trust and enables better decisions.

Concrete example

"We discovered the integration takes longer because the legacy API requires data normalization. Impact: 2-week delay. Options: extend timeline by two weeks; defer advanced reports; investigate alternative API for 2 days."

Common mistake
Waiting for certainty before sharing bad news — early sharing invites collaboration and reduces surprises.
Pro tip
Always include one recommended option and at least one alternative to guide decision-making.
Check your tone
Focus on facts and options, not blame. That keeps stakeholders engaged and solutions-focused.
