Comparison

Linear planning versus short cycles

Understand the trade-offs in predictability, cost of change, and stakeholder feedback.

Waterfall: characteristics
  • Sequential phases with handoffs and late user feedback.
  • High cost for late changes because many decisions are already fixed.
  • Predictable in scope but fragile under changing requirements.
Iterative: characteristics
  • Short cycles producing working increments and rapid feedback.
  • Adaptation through learning: requirements evolve with actual usage.
  • Requires automation and clear acceptance to stay sustainable.
Key Takeaway

Choose iterative delivery when requirements are uncertain; use waterfall when requirements are stable and well-known.

Concrete example: shopping cart
  1. Build add-to-cart first — users can begin collecting items.
  2. Add cart management — update quantities and remove items.
  3. Introduce a simple checkout flow for order confirmation.
  4. Integrate payment as a final increment.
Common misconception

Iterative development is not no-planning. It requires continuous planning and prioritization. The mistake is to run iterations as mini-waterfalls by doing all design unknowingly up front.
