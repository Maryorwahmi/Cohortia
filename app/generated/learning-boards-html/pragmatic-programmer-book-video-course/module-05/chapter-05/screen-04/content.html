Module 05 › Chapter 5.5 › Why estimation is hard
Definition

Estimation is an informed guess

Because software includes unknown technical risks and evolving requirements, estimates are provisional and improve with information.

• Creative tasks expose unknowns when implemented.
• Early-stage estimates have high variance—the cone of uncertainty.
• Good planning uses estimates as decision tools, not promises.
Key Takeaway

Treat estimates as hypotheses to be tested and updated as you learn more.

Example
Imagine starting a dashboard without inspecting the data source. Early estimate: two days. After a spike you discover slow joins and missing indices, turning the task into five days. The estimate was never wrong — it reflected what you knew. The change matters because you must communicate the new range quickly and revise the plan.
Misconception
Add a flat padding to avoid being wrong.
Why it's wrong: it hides the unknowns and discourages breaking tasks into smaller, discoverable units.
Pro Tip
Break work into the smallest meaningful chunks and estimate those separately to reduce variance.
Check Your Understanding
Name one unknown you would spike before confirming an estimate for a new integration and explain how the spike changes your estimate.
