Module 1 › Chapter 1.3 › Change Is Inevitable
Definition

Change is a constant in software development. Treat requirements as evolving constraints, not unchangeable facts.

Why it matters

Planning for change reduces cost and risk when requirements shift.

Common misconception

Believing that perfect upfront design prevents change; in reality it often increases brittleness.

Real-world analogy

Compare a monolith to a rigid building that costs more to modify than a modular structure with removable panels.

Key Takeaway

Assume change and design to keep its cost low.

Concrete example

A tightly coupled order processor directly instantiates payment and inventory services. When requirements change to add another payment provider, the single class must be edited in many places. This causes bugs and slows delivery.

What to do instead

Introduce abstractions and inject implementations so swapping a payment provider is a configuration change, not a code rewrite.

Check your understanding

Identify one module in your codebase that would benefit from a clear interface and a strategy to swap implementations.
