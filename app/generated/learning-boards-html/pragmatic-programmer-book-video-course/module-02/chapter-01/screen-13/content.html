Definition
Pragmatic limits of DRY
Not all repetition is harmful. Balance the cost of abstraction against long-term maintenance savings. Prefer incremental, tested refactors.
When to avoid DRY
If repetition is unlikely to change or abstraction would add significant complexity, keep duplication.
When to refactor
When duplicated knowledge affects correctness or will likely change, extract and centralize it.
Check your approach
Ask: Does the duplication express the same intent? Will the intent change? How many places must be updated if it changes?
Analogy
Don't replace a simple repeated sentence with a complex macro unless it clearly reduces future work and improves clarity.
