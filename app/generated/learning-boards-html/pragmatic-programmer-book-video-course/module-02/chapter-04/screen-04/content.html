Core Concept What 'Good Enough' Means
A practical definition for pragmatic teams
Definition
Software that satisfies current requirements while remaining robust, maintainable, and extensible where it matters.
  • High quality for shipped parts; avoid speculative expansion.
  • Favor clear tests and documentation for active functionality.
  • Limit abstraction until multiple concrete uses appear.
Concrete Example
A stable API implemented to support current clients, with tests and clear schema, but without a plugin system for hypothetical future clients.
Key Takeaway

Quality matters; scope is limited by current, verified needs.

Why teams confuse the idea
Emotional attachment to code and fear of deletion make teams hoard features. 'Future-proofing' language masks speculative work.
Evaluation Questions
Who benefits now? What problem is solved today?
Misconception
Good enough does not mean low quality or no tests.
