Merkle tree summary

Transactions are hashed and paired to form a tree; the root hash summarizes all transactions and is stored in the block header.

Benefit
Efficient proofs: a verifier can check a single transaction against the Merkle root using a short witness path.
Confirmations
Each new block increases confirmations, making reversion increasingly expensive.
Concrete example
A transaction in block 100 has one confirmation. When block 101 is mined, it has two. Many services require multiple confirmations before crediting funds.
Misconception
Confusing cryptographic integrity with business finality — legal or business processes may require additional checks beyond confirmations.
Key takeaway
Merkle roots provide compact proofs; confirmations measure how hard it would be to change history after a transaction.
