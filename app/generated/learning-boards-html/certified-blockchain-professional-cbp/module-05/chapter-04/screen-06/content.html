Module 5 › Chapter 5.4 › State Channels
How they work
Open a channel by locking funds on L1, exchange signed off-chain states, close by submitting final state to L1 or triggering dispute resolution.
Concrete example
Two merchants send rapid micropayments back and forth during a trading session and finally settle the net balance on-chain.
Security note
Participants must watch the chain for fraud attempts or delegate watchers; offline participants risk losing funds if they miss a dispute.
When to use

Use state channels for repeated bilateral or small-group interactions where participants are known and online monitoring is feasible.

Analogy
Like keeping a running tab between two friends and settling the balance later at the cashier; you only record the start and final totals on the receipt.
Common mistake

Expecting channels to scale a public DApp with many dynamic users; they are optimized for fixed participant sets.

Key Takeaway

State Channels deliver instant, cheap exchanges for fixed parties but depend on active monitoring for disputes.
