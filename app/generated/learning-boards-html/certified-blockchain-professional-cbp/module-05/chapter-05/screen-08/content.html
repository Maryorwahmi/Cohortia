Module 6 › Chapter 5.5 › Centralized vs Decentralized
Centralized Bridge
A custodian holds locked assets and issues wrapped tokens on the destination chain.
• Simpler, often faster confirmations.
• Single point of failure and custodial risk.
• Useful when trust in a known operator is acceptable.
Decentralized Bridge
A network of validators, multisig, or cryptographic proofs enforces transfer rules without a sole custodian.
• Reduces single-point failures.
• Introduces complexity: coordination, key rotation, and economic incentives.
• Requires careful analysis of validator thresholds and bonding.
Recommendation checklist
For high-value transfers prioritize decentralized validators, audited contracts, and multisig or threshold schemes. For convenience and speed where custody is acceptable, centralized bridges can be used with heightened operational and legal checks.
