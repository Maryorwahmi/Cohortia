Module 6 › Chapter 5.5 › What is Blockchain Interoperability?
Definition
Interoperability enables chains to exchange data, value, and logic under agreed trust assumptions.
Data Interoperability
Read and verify state from another chain using light clients or proofs.
Asset Interoperability
Move tokens between chains using lock-and-mint or escrow patterns.
Logic Interoperability
Trigger contracts on another chain to build multi-chain applications.
Key Takeaway

Interoperability is purpose-driven. Choose the type based on whether you need state verification, asset custody, or cross-contract actions.

Concrete example
A DeFi app on Ethereum may verify an identity credential stored on a privacy-focused chain (data), move tokens to a high-throughput chain to access liquidity (asset), or trigger a cross-chain settlement contract after an off-chain event (logic).
Misconception
Believing a single bridge is universally secure ignores differing threat models and cryptographic primitives across chains.
Why this matters
Correctly matching the interoperability type to your need prevents unnecessary risk and ensures the application behaves as intended.
