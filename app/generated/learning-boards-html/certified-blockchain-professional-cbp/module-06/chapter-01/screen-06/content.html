Process Flow

How pools work and where impermanent loss appears

Step 1 — Supply

User deposits equal value of two tokens and receives pool share tokens.

Step 2 — Trade

Traders swap tokens against reserves; the smart contract updates balances and price.

Step 3 — Withdraw

Provider redeems the share token for their portion of the updated reserves and keeps accrued fees.

Impermanent loss explained

If token prices diverge, rebalancing can leave you with less dollar value than holding the tokens outside the pool.

Mitigations
  • Provide liquidity to stable pairs.
  • Use pools that compensate volatility with higher fees.
  • Monitor and withdraw when divergence risk rises.
Concrete example

You deposit one token and an equivalent value of another. If one token's market price doubles, the pool will shift balances through trades and you may end up with less dollar value than simply holding both tokens.

Key Takeaway

Impermanent loss is real; compare expected fees to modeled loss before committing large funds to a pool.
