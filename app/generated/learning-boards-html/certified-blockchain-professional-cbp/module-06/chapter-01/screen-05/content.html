Comparison

AMM mechanics vs traditional order book

AMM (Liquidity pools)
  • Traders swap against a pool.
  • Pricing via formula tied to pool balances.
  • Fees distributed to liquidity providers.
Order Book (Centralized)
  • Buy and sell orders matched by an exchange.
  • Liquidity often concentrated with market makers.
  • Custody typically held by the exchange.
Formula
Many AMMs maintain a constant product relationship that updates prices as balances change.
Provider outcome
Providers earn fees but may face impermanent loss if relative token prices move.
Trader experience
Immediate swaps with slippage determined by pool depth and trade size.
Common Mistake

Assuming high fee income always offsets price divergence. Always model impermanent loss alongside expected fees.

Concrete scenario

You deposit equal values of two tokens into a pool. Traders swap into and out of that pool; you earn a share of fees but your value relative to holding may change if prices diverge.

Check

Ask: what happens to your token balances if one token doubles in price against the other?

Key Takeaway

AMMs enable permissionless trading without an order book, trading off continuous liquidity against provider exposure to price divergence.
