Module 6 › Chapter 6.5 › Quantum-Resistant Cryptography
What is post-quantum cryptography?
Algorithms designed to resist attacks by quantum computers that can run algorithms such as Shor's and Grover's.
  • Definition: cryptographic primitives that maintain security even against quantum adversaries.
  • Why it matters: recorded traffic and long-lived signatures are vulnerable to future quantum decryption.
  • Concrete mitigation: design upgradeable key formats and support algorithm agility in wallets and nodes.
  • Misconception: quantum risk is distant, but passive collection by adversaries makes early planning prudent.
Example
A security team may prepare a migration plan that allows accounts to add a post-quantum public key alongside existing keys, enabling gradual transition without losing access.
Key Takeaway

Treat quantum resistance as a long-term design requirement: inventory cryptographic dependencies and enable algorithm agility.
