Module 6 › Chapter 6.5 › Sustainability
Comparison
Proof-of-Work (PoW)

Security via computational work. High energy use from mining hardware and continuous computation.

  • Robust and battle-tested security model.
  • High electricity consumption and cooling needs.
Proof-of-Stake (PoS)

Security via staked funds and validator selection. Much lower runtime energy.

  • Energy efficient; reduces environmental impact.
  • Different centralization and economic incentives to assess.
Example

When a major blockchain moves from PoW to PoS, energy consumption falls sharply, but architects must re-evaluate validator incentives, slashing rules, and decentralization goals to maintain security.

Common misconception

Lower energy does not automatically equal stronger decentralization or better security; different risks appear and must be managed.

Why it matters

Sustainability shapes public acceptance and regulation. Energy-intensive networks may face restrictions, while efficient designs can ease adoption and reduce operational cost.

Green initiatives

Renewable sourcing, carbon accounting, and protocol efficiencies.

Protocol design

Consensus choice, sharding, and fewer on-chain writes reduce energy.

Reporting

Transparent energy and emissions reporting builds trust with users and regulators.

Perception risk

High energy use can slow mainstream adoption and invite policy action.

Key Takeaway

Design choices determine energy profile. Aim for transparent, energy-aware protocols while managing new governance trade-offs.
