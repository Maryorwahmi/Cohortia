Cryptographically Secure Proofs of Attributes: Verifiable Credentials (VCs)
How VCs enable selective disclosure and trusted verification.
The Verifiable Credential Flow

1. Issuer

A trusted entity (e.g., university) issues a cryptographically signed VC.

2. Holder

You, the individual, store the VC securely in your digital wallet.

3. Verifier

A third party verifies the VC's authenticity and specific attributes.

Selective Disclosure

Present only the necessary information, not your entire credential.

Example Use Case

Proving you have a university degree to an employer without revealing your full transcript or personal details.

Benefit

Significantly enhances privacy and reduces fraud by allowing minimal data sharing for verification.

Key Takeaway

Verifiable Credentials are cryptographically signed digital proofs that enable selective disclosure, enhancing privacy and streamlining identity verification.
