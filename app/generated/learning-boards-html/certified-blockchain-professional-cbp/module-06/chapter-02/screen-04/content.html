Module 6 › Chapter 6.2 › Problems in Traditional Supply Chains
Definition
Opacity, Fragmentation, and Trust Gaps
Opacity means incomplete visibility; fragmentation means many disconnected records; lack of trust follows when records are unverifiable.
Concrete example
Tracing a batch of organic coffee requires farmer certificates, transport logs, and storage receipts. Siloed records make this slow and error-prone.
Common mistake
Assuming a central database owned by one party is sufficient — it removes shared verification and keeps a single point of failure.
Process visualization
Step 1 — Many records
Participants create separate logs for the same batch, causing mismatch and delays.
Step 2 — Recall complexity
When contamination occurs, reconciling multiple sources delays action and expands recall scope.
Key Takeaway

Diagnose custody and proof at every transfer point. If proof cannot be independently verified, the system is at risk.
