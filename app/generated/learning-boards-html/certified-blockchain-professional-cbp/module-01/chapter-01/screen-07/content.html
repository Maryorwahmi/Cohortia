The four pillars that define DLT

Decentralization
Control and validation are shared across multiple nodes, reducing single points of failure.
Immutability
Validated entries are linked cryptographically so past records are tamper-evident.
Transparency
Participants can verify history. Privacy is handled separately using permissioning or pseudonymity.
Cryptography
Signatures and hashes prove origin and integrity, enabling trust without a central intermediary.
Concrete example
In supply chain, decentralization shares records among participants; immutability records each handoff; transparency and cryptography let auditors verify provenance without trusting a single party.
Design trade-offs
Many systems choose permissioning to limit public visibility while keeping distributed validation, balancing privacy with auditability.
Quick checks
  • Can a single node rewrite history unnoticed? If yes, it's not a DLT.
  • Does the system provide cryptographic proofs of origin? If no, immutability is weak.
  • Is transaction visibility appropriate for the domain? Tune permissioning accordingly.
