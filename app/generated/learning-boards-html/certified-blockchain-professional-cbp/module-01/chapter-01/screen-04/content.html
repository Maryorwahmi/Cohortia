Module 1 › Chapter 1.1 › What is a Distributed Ledger?
Definition

Distributed Ledger explained

A system where multiple participants maintain identical ledger copies and validate new entries through agreed rules and cryptography.

Analogy: public notebook
Multiple clerks keep identical notebooks and check new notes before copying them, preventing unilateral edits.
Why it matters
Tamper-evidence and distributed verification reduce reliance on any single trusted party.
Common misconception
Replicated databases are not necessarily DLTs; DLT also distributes validation and consensus.
Key Takeaway

DLT combines shared copies with shared validation to produce a tamper-evident, resilient ledger.

Concrete example

Shipment handoff recorded as ledger entries

Each handoff creates an entry with a timestamp and signatures. All participants keep the same log so provenance is verifiable.

Step 1 — Create entry
Sender records transaction with time and identifiers.
Step 2 — Validate
Nodes verify the entry against rules before accepting it.
Step 3 — Replicate
Once valid, the entry is added to each node's copy, visible for audit.
