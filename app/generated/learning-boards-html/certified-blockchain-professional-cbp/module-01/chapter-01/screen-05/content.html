Module 1 › Chapter 1.1 › DLT vs Centralized Databases
Centralized

Single authority model

Control
A single entity owns the master record and enforces access.
Failure mode
Single point of failure and potential for undetected internal tampering.
Performance
Typically high throughput and low latency under central control.
Distributed Ledger

Shared validation model

Control
Control is shared; nodes follow consensus rules rather than a single admin.
Failure mode
Resilient to single-node failures but can be slower to reach global agreement.
Performance
Throughput depends on consensus design; trade-offs often made for decentralization.
Example — Cross-border payments
Traditional
Multiple banks and clearinghouses reconcile separately, causing delays and fees.
DLT alternative
A shared ledger can enable near-instant settlement and a single auditable history.
Trade-off
Design must address throughput and regulatory compliance to match bank performance and rules.
