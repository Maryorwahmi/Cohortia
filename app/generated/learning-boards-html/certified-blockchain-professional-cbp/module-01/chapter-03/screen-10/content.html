Module 2 › Chapter 1.3 › Proof of Work
How PoW works
Miners iterate nonces to find a block hash below a difficulty target. The first to succeed broadcasts the new block and collects rewards.
Security model
Attackers need large computational resources; costliness deters tampering.
Costs
High energy and hardware costs are the main trade-offs of PoW.
Concrete example

In PoW, miners worldwide try different nonces until one finds a hash below the target. That winner proposes the next block and nodes accept it after verifying the proof of work.

51% attack
If an attacker controls a majority of hash power, they can outpace honest miners and create an alternative chain to double-spend.
Key Takeaway

PoW secures by making rewriting history materially expensive, but it uses substantial energy and can centralize mining where costs are lowest.
