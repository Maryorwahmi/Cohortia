Module 2 › Chapter 1.3 › Decentralization
Definition

Distribution of control and data

Nodes independently store and verify the ledger, removing single points of control and failure.

Why it matters
Resilience and censorship resistance come from many independent verifiers rather than one trusted intermediary.
Common misconception
Decentralized does not mean ungoverned — projects often have governance, but ledger validation remains distributed.
Key Takeaway

Decentralization shifts trust from a single authority to protocol rules and many independent validators.

Concrete scenario

Imagine a centralized payment platform ordered to freeze accounts — it can comply instantly. On a decentralized ledger, the same order would require controlling a majority of nodes worldwide to be effective, making censorship much harder.

Mnemonic
Many Mirrors — each node holds a mirror copy of the ledger.
