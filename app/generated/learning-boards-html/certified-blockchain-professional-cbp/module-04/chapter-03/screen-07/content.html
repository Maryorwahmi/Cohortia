Zero-knowledge proofs
A ZK proof attests that a statement about a computation is true without revealing the internal details of that computation. ZK-rollups use these proofs to show that an off-chain batch produced a correct state root.
Result: the Layer 1 verifies the proof and the batch is final immediately; no challenge period required.
Key Takeaway

ZK-rollups provide cryptographic finality for batched transactions, trading higher proof cost for faster and stronger withdrawal guarantees.

Concrete example and nuance
A settlement system that must move funds back to L1 frequently would prefer a ZK-rollup so withdrawals are immediately defensible on L1. However, creating proofs for complex smart contract logic can be more expensive and requires specialized tooling.
Example
zkSync and StarkNet illustrate architectures where validity proofs reduce withdrawal delays compared to optimistic approaches.
Misconception
ZK-rollups do not always reduce total system cost; they shift cost into off-chain proof generation which can be heavy for complex contracts.
