Module 5 › Chapter 4.4 › Public vs Permissioned
Definition

Permissioning and membership

Permissioned systems require authorized identities for joining and for specific actions like reading or validating transactions, enabling governance and compliance that public networks cannot natively provide.

Example
A group of logistics providers uses a permissioned ledger so shipping details are visible only to the sender, receiver, and customs, not the entire network.
Common misconception
Permissioned does not automatically mean a single centralized operator. Consortium governance can distribute authority while maintaining identity controls.
Comparison
Public
  • Open membership
  • Global visibility
  • Proof-of-work/permissionless consensus
Permissioned
  • Authenticated participants
  • Selective visibility
  • Configurable ordering and governance
Key Takeaway

Choose permissioning when your application needs regulated identities, selective disclosure, and formal governance instead of full public transparency.
