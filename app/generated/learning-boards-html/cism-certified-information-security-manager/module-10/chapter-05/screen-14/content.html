Module 10 › Chapter 10.5 › Verifying Containment Effectiveness

The Need for Verification

It's a mistake to assume containment measures are effective without active verification. Attackers are persistent and seek alternative routes.

Prevents attacker re-entry or bypass.
Ensures complete threat neutralization.

Methods of Verification

Actively test and confirm that containment strategies are truly working through various checks and simulations.

  • Monitoring: Network traffic, system logs.
  • Testing: Vulnerability scans, attempting blocked actions.
  • Drills: Regular simulations for IRT practice and procedure validation.

Example: Firewall Rule Verification

A new firewall rule is implemented to block traffic from a malicious IP address.

  • Action: From an internal system, attempt to ping or connect to the malicious IP address.
  • Expected Result: Connection should fail, confirming the rule is active and effective.
  • Consequence of Failure: Attacker could still communicate with internal systems.
Key Takeaway

Verification is the final, crucial step in containment, ensuring that all measures are robust and the threat is truly isolated.
