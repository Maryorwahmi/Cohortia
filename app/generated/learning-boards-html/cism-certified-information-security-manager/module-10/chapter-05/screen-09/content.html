Module 10 › Chapter 10.5 › Disabling Accounts and Blocking Indicators

Disabling Compromised Accounts

If user credentials are stolen, immediately disabling or resetting passwords for those accounts is paramount to prevent further unauthorized access.

Prevents lateral movement and privilege escalation.
Essential for cutting off attacker's foothold.

Blocking Malicious Indicators (IOCs)

Update firewalls, IDS/IPS, and EDR solutions with identified IOCs (malicious IP addresses, domain names, file hashes) to prevent further communication.

Disrupts attacker command-and-control (C2).
Prevents further infection and data exfiltration.

Example Scenario: Phishing Attack

An employee falls for a phishing email, and their credentials are stolen. The attacker uses these credentials to access internal systems.

  • Action: Immediately disable the compromised employee account and force a password reset.
  • Impact: Attacker loses access, but the employee cannot work until a new password is set.

Example Scenario: Malware Outbreak

Malware spreads through the network, attempting to communicate with a known malicious C2 server.

  • Action: Update perimeter firewalls and internal IDS/IPS with the C2 server's IP address and domain name.
  • Impact: Malware communication is blocked, preventing further instructions or data exfiltration.
Key Takeaway

Rapid response in disabling accounts and blocking IOCs is crucial for containing an incident and preventing its escalation.
