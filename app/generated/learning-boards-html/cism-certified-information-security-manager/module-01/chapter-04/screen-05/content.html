Process Flow

Development to Review: numbered steps

1
Development
Gather business requirements and risk context; draft the policy focusing on intent and obligations.
2
Review & Approval
Engage legal, HR, and steering committee to confirm enforceability and alignment.
3
Communication
Publish, train, and document acknowledgements so stakeholders know obligations.
4
Enforcement
Monitor compliance, conduct audits, and apply defined consequences consistently.
5
Review & Update
Schedule periodic reviews and update based on new threats or regulatory change.
Key Takeaway

A documented, repeatable process with named owners prevents policies from stagnating or becoming irrelevant.

Common mistake

Skipping stakeholder input or approval produces policies that cannot be enforced in practice.

Example checklist
  • Inputs: risk assessment, legal, business requirements
  • Draft: plain-language policy with defined scope and owner
  • Approval: steering committee sign-off documented
  • Communication: publish, train, collect acknowledgements
  • Enforcement: monitoring, audit schedule, defined consequences
  • Review date: annual or on significant change
Scenario

When a regulation changes, the review step triggers standards and procedures updates. Without a named owner, the update may be delayed and compliance risk increases.
