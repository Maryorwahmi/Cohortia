Module 1 › Chapter 1.5 › Major Regulations
GDPR
A comprehensive EU data protection law that governs personal data processing. Note its extraterritorial reach and strong emphasis on breach notification and data subject rights.
HIPAA
A U.S. law protecting patient health information. It focuses on technical safeguards like encryption, access control, and audit logs, and on Business Associate Agreements for third-party providers.
SOX
Mandates accurate financial reporting and strong internal controls. Evidence and audit trails are essential for compliance and corporate accountability.
PCI DSS
A standard for protecting payment card data with prescriptive technical and operational requirements for any organization that processes cardholder data.
Application & Example
Mapping exercise
  • List each data category you store: customers, employees, PHI, payment data.
  • For each category, mark which regulations apply and what evidence is needed.
  • Prioritize controls where categories overlap, focusing on least privilege, encryption, and logging.
Common misconception
Believing one control satisfies all laws. In practice, the same control may help multiple regulations but different evidence and contractual terms are often required.
Concrete example
An EU customer’s personal data processed by a U.S. company can trigger GDPR obligations even if the company is not in the EU. That requires data subject handling and breach notification planning.
Key takeaway
Identify scope first. Then map each regulation to the specific controls and the evidence auditors will expect.
