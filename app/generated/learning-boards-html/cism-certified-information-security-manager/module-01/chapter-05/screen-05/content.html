Module 1 › Chapter 1.5 › GDPR
Core claims

GDPR protects personal data and grants individuals rights over their data. The regulation’s extraterritorial scope means processing EU personal data triggers obligations regardless of company location.

Practical controls
  • Data mapping and minimization
  • Lawful basis documentation and privacy notices
  • Encryption and access controls for personal data
  • Breach notification playbook with timelines
Concrete example
A marketing list including EU emails requires lawful basis and opt-out handling. Failure to honor data subject requests or to notify breaches promptly can escalate into regulatory action.
Common misconception
Treating GDPR solely as a fines problem. Fines matter, but operational disruption and loss of trust often have longer-term business effects.
Breach & notification
Prepare a timeline and roles for breach notification. Quick, documented action reduces regulatory exposure and shows good governance.
Key takeaway
Map data and business processes to GDPR obligations and translate legal language into clear operational steps and evidence for audits.
Reflection
Which customer-facing systems hold EU data, and what immediate actions would you take to reduce exposure?
