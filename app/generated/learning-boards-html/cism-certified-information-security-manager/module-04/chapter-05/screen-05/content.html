Qualitative Risk Assessment

Understanding subjective and descriptive risk evaluation.

Key Characteristics

  • •Subjective: Relies on expert judgment and experience.
  • •Descriptive: Uses terms like High, Medium, Low for likelihood and impact.
  • •Non-monetary: Does not assign financial values to risks.
  • •Quick & Easy: Generally faster and less resource-intensive to conduct.

Strengths & Weaknesses

Strengths
  • •Fast to implement
  • •Low resource cost
  • •Easy for non-technical stakeholders to understand
Weaknesses
  • •Subjectivity leads to inconsistency
  • •Difficult to compare risks across departments
  • •Challenging to track risk changes over time

Example Scenario

A qualitative assessment might rate the likelihood of a phishing attack as "High" and the impact of a successful attack as "Medium" (e.g., temporary data loss, reputational damage), resulting in an overall "Medium-High" risk level.

Key Takeaway

Qualitative assessments are quick and accessible but require clear definitions to mitigate inherent subjectivity and ensure consistency.
