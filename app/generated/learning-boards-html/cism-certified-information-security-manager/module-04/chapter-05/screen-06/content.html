Quantitative Risk Assessment

Measuring risk with objective, financial values.

Core Principles

  • •Objective: Uses data and formulas for measurable results.
  • •Monetary: Expresses impact in financial terms (e.g., dollars).
  • •Probabilistic: Likelihood is expressed as a probability or frequency.
  • •Data-driven: Requires historical incident data and cost figures.

Benefits & Challenges

Benefits
  • •Clear ROI for controls
  • •Informed executive decision-making
  • •Comparable risks across the organization
Challenges
  • •Complex and time-consuming
  • •Requires significant data collection
  • •Risk of 'false precision' with insufficient data

Example: Annualized Loss Expectancy (ALE)

ALE is a common quantitative metric calculated as: ALE = ARO × SLE, where ARO is the Annualized Rate of Occurrence (likelihood) and SLE is the Single Loss Expectancy (impact in dollars). This provides a clear financial expectation of loss.

Key Takeaway

Quantitative assessments provide objective financial data for risk, but demand robust data collection to avoid misleading precision.
