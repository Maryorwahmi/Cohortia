Module 2 › Chapter 2.4 › Segregation of Duties (SoD)
Definition

Segregation of Duties

A control pattern that divides critical duties so no single person holds complete control of a process.

Purpose

Prevent fraud and errors by requiring multiple people to complete critical processes and enabling independent checks.

Example

Payment workflow: request, approve, execute by three distinct roles to reduce risk of misuse.

Common Misconception

SoD is only a finance control. In truth, it applies to systems, access, keys, deployments, and audits.

Key Takeaway

Design roles so that no single person can both introduce a vulnerability and hide its traces.

Analogy

Two-Key Bank Vault

Like a vault that requires two independent keys, SoD requires separate approvals so a single actor cannot open and conceal access alone.

When to apply
  • Any process that changes production state or controls critical data.
  • Areas where audit logs or forensic trails can be modified.
  • Processes that can lead to financial loss or regulatory exposure.
Check Your Understanding

Identify one critical process you rely on. Who performs each step today, and is any single person able to bypass checks?
