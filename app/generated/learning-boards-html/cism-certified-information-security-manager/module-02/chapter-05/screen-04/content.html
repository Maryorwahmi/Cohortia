Module 2 › Chapter 2.5 › Types of Requirements
Definition

Legal • Regulatory • Contractual

Each source creates different obligations and enforcement mechanisms. You must identify the source, interpret its requirement, and assign control owners.

Example: a data breach law may set reporting timelines; a regulator may require particular safeguards; a contract may require audit access and data processing agreements.
Common mistake
Treating all obligations the same
Not all obligations have the same timelines, remedies, or evidence requirements. Catalog and prioritize by impact and enforceability.
Key Takeaway

Record each obligation with its owner, compliance evidence, and monitoring method.

Legal
Laws passed by governments that create obligations such as reporting timelines and penalties.
Regulatory
Agency or industry rules requiring specific safeguards or controls like HIPAA or PCI DSS.
Contractual
Agreed obligations with customers or vendors, such as SLAs, NDAs, BAAs, or DPAs.
Practical example
A cloud provider may be legally required to notify a regulator after a breach. A regulator may require encryption and DPIAs for high-risk processing. A contract with a healthcare customer may require a BAA that adds obligations similar to HIPAA, creating both contractual and regulatory expectations.
