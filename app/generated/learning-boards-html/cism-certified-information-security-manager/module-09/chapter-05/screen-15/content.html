Common Mistakes and Safety Notes

Avoiding Pitfalls in Detection and Analysis

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Alert Fatigue: Too many false positives leading to ignored alerts.
  • Insufficient Logging: Critical data not collected or retained.
  • Lack of Tool Integration: Siloed information, preventing holistic view.
  • Outdated IoCs/Rules: Failure to update detection capabilities.

Concrete Example: Alert Fatigue

A SIEM generates hundreds of low-priority alerts daily. Analysts become desensitized and miss a critical alert indicating a sophisticated attack because it's buried in the noise. This highlights the need for alert tuning and prioritization.

Safety Notes for Analysis

  • Preserve Evidence: Do not inadvertently alter or destroy forensic evidence.
  • Work on Copies: Always analyze copies of data where possible.
  • Chain of Custody: Maintain a strict chain of custody for original evidence.
Key Takeaway

Proactive management of detection systems and strict adherence to forensic safety protocols are essential for effective and legally sound incident analysis.
