Phase 1: Containment

Containment: Stopping the Incident's Spread

Immediate Actions to Prevent Further Harm

What is Containment?

Containment is the phase where immediate actions are taken to limit the scope and impact of a security incident, preventing it from spreading further and causing additional damage.

  • Primary Goal: Stop the incident's progression.
  • Key Challenge: Balance speed with evidence preservation.
  • Strategy Varies: Depends on incident type and severity.
  • CISM's Role: Provide clear decision trees and guidelines.
Common Mistake

Acting too quickly without understanding the full scope can destroy critical forensic evidence or alert the attacker prematurely.

Containment Objectives

Limit Damage: Prevent further compromise of systems, data, and services.

Minimize Downtime: Reduce the period of disruption to business operations.

Preserve Evidence: Collect and protect forensic data for root cause analysis and legal purposes.

Key Takeaway

Effective containment is about swift, informed action to stop an incident's spread while carefully preserving evidence for future analysis.
