Containment Strategies

Long-Term & Strategic Containment

Sustained Solutions and Intelligence Gathering

Long-Term Containment

  • Purpose: Allow business operations to continue while eradication and recovery are planned.
  • Actions:
    • Re-routing traffic to clean systems.
    • Implementing temporary security controls (e.g., stricter firewall rules).
    • Deploying honeypots to monitor attacker activity in a controlled environment.
  • Nature: More robust and often temporary solutions.
Example

After a web server compromise, traffic is temporarily re-routed to a hardened, clean server while the original server undergoes forensic analysis and rebuilding.

Strategic Containment (APT)

  • Purpose: Monitor attacker activity to gather intelligence (TTPs) before full eradication.
  • Context: Typically used in advanced persistent threat (APT) scenarios.
  • Requirements:
    • Significant expertise and resources.
    • Executive approval due to high risk.
    • Controlled environment to prevent further damage.
  • Benefit: Gain insights to strengthen future defenses.
Key Takeaway

Long-term containment supports business continuity, while strategic containment offers intelligence gathering for sophisticated threats, both requiring careful planning.
