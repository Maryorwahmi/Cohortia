Module 8 › Chapter 8.1 › Common TPRM Mistakes
Frequent Errors
  • "Set it and forget it": Neglecting ongoing monitoring after initial assessment.
  • Inconsistent Classification: Applying a one-size-fits-all approach to risk assessment.
  • Ignoring Nth-Party Risk: Overlooking vulnerabilities in a vendor's own supply chain (sub-processors).
  • Siloed Responsibility: Treating TPRM as solely a security team function.
Key Takeaway

Proactive and continuous risk management, coupled with cross-departmental collaboration, is essential to avoid common TPRM failures.

Risk Classification Detail

Proper classification means prioritizing vendors based on their access to sensitive data (like PII or financial records) and their criticality to business operations. A high-risk vendor requires more intensive due diligence and continuous monitoring than a low-risk one, ensuring resources are focused effectively.

Nth-Party Risk Example

If your software development vendor uses an open-source library with a known vulnerability, that vulnerability becomes an Nth-party risk to your organization, even if you have no direct relationship with the library's creators.
