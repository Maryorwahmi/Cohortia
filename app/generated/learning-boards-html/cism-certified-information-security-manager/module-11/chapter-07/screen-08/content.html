Understanding Key Risk Indicators (KRIs)

Early warning signals for potential non-compliance.

Definition of KRIs

Metrics that provide an early warning signal of increasing risk that could lead to non-compliance.

Purpose

To enable proactive intervention and mitigation before risks materialize into actual compliance failures.

Key Takeaway

KRIs are crucial for proactive risk management, providing early warnings that allow for intervention before potential non-compliance issues escalate.

KRI Examples for Compliance
  • Number of failed attempts to access sensitive data.
  • Increase in unauthorized changes detected on critical systems.
  • Number of overdue patch deployments for critical vulnerabilities.
  • Growth in the volume of unclassified sensitive data.
Why KRIs Matter

KRIs provide foresight, allowing organizations to identify and address underlying issues that could lead to non-compliance. They shift the focus from reactive problem-solving to proactive risk prevention.

Practical Application

Monitoring the 'number of failed authentication attempts to network devices' can signal a potential brute-force attack or weakness in access controls, prompting investigation before a breach occurs.
