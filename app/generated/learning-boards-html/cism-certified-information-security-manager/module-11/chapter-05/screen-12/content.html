Framework Spotlight

SOX: Financial Reporting and IT Controls

Mandating internal controls for publicly traded companies

The Sarbanes-Oxley Act (SOX) primarily impacts publicly traded US companies, focusing on financial reporting accuracy through mandated internal controls.

  • •Financial Reporting: Ensures accuracy and reliability.
  • •Internal Controls: Mandates robust controls over financial reporting.
  • •ITGCs: Often translates to stringent IT General Controls.
  • •Auditable: Requires controls to be auditable for verification.
Key Takeaway

SOX compliance requires CISMs to ensure that IT controls are meticulously designed, implemented, and monitored to support financial integrity and prevent fraud.

CISM's Role

Ensuring SOX Compliance

  • •Access Management: Control who can access financial systems.
  • •Change Management: Manage changes to financial applications and infrastructure.
  • •System Operations: Ensure reliability and availability of financial systems.
  • •Auditability: Ensure IT controls are documented and auditable.
Example: Implementing segregation of duties within financial systems to prevent a single individual from controlling an entire transaction.
Common Pitfall

Accounting-Centric View

A common pitfall is to view SOX as purely an accounting or finance issue, underestimating its significant implications for IT security and controls. Many internal controls over financial reporting are heavily reliant on the integrity and security of underlying IT systems, making ITGCs a critical component of SOX compliance.
