Defining the Audit Scope

Precisely Delineating What's In and What's Out

Included in Scope

  • Specific ERP systems and associated databases.
  • User provisioning processes for the finance department.
  • Data flows related to financial transactions.
  • Relevant internal policies and procedures.
  • Timeframe: Last 12 months of activity.

Excluded from Scope

  • Human Resources (HR) systems and data.
  • Physical security controls of data centers.
  • Third-party vendor systems not directly managed.
  • General IT infrastructure not supporting financial systems.
  • Historical data older than 12 months.
Supporting Explanation
  • Scope defines audit boundaries to prevent expansion.
  • It ensures focus on objectives and efficient resource use.
  • Collaboration with stakeholders is vital for realistic scope.
  • A detailed scope document avoids misunderstandings.
Common Mistake

Defining a scope that is either too broad, making the audit unmanageable, or too narrow, missing critical interconnected components.

Key Takeaway

A well-defined audit scope is essential for preventing scope creep, focusing efforts, and ensuring the audit is manageable and effective.
