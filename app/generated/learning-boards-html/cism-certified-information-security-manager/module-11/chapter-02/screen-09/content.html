Core Process

Engaging Stakeholders and Communicating Effectively

Proactive communication is key to a smooth and successful audit process.

The Communication Loop

1. Initiate Dialogue

Hold kickoff meetings with management to define objectives and scope.

2. Inform & Prepare

Notify department heads and provide audit plan/schedule.

3. Maintain Open Lines

Establish clear communication channels for requests and inquiries.

Key Takeaway

Proactive and transparent communication with all stakeholders minimizes resistance and ensures a collaborative audit environment.

Why it Matters

  • Manages expectations and reduces surprises.
  • Fosters cooperation and reduces resistance.
  • Ensures resource availability from audited departments.
  • Aligns audit activities with business operations.
  • Builds trust and credibility for the audit process.

Real-world Connection

Imagine an audit team showing up unannounced to IT. Without prior communication, IT might perceive it as an intrusion, leading to delays and uncooperative behavior. Proactive scheduling and clear objectives prevent this.

Common Misconception

Some believe communication is only needed at the end for findings. However, continuous dialogue from planning to reporting is essential for a constructive audit.
