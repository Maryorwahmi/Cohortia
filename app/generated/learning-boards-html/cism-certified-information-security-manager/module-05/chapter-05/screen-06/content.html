Guiding Your Security Posture with KRIs and KPIs
Understanding the two critical types of security indicators

Organizations rely on Key Risk Indicators (KRIs) and Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) to make monitoring effective. These metrics provide insights into risk exposure and control effectiveness.

Why it Matters

Using both KRIs and KPIs provides a holistic view of security, enabling both proactive risk mitigation and reactive performance assessment.

Common Mistake

Collecting too many metrics without a clear purpose leads to 'data overload' and a lack of actionable insights. Focus on meaningful, measurable metrics.

Key Takeaway

KRIs are forward-looking early warnings, while KPIs are backward-looking performance measures. Both are essential for a balanced security program.

KRI vs. KPI: A Comparison
Key Risk Indicator (KRI)

Provides early warning of increasing risk exposure.

  • Forward-looking
  • Predicts future problems
  • Example: Increase in failed login attempts
Key Performance Indicator (KPI)

Measures effectiveness and efficiency of controls.

  • Backward-looking
  • Indicates past performance
  • Example: Percentage of systems patched
