Risk Analysis

Understanding Likelihood and Impact

Quantifying potential threats to prioritize mitigation efforts.

Likelihood

The probability or frequency of a specific threat exploiting a vulnerability. It answers: How often is this likely to happen?

  • •Expressed qualitatively (e.g., Rare, Unlikely, Moderate, Likely, Frequent) or quantitatively (e.g., 1 in 100, 5% chance).
  • •Influenced by threat actor capabilities, existing controls, and system exposure.
Example

A CISM assesses the likelihood of a successful phishing attack as 'Moderate' due to recent employee training, but still a persistent threat.

Impact

The magnitude of harm or adverse effect that would result if the risk materializes. It answers: How bad would it be if this happened?

  • •Expressed qualitatively (e.g., Minor, Moderate, Major, Catastrophic) or quantitatively (e.g., $50,000, 3 days downtime).
  • •Includes financial, reputational, operational, and legal consequences.
Example

A CISM determines the impact of a database compromise to be 'Catastrophic' due to potential customer data theft, regulatory fines, and severe reputational damage.

Key Takeaway

Risk is a function of both likelihood and impact. A CISM must accurately assess both to prioritize risks effectively and make informed decisions about resource allocation for mitigation.
