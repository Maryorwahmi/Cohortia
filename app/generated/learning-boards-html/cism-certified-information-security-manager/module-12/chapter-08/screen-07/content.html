Risk Response Strategies

Selecting the optimal approach to manage identified information security risks.

Mitigate

Implement controls to reduce the likelihood or impact of a risk. This is the most common strategy.

Example

Deploying a Web Application Firewall (WAF) to protect against SQL injection attacks.

Accept

Acknowledge the risk and its potential impact, but take no further action, often due to low likelihood or high mitigation cost.

Example

Deciding not to patch a legacy system with a minor vulnerability if the cost of patching exceeds the potential impact.

Transfer

Shift the financial consequences or responsibility of a risk to a third party, such as through insurance or outsourcing.

Example

Purchasing cybersecurity insurance to cover potential financial losses from a data breach.

Avoid

Eliminate the activity or asset that gives rise to the risk. This is often the most drastic, but sometimes necessary, option.

Example

Decommissioning an outdated, insecure application that is no longer critical to business operations.

Key Takeaway

A CISM must strategically choose the most appropriate risk response, considering the organization's risk appetite, cost-benefit analysis, and overall business objectives, rather than defaulting to a single approach.
