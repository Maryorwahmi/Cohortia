Qualitative vs. Quantitative Risk Assessment

Choosing the right approach to evaluate risk likelihood and impact.

Qualitative Assessment

  • •Uses descriptive terms (e.g., High, Medium, Low).
  • •Based on expert judgment and experience.
  • •Quicker and less resource-intensive.
  • •Good for initial assessments and prioritization.
Example

A CISM rates the likelihood of a new insider threat as 'Medium' and its impact as 'High' based on recent incidents and internal discussions.

Quantitative Assessment

  • •Uses numerical values (e.g., $100,000, 5% probability).
  • •Requires extensive data and complex calculations.
  • •Provides objective, measurable results (e.g., ALE).
  • •Ideal for detailed financial impact analysis.
Example

A CISM calculates an Annualized Loss Expectancy (ALE) of $500,000 for a specific system outage, based on historical data and asset value.

Key Takeaway

Both qualitative and quantitative methods have their place. A CISM must understand when to apply each to effectively communicate risk and support decision-making, often using a blend of both.
