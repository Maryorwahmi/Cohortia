Avoiding Common Mistakes in Security Metrics

From Vanity Metrics to Data Overload and Integrity Issues

Vanity Metrics

Metrics that look good but don't provide actionable insights or contribute to strategic decision-making.

Example: Simply counting blocked attacks without analyzing their impact or nature.

Data Overload

Collecting too much data without proper analysis, making it difficult to identify trends or draw conclusions.

Example: Presenting raw SIEM logs to executives instead of aggregated insights.

Data Accuracy & Integrity

Flawed or inaccurate data leads to incorrect conclusions and poor decision-making. Ensure data sources are reliable.

Example: Inaccurate vulnerability scan results leading to misprioritized patching efforts.

Safety Notes

Ensure metrics collection and reporting do not inadvertently expose sensitive information or create new vulnerabilities.

Example: Restricting access to detailed vulnerability reports to authorized personnel only.

Key Takeaway

Avoid vanity metrics and data overload by focusing on accurate, actionable, and relevant metrics that genuinely contribute to security posture and business resilience.
