Module 7›Chapter 7.4›Recovery Time Objective (RTO)
Definition

What is RTO?

The maximum acceptable duration of time that a computer system, application, or network can be down after a disaster or disruption.

  • Determined by the Business Impact Analysis (BIA).
  • Must be equal to or less than the MTD.
  • Drives the choice of disaster recovery strategies.
Key Takeaway

RTO is a critical metric that defines the urgency of system restoration, directly impacting the selection and cost of DR solutions.

RTO in Practice

Aligning with Business Needs

High RTO (e.g., 24+ hours)

Less critical systems, lower cost DR solutions (e.g., backup & restore).

Low RTO (e.g., <4 hours)

Mission-critical systems, higher cost DR solutions (e.g., hot sites, replication).

Example: Online Banking Platform

If the RTO for an online banking platform is 2 hours, the DR plan must ensure all banking services are fully restored and accessible to customers within that timeframe, including all underlying IT systems and data.

Common Mistake

Setting an RTO that is technically impossible or prohibitively expensive to achieve for a given system, leading to unrealistic expectations and failed recovery efforts.
