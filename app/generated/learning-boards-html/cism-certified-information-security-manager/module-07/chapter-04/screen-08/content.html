Module 7›Chapter 7.4›Recovery Point Objective (RPO)
Definition

What is RPO?

The maximum tolerable amount of data that can be lost from an IT service due to a major incident.

  • Determined by the Business Impact Analysis (BIA).
  • Influences backup frequency and replication methods.
  • Directly impacts data integrity and business continuity.
Key Takeaway

RPO is crucial for data protection, dictating how frequently data must be saved to meet business tolerance for loss.

RPO in Practice

Balancing Loss Tolerance & Cost

Low RPO (e.g., 0-1 hour)

Mission-critical data, requires continuous replication or very frequent backups.

High RPO (e.g., 4-24 hours)

Less critical data, allows for less frequent backups.

Example: Financial Transaction Database

If the RPO for a financial transaction database is 15 minutes, the system must have a backup or replication strategy that ensures no more than 15 minutes of transaction data is lost in a disaster.

Common Mistake

Underestimating the true cost of data loss, leading to an RPO that is too high and results in unacceptable business impact during recovery.
