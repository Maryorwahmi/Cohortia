Module 4 › Chapter 4.1 › What is Realtime Lighting?
Definition

Realtime lighting explained

Computed every frame to support moving lights and dynamic scenes. Use when source or receiver movement matters.

Concrete example
A player carries a torch: attach a Point or Spot Light and let it update as the player moves. This keeps shadows and highlights aligned with the torch's position.
Common misconception
Believing all lights must be realtime. Many static lights are cheaper and look the same when baked.
Why it matters
Realtime lighting enables interaction and motion-driven effects but requires careful budgeting to avoid performance degradation.
Key Takeaway

Only use realtime lights where movement or interaction requires it; bake others for cheaper, stable lighting.

Contrast

Realtime vs Baked vs Probes

  • Realtime: dynamic, costly, per-frame updates.
  • Baked: precomputed, cheap at runtime, static only.
  • Light probes: blend baked indirect lighting for dynamic objects.
Pro tip
During iteration, reduce shadow resolution and shadow distance to speed up previewing changes.
