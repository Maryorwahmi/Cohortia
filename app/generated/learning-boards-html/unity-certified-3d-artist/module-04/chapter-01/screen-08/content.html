Module 4 › Chapter 4.1 › Area Lights
Purpose

Soft, surface-based illumination

Area lights mimic large emitters and produce diffuse, soft shadows ideal for interiors and studio lighting.

Concrete example
Use an area light to simulate sunlight through a wide window for softer, more realistic interior illumination when baking is acceptable.
Common misconception
Thinking area lights are the same as scaled point lights; they differ because their emitting surface alters shadow hardness and distribution.
Pipeline note
Area lights are typically baked or supported in HDRP; verify your render pipeline before relying on realtime area lights.
Key Takeaway

Prefer area lights for soft, high-fidelity interior lighting when your pipeline supports them; otherwise simulate with baked or emissive techniques.

Alternatives

Simulating area light effects

  • Baked area lights: high quality, static.
  • Emissive geometry: good for subtle ambient light when combined with probes.
  • Multiple low-intensity spots: approximate a soft, wide source in realtime with careful tuning.
Pro tip
If HDRP or baking isn't available, place emissive surfaces and bake indirect lighting to approximate area softness.
