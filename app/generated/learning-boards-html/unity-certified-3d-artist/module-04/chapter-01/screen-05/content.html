Module 4 › Chapter 4.1 › Directional Light
Core Detail

Parallel rays and global illumination

Use when you need a single, scene-wide directional source like sun or moon. Rotation controls the light angle and shadows.

Concrete example
Rotate the directional light to X:45, Y:-30 to suggest morning; tint the color with a warm orange for sunrise feel.
Common mistake
Adding multiple directional lights to simulate several suns. Use additional point or spot lights for local sources instead.
Performance note
Directional shadows affect large areas; reduce shadow distance and resolution when distant detail isn't needed.
Key Takeaway

One directional light for the main sun/moon, tuned for rotation, color, and appropriate shadow distance.

Visual Comparison
Directional
Global, rotation-based, ideal for outdoor light across entire scene.
Point
Local, position dependent, best for bulbs or lanterns.
Spot
Cone-shaped; good for flashlights and focused highlights.
