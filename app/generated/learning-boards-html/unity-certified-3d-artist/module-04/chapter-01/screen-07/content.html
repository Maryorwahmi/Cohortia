Core Terms

Spot Angle and Penumbra

Spot Angle defines the core cone; Penumbra Angle defines the outer soft falloff. Balance both to avoid unnatural edges.

Concrete example
A flashlight can use Spot Angle 30 and Penumbra 55 for a focused beam with a soft edge that looks natural in motion.
Common misconception
Using a tiny Spot Angle with zero penumbra produces a harsh beam that rarely appears natural in real-world lighting.
Performance note
Spot lights that cast shadows have a per-light cost—prefer fewer shadowed spots or lower shadow resolution for ambient spots.
Key Takeaway

Use spotlights for directed beams and tune penumbra for natural falloff; restrict shadow casting to critical spots.

Placement Scenario

Flashlight on a character

Attach a spot light to the player's camera. Set moderate Spot Angle and larger Penumbra to avoid a hard-edged beam while keeping a clear central highlight for navigation.

Check your understanding

Pro tip
Animate Penumbra slightly to simulate a handheld torch wobble for added realism without heavy cost.
