Overview

Key, Fill, Back — Purpose and Ratios

- Key: main direction and shadow, high intensity, defines form.
- Fill: softens shadows, lower intensity, often shadowless to avoid new shapes.
- Back: rim or edge highlight to separate subject from background.
Practical example values
Spot Light as Key — Color #FFFFDD, Intensity 1.5, Range 10, Spot Angle 40, Soft Shadows enabled.
Fill — Color #DDDDFF, Intensity 0.5, No Shadows, Range 15.
Back — Saturated rim color e.g., #00AAFF or #FF8800, Intensity 0.8, Spot Angle 30.
Common mistake
Using two equal-intensity lights flattens the scene. Keep clear intensity hierarchy.
Scaling to environments
Map roles: Directional Light = Key for exteriors; baked GI or ambient probes = Fill; localized spotlights, emissives, or area lights = Rim/highlights on architecture or props.
Key Takeaway

Use role-based lighting and intensity ratios rather than duplicating lights to preserve contrast and depth.
