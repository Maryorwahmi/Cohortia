Definition

Lighting as Visual Storytelling

Use color, intensity, and shadow shape to create mood, guide attention, and support narrative in a scene.

Analogy: Lights are brushes; shadows are negative space that define form and depth.
Misconception: Even lighting is not always better. Purposeful contrast improves readability and emotion.
Why it matters: Art-driven lighting supports player focus and reinforces gameplay intent.
Practical steps
Choose the emotion
Decide if scene should feel safe, tense, or mysterious.
Anchor with a key
Place a main light that defines direction and time of day.
Soften with fill
Use low-intensity, no-shadow fills to preserve detail.
Add focal highlights
Add spot or emissive lights to mark interactive objects.
Key Takeaway

Define the emotion you want, then use light intensity, color, and shadow to tell that story.

Try this
Dampen global ambient then raise a warm key to see how form and mood change.
