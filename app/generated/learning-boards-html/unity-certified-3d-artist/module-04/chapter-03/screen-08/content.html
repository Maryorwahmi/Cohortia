Module 4 › Chapter 4.3 › Reflection Probes
What they store

A Reflection Probe captures a cubemap — six square faces representing the surrounding environment — which reflective materials sample to produce reflections.

Types
Baked, Realtime, or Custom cubemaps depending on whether reflections must update dynamically.
Influence
Box Size and Offset define the influence volume where the probe's cubemap applies to objects.
Key Takeaway

Use baked probes for static interiors and realtime probes only for special dynamic reflections due to cost. Localize probes and use blending for smooth results.

Concrete example
Place a baked probe at room center with resolution 128–256, set box size to encompass the room, and bake. A metallic sphere in that room will then reflect the stored cubemap and show room features instead of a generic skybox.
Resolution
Higher resolution gives sharper reflections at a cost to texture memory.
Blend Distance
Smooths transitions when probes overlap so reflections change gradually.
Misconception
Realtime probes automatically fix everything. They are costly and do not fix parallax errors if used as a single large probe for a big area.
