Module 4 › Chapter 4.3 › What Light Probes Do
Definition

Light Probes are spatial sample points that store baked indirect lighting so dynamic objects can receive interpolated ambient illumination matching the environment.

Why they matter
Without probes, dynamic objects in a baked scene look flat because they lack the indirect light and color bounces that static objects show.
Concrete example
A player walking from a warm corridor into a cool room will inherit the corridor's warm tint near the doorway if probes are placed across the transition.
Common mistake
Placing probes inside geometry or too few probes over a transition causes popping or abrupt lighting changes on dynamic objects.
Key Takeaway

Light Probes are a low-cost way to carry baked indirect illumination to moving objects, but they require deliberate placement and rebaking to be effective.

Probe visualization & usage
A Light Probe Group starts as a small grid. You can select and move individual probes, duplicate them, and build coverage where dynamic objects travel. Remember to rebake lighting after adjustments so probes store updated samples.
Placement tip
Place probes at varying heights to capture vertical light differences when characters jump or climb.
Surface proximity
Place probes near colored walls or floors to capture color bounces that affect object tint.
Check your understanding
If a dynamic prop looks flat in a baked room, ask whether probes are present and whether the Mesh Renderer is set to Blend Probes.
