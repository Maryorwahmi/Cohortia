Module 4 › Chapter 4.5 › What is Post-Processing?
Definition
Full-screen effects applied after the main render pass; they alter the final image but not scene geometry.
Purpose
Enhance realism and artistic style, guide attention, and add photographic effects like bloom or grain.
Misconception
Post-processing cannot fix bad geometry or replace good lighting; it's a polish layer, not a corrective tool.
Key Takeaway

Use post-processing to unify visual style and draw focus, but ensure base assets and lighting are solid first.

Concrete analogy
Like a photographer adjusting exposure, contrast and color in editing software. You alter the frame-level look without changing the original scene composition.
Example
Apply a subtle color grading to warm highlights and cool shadows to create a cinematic contrast while keeping asset detail intact.
Check your understanding
Why wouldn't you use heavy DoF on a fast action camera? Consider gameplay readability and visual clarity.
