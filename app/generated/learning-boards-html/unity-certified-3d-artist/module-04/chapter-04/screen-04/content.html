Module 4 › Chapter 4.4 › Shadow Cascades
Definition

Shadow cascades divide the camera frustum into multiple ranges, assigning separate shadow map resolution to each range so near objects receive higher resolution shadows.

Key points
  • Use 2 or 4 cascades for most outdoor scenes.
  • Tune split distances to put the high-resolution cascade where the player spends time.
  • Balance between quality and draw call cost; more cascades increase overhead.
Key Takeaway

Cascades focus shadow detail near the camera to improve perceived quality without increasing global shadow resolution.

Practical example
Start with "No Cascades" and observe blocky close shadows. Switch to two cascades and see near shadows sharpen. Move to four cascades and use split values such as 0.1, 0.3, 0.6 to refine transitions. Watch for pop or seam artifacts at cascade boundaries and adjust splits to smooth them.
Common mistake

Using a single cascade across a wide view yields either blocky close shadows or wasted detail far away. Avoid default splits without testing movement through the scene.

Check your understanding

If near objects still look pixelated after enabling cascades, what should you inspect first? Consider split distances and the resolution allocated to the near cascade.
