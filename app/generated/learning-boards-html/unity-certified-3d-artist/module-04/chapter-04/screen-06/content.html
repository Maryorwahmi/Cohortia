Module 4 › Chapter 4.4 › Shadow Bias
What they do
  • Shadow Bias: shifts the shadow depth comparison to reduce self-shadowing.
  • Normal Bias: moves shadow sample along surface normal to reduce acne on detailed geometry.
  • Near Plane: determines closeness of caster to light frustum and can affect thin objects.
Practical tip

Start bias at small increments. If acne persists and geometry has normal maps, increase normal bias before raising shadow bias too far.

Example workout
  1. Create a tall thin cube and rotate the light to cast self-shadows.
  2. Note acne. Increase shadow bias in small steps until acne softens.
  3. If detachment appears, reduce shadow bias and increase normal bias instead.
  4. Test from multiple camera angles to ensure consistency.
Common mistake

Using very large bias values hides the problem but makes shadows lose contact with surfaces. Tune gradually.
