Module 6 › Chapter 6.6 › Frustum Culling
Definition

What is the camera frustum?

A pyramid-shaped view volume that determines which objects may be visible to the camera. Anything fully outside is culled.

  • Frustum culling is automatic and frame-cost low.
  • Partial overlap means the object is still rendered.
  • Does not remove objects hidden behind opaque geometry.
Key Takeaway

Frustum culling is the first-line, automatic check for visibility; use occlusion culling when geometry inside the frustum can be blocked.

Example
Imagine a hallway: when the camera points away, objects at its end leave the frustum and are skipped. This simple rule reduces draw calls for distant or off-camera objects.
Misconception: Frustum hides occluded objects — false.
Check: Rotate camera around scene to verify objects disappear only when fully out of view.
