Module 6 › Chapter 6.2 › What is Prop Dressing?
Definition & Purpose
Prop dressing arranges objects to imply history, activity, and ownership. Each prop should serve the scene's narrative and composition.
Metaphor: Think of props as short sentences that together tell the room's story.
Misconception: Avoid 'more is better' — intentional choice beats random clutter.
Why it matters: Proper dressing increases immersion and directs player attention.
Concrete example
Medieval market corner: stacked crates, spilled grain sacks, a knocked-over barrel, and a broom by a stall suggest recent activity and commerce.
Example Card
Clustered crates create believable focal points. Group 3–5 items with varied scale and rotation, then duplicate the cluster rather than single items.
Key Takeaway
Every prop should answer 'why is this here?'. Use clusters and small variations to imply use without overwhelming performance.
