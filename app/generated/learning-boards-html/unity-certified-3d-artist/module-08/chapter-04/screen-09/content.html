Occlusion Problems & Fixes

Decide whether occlusion should occur and implement rules to keep critical UI visible.

Detect occlusion
Perform a raycast from camera to UI; if obstructed, consider moving the element or toggling visibility.
Repositioning
Shift the Canvas slightly toward the camera or around the obstacle to restore visibility while preserving world relation.
Always-on-top fallback
Use a shader or render queue to draw critical UI on top; use sparingly because it removes depth cues.
Scenario
A pickup prompt behind a crate will be invisible. Raycast detection triggers a small outward offset or a quick fade and reposition to keep it readable without teleporting it away from the object.
Misconception
Assuming occlusion always improves realism. If players miss critical info, immersion harms gameplay; design for clarity first.
Key Takeaway

Treat occlusion as a design choice and provide visibility fallbacks for essential feedback.
