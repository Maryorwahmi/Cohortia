Module 4 › Chapter 3.5 › Light Types Comparison
Light type behaviors
Compare how each light affects highlights, falloff, and shadows on PBR materials.
Directional
Parallel rays, consistent shadows, good for sun/moon. Produces broad highlights on roughness contrasts.
Point
Omnidirectional falloff, bright near source, sharp speculars on smooth metals.
Spot
Cone-shaped, useful for focused illumination with directional falloff and penumbra control.
Concrete example and practice
A smooth metallic sphere shows a crisp bright dot under a Point light, while a rough non-metallic sphere reads as soft under the same light. Use this difference to sell material type.
Practice
  • Place a neutral PBR sphere and add a Point light. Observe highlight size and intensity.
  • Increase smoothness and note the highlight narrowing and brightening.
  • Swap to a Directional light and compare shadow direction and global contrast.
Common misconception
Not all lights are interchangeable: using a directional light to mimic a nearby lamp will flatten falloff and break believable proximity cues.
Key Takeaway

Match light type to physical source: choose Directional for sun, Point for bulbs/embers, and Spot for cones; combine them with material properties to craft believable highlights and shadows.
