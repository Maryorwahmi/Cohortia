Core Concept

Metallic and Smoothness

Fundamental controls over reflectivity and the look of highlights on a PBR surface.

Metallic
High values make surfaces act like metal; reflections carry tint and specular dominates.
Smoothness
Controls microfacet roughness. High Smoothness = sharp reflections; low = blurred reflections.
Use maps for wear
Assign Metallic and Smoothness maps to vary reflectivity and roughness across the surface to simulate wear and material transitions.
Example
A scraped metal band on a barrel uses a Metallic map to show metal areas and a Smoothness map to show polished versus rusty rough spots.
Misconception
Do not treat Smoothness as purely decorative; it changes the math that shapes reflection lobes in the shader.
Key Takeaway

Model both the metallicness and micro-surface roughness to achieve believable reflective behavior; use maps for spatial detail.

Visual examples
Polished metal
High Metallic, high Smoothness — mirror-like reflections that pick up environment color.
Rough painted surface
Low Metallic, low Smoothness — diffuse color dominates, reflections are soft and scattered.
