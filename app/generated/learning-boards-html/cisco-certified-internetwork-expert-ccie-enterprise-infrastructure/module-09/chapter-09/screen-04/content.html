Module 10 › Chapter 9.9 › Comprehensive Network Assurance Strategy
Definition
What a comprehensive strategy is
A plan that aligns critical assets to KPIs, employs layered telemetry, and uses a centralized platform to correlate and act on data at scale.
Layered Telemetry
  • Device health: CPU, memory, interface status via SNMPv3 and streaming telemetry.
  • Events: syslog for critical errors and changes.
  • Traffic patterns: NetFlow/IPFIX for top talkers and application usage.
  • End-to-end: IP SLA for latency, jitter, and loss.
Architectural Principles
Centralized ingestion, scalable storage, redundancy for collectors and analytics, and retention aligned with compliance needs.
Key Takeaway

Map telemetry to business KPIs, combine multiple data sources, and centralize for correlation and scale to enable reliable assurance.

Example: Prioritizing Assets
Label a payment processing API as critical. Set SLO: response time <200ms during business hours. Ensure IP SLA probes and application health metrics are applied to paths serving this API.
Common Mistake
Relying on a single telemetry source. For example, low link utilization alone doesn’t rule out routing instability or intermittent errors.
Why it matters
When monitoring is aligned to business outcomes, incidents are detected earlier and prioritized correctly, reducing impact and operational noise.
