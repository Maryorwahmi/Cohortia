Module 11 › Chapter 10.10 › Why Incident Management
Definition & Contrast

Incident management is a set of coordinated phases that include preparation, identification, containment, eradication, recovery, and post-incident analysis. Troubleshooting is an essential skill inside identification and eradication, but on its own it doesn't guarantee prevention.

  • Scope: Troubleshooting fixes symptoms; incident management fixes systems.
  • Timeframe: Troubleshooting is immediate; incident management includes pre- and post-event work.
  • Outcome: Troubleshooting restores service; incident management prevents recurrence.
Concrete Example
Adjusting a queue to relieve congestion is troubleshooting. Documenting missing monitoring and adding an alert plus a capacity plan is incident management.
Key Takeaway

Treat each outage as both a service event and a source of systemic insight. Fast fixes plus follow-up actions reduce future outages.

Supporting Cards
Common Mistake
Skipping post-incident work because the service is restored. This delays prevention steps.
Pro Tip
Document the timeline during the incident; contemporaneous notes greatly improve RCA accuracy.
Check Your Understanding
Name one systemic change you could propose after your last outage and how you'd verify it worked.
