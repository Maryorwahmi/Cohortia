Definition
Congestion and queue buildup
When incoming traffic exceeds link capacity, packets buffer and may be dropped when buffers are full.
  • • Packet loss from tail drop causes retransmits and throughput degradation.
  • • Latency and jitter increase for real-time traffic as packets wait in queues.
  • • TCP reacts to drops; UDP does not — different implications for mitigation.
Analogy
An oversubscribed highway on-ramp where excess cars are forced to wait or be turned away.
Observables and evidence
Show commands such as show policy-map interface GigabitEthernet0/0 output reveal queue depths and drops.
Look for rising queue depth and increasing drops; correlate with user complaints of jitter or slowness.
Misconception: zero packet loss doesn't mean no congestion — queueing delay may be high even without drops.
Key Takeaway

Congestion affects throughput and timing; successful mitigation requires both scheduling and avoidance techniques.
