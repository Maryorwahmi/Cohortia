Definition

Security Group Tags (SGTs)

An SGT is a 16-bit identity label assigned by ISE after successful authentication. It travels with traffic across TrustSec-enabled devices and is used for enforcement decisions.

Analogy

Think of SGTs like access badges: the badge shows group affiliation, and doors enforce who can pass.

Concrete example

When ISE labels a user as "Employees," that traffic is allowed per SGACL to reach specific application servers without depending on the user's IP address.

Common misconception

TrustSec does not eliminate the need for endpoint security or firewalls; it complements them by simplifying segmentation inside the fabric.

SGT Flow
Step-by-step
  1. Client authenticates via 802.1X to the switch.
  2. Switch forwards credentials to ISE for authentication.
  3. ISE assigns an SGT and sends it back to the switch.
  4. Traffic is tagged and SGACLs are enforced at policy points.
Key Takeaway

SGTs let you define policies by role, reducing the operational cost of IP-based ACLs.
