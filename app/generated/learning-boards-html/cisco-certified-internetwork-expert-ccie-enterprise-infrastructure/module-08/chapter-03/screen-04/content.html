Module 08 › Chapter 8.3 › What the Control Plane Does
Definition

Control plane responsibilities

The control plane handles routing, ARP, ICMP, AAA, and management traffic destined to the device itself. Because these consume CPU, they must be protected from floods and malicious packets.

Common control-plane flows
  • Routing protocol exchanges (OSPF, BGP): topology updates and hellos.
  • AAA and management protocols (SSH, SNMP): authentication and monitoring.
  • ICMP and ARP: diagnostics and neighbor resolution.
Misconception
Assuming control-plane traffic is always low volume. During convergence, legitimate traffic can spike and must be allowed sufficient rate to keep adjacencies alive.
Analogy & Example

Think of the control plane as a switchboard operator inside the device. If the operator is overwhelmed, calls fail even if the external wires are intact. For example, if a router under attack cannot process OSPF LSAs, routing adjacencies drop and packets are blackholed.

Key takeaway
Identify critical control-plane traffic before applying policies and reserve headroom for bursts during convergence.
