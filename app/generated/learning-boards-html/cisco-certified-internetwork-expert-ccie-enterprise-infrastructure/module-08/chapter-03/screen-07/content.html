Parameter details

CIR and burst explained

CIR is the steady allowance. Burst (BC) permits short spikes. Exceed-action determines whether excess packets are dropped.

Example from config
SSH policed to 8000 bps with bc 8000; OSPF policed to 16000 bps with bc 16000.
Operational risk
Setting CIR or BC too low can drop LSAs or update messages, breaking routing adjacencies during convergence.
Visual comparison
Steady-state (CIR)
Defines the long-term permitted rate in bits per second.
Burst (BC)
Allows short bursts above CIR so legitimate transient traffic isn’t dropped.
Practical tip
Monitor counters after deployment and increase CIR or BC for classes showing legitimate drops during spikes.
