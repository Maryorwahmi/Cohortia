Module 2 › Chapter 2.2 › What are Operators and Expressions?
Definition
Operator: a special symbol that acts on operands. Expression: operators plus operands that evaluate to a single value.
Example: int r = (a + b) * c; Here the sum is computed, then multiplied.
Analogy: verbs and nouns. Operators are verbs that change or relate values.
Common misconception
Relational results are boolean. In C they are integer 1 or 0 and can participate in arithmetic.
Key Takeaway

Operators give actions to operands; expressions are evaluated with type and precedence rules to produce values.

Annotated example
Expression: (5 + 3) * 2
Step 1: Parentheses force 5 + 3 = 8.
Step 2: Multiply 8 * 2 = 16. Parentheses override normal precedence.
