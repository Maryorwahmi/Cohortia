Module 7 › Chapter 7.3 › Workspace vs C-space
Definition

Workspace and Configuration Space

Two representations: physical positions the robot can reach, versus the set of joint angles that parameterize its pose.

Concrete example

For a 6-DOF arm, workspace is a 3D volume of reachable end-effector poses; C-space is 6-dimensional, one axis per joint angle.

Misconception

Planning only in workspace may miss link collisions. Always check full body motion by mapping to C-space.

Key Takeaway

Plan in C-space for correctness; use workspace for intuitive visualization and goal specification.

Visual comparison
Workspace

3D positions and orientations the end-effector can reach; obstacles appear as forbidden volumes.

Configuration Space

High-dimensional joint-angle space where collision constraints become C-obstacles for planner search.

Why it matters

Planners that search C-space correctly capture self-collisions and swept-volume constraints; workspace-only planners risk unsafe motions.
