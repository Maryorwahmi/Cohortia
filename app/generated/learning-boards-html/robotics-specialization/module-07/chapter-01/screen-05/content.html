Module 7 › Chapter 7.1 › Nonlinear dynamics
Key equations (verbal)
  • Wheel equation: inertia plus pendulum coupling equals applied force.
  • Pendulum equation: inertia and gravity balance acceleration coupling and applied torque effects.
  • Nonlinear terms: sin(θ), cos(θ), and θ̇² create angle-dependent behaviors.
Concrete scenario

A sudden forward torque when θ is positive produces larger forward acceleration than at small θ because cos(θ) scales the coupling terms.

Simulation advice
  • Implement the full nonlinear function for ode45 to observe realistic behavior.
  • Use small time steps and check for energy growth that signals numerical issues.
  • Keep initial tilt small when testing linear controllers.
Misconception

Treating θ̇² as negligible for all conditions. It can dominate at large rotation rates and change stability.

Why this matters

Nonlinear simulation reveals the true recovery region and helps you choose safe initial conditions and gain ranges.

Example implementation

Code the two differential equations directly in your dynamics function for ode45 and test different initial angles.

Key takeaway

Use the nonlinear model for simulation fidelity; reserve linear models for controller synthesis around small angles.
