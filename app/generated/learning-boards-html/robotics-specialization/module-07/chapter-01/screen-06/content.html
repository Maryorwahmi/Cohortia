Linearization steps
  1. Set θ = 0 and θ̇ = 0 at equilibrium.
  2. Approximate sin(θ) ≈ θ and cos(θ) ≈ 1 for small θ.
  3. Neglect θ̇² terms near equilibrium.
  4. Solve algebraically to write ẍ and θ̈ as linear functions of states and input F.
  5. Assemble A and B matrices for ẋ = A x + B u.
Misconception

Linearization guarantees local validity only. Always test the linear controller on the nonlinear model to confirm stability margins.

What A and B reveal
  • A shows how states influence derivatives; cross-terms indicate coupling between x and θ.
  • B shows how motor force affects accelerations based on system masses and geometry.
Key takeaway

Compute A and B to design state-feedback or LQR controls; then verify with nonlinear simulation to ensure the controller works under realistic conditions.
