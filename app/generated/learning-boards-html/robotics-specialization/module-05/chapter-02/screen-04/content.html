Module 5 › Chapter 5.2 › What is a feature?
Feature properties
  • Repeatability: Detected across viewpoints and lighting.
  • Distinctiveness: Descriptor uniquely identifies the region.
  • Locality: Small support resistant to occlusion.
  • Quantity: Enough reliable points to estimate transforms.
Example
A corner on a patterned rug is repeatable and distinctive across small rotations and scale changes.
Non-example
A smooth white wall yields few or no useful features because local patches look identical.
Key Takeaway

Good features are local, distinct, repeatable, and available in sufficient quantity for robust matching.

Common misconceptions
  • More keypoints is not always better; many weak points create false matches.
  • A detector alone is insufficient; you need a distinctive descriptor for reliable matching.
Why this matters
Robots rely on consistent matches to compute motion. Poor features lead to drift or wrong loop closures.
