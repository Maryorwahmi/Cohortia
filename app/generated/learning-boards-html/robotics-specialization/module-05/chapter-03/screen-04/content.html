Module 6 › Chapter 5.3 › Epipolar Geometry
Definition

Given two camera centers and a 3D point, the epipolar plane intersects each image plane in an epipolar line. Corresponding image points lie on these paired lines.

Concrete example

If a corner is located at pixel coordinates in image one, compute the corresponding epipolar line using the fundamental matrix and restrict matching to that line. This reduces false correspondences and speeds up RANSAC.

Key Takeaway

Epipolar constraints convert a 2D search into a 1D search, making matching and geometric estimation tractable.

Annotated concept cards
Epipolar plane
Plane through two camera centers and the 3D point.
Epipolar line
Intersection of epipolar plane with each image; matches must lie here.
Epipole
Projection of the other camera center; all epipolar lines pass through it.
Misconception
Epipolar lines are not image axes; their orientation encodes camera pose differences.
Check your understanding
If you have a perfect essential matrix and a point in image one, what geometric locus contains its match in image two? Answer: the corresponding epipolar line. Why does this help matching? It reduces dimensionality of search and supports robust estimation with RANSAC.
