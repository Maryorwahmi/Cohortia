Module 6 › Chapter 5.3 › Scale Ambiguity
Why scale is ambiguous

Multiplying translation by any nonzero scalar while compensating by scaling 3D points produces identical image projections, so magnitude of t is not determined by images alone.

How to recover scale

Introduce metric cues: a known object size, stereo baseline, inertial measurements, or GPS fixes to anchor the reconstruction in absolute units.

Design implication

If your system must report metric distances, include a method to resolve scale — otherwise treat reconstructions as relative geometry only.

Concrete example
Two images of a statue yield a reconstruction that could equally represent a tabletop model or the full statue; a ruler or known camera baseline resolves this ambiguity.
Misconception

More images alone do not remove the scale ambiguity in a purely monocular system.
