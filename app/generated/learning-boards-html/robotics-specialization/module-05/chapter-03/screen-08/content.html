Module 6 › Chapter 5.3 › Triangulation
Concept

Find the 3D point P whose projections best match observed image points given camera poses. Solve a small linear system per point or use midpoint least squares for two views.

Quality factors
Baseline magnitude, image noise, and pose accuracy determine triangulation precision.
Reprojection error
Measure of how well the triangulated point projects back to observed image locations.
Procedure
Form projection matrices for both cameras, set up linear equations from image coordinates, solve for P using SVD or least squares.
Pitfall
Using inaccurate camera poses yields biased 3D points; this is why refinement is necessary.
Concrete scenario
Triangulate ORB matches from two images of a small object. Observe that points reconstructed from a narrow baseline appear noisy and elongated along depth.
Key Takeaway

Triangulation converts 2D correspondences into 3D points, but its accuracy depends on baseline and pose correctness; refinement is required for high quality.
