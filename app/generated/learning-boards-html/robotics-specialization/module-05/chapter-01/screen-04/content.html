Module 5 › Chapter 5.1 › Perception
Definition
A camera maps rays from 3D world points to a 2D pixel grid. This process is geometric and described by projection equations and matrices used in robotics.
Concrete example
A mobile robot estimates a door's distance by projecting door corner coordinates into image pixels and solving for depth using known focal length.
Misconception
A single camera does not measure depth without geometry. Depth must be inferred from scale, stereo, or motion.
Key Takeaway

Treat cameras as calibrated geometric instruments to convert pixels into spatial information for perception tasks.

Analogy
Think of a camera as a window with a fixed magnifying glass: the view is rich but you must know the glass properties to measure sizes and distances behind it.
Why this fills the robot's needs
  • Rich visual features for recognition and localization.
  • Geometry lets you turn pixels into metric estimates with calibration.
  • Calibration and undistortion remove systematic biases for accurate mapping.
