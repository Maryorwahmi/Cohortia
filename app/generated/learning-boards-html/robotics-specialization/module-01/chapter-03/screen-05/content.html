Definition

Gaussian distribution

A bell-shaped distribution fully specified by mean μ and standard deviation σ; commonly used for sensor noise modeling.

Formula
p(x) = (1 / (σ * sqrt(2π))) * exp(- (x - μ).^2 / (2 * σ.^2))
Use this to compute densities; for decision thresholds you convert σ into intervals instead of reading raw density values.
Confidence
Approximately 95% of samples fall within ±2σ. Use this to set conservative bounds for planning or safety checks.
Key Takeaway

Gaussians let you summarize uncertainty with two numbers and compute the effects of adding independent noise.

Practical example
Suppose a sensor reports μ=10, σ=2. Use σ to produce a 95% interval and to reason about how confident the robot should be before taking an action.
% Compute 95% interval from mean and sigma
mu = 10;
sigma = 2;
ci_low = mu - 2*sigma;
ci_high = mu + 2*sigma;
fprintf('95%% interval: %.1f to %.1f
', ci_low, ci_high);
Misconception check: σ is not a hard bound. Outliers occur more frequently when noise is non-Gaussian.
