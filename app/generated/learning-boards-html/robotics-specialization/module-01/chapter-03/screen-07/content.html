Process Flow

Covariance → Ellipse → Insight

Translate Σ into a geometric shape you can reason about for safety margins and planning decisions.

Step 1: sample
Draw many samples from the multivariate Gaussian to empirically see spread and orientation.
Step 2: compute ellipse
Use eigen-decomposition on Σ to get axis lengths (sqrt eigenvalues) and rotation (eigenvectors).
Step 3: reason
Use the ellipse to determine safe corridors and to detect directions where uncertainty could undermine a maneuver.
Key Takeaway

Visualization via ellipses turns abstract covariance numbers into actionable geometric insight for planning.

Concrete scenario
If Σ = [4,2;2,4], the ellipse is tilted and large along the diagonal. The robot should avoid trajectories aligned with that diagonal unless uncertainty is reduced.
How to compute ellipse (conceptual)
  1. Compute eigenvalues λ1, λ2 and eigenvectors v1, v2 of Σ.
  2. Ellipse axes lengths are sqrt(λ1) and sqrt(λ2) scaled to desired confidence.
  3. Rotate the ellipse by the eigenvector directions to match covariance orientation.
Misconception check: diagonal variances alone do not capture the full uncertainty shape when off-diagonal covariances are non-zero.
