Comparison

Properties that help

These properties reduce algebraic complexity when combining sensors and propagating uncertainty.

Sum of Gaussians
Independent Gaussian noises add to produce a Gaussian with summed variances. This simplifies fusion math for independent errors.
Linear transform
A linear mapping of a Gaussian yields another Gaussian; propagate μ and Σ using linear operations instead of sampling.
Compactness & entropy
Mean and covariance fully describe the distribution, and Gaussian is the maximum-entropy choice given those moments.
Key Takeaway

Use Gaussians when noise is well-modeled as Gaussian; otherwise consider robust or sampling-based alternatives.

Practical contrast
If a camera returns occasional large outliers or a detection algorithm produces multiple modes, the Gaussian assumption fails and you should not rely solely on Gaussian filters.
When good
Small, symmetric noise; linear approximations valid; many independent small errors.
When not
Outliers, multimodal errors, non-linear measurement models without linearization.
Misconception check: closure properties ease math, but they do not justify using Gaussians for every sensor—validate assumptions with data.
