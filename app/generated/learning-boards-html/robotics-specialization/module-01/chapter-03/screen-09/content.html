Core Concept

Bayesian inference

Combine prior and likelihood to compute a posterior belief after receiving a measurement.

Bayes' theorem
P(state | measurement) = P(measurement | state) * P(state) / P(measurement)
Terms
Prior: what you believed. Likelihood: measurement model. Posterior: updated belief. Evidence: normalization constant.
Key Takeaway

Bayesian inference provides a principled way to combine prior knowledge with sensor data to refine state estimates.

Concrete walkthrough
Prior: robot near 10 ± 3. Measurement: 12 ± 2. The posterior balances them; because the measurement has smaller variance, it pulls the posterior closer to 12 while reducing overall uncertainty.
Misconception check
Don't skip normalization when comparing different hypotheses. The evidence makes different posteriors comparable as proper probability distributions.
