Algorithms and architecture

Bayesian thinking appears in Kalman filters, particle filters, and Bayesian networks; choose algorithms based on noise and model properties.

Kalman filters
Analytic mean and covariance propagation for linear-Gaussian systems; very efficient and common in robotics.
Particle filters
Represent arbitrary distributions with weighted samples, useful when distributions are non-Gaussian or models are non-linear.
Bayesian networks
Structure dependencies explicitly for complex systems and perform structured probabilistic inference.
Key Takeaway

Estimator choice should be guided by model fidelity, noise shape, and computational constraints — validate with data before deployment.

Practical checklist
  • Validate sensor noise distribution with collected samples.
  • Preserve covariances during coordinate transforms and sensor alignment.
  • Use gating or outlier rejection when sensors occasionally fail.
Example: GPS+IMU+odometry fusion often uses Kalman or extended Kalman filters because IMU and odometry errors are approximately Gaussian after preprocessing; if that is false, consider particle approaches.
