Module 4 › Chapter 3.3 › Reactive Control
Core Concept

Fast local reactions

Potential fields compute forces from simple formulas, enabling millisecond-level responses to moving obstacles.

Why speed matters
Dynamic obstacles can appear suddenly; a local force lets the robot avoid without full replanning.
When it's insufficient
Local-only methods can trap the robot in a non-goal equilibrium — a local minimum.
Key Takeaway

Use potential fields for low-latency avoidance; pair with a global planner when you need guaranteed arrival.

Concrete example
Imagine a delivery robot in a crowded corridor. A person steps into the corridor and slows. A potential field controller immediately generates a repulsive force from the person and an attractive force toward the goal; the resulting net force steers the robot around the person in one control cycle. No global recomputation is necessary for this reactive correction.
Common misconception
Because potential fields avoid obstacles quickly, some assume they always reach the goal. In fact, without a global view, the robot can be trapped. Always test for local minima and consider hybridization.
