Module 4 › Chapter 3.3 › Repulsive Potential
Definition

Short-range inverse potential

U_rep(q) = 0.5*K_rep*(1/d(q) - 1/d0)^2 if d(q) ≤ d0; otherwise 0.
Repulsive force
F_rep(q) = K_rep*(1/d - 1/d0)*(1/d^2)*∇d(q), grows rapidly as d decreases.
Design knobs
K_rep controls strength; d0 controls how far obstacles influence motion.
Key Takeaway

Repulsion is short-range and highly nonlinear; tune influence distance to avoid unnecessary slowing or last-moment reactions.

Concrete example
Suppose an obstacle center yields a distance d of 0.5 meters and d0 equals 3 meters. The repulsive term is active and grows quickly as d approaches zero, producing a strong push away from the obstacle. If d increases past 3 meters the term disappears and the obstacle no longer affects motion.
Misconception check: Treating repulsion as globally active confuses behavior; repulsion is usually limited to an influence radius, so distant obstacles should not slow global progress.
