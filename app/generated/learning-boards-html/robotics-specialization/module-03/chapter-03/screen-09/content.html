Module 4 › Chapter 3.3 › Local Minima
Core Concept

When forces cancel

Local minima are positions where the net potential gradient vanishes but the position is not the goal.

Classic trap
Two symmetric obstacles between start and goal can create a stable equilibrium where repulsions cancel the attraction.
Detection
If the robot's position stalls for several iterations, suspect a local minimum and log nearby force vectors to confirm.
Misconception

Noise always fixes minima. It can help, but may be unsafe or inefficient in cluttered spaces; prefer principled escapes or hybrid planners when traps are frequent.

Concrete scenario
Place two circular obstacles symmetrically between (0,0) and (10,10). The robot moving under the potential field may stop halfway because the repulsive forces balance the attractive pull. This is the hands-on example to reproduce in MATLAB.
Reflection: Is the trap caused by parameter choice or scene topology? If parameter tuning fails repeatedly, use a global planner to provide a path that avoids the trap.
