Heuristics at a glance
Each heuristic encodes assumptions about allowed motion and cost structure.
Euclidean
Straight-line distance; admissible and effective for diagonal and continuous moves.
Manhattan
Sum of axis differences; tight for 4-connected grids with axis-aligned moves.
Zero
No guidance; A* reduces to Dijkstra. Safe but may explore broadly.
Key Takeaway

Match your heuristic to the motion model; tighter admissible heuristics usually reduce exploration while preserving optimality.

Concrete example
On a 4-connected grid: Manhattan distance often yields fewer explored nodes than Euclidean because it better matches allowed moves. On an 8-connected grid, Euclidean typically performs better.
Measure explored-node counts in your experiments to confirm which heuristic is best for your map and connectivity. Practical tuning and measurement beat guesswork.
