Algorithmic bounds
Relate V and E on grids to expected time and memory.
• BFS: time O(V + E), space O(V). Works for unweighted graphs.
• Dijkstra: time O((V + E) log V) with a heap, space O(V). Handles weights.
• A*: worst-case O((V + E) log V) but often far fewer expansions with a good heuristic.
Key Takeaway

Estimate V and E from grid resolution before choosing a planner. For many mobile robot tasks, A* with an appropriate heuristic is the practical sweet spot.

Grid example
50×50 grid: V = 2,500. With 4-connected neighbors, E ≈ 10,000. These sizes are trivial for modern machines, but grids that represent high-dimensional manipulators become infeasible quickly.
If memory is limited, consider coarser discretization, hierarchical planning, or sampling-based planners that avoid full grid enumeration.
