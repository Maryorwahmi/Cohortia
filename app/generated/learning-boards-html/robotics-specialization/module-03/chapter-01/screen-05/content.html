Module 3 › Chapter 3.1 › C-obstacles & Minkowski
From workspace to C-obstacles
Apply Minkowski expansion or swept-volume reasoning to mark forbidden configurations for the robot point.
Minkowski sum (circular robot)
Expand each workspace obstacle by the robot radius. The robot reduces to a point and must avoid expanded areas.
Swept volume (rotating/polygonal)
Union the workspace area occupied by the robot across relevant orientations or motions to mark forbidden configurations.
Common mistake
Assuming obstacle expansion is negligible. For non-circular robots orientation changes the shape of C-obstacles significantly.
Key Takeaway

Compute C-obstacles conservatively when exact geometry is expensive; correctness is more important than tightness for safety.

Illustrative steps
  1. Model robot shape (disk, rectangle, polygon).
  2. If disk: inflate obstacles by radius (Minkowski).
  3. If polygonal/rotating: compute sweeps over orientation samples or conservative envelope.
  4. Mark grid cells or configurations whose robot shape intersects any workspace obstacle.
These steps produce a binary occupancy indication in C-space used to build graphs or to reject sampled configurations during planning.
