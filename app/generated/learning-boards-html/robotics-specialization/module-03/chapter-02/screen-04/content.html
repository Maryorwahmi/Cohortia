Module 3 › Chapter 3.2 › Why sampling is necessary
Definition
Curse of dimensionality
Grid-based search grows exponentially as degrees of freedom increase. Sampling avoids this by selecting representative configurations rather than enumerating every discrete cell.
Concrete example
Ten discretizations per joint: 6 joints → 10^6 nodes. Sampling focuses effort on reachable, collision-free regions instead.
Common misconception
Random sampling is not unreliable — probabilistic completeness means more samples increase the chance of success when a path exists.
Key takeaway
Sampling scales to high-dimensional C-spaces by trading completeness for probabilistic coverage and tractability.
Analogy
Exploring a city by walking samples
Rather than mapping every street block, you walk to representative points and check whether each is reachable from others. Over time these visits reveal the connected streets without mapping every corner.
Why it matters
  • Enables planning for manipulators and humanoids with many joints.
  • Reduces computation and memory compared to exponential grids.
  • Makes online planning possible in high-dimensional tasks.
