Sampling-Based Motion Planning
Use random samples to build sparse graphs or trees that reveal connectivity in high-dimensional C-spaces.
Why this matters
High degrees of freedom make grid search infeasible; sampling gives tractable planning that still finds paths with high probability.
Two families
Roadmaps for multi-query (PRM) and trees for single-query or changing environments (RRT, RRT*).
In this lesson
PRM, RRT, RRT* and practical trade-offs for planners in high-dimensional spaces.
Explore Key Topics
PRM Construction
Sampling, neighbor search, and collision checks to build an offline roadmap.

Local rewiring and optimal parent selection for improving path cost.

Visual summary
PRM
Cloud of samples
Samples are connected to k neighbors. Construction is offline; queries are fast once the roadmap exists.
RRT
Tree that grows
Tree extends toward random samples. Good for single queries and dynamic environments but may produce jagged paths.
RRT*
Optimizing tree
After each insertion the tree may rewire to reduce cost. With many samples the path approaches optimal.
When to choose
Trade-offs
Choose PRM for many queries in static maps, RRT for single-query speed, and RRT* when path quality matters.
