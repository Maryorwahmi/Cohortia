Module 3 › Chapter 3.2 › Collision Checking and Validity
Why it matters
Collision tests dominate runtime
Every sample and every candidate edge requires a validity check. For manipulators this means checking many degrees-of-freedom across geometry, and naive checks become the bottleneck.
Optimization
Use bounding volumes, spatial hashing, and lazy collision checks to reduce expensive geometry tests.
Lazy checking
Defer detailed checks until a path candidate is found. This amortizes cost when many edges are never used.
Concrete scenario
A PRM for a manipulator near clutter requires thousands of segment checks. Pre-filtering with coarse collision geometry and then running precise checks only on promising edges speeds construction significantly.
Misconception
Believing collision checks cannot be optimized leads to slow planners. Proper spatial structures and lazy strategies are effective.
Key Takeaway

Count and reduce collision checks: choosing a planner means understanding its collision-check profile and optimizing accordingly.
