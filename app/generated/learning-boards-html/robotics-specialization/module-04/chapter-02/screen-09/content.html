Definition
Series Elastic Actuators (SEAs)
Motor — spring — load. Force relates to spring deflection via F = k·Δx.
Core relation
F = k * Δx
% where k is spring stiffness and Δx is spring deflection
Force sensing: measure Δx to estimate transmitted force without a dedicated force sensor.
Shock tolerance: spring compresses under impact, protecting motor and gearbox.
Energy storage: spring stores elastic energy during compression and releases it on extension.
Key Takeaway

SEAs provide sensing, safety, and energy benefits for legged robots when paired with controllers that sense and compensate for spring deflection.

Concrete example
Robots that run use SEAs to capture elastic energy at touch-down and return it for push-off, lowering motor energy compared to fully rigid designs.
Misconception check
Claim: springs always hurt performance. Reality: with proper sensing and control, springs enable stable force control and energy savings.
Design prompt
Choose k so that the spring deflection at expected peak loads gives measurable Δx but does not induce unacceptable position error for the task.
