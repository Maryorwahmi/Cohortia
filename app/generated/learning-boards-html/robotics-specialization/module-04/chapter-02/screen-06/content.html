Definition
Fixed points, equilibria, and stability
Classify equilibria to predict how the system responds to small disturbances.
Stable vs unstable
Stable: nearby trajectories return. Unstable: small perturbations grow and move the system away.
Linearization caveat
Linear analysis informs local behavior but may miss effects of switching dynamics or global nonlinearities.
Concrete scenario
A leg that lands earlier may alter the equilibrium from flight to stance, changing the stability outcome; controllers must be robust to these events.
Key Takeaway

Use fixed-point analysis to guide controller design, but always validate with full gait-cycle tests including contacts.

Quick method
  1. Find state where derivatives are zero.
  2. Linearize dynamics around that state.
  3. Compute eigenvalues; negative real parts indicate local attraction.
Example: pendulum down position yields stable eigenvalues after adding damping.
Pitfall: eigenvalue stability during swing may not hold after touchdown because constraints change.
