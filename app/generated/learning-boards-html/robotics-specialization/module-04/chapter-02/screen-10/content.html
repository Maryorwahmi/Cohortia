Actuator selection: torque, speed, power, bandwidth
Compute joint requirements and compare actuator traits to meet performance and safety goals.
Torque
Must support weight and accelerate limbs. Example guideline in the chapter shows hip/knee torques of 50–100 N·m for humanoid-scale joints.
Speed & Power
Speed determines required angular velocity. Power = torque × angular velocity, so verify motor power at operating point, not only stall torque.
Bandwidth
Typical legged bandwidth is 10–20 Hz to reject disturbances and track trajectories; low bandwidth can destabilize balance control.
Comparison table (summary)
DC motor + gearbox
High torque, low speed, rigid, may have low bandwidth
Servo motor
Integrated control, convenient, often underpowered for large robots
SEA
Compliant, force sensing, energy storage, safer but requires spring tuning
Hydraulic
Very high power density, used where explosive force is required but noisy and complex
Pneumatic
Light and compliant, low power density and hard to control precisely
Design prompt
Calculate torque and speed for your joint, then compute power and confirm actuator bandwidth. Consider SEAs when safety and energy recovery are priorities.
