Core Concept
Why legged robots are nonlinear
Trigonometric coupling, contact switching, and velocity forces produce behavior that can't be treated as globally linear.
Defining features
  • Trigonometric terms from joint kinematics couple states nonlinearly.
  • Switching dynamics when contact changes equations of motion.
  • Velocity-dependent forces such as damping change behavior with speed.
Concrete example
A leg torque that works in swing may produce unexpected load in stance because contact alters the robot's effective boundary conditions.
Common misconception
Treating the whole gait as linear ignores switching events; linearization is local and must be validated across contacts and cycles.
Key Takeaway

Nonlinear analysis is essential for predicting gait-level behavior and for designing actuators and controllers that remain robust across contacts.

Layered explanation
Trigonometric coupling
Joint torques multiply with sine and cosine of angles, producing multiplicative, not additive, behaviors.
Switching contacts
Stance vs flight change constraints and the effective equations of motion for the robot.
Velocity-dependent forces
Damping and friction alter trajectories based on speed, producing spiral in or out behaviors in phase space.
Analogy
Think of a puppet with elastic joints and intermittent strings attaching and detaching — the motion depends on when attachments change.
