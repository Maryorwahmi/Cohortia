Core Concept
Limit cycles and stable periodic gaits
A limit cycle is a closed, isolated loop in phase space that represents sustained periodic motion.
Why controllers aim for limit cycles
  • They provide repeatability: the gait repeats indefinitely under nominal conditions.
  • Stable cycles attract nearby trajectories, enabling recovery from small perturbations.
  • Cycle stability depends on dynamics during stance and flight; contact modeling matters.
Concrete: a running gait with elastic tendons shows a closed energy cycle K→P→E→K and an attracting loop in phase space.
Misconception: a periodic-looking trajectory in one slice of state space may not be a true limit cycle unless it attracts neighbors.
Check your understanding
Simulate trajectories from slightly different initial conditions. If they converge to the same closed loop, you have a stable limit cycle.
Design implication
Adjust actuator compliance and timing to shape the cycle and enlarge the basin of attraction for robustness.
Key Takeaway

Limit cycles capture repeatable gaits; controllers and compliant actuators work together to produce stable, energy-efficient cycles.
