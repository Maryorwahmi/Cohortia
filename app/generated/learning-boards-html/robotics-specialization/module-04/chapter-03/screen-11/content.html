Comparison Pure Pursuit vs Stanley
Two practical path followers
Pure Pursuit
Steer toward a point on the path at a fixed lookahead distance. Computes curvature to reach that point along a circular arc.
Pros: simple, intuitive. Cons: lookahead tuning required, can be oscillatory with too small a lookahead.
Stanley method
Uses lateral cross-track error and heading error to compute steering, with a proportional term for lateral error and a heading correction term.
Pros: robust at higher speed, handles cross-track error explicitly. Cons: needs gain tuning for different speeds and path curvatures.
Practical guidance
  • Use pure pursuit for low-speed, smooth path following and quick prototyping.
  • Use Stanley when operating at higher speeds where explicit cross-track correction improves performance.
  • Always limit curvature commands to match vehicle steering constraints to respect nonholonomic behavior.
