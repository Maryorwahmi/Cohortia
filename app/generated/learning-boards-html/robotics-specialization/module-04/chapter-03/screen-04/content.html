Core Concept Differential-drive
Two driven wheels, many behaviors
Simple hardware, coupled motion — learn the practical implications for control and estimation.
What it is
  • Two driven wheels on a shared axle, independent motors.
  • Passive castors add stability but do not drive motion.
  • Wheel speeds v_L and v_R combine to set body motion.
Common-mistake
Assuming each wheel causes only translation. Wheels act as a coupled pair that define both forward and angular velocity.
Key Takeaway

Differential drive trades mechanical simplicity for kinematic coupling; design controllers with that coupling in mind.

Concrete example
A floor robot with v_L = 0.4 m/s and v_R = 0.6 m/s on a 0.5 m track will produce forward motion and a turning rate. Use formulas in the next screen to compute them precisely.
Why it matters
Understanding this configuration lets you convert encoder readings into pose updates and design steering commands that produce desired trajectories.
Misconception check: equal wheel speeds mean straight motion only if wheels do not slip and ground is flat.
