Core Concept Nonholonomic constraints
Velocity limits that shape control
Definition & example
A nonholonomic constraint restricts instantaneous velocity directions but is not integrable to a position constraint. Example: a car cannot produce lateral velocity, so it cannot slide directly sideways.
Implication for control
Controllers must generate sequences of admissible velocities. Path planning must respect curvature and steering limits.
Misconception check
Nonholonomic does not mean non-reachable. A car can reach any pose by a sequence of forward and turning motions; its instantaneous motion options are simply limited.
Key Takeaway

Design planners and controllers that respect velocity-level constraints; otherwise commands will be unrealizable on the real robot.
