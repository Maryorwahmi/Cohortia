Definition Ackermann steering
Geometry for slip-free turning
Formulas
tan(θ_inner) = L / (R - w/2)
tan(θ_outer) = L / (R + w/2)
Practical example
If wheelbase L and track w are known, choose a turning radius R and compute the required inner and outer wheel steering angles to avoid slip.
Why it matters
  • Ensures tires roll rather than slide, improving efficiency and reducing wear.
  • Changes how steering commands map to vehicle curvature, important for path tracking.
  • Is a kinematic constraint used by many ground vehicles and simulators.
Misconception check: identical front wheel angles are not correct for tight turns because they would force tire slip.
