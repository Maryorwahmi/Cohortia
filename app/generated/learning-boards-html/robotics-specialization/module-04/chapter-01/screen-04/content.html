Module 4 › Chapter 4.1 › Legs vs Wheels
Comparison overview

A concise comparison highlighting strengths and limitations of each mode for different terrain types.

Wheeled
  • Efficient on flat, hard surfaces.
  • Simple kinematics: velocity driven by wheel speed.
  • Limited at obstacles, soft ground, or steps.
Legged
  • Can step, clear obstacles, and adapt foot placement.
  • Handles discontinuous terrains and slopes.
  • Requires coordination, contact management, and balance control.
Example
A search-and-rescue robot must step over rubble and enter unstable spaces; legs enable access where wheels cannot.
Common misconception
More legs always improve performance. In fact, more legs increase control complexity and energy consumption even though they improve static stability.
Key Takeaway

Choose locomotion mode based on mission terrain: legs for adaptability, wheels for efficient, predictable surfaces.

Decision checklist
  1. Identify surface irregularities: stairs, rubble, soft patches.
  2. Estimate need to step over or adjust foot placement.
  3. Assess energy constraints and control budget.
  4. Map final choice to leg count and sensor/actuator capability.
This checklist helps you justify legged designs when environmental adaptability outweighs added control complexity.
