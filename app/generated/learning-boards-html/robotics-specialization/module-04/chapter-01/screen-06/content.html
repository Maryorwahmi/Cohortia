Module 4 › Chapter 4.1 › SLIP Parameters
Parameter cards
Leg stiffness (k)
Higher stiffness yields shorter, springier stance and larger restoring forces when compressed.
Rest length (L0)
Sets nominal height; interacts with touchdown geometry to determine maximum compression.
Touchdown angle (α)
Controls vertical impulse and trajectory shape; steeper angles tend to increase hop height.
Initial velocity (v)
Horizontal speed at touchdown; faster speeds change flight time and required stance impulse.
Key Takeaway

Treat SLIP parameters as design knobs: vary one at a time to understand its effect on hop height, stance duration, and forward distance.

Example scenario

Starting with m = 1 kg, L0 = 1 m, α = 70°, v = 2 m/s, increasing k from 500 to 1000 N/m shortens stance and increases peak height. This is a direct parameter experiment you will run in the hands-on activity.

Misconception check: SLIP does not model joint torques or limb inertia. It models center-of-mass forces via an idealized springy leg.
