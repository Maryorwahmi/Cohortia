Template description

Two rigid legs, a hip mass, and a pin joint at the hip produce walking through alternating falls and catches.

Passive dynamic walking
Gravity and leg geometry produce a limit cycle that can be stable on a slope without active motor inputs.
When to add actuation
Motors and control are added when terrain varies or when higher adaptability and robustness are required.
Key Takeaway

Compass gait shows that mechanical design can reduce control needs. Use it to identify opportunities for passive efficiency.

Concrete example

A passive dynamic walker on a shallow slope will step forward repeatedly as it falls and catches with the next leg, demonstrating walking without motors. This example highlights the energy-saving potential of exploiting mechanics.

Misconception check: passive walkers work only on slopes. They reveal mechanics but need actuation for level ground or variable terrain.
