Module 2 › Chapter 2.2 › Control loop components
Definition
What each component does
Reference (setpoint)
The desired state, for example z_des = 2 m or roll = 0 degrees.
Plant
The quadrotor and its physics; it responds to thrust and torque inputs.
Sensor
IMU, barometer and other sensors measure the plant state and can introduce noise and latency.
Controller
Computes inputs from the error e(t) = r(t) - y(t) to drive the plant toward the reference.
Key Takeaway

The error signal is the central quantity; design choices for controller, sensors, and actuators determine whether the error is reduced robustly under realistic conditions.

Concrete example
Hover setpoint z_des=2 m; measured z=1.8 m gives e=0.2 m. A proportional term produces thrust proportional to that 0.2 m error. If a persistent downward wind exists, the error will remain unless the controller has integral action to accumulate and correct steady offsets.
Misconception
Sensors are not perfect. Treat measurements as noisy and delayed; controllers should avoid overreacting to instantaneous sensor noise.
Try this
In simulation, add sensor noise and a small time delay and observe how the closed-loop response changes. Note how derivative action reacts to noisy signals.
Check

What happens if the controller ignores actuator limits? The controller could command unattainable inputs and integral accumulation could worsen overshoot once saturation clears.
