Control Theory for Aerial Vehicles
Design PD/PID controllers, analyze stability, and create smooth quadrotor trajectories suitable for real motors.
In this lesson

You will see how an error signal drives control actions, how proportional, derivative, and integral terms shape response, and how polynomial trajectories ensure continuous acceleration for safe flight.

Why it matters
Good control keeps a drone safe, efficient, and responsive under disturbances like wind.
What you'll practice
Tuning PD and PID gains in simulation and generating cubic trajectories through waypoints.
Explore Key Topics
Control Loop
Reference, error, controller, plant and sensor form the loop that drives convergence to the setpoint.
PD & PID
Proportional, derivative, and integral terms shape response speed, damping, and steady-state accuracy.
Trajectories
Cubic and minimum-snap polynomials ensure smooth position, velocity and acceleration profiles for flight.

Visual concept
Reference
Desired altitude or orientation.
Sensor
IMU or barometer reading the actual state.
Controller
PD or PID computing thrust and torques.
Plant
The quadrotor responds to inputs and physics like gravity and drag.
Getting started
Begin with the control loop and a PD altitude controller in simulation. Then add integral action and explore trajectory polynomials for waypoint navigation.
