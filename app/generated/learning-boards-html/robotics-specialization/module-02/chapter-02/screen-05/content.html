Process: PD action
How proportional and derivative terms combine
Formula
u(t) = Kp * e(t) + Kd * de(t)/dt
Spring-damper analogy
Proportional is like a spring force; derivative is like a damper resisting velocity to reduce overshoot.
Design notes
  • Increase Kp for faster response but watch for oscillation.
  • Add Kd to damp oscillations, but be cautious with noisy sensors.
  • Check step responses to compare rise time and overshoot.
Concrete example: altitude
If you apply a PD law to altitude, a small Kp with no Kd often yields oscillation. Adding Kd damps vertical motion and reduces overshoot. Observe with four gain sets in simulation: the change in Kp and Kd changes stability and settling time.
Common Mistake
Applying large derivative gains without filtering sensor noise can make inputs jitter and destabilize the system.
Key Takeaway

Use Kp for speed and Kd for damping. Tune them together and always consider sensor noise when adding derivative action.
