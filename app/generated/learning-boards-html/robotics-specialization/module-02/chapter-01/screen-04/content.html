Quadrotor and underactuation
Four rotors, six degrees of freedom — tilt to translate.
State definition
x, y, z — position; vx, vy, vz — linear velocity; roll, pitch, yaw — orientation; ωx, ωy, ωz — angular velocity.
Why twelve?
You need both configuration and rates to form a first-order ordinary differential equation system. Integration of these rates predicts future pose and motion under inputs.
Misconception check
More sensors don't increase actuation. Underactuation is about actuators, not observability. A system can be observable and still underactuated.
Key Takeaway

The full state includes pose and rates; controllers and simulators must propagate all twelve variables to predict motion correctly.

Concrete example
Imagine a hover where motor commands change so the vehicle tilts by five degrees pitch. The body z-axis thrust is rotated, producing a forward acceleration component. That forward acceleration appears in the vx equation even though only thrust changed; orientation and translation are coupled.
State quick reference
Position: x, y, z
Velocity: vx, vy, vz
Angles: roll, pitch, yaw
Angular rates: ωx, ωy, ωz
Question to reflect on: given these twelve variables, which can you control directly with motor thrusts, and which only indirectly through attitude changes?
