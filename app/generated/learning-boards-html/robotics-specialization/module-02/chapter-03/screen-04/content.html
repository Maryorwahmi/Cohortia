Definition cards
Motion Planning
Finds collision-free geometric paths that respect coarse dynamics and environment constraints.
Trajectory Generation
Converts paths into time-parameterized curves that specify position, velocity and acceleration.
Trajectory Tracking
Feedback control that commands thrust and torques so the vehicle follows the timed reference.
Key Takeaway

Always verify generation against the tracker and expected disturbances; a plan is not flight until the controller can track it.

Illustrative example
Imagine a mission that requires passing through four waypoints near tall structures. The planner selects a path that avoids obstacles. Generation assigns time so the rotorcraft reaches each waypoint at a specific timestamp and ensures velocity continuity. The tracker receives position, velocity, and acceleration references and produces thrust commands. If the timing asks for high accelerations near gusty wind, the tracker may fail, which reveals a mismatch between generation and tracking assumptions.
Misconception: Planning alone ensures safety.
Reality: Generation and tracking must be compatible with vehicle limits.
Check your understanding
  • Define a simple test where you perturb the trajectory with a small wind and measure tracking error.
  • Compare two time allocations and record maximum required thrust for each.
