Differential flatness and snap
Differential flatness means we can plan in position space and compute the remaining states and inputs from derivatives. For quadrotors, motor thrust correlates to acceleration derivatives up to snap. Minimizing the L2 norm of snap across the trajectory yields a smooth control signal.
QP formulation
Set up a quadratic cost on snap with equality constraints for waypoint positions and derivative continuity. Solve to obtain polynomial coefficients for each segment.
Concrete comparison
Solve the same four-waypoint mission with cubic, quintic, and minimum-snap. Observe that minimum-snap reduces high-frequency components in acceleration and produces lower peak motor commands in simulation, though the path may be less time-optimal.
Pro tip: include derivative constraints at waypoints if you need the optimizer to match specific velocities or accelerations.
Misconception: Minimum-snap equals minimum time. It minimizes smoothness cost, not flight duration.
Key Takeaway

Minimum-snap produces controller-friendly trajectories by directly penalizing high-order derivatives tied to actuators.
