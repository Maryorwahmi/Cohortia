Step-by-step: From waypoints to timed segments
  1. Select mission waypoints and check obstacle clearance.
  2. Estimate per-segment durations respecting max velocity and acceleration.
  3. Fit polynomial segments (cubic/quintic) or set up a minimum-snap optimization.
  4. Simulate position, velocity, and acceleration profiles; adjust timing if peaks are too large.
  5. Validate with the intended controller under small disturbance.
Common mistake: equal time allocation ignores segment length and curvature.
Concrete example
Consider a mission with waypoints: takeoff at origin, then (10,0,5), (10,10,5), (0,10,5), and land at origin. If every segment uses equal time, the short return segment may produce excessive jerk. Instead, allocate time proportional to segment length and expected curvature so that acceleration peaks remain within motor limits.
Misconception: Straight lines are always optimal.
Reality: Straight polylines are easy to plan but produce discontinuities at vertices.
Check your understanding
If one segment doubles in length, increase its allocated time proportionally to keep peak acceleration similar. Verify by plotting acceleration across the trajectory.
