Particle Filter Localization
Monte Carlo approach to maintain multiple hypotheses and handle complex, multimodal beliefs.

Why this matters: Kalman-style filters assume a single Gaussian hypothesis. In many startup or symmetric scenes that assumption fails. Particle filters keep many hypotheses and let sensor evidence shape which survive.

What you will do: implement prediction, compute sensor likelihoods, resample systematically, and experiment with adaptive strategies to avoid degeneracy and impoverishment.

Explore Key Topics
Prediction
Propagate particles by motion plus noise so hypotheses move with the robot's odometry.
Update
Weight each particle by how well it predicts the sensor measurement.
Resampling
Draw particles proportional to weight to concentrate samples in probable regions.

Interactive 16:9 Masterclass
Probability landscape
Imagine a continuous map of belief: peaks where pose is likely, valleys otherwise. Particles sample that landscape; dense clusters show peaks.
Particles as hypotheses
Each particle holds a pose and weight. Over time prediction moves them, updates scale their weight, and resampling focuses them on peaks.
Quick example
Start with a uniform spread of 1000 particles. After sensing and motion, high-weight particles cluster near the true pose. If the robot is kidnapped, inject random particles and allow sensing to re-establish a cluster.
