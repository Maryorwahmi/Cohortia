Module 7 › Chapter 6.3 › Why Kalman Filters Fail
Key claim
Kalman filters assume a single Gaussian; when a belief is multimodal or uniform, that assumption produces misleading averages.
Concrete example
Two identical corridors produce two peaks in belief. A Kalman filter returns their mean, often in a wall or midway location — an incorrect pose estimate.
Misconception
More sensors always fix it. Not necessarily: if sensors produce ambiguous or non-Gaussian likelihoods, the Gaussian assumption still fails.
Key Takeaway

When beliefs are multimodal, prefer a non-parametric representation like particles to avoid averaged, incorrect estimates.

Analogy
Think of the Kalman filter as summarizing a multi-peaked mountain range by a single average height. That loses essential structure. A particle set is like many surveyors sampling the landscape; peaks remain visible as clusters.
