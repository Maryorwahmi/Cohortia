Managing Environment Lifecycle with `on_stop`

Automating Cleanup for Dynamic Environments

Why `on_stop` is Essential

  • Resource Management: Prevents accumulation of unused, costly environments.
  • Cost Optimization: Avoids unnecessary cloud infrastructure expenses.
  • Security: Reduces the attack surface by de-provisioning temporary resources.
  • Cleanliness: Keeps your GitLab Environments dashboard tidy and relevant.

Code Example: `stop_review` Job

stop_review:  stage: cleanup  image: alpine/git  script:    - echo "Stopping review app for branch: $CI_COMMIT_REF_SLUG"    - chmod +x ./stop_review_app.sh    - ./stop_review_app.sh "$CI_ENVIRONMENT_SLUG"  environment:    name: review/$CI_COMMIT_REF_SLUG    action: stop # Crucial for triggering cleanup  rules:    - if: '$CI_COMMIT_BRANCH =~ /feature\/.*/'      when: manual # Or on_success for automatic cleanup on branch delete

The `action: stop` keyword ensures this job is triggered when the environment is stopped, running the cleanup script.

Key Takeaway

The `on_stop` keyword, paired with a cleanup job, is vital for managing the lifecycle of dynamic environments, ensuring resources are de-provisioned efficiently and securely when no longer needed.
