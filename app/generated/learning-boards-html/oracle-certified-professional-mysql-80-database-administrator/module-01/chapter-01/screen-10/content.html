Process

From in-memory change to durable storage

Writes go to the Buffer Pool and the redo log. Background flushers write dirty pages to disk. On crash, redo log replay restores committed changes.

Concrete example
A transaction commits after redo log write. If the server crashes before dirty pages are flushed, redo log replay recovers the committed state at restart.
Misconception
Believing in-memory writes alone are durable ignores the role of the redo log and flush to disk in ensuring persistence.
Operational choices
  • Tune redo log size to balance throughput and recovery time.
  • Adjust flush policies to control latency vs safer recovery.
  • Monitor dirty pages and flush metrics during heavy write periods.
Key Takeaway

Durability depends on redo logging and timely flushes; tune these according to your recovery time objectives and throughput needs.
