Module 7 › Chapter 7.1 › Why Group Replication
Asynchronous / Semi-sync
Asynchronous replication replicates changes after commit and requires manual promotion on failure. Semi-synchronous adds some acknowledgement guarantees but still lacks a group-wide ordering that prevents divergence after partitions. Operators commonly face manual recovery, split-brain risk, and inconsistent replicas during failover.
Pros: simple, low overhead for writes.
Cons: manual failover, possible divergence, limited automation.
Group Replication (MGR)
MGR uses a messaging and consensus layer so members agree on transaction order. That gives strong consistency and automatic membership reconfiguration. MGR reduces manual intervention for failover, but requires careful configuration and low-latency networks.
Benefit: automatic failover and uniform state.
Trade-off: stricter prerequisites and sensitivity to latency.
Example: Two-node split in async requires manual reconciliation after rejoin.
Misconception: MGR automatically solves all scaling problems—no, it enforces ordering and consistency which can limit write latency.
Takeaway: Choose MGR when strong consistency and automated membership are priorities.
