Definition
Recovery Time Objective (RTO)
Maximum acceptable downtime after a disaster before services must be restored.
Recovery Point Objective (RPO)
Maximum acceptable data loss measured in time. Lower RPO means less data loss but higher cost.
Concrete example
If RTO = 4 hours and RPO = 15 minutes, you need near-continuous replication and a DR site that can be promoted within four hours.
Common misconception
Backups alone satisfy data recovery but usually do not meet small RTOs without pre-provisioned infrastructure and automation.
Key Takeaway

Translate business impact into RTO and RPO, then design replication and standby provisioning to meet those targets and cost constraints.

Timeline Visual (conceptual)
Event: Data corruption at 10:00
RPO 15m means recoverable to at least 9:45; RTO 4h means service restored by 14:00.
Example decision:
  • RPO <= 15m → use continuous async replication to DR cluster.
  • RTO <= 4h → provision warm standby that can scale quickly, or a hot standby for immediate takeover.
