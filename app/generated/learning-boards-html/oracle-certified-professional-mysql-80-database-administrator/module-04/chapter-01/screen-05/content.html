Definition

RTO and RPO

Translate business tolerance into time-bound recovery targets that guide backup frequency and architecture.

RTO
Maximum acceptable downtime. Drives how quickly you must restore or fail over.
RPO
Maximum acceptable data loss in time. Drives backup frequency and log archiving.
Concrete example
Peak sales: RTO 30 minutes, RPO 5 minutes. This requires near-continuous replication or binary log shipping and very fast restores or failover.
Key Takeaway

Choose backups and recovery processes that achieve RTO and RPO in timed drills, not only on paper.

Misconception
Lower RPO always costs more
Lower RPO often increases cost, but smart use of log archiving and replication can reduce expense compared to brute-forcing frequent full backups.
Quick plan card
If RTO is short
Use fast physical restores or replicate to a warm standby for near-instant failover.
If RPO is short
Archive binary logs continuously and use frequent backups or replication to limit data loss.
