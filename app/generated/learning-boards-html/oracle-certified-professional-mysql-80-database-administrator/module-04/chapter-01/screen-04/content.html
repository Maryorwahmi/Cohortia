Core Concept

Why backups are essential

Backups support business continuity, allow recovery after diverse failures, and enable safe maintenance and testing.

Key points
  • Backups are necessary for hardware, software, human, malicious, and environmental failures.
  • They must be consistent and validated; otherwise they may be unusable when needed.
  • Backups enable maintenance windows, migrations, and disaster recovery plans.
Concrete example
During a Black Friday outage, a tested backup and a short restore window can prevent prolonged revenue loss and preserve customer trust.
Common mistake
Relying on backups without a documented and tested restore procedure—many teams discover unusable backups only after a failure.
Key Takeaway

Backups protect operations only when they are consistent, tested, and aligned with the business's acceptable downtime and data loss.

Analogy
Think of backups like a bank vault and an offsite copy. The vault secures current assets; offsite copies let you recover if the vault is damaged.
Why it matters

Planning and testing backups reduces the risk of extended outages, regulatory fines, and permanent data loss. The cost of prevention is typically far lower than the cost of recovery from an unprepared disaster.
