Module 04 › Chapter 4.4 › What the Binlog Records
Definition

Binary Log (Binlog)

A sequential record of data-modifying operations that can be replayed to reproduce database state changes. It supports replication and point-in-time recovery.

What it contains
DML and DDL events, with details depending on binlog_format.
Why it matters
Enables restoring to precise times and keeping replicas synchronized.
Key Takeaway

Treat the binlog as part of your backup chain; without it PITR is not possible.

Analogy
Photo + Film
Full backup is the photograph; binary logs are the film strip recording subsequent changes.
Concrete example
You take a backup at 08:00, operations occur, and at 10:30 an accidental DELETE happens. Restoring the backup and applying binlogs up to 10:29:59 preserves everything except the DELETE.
Common misconception
Assuming backups alone suffice. Without binlogs you cannot recover changes made after the backup time.
