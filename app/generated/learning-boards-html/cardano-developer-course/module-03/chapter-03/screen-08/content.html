Module 3 › Chapter 3.3 › Policy Scripts & Collection Policies
Architecture
Minting policies are ledger scripts. Their hash is the Policy ID used to scope assets and connect metadata.
Single-mint policy example
A script requiring a single signature at mint time and preventing further mint operations for that asset name.
Time-locked policy
Policies can include time windows so minting is only allowed during a sale period.
Policy hash → Policy ID
The policy script's hash is the immutable Policy ID used in metadata to link assets to their rules.
Concrete scenario
Design a policy that requires the creator's signing key and enforces exactly one unit minted per asset name. When you mint, the transaction includes the script hash. The resulting Policy ID appears in CIP-25 metadata so wallets and explorers can tie metadata to the collection.
Misconception

A policy enforces mint/burn conditions only. It does not control who can transfer or hold the token after minting.

Why it matters

Correct policy design ensures scarcity, prevents accidental re-mints, and preserves collector trust. Link the policy id to CIP-25 metadata to keep discoverability intact.
