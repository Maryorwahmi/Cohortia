Module 6 › Chapter 6.4 › Access Control
Physical Security
Prevent direct access to equipment to stop bypassing logical controls.
Examples
  • Locked server rooms and racks
  • Access control systems and visitor logs
  • Video surveillance and environmental sensors
Logical Security
Authentication and authorization practices that restrict privileges.
Core practices
  • Strong, unique passwords and regular rotation
  • Multi-factor authentication for admin access
  • Least privilege and role-based access
Scenario

A technician needs console access to configure a switch. Instead of giving a permanent admin account, grant a temporary, auditable account tied to their identity and remove it afterward. Record the change and back up the configuration before and after the work.

Misconception

Assuming a password policy alone prevents misuse ignores the risk of physical tampering or compromised privileged accounts. Combine controls for defense in depth.

Key Takeaway

Access control must be both physical and logical; apply least privilege and temporary accounts for safer operations.
