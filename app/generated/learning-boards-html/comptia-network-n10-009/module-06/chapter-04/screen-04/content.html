Network Hardening
Reducing attack surface and strengthening defenses through configuration and operational controls.
Analogy

Like securing a building: locks, restricted access, fewer doors, and alarms. Each layer reduces the chance of an intruder succeeding.

Common elements
  • Patch management to close known vulnerabilities.
  • Configuration backups and version control for recovery.
  • Access controls, service minimization, and segmentation.
Key Takeaway

Hardening is ongoing maintenance: patch, audit, restrict, and monitor to keep risk low.

Practical checklist for a new device
  1. Change default passwords and disable default accounts.
  2. Apply latest firmware or OS patches after testing.
  3. Disable unused services and close unnecessary ports.
  4. Back up and encrypt configuration, then store offsite.
Misconception to avoid

Do not treat hardening as a one-time checklist. New vulnerabilities and configuration drift require continuous attention.
