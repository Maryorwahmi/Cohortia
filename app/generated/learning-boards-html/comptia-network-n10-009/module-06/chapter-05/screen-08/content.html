RTO and RPO explained

Define acceptable downtime (RTO) and acceptable data loss (RPO). Use these figures to choose replication, backup cadence, and failover architecture.

RTO example
If RTO is 4 hours, your recovery steps and personnel must restore services in under four hours, including testing and validation.
RPO example
If RPO is 1 hour, backups or replication must ensure no more than an hour of transactions are lost.
Design implication
Low RTO/RPO require higher-cost solutions like synchronous replication, active-active sites, or continuous data protection.
Timeline (conceptual)
Incident timeline
Start: incident occurs. Point of last replicated data marks the RPO. The time until services are restored marks the RTO.
Misconception check

RTO is not how old your backup is; it is how long it takes to restore. RPO is how recent the restored data can be.

Key Takeaway

Set RTO and RPO with business stakeholders, then design replication and backup strategies to meet those targets and validate with tests.
