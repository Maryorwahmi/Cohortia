Storage Comparison
Trade-offs: speed, safety, cost, and retrieval
Onsite

Fast restores and quick access. Vulnerable to site disasters if copies are only local.

Best when low RTO is primary and physical protection is strong.
Offsite

Physically separate copies protect against site loss. Consider transfer and retrieval time.

Essential for site-wide disaster resilience.
Cloud

Geographic redundancy and managed storage. Check bandwidth, egress costs, and restoration speed.

Good for distributed recovery and managed redundancy.
Storage Considerations
  • Encryption in transit and at rest to protect backup data.
  • Retrieval time and bandwidth affect whether you meet RTO/RPO goals.
  • Access controls and documented procedures for restoring offsite copies.
  • Retention policies that balance regulatory needs and storage cost.
Real Example

A company with a flooded server room used offsite backups stored in a separate city to rebuild services. They planned bandwidth and verified retrieval steps beforehand, so the restore completed within acceptable downtime.

Common Mistake

Assuming offsite copies are instantly accessible. Retrieval delays and policies can lengthen real recovery time.

Key Takeaway

Offsite or cloud copies protect against site loss, but you must plan for retrieval speed, encryption, and verification to meet recovery objectives.
