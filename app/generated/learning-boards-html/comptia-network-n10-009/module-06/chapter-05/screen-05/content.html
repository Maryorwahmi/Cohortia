Process

Implementing hardware redundancy

Follow a stepwise approach: identify single points of failure, prioritize by business impact, choose appropriate redundancy, then test and document the configuration.

Step 1: Inventory
List components and note single points like single PSUs, NICs, or disks.
Step 2: Prioritize
Rank systems by business impact to decide where to spend for redundancy.
Step 3: Select options
Choose dual PSUs, multiple NICs, RAID levels, and power connectivity to separate circuits as needed.
Step 4: Test and document
Perform failover tests and record procedures, owners, and expected recovery times.
Visual examples
Dual PSU example
Each power supply connects to a separate UPS or circuit so the server stays powered if one supply or circuit fails.
RAID example
RAID 1 mirrors disks for immediate protection against single-disk failure; RAID 5/6 adds parity for capacity and resilience.
Key Takeaway

Hardware redundancy prevents single-component failures from causing downtime, but it does not replace backups or offsite protection.
