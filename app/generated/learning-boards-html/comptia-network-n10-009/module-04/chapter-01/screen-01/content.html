Introduction to Network Security Principles

Build a practical framework to evaluate risks and defenses across confidentiality, integrity, and availability. This lesson connects common threats to concrete controls and policies you can apply today.

Confidentiality
Protects data from unauthorized disclosure, often via encryption.
Integrity
Ensures data remains accurate and unaltered using hashes and signatures.
Availability
Keeps systems and data accessible through redundancy and backups.
Why this matters

Network professionals make trade-offs every day. A control that strengthens confidentiality might reduce availability if applied without care. This chapter teaches you how to reason about those trade-offs, spot common mistakes like unencrypted internal data or poorly configured password policy, and choose layered defenses that reduce overall risk while preserving usability.

Visual concept
Layered defense
Layered controls
Combine technical, administrative, and physical controls so attackers face multiple barriers.
Example
Use encryption for confidentiality, hashing for integrity, and redundancy for availability to cover the main risks to a customer database.
Common mistake
Relying only on a single control type, such as a firewall, leaves gaps that attackers or insiders can exploit.
