Module 4 › Chapter 4.1 › The CIA Triad
Definition

What the triad means

A simple model: Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability are the intended security properties of information and services. Use them to categorize risks and plan controls.

Confidentiality
Prevent unauthorized disclosure. Example: HTTPS encrypts data in transit so eavesdroppers can’t read it.
Integrity
Ensure accuracy and detect tampering. Example: Hashes signal if a file changed since it was signed.
Availability
Keep services accessible. Example: Redundancy and backups maintain access during failures or attacks.
Key Takeaway

Treat the triad as a checklist for each asset: which of the three matters most, and what layered controls meet that need?

Examples, Misconceptions, and Balancing
Concrete example
A customer database: encrypt sensitive fields for confidentiality, use integrity checks for backups, and deploy failover to preserve availability.
Common misconception
Believing one control, like a firewall, fully secures an asset. Firewalls help but must be paired with encryption and monitoring.
Why balance matters
Strong confidentiality can reduce availability if access becomes cumbersome. Design policies and tools so protections do not block legitimate use.
