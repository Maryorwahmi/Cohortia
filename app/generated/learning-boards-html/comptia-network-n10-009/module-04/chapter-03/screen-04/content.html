Core Concept › Why wireless needs special security
Definition and threat model
Radio is publicly observable, so confidentiality and access control must be enforced even when attackers are not physically present on wiring.
  • • Passive interception is easier because frames travel through open air.
  • • Attackers can collect frames for offline analysis and cracking.
  • • Unauthorized APs can mimic legitimate infrastructure and capture credentials.
Key Takeaway

Treat wireless as an adversarial channel: rely on strong encryption and per-user authentication, not hiding or filtering tricks.

Common misconception
Hiding the SSID or using MAC filtering prevents discovery.
Reality: management frames reveal network names and MACs can be spoofed; both are supplementary, not defensive.
Concrete example
A hidden corporate SSID discovered by an attacker with a packet sniffer can be reproduced on a malicious AP. Once clients probe for that SSID, they can be induced to connect to the attacker. The robust defense is to require strong authentication and encryption so even if a client connects, credentials or session keys protect confidentiality.
Why it matters
Protecting wireless reduces risk of credential theft and data exposure from casual or remote attackers.
Check your setup
Do you use WPA2/WPA3 or legacy WEP/WPA? If legacy, plan an upgrade immediately.
