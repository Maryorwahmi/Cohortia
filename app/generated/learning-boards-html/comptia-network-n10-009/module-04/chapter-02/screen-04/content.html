Module 4 › Chapter 4.2 › Device Hardening Fundamentals
Definition
Device hardening is the process of reducing a device's attack surface by disabling unnecessary services, removing default credentials, and restricting management access.
Change default usernames and passwords immediately. Use strong, unique secrets combining letters, numbers, and symbols.
Disable unused services and ports such as Telnet, HTTP, and SNMP if they aren't needed for operations.
Limit management access to specific IPs or networks using ACLs or out-of-band management networks.
Check: run a port scan and attempt authentication to confirm services are disabled and credentials changed.
Analogy & Example
Think of a device as a building: change the locks, close unused doors, and only give keys to authorized staff. Example failure: leaving Telnet enabled with factory passwords allows easy takeover of routing or switch forwarding tables.
Key Takeaway

Hardening removes obvious attack routes first: credentials, services, and open ports are priority one.
