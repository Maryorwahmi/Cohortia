Core Concept

What is a VLAN?

A VLAN groups devices logically so broadcasts and traffic are limited to that group.

Analogy
Think of VLANs as separate rooms in an open-plan office; people still work in the same building but conversations don't carry into every room.
Why it matters
Less broadcast noise, easier policy application, and clearer management by department or function.
Common misconception
VLANs are not firewalls. They isolate at Layer 2 but need routing to allow inter-VLAN traffic.
Key Takeaway

VLANs divide a physical switch into multiple logical networks to limit broadcast domains and improve management.

Example scenario
Office departments
Create VLAN 10 for Sales, VLAN 20 for Marketing, VLAN 30 for IT. Broadcasts from Sales only reach VLAN 10.
Physical vs logical
Devices on different switches can belong to the same VLAN when trunks carry VLAN tags between switches.
Check your understanding
Question
If two devices are in different VLANs, can they communicate by default?
Answer hint
No — they require a Layer 3 device (router or Layer 3 switch) to route between VLANs.
