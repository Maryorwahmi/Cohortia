Module 5 › Chapter 5.1 › Packet Sniffers
When to capture
Capture when higher-level indicators (alerts, NetFlow, SNMP counters) point to persistent issues, or when you need to inspect protocol fields to confirm behavior.
Filtering
Apply capture filters for specific IPs, ports, or protocols to limit data. Example capture filter for HTTP: "host 10.0.0.5 and tcp port 80".
Example analysis
Look for TCP retransmissions, duplicate ACKs, or large gaps between sequence numbers. These patterns indicate packet loss or reordering that explains slow application behavior.
Analogy
Packet capture is like reading raw network mail
It shows each packet's envelope and content. That level of detail helps you see exact failures, but it is heavy and requires filtering and respect for privacy.
Pro Tip

Capture a short window around the issue and filter aggressively to avoid large captures and to speed analysis.

Key Takeaway

Use packet sniffers to confirm the packet-level evidence of problems, then combine that evidence with flow and device counters to locate the source of loss or delay.
