Definition

What the physical layer does

It defines the physical means of moving raw bits — cables, connectors, and signaling conventions.

Concrete example
A Cat5e UTP patch connects a workstation to a switch at 1 Gbps for up to 100 meters in typical office conditions.
Common misconception
Higher category copper always solves performance issues — sometimes distance or EMI demands fiber or shielded cabling instead.
Pro tip
Always keep twists intact as close to the RJ‑45 as possible; untwisting increases crosstalk.
Key Takeaway

Select cabling based on distance, noise, and bandwidth; inspect terminations to avoid intermittent failures.

Physical attributes comparison
Twisted-pair (UTP/STP)
Pairs of copper wires twisted to reduce EMI; UTP is flexible and common; STP adds a shield for noisy environments.
Fiber Optic
Carries light, immune to EMI, long distances; requires careful termination and safety precautions.
Coaxial
Central conductor with shield, used for cable TV and some internet links; robust but less common for LANs today.
Connector examples
RJ‑45 for Ethernet, SC/LC for fiber, F‑type or BNC for coax.
