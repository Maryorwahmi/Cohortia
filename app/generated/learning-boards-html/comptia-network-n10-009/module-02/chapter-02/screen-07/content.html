Historic Concept

Bus: shared backbone

Devices tap into a single backbone cable. Termination is required at both ends. Simpler cabling but a single point of failure and one collision domain.

Strengths

Simple for small networks, minimal cabling for few devices.

Weaknesses

Single backbone failure disables the segment; performance degrades with contention.

Example

A coaxial Ethernet link used in early LANs required terminators at each end; a cut in the cable broke connectivity for all attached devices.

Misconception: bus is always cheaper — not when collision and downtime costs are considered for larger networks.
Design Notes
When you’ll see it

Mostly in legacy labs and exam scenarios. Modern LANs typically favor switched star designs.

Key Takeaway

Bus topology is simple but fragile; understand it for historical context and exam questions about a single shared backbone and termination.
