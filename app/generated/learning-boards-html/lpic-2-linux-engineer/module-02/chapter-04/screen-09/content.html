Introduction to Access Control Lists (ACLs)

Beyond Standard rwx Permissions for Granular Control

What are ACLs?

A more flexible permission system allowing specific permissions for any user or group, not just owner, group, and others.

Why use ACLs?

Essential for shared directories and complex access requirements where standard rwx permissions are insufficient.

Analogy

Think of standard permissions as a house with one key for the owner, one for the family, and a general rule for visitors. ACLs are like a smart lock system where you can give unique digital keys to specific individuals or groups, each with custom access times and door privileges.

Permission Models Compared

Standard `rwx`
Owner, Group, Others
ACLs
Any User, Any Group
Key Takeaway

ACLs provide a powerful extension to traditional Unix permissions, enabling highly specific access control for complex scenarios.
