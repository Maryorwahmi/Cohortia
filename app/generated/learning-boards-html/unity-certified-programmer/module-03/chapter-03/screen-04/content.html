Module 3 › Chapter 3.3 › What is a Physics Material?
Definition & properties
Physics Materials expose Dynamic Friction, Static Friction, and Bounciness. They also provide Friction Combine and Bounce Combine choices which determine how two materials blend during collision. These settings let you model surfaces from ice to rubber to springs.
Dynamic Friction
Resistance while sliding. 0 means no sliding resistance, 1 means strong resistance.
Static Friction
Resistance preventing initial motion. Often slightly higher than dynamic friction.
Bounciness
Amount of energy conserved in a collision. 0 is dead, 1 is perfectly elastic.
Combine Modes explained
  • Average: mean of the two values.
  • Minimum: uses the smaller value, good for slippery interactions.
  • Maximum: uses the larger value, preserves stickiness or bounce.
  • Multiply: multiplies values for compounded effects.
Concrete example
Create a 'BouncyMaterial' with bounciness 0.9 and Bounce Combine set to Maximum. Assign it to a sphere's collider and drop it onto a plane. Observe the high bounce retained by Maximum combine.
Key Takeaway

Physics Materials are the first control over contact behavior. Combine modes resolve interactions when two materials meet.
