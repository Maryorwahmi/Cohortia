Module 6 › Chapter 5.6 › Occlusion & Obstruction
Process

Raycast-based Occlusion

  1. Cast a ray from the Audio Listener to the Audio Source each update or on position changes.
  2. If the ray hits geometry, classify as occluded or partially obstructed.
  3. Adjust volume and set a low-pass cutoff parameter exposed on the mixer or source.
  4. Smooth changes over 100–300ms to avoid pops when entering/exiting occlusion.
Tuning hints
Use multiple rays for wide sources, or sample several points on a mesh when accuracy matters. Blend the filter values rather than snapping.
Example
Imagine a firefight occurring behind a metal door. A single raycast hits the door and you set the SFX group's low-pass cutoff to 1500Hz and reduce volume by 6dB. For a partially open door you lerp to 4000Hz and -3dB. Smooth transitions avoid noticeable jumps as doors open or players move.
Obstruction
Partial attenuation + mild low-pass
Occlusion
Stronger attenuation + lower cutoff
Key Takeaway

Raycasts plus smooth parameter blending give believable occlusion without heavy per-source CPU cost.
