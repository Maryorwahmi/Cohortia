Module 6 › Chapter 5.6 › Spatial Audio & Audio Zones
Definition

Audio Reverb Zones

Zones apply environmental effects to any audio within their bounds. Use colliders as the trigger volume and tune reverb parameters in the zone inspector.

Use case
A cavern zone adds long decay and low dampening so echoes feel distant and atmospheric.
Best practice
  • Keep source clips dry; apply ambience via zones for flexibility.
  • Overlap zones slightly to avoid hard transitions.
  • Tune rolloff curves and Max Distance for consistent loudness.
Common mistake
Embedding reverb in clips makes global changes tedious and inconsistent across areas.
Example
Cave Scenario

Place a Box Collider as an Audio Reverb Zone around a cave. Set reverb decay high and wet level moderate. When the player crosses the trigger, footsteps and ambient wind will gain echo and warmth.

Parameter
Decay: long
Effect
Wet reverb on all sources inside
Key Takeaway

Zones let you control room acoustics per area, keeping source audio clean and adaptable.
