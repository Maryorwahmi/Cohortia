Concept Cards
Position vs Direction
A position marks a point. A direction is the difference between two positions and shows where to go.
Normalization
Converting to a unit vector preserves direction while removing magnitude, enabling fixed-speed movement.
Magnitude and Clamping
Very small magnitudes near the target can cause jitter; clamp or use MoveTowards to stop cleanly.
Concrete Example
Imagine an agent at x equals zero z equals zero and a target at x equals ten z equals zero. The raw difference is ten along x. Normalizing produces one along x, so multiplying by speed will move the agent exactly at the chosen speed rather than ten times that speed. If you forgot to normalize, your agent would move faster the further it starts from the target, which usually feels wrong.
Key Takeaway

Normalize direction vectors to separate direction from distance and keep motion predictable.

Common Mistake
Not zeroing the vertical component leads to unintended vertical movement when the target is above or below the agent.
