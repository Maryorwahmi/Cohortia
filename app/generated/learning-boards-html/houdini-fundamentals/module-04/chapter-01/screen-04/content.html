Core Concept › Chapter 4.1 › What is a Material and What is a Shader?
Definitions
Material and shader roles explained with examples and a misconception check.
Material
A collection of visual parameters: base color, metallic, roughness, normal, transparency.
Shader
A program that reads material inputs and lighting to compute the final pixel color and reflection response.
PBR Insight
PBR constrains parameters so materials remain plausible under different lighting environments.
Key Takeaway

Think of materials as data and shaders as the calculator that turns that data into the image you see.

Concrete Example
Compare three parameter sets to see how the shader changes appearance.
Polished Metal
Metallic: 1.0 · Roughness: 0.05 · Reflective, color influenced by Fresnel mixing.
Glossy Plastic
Metallic: 0.0 · Roughness: 0.05 · Strong highlights but different albedo behavior.
Matte Concrete
Metallic: 0.0 · Roughness: 0.8 · Scattered, soft reflections.
Common Misconception
Adjusting only base color will not make a surface look metallic. Metal behavior is driven by the metallic parameter and how it changes reflection and albedo interaction.
