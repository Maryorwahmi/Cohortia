Definition

Rendering explained

Rendering converts three-dimensional scene data into two-dimensional images by evaluating lighting and material interactions. It can output a single beauty image or multiple specialized passes for compositing.

  • Rendering computes shading, shadows, reflections and volumetrics into pixels.
  • AOVs capture separate per-pixel data like depth, normals and diffuse color.
  • Preview renders use lower samples to iterate quickly; final renders use higher samples for quality.
Example
Textured sphere + three-point light, render to produce beauty and AOVs for compositing.
Key Takeaway

Plan passes before long renders to save time and preserve flexibility.

Analogy
Think of rendering like developing film: the beauty pass is the final photograph, while AOVs are separate exposures that let you adjust light and color afterwards.
Common misconception
Higher render time always equals better image. Not necessarily — a poorly configured shader or incorrect lighting can produce bad results no matter how long you render. Sampling and correct material setup matter more than raw render time.
