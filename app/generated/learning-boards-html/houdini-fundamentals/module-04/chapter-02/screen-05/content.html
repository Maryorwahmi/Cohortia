Process › Chapter 4.2 › Procedural Masks
Step 1 — Choose noise type
Worley for cellular patches, fractal for layered detail, curl for organic distortions. Each creates distinct mask character.
Step 2 — Tune frequency & amplitude
Frequency sets feature scale. Amplitude sets range; use Fit to remap for precise thresholds.
Step 3 — Combine & distort
Multiply fine detail over low-frequency patches, or use one noise to distort another for organic edges.
Concrete example
// Simple node flow
noise (Type: Worley, Frequency: 8)
noise_detail (Type: Fractal, Frequency: 40)
multiply (noise * noise_detail)
fit (src_min: 0.3, src_max: 0.7, dst_min:0, dst_max:1)
-> materialblend.mask
This multiplies a large-scale patch with fine grain and remaps the result into a usable blend mask. Render and tweak frequency until patches read at the scale of the object.
Key Takeaway
Mix noise types and remap their output to control blend edges and scale.
Misconception
A single noise rarely looks natural alone; layering produces believable variation.
