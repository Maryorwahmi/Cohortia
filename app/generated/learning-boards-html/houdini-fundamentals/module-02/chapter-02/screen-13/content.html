Building a Keyhole: A Procedural Workflow

Combining Transform, Merge, Boolean, and PolyExtrude

Keyhole Construction Steps

  1. **Start with a Box:** This forms the main body of the keyhole.
  2. **Create Cylinder & Sphere:** These will be the cutting shapes.
  3. **Transform & Position:** Use `Transform` SOPs to size and place the cylinder and sphere to form the keyhole shaft.
  4. **Merge Cutter Shapes:** Combine the cylinder and sphere using a `Merge` SOP.
  5. **Boolean Subtract:** Use a `Boolean` SOP (Subtract mode) to cut the merged shape from the box.
  6. **PolyExtrude Inwards:** Apply `PolyExtrude` to the resulting keyhole face for depth.

Visualizing the Node Graph

Imagine a simple node graph: `Box` node connects to `Boolean` input 1. `Cylinder` and `Sphere` nodes connect to a `Merge` node, which then connects to `Boolean` input 2. The `Boolean` output then feeds into a `PolyExtrude` node. This chain demonstrates the power of sequential operations.

Key Takeaway

Combining fundamental SOPs like `Transform`, `Merge`, `Boolean`, and `PolyExtrude` in a procedural chain allows for the efficient and non-destructive creation of complex shapes.

Challenge: Soften Edges

After creating the keyhole, try using a `Group` node to select specific edges around the keyhole and then apply a `Bevel` SOP to soften those edges. This adds another layer of detail and refinement.
