Module 6 › Chapter 5.5 › Particle Forces
Process
Iterative layering of POP forces
  1. Add a single POP force and play a short frame range.
  2. Observe velocity and particle spread; note areas of unnatural jitter.
  3. Add complementary forces (e.g., add drag to tame wind). Adjust strengths.
  4. If needed, add noise or POP Curve Force to guide streams.
Comparison Cards
POP Wind
Directional push. Use amplitude and direction vector.
POP Drag
Damps velocity. Useful to reduce chaos from strong forces.
POP Attract
Pulls particles to a point or geometry for clustering effects.
POP Curve Force
Guides particles along a path for streams and trails.
Example

Combine POP Wind (amplitude 2) with POP Drag (small coefficient) so wind directs flow while drag keeps particles from overshooting or jittering.
