Core Concept
POP Grains: granular behavior
Introduce volume and repulsion for realistic piling and cohesive flow.
Important parameters
  • Radius — effective particle size and packing
  • Repulsion/Stiffness — strength of neighbor separation
  • Search radius — how far neighbors are considered
Performance note
Grains requires neighborhood searches; reduce birth rate while tuning to keep iteration interactive.
Example scenario
Sand pour
POP Source emits from a sphere, POP Grains with Radius 0.05 creates a pile that fills the tube rather than particles passing through each other.
Tip: Tweak radius to change packing
Pitfall: High radius with high birth rate is costly
Key Takeaway

Use POP Grains when you need particles to behave as a packed material; adjust radius and repulsion early while testing with low particle counts.
