Rigid Body Dynamics explained
RBDs compute motion and collisions for solid pieces. The solver updates translation and rotation while applying forces.
Example: a shattered vase — each shard is a rigid body with mass and inertia that the solver moves and collides.
Misconception: Using Concave collision for every piece guarantees realism. It is accurate but may be prohibitively slow for many pieces.
Why it matters: choosing the right RBD setup decides whether a shot is believable and whether you can iterate in a reasonable time.
Key Takeaway

RBDs are for non-deforming pieces; balance collision accuracy and solver cost for your shot's needs.

Quick concept cards
Solver role
Applies forces, integrates motion, and resolves collisions between packed objects.
Packed geometry
Pieces are packed so the solver sees one lightweight primitive per shard.
Collision shapes
Choice affects both accuracy and performance; test on a small scale before running large sims.
Reflect: for your next shot, pick one object and decide whether exact surface-to-surface contact is required. That decision guides your collision shape and solver configuration.
