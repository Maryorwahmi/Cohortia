Module 6 › Chapter 6.2 › Why Expose Parameters?
Definition

Expose selected internal controls as HDA parameters

Parameters are the public variables of your HDA. They let users change behavior without diving into nodes.

Good parameter design balances power and simplicity: expose what matters, hide the rest.

Concrete example
A box HDA exposes three floats for X, Y, Z sizes. Changing those sliders changes the Box SOP's size channels without entering the HDA.
Common mistake
Exposing every internal parameter creates a confusing surface and increases maintenance burden.
Key Takeaway

Expose parameters that represent meaningful, artist-facing controls and set sensible defaults to guide use.

Design considerations
  • Names: concise internal identifiers used in expressions. Avoid spaces.
  • Labels: friendly names users see.
  • Defaults and soft ranges guide typical use while allowing full range via typing.
Analogy

Think of the HDA interface as the instrument panel for a machine: show the essential knobs, hide the internal wiring.

Try this
Add one float to an HDA and paste a relative reference into an internal node; verify behavior before adding more.
Why it matters
A focused interface increases adoption and reduces the chance of user error when artists use your asset.
