Common CI Pitfalls

  • Infrequent Commits

    Leads to large integration challenges and undermines the 'continuous' aspect.

  • Ignoring Build Failures

    A broken build should be a high-priority incident, not something to address later.

  • Insufficient Automated Tests

    Relying on manual testing after CI defeats the purpose of rapid feedback.

  • Slow or Unreliable CI Pipeline

    Frustrates developers and leads to workarounds that bypass the CI process.

Key Takeaway

Proactively identifying and addressing common CI pitfalls is crucial for maintaining a robust and effective continuous integration workflow.

Consequences of Ignoring Pitfalls

Increased Integration Conflicts

Larger change sets lead to more complex and time-consuming merges.

Delayed Defect Detection

Bugs are found later, making them more expensive and difficult to fix.

Unstable Main Branch

Loss of trust in the codebase, blocking other developers' work.

Reduced Developer Productivity

Frustration and wasted time waiting for or troubleshooting builds.

Example: The 'Broken Window' Theory

Just like a broken window in a building, if a broken build is left unfixed, it signals that no one cares, leading to further neglect and deterioration of the codebase's quality and stability over time.
