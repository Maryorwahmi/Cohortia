Core Concept

The Role of Artifacts in Release Pipelines

Bridging CI builds to CD deployments for continuous delivery.

Artifacts are the deployable units: These are the outputs of your build pipelines, such as compiled code, Docker images, or configuration files, ready for deployment.

Release pipelines consume artifacts: They are designed to take these artifacts and automate their deployment to various target environments.

Ensures reliability and consistency: This integration guarantees that tested components are delivered predictably, reducing deployment risks.

Key Takeaway

Artifact integration is the cornerstone of Continuous Delivery, automating the journey of software components from build to deployment.

Analogy

The Software Delivery Truck

Imagine your CI build as a factory producing finished goods (artifacts). The release pipeline is the delivery truck that picks up these goods and transports them safely to various warehouses (environments). This systematic process ensures the right products reach the right destinations reliably.

Common Mistake

Manual Component Handling

Relying on manual copying or ad-hoc fetching of build outputs for deployments. This introduces human error, inconsistency, and makes rollbacks or audits extremely difficult. Always automate artifact consumption.

Why it matters

Ensuring Reproducibility

Proper artifact integration ensures that every deployment is based on a known, versioned set of components. This eliminates 'works on my machine' issues and guarantees that what was tested is exactly what gets deployed.
