Preventing Over-Privilege and Ensuring Secret Rotation

Advanced security measures for robust secret management

Common Mistake

Over-privileged Service Principals

Granting "Contributor" or "Owner" roles at the subscription level to a Service Principal used by a service connection is a common and dangerous mistake. This violates the principle of least privilege.

# Example of over-privileged role assignment (BAD PRACTICE!) az role assignment create --role "Contributor" \ --assignee <SERVICE_PRINCIPAL_APP_ID> \ --scope "/subscriptions/<YOUR_SUBSCRIPTION_ID>"

Risk: If compromised, the Service Principal could gain control over the entire subscription, leading to widespread damage.

Key Takeaway

Adhere to the principle of least privilege for service principals and implement regular secret rotation to minimize the impact of potential compromises and enhance overall security.

Best Practice

Regular Secret Rotation

Secrets should be rotated regularly to minimize the impact of a potential compromise. Azure Key Vault supports secret rotation, and your processes should incorporate this.

Automated Rotation
Reduced Compromise Impact

Other Safety Notes: Ensure strict access control on Key Vault itself. Never commit certificates or private keys to source control; store them in Key Vault.

Pro Tip

Implement Pipeline Policies

Consider implementing Azure DevOps pipeline policies or static analysis tools to automatically detect and flag common security mistakes, such as hardcoded secrets or `echo` commands that might expose sensitive variables in logs. This adds an extra layer of automated protection.
