The Error Budget: Balancing Reliability and Innovation

Making Informed Trade-offs Between Stability and Speed

What is an Error Budget?

The Error Budget is the allowable amount of unreliability (downtime or errors) that a service can incur over a specific period, derived directly from its Service Level Objective (SLO).

  • •Derived from SLO: If SLO is 99.9% availability, error budget is 0.1% unavailability.
  • •Quantitative Limit: Provides a clear, measurable boundary for acceptable failures.
  • •Strategic Tool: Guides decisions on prioritizing new features vs. reliability work.
Key Takeaway

The error budget provides a data-driven way to balance innovation and reliability, ensuring that teams make conscious trade-offs to meet user expectations.

Error Budget in Practice

Within Budget: Innovate

Teams can take more risks, deploy new features, and experiment more aggressively.

Exceeding Budget: Prioritize Reliability

Teams must halt new feature development and focus solely on improving reliability, fixing bugs, or reducing technical debt.

Benefit: Prevents Over-Engineering

Avoids the costly and often unnecessary pursuit of 100% reliability, focusing efforts where they matter most.
