Module 4 › Chapter 3.4 › ConstraintLayout — what and why
Definition

ConstraintLayout

A ViewGroup that arranges child views using explicit positional constraints, guidelines, and chains to form flat hierarchies.

Guideline
An invisible anchor at a percentage or fixed offset used to align views consistently across sizes.
Chain
Distributes space between a sequence of views, supporting packed, spread, or weighted alignment modes.
Common mistake
Missing either a horizontal or vertical constraint causes a view to be placed at the top-left corner at runtime.
Key Takeaway

Think in relationships: every view needs at least one horizontal and one vertical constraint to behave predictably.

Analogy
Imagine a stage where each actor is given ropes that tie them to other actors or stage edges. ConstraintLayout is the rigging system; constraints are the ropes that keep each actor positioned no matter the audience size.
Example XML
<androidx.constraintlayout.widget.ConstraintLayout
    xmlns:android="http://schemas.android.com/apk/res/android"
    xmlns:app="http://schemas.android.com/apk/res-auto"
    android:layout_width="match_parent"
    android:layout_height="match_parent">

    <TextView
        android:id="@+id/titleTextView"
        android:layout_width="wrap_content"
        android:layout_height="wrap_content"
        android:text="Welcome!"
        android:textSize="32sp"
        app:layout_constraintTop_toTopOf="parent"
        app:layout_constraintStart_toStartOf="parent"
        app:layout_constraintEnd_toEndOf="parent"
        android:layout_marginTop="64dp" />

</androidx.constraintlayout.widget.ConstraintLayout>
