Network Evasion

Reverse Shell vs. Bind Shell

Understanding the difference in connection initiation is crucial for comprehending how attackers bypass network security controls.

Bind Shell

Victim Listens, Attacker Connects

  • Connection: Attacker initiates connection to victim.
  • Victim Action: Opens a listening port and waits.
  • Firewall Impact: Often blocked by inbound firewall rules.
  • Use Case: Less common in modern client-side attacks due to firewalls.
Inbound Connection
Reverse Shell

Victim Connects, Attacker Listens

  • Connection: Victim initiates connection to attacker.
  • Victim Action: Makes an outbound connection.
  • Firewall Impact: More likely to bypass outbound firewall rules.
  • Use Case: Preferred for client-side attacks to evade detection.
Outbound Connection
Key Takeaway

Reverse shells are more effective in bypassing firewalls due to their outbound connection nature, making them a staple in client-side exploitation.
