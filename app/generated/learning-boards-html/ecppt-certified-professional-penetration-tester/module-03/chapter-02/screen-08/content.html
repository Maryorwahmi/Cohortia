Reverse vs. Bind Shells

Establishing communication with the target

Reverse Shell

A shell initiated from the target machine back to the attacker's machine. The victim 'calls home'.

  • • Commonly used to bypass firewalls.
  • • Attacker sets up a listener.
  • • More reliable in most scenarios.
Practical Example

A victim behind a corporate firewall executes a payload that connects back to your Kali Linux machine on port 4444.

Bind Shell

A shell where the target machine opens a listening port, and the attacker connects to it.

  • • Requires an open incoming port on the target.
  • • Often blocked by firewalls.
  • • Less common in modern networks.
Limitation

If the target's firewall blocks incoming connections, a bind shell will fail, making it unsuitable for many real-world scenarios.

Key Takeaway

Reverse shells are generally preferred due to their ability to bypass firewalls by initiating an outbound connection from the target.
