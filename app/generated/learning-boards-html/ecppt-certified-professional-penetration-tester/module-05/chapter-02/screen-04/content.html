Understanding Privilege Escalation

Moving from Low-Privilege User to Root on Linux

What is Privilege Escalation?

It's the process of increasing your access level on a system, typically from a standard user to an administrator (root on Linux). This is a critical step in a penetration test to gain full control.

Key Takeaway

Privilege escalation is essential for achieving complete system control after initial access, transforming limited user access into full administrative power.

Why it Matters

  • Full System Control: Root access allows for complete manipulation of the system.
  • Data Exfiltration: Access to sensitive files and databases.
  • Persistence: Ability to establish backdoors and maintain access.
  • Lateral Movement: Pivoting to other systems within the network.

SUID & SGID Explained

SUID (Set User ID)

Executable runs with the permissions of its owner, regardless of the executing user. If owner is root, it runs as root.

SGID (Set Group ID)

Executable runs with the permissions of its group owner. Useful for shared directories but can be exploited.

Common Mistake

Blindly attempting to exploit every SUID binary. Many are legitimate and secure; research is key.
