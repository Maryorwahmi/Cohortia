IDS/IPS Evasion: Encryption and Tunneling

Hiding malicious traffic within encrypted channels.

Encryption as a Shield

  • **Payload Obscurity:** Encrypted traffic prevents IDS/IPS from inspecting the actual payload content.
  • **Resource Intensive Decryption:** SSL/TLS decryption is costly for IDS/IPS, often not applied to all traffic.
  • **Trusted Protocols:** Leveraging protocols like HTTPS (SSL/TLS) or SSH, which are inherently encrypted.
  • **Blends with Legitimate Traffic:** Encrypted traffic is common and expected, making malicious activity harder to spot.
Key Principle

If the IDS/IPS cannot see the content, it cannot detect signatures or anomalies within the payload.

Tunneling Techniques

  • **Custom Protocols:** Encapsulating malicious traffic within a custom, less common protocol.
  • **Common Port Tunneling:** Tunneling over standard, allowed ports like 80 (HTTP) or 443 (HTTPS).
  • **DNS Tunneling:** Exfiltrating data or establishing C2 channels over DNS queries.
  • **ICMP Tunneling:** Using ICMP echo requests/replies to tunnel data.
Example

Using an SSH tunnel over port 443 to bypass firewall rules and IDS inspection for C2 traffic.

Key Takeaway

Encryption and tunneling are highly effective IDS/IPS bypass methods, as they prevent deep packet inspection by obscuring malicious payloads within trusted communication channels or disguising them as legitimate traffic on common ports.
