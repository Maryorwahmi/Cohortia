Understanding Reconnaissance Phases: Passive vs. Active
Passive Reconnaissance

Gathering information without direct interaction with the target system. This minimizes the risk of detection and is often the first step.

  • Low risk of detection.
  • Utilizes publicly available information.
  • Examples: DNS queries, public search engines, social media.
  • Provides a broad overview of the target.
Key Takeaway

Reconnaissance is the essential first step, providing a detailed map of the target before any direct attacks are launched.

Active Reconnaissance

Involves direct interaction with the target system. While more intrusive, it yields specific and up-to-date information.

  • Higher risk of detection.
  • Directly probes the target's infrastructure.
  • Examples: Port scanning, directory enumeration, technology fingerprinting.
  • Provides granular details for targeted attacks.
Why it matters

Choosing the right reconnaissance technique at the right time minimizes risk and maximizes the effectiveness of your penetration test.
