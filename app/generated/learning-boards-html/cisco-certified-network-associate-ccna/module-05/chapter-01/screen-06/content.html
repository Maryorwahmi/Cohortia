Social Engineering Steps
Recognize the pattern: lure → interaction → compromise.
Phishing
Deceptive emails or sites that ask for credentials. Defenses: email filtering, link scanning, MFA.
Pretexting
Fabricated scenarios to obtain information. Defenses: verification procedures and policy-driven disclosure limits.
Baiting
Offers that entice users to install malicious content. Defenses: restricted installs and user training.
Concrete example
A fake invoice email asks an employee to open an attachment. The attachment installs spyware. If the employee was trained to verify invoices and the system blocked unapproved attachments, the attack would fail.
Prevention stack
  • Email filtering and attachment scanning.
  • Multifactor authentication on critical accounts.
  • Regular security awareness training and reporting channels.
Misconception
Relying only on technology is insufficient. Users need clear policies and practice to spot deceptive requests.
Key Takeaway

Combine filtering, MFA, and training. Human verification rules and reporting procedures make social engineering much less effective.
