Module 5 › Chapter 5.1 › Availability & DoS Mitigation
Availability Controls
Redundancy, backups, and tested recovery keep services available.
Redundancy
Duplicate critical components so a failure does not stop service.
Backups & recovery
Offline, regular backups plus recovery plans restore operations after corruption or loss.
DoS/DDoS defenses
Filter, rate-limit, and absorb traffic to keep services reachable for legitimate users.
Concrete example
A hardware failure takes a server offline. With redundancy and tested failover, traffic shifts to a standby system and downtime is minimal. If the outage is caused by ransomware encrypting data, offline backups allow restoration without paying ransom.
Misconception
Focusing only on hardware redundancy misses the need for backups and tested recovery workflows.
Key Takeaway

Design for failure: redundancy, backups, and playbooks reduce downtime and speed recovery from outages or attacks.
