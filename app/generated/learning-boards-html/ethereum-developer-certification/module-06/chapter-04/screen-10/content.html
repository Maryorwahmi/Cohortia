Lock & mint pattern
A contract on Chain A locks tokens. A relayer or bridge operator observes the event and triggers minting of a wrapped token on Chain B. Reverse flow burns the wrapped token and releases the original on Chain A.
Example: to send 100 tokens from Ethereum to a sidechain, tokens are transferred to the bridge contract on Ethereum and the sidechain mints 100 wrapped tokens for the recipient.
Bridge types & risks
  • Canonical: official, project-maintained bridges.
  • Third-party: broader chain support but varying trust.
  • Centralized: custodial risks and single point of failure.
  • Decentralized: aim for trust minimization but can be complex.
Security Note

Bridges hold value and therefore deserve extra scrutiny: audit reports, bug bounties, and operational transparency matter.
