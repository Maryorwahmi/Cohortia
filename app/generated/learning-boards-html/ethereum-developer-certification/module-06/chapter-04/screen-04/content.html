Module 6 › Chapter 6.4 › Why L2s are needed
Definition
Layer 2s are protocols that process transactions off-chain, bundle them, and post compressed summaries or proofs to the Ethereum mainnet to scale throughput and lower fees.
  • They increase throughput by batching transactions.
  • They reduce per-transaction cost by sharing L1 settlement across many operations.
  • Security depends on whether the L2 posts data or proofs to L1.
Concrete example
An L2 batches 1,000 micro-payments, posts a single summary to L1, and users pay a small share of the L1 fee instead of full per-transaction gas.
Key Takeaway

L2s preserve L1 settlement while making interactions cheaper and faster for users.

Common misconceptions
Moving work off-chain always reduces security. Not always — some L2s publish full data or validity proofs to L1, which maintains strong settlement guarantees. Sidechains, however, accept different security assumptions.
Throughput vs Security: trade-offs matter for design choices.
User cost: L2s can make frequent interactions feasible.
Withdrawal latency: some designs add delays or challenge periods.
Why this fills the frame: developers must weigh costs, finality, and attack models when selecting where to run a DApp.
