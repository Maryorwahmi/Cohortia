Module 06 › Chapter 6.5 › What is a DAO?
Definition

DAO as smart-contract organization

A DAO encodes rules on-chain so members can propose, vote, and trigger automated actions. This reduces trust on a central operator and makes on-chain records auditable.

Principle
Decentralization
Distributed decision making avoids single points of control.
Principle
Transparency
Proposals and votes are public and auditable on-chain.
Principle
Autonomy
Approved actions execute automatically via contract logic.
Principle
Community Governance
Members steer the organization through proposals and votes.
Analogy & example

Club / co-op analogy

Imagine a coop where the bylaws say how funds are spent. In a DAO those bylaws are smart contracts: a passed vote automatically releases funds or upgrades code.

Concrete scenario

A grant proposal requests 10 ETH. Token holders vote; if the proposal passes and any timelock expires, the contract executes the transfer to the grantee.

Common mistake
Assuming code removes social coordination. DAOs still need off-chain discussion and norms.
Key takeaway
A DAO is both code and community; both must be designed together.
