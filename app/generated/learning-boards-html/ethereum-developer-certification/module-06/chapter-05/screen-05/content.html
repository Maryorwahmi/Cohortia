Module 06 › Chapter 6.5 › Token-based governance
Claim

Voting power follows token holdings

Token-based models assign votes in proportion to holdings. This creates financial alignment but can concentrate control.

Simple majority
Easy and on-chain, but vulnerable to whale dominance when tokens are unevenly distributed.
Quadratic voting
Costs scale quadratically to reduce marginal influence of large holders and favor broad preference intensity.
Delegated voting
Token holders delegate voting to trusted addresses, enabling representation without transferring tokens.
Example & mitigation
Example

If one address holds 40% of tokens, a simple majority vote can be shaped by that holder. Applying quadratic voting or vote caps reduces that holder’s effective influence.

Common misconception
Delegation always decentralizes power. In practice, delegates can accumulate power and recreate concentration.
Pro-tip
Combine mechanisms: require token lock-ins, use quadratic weights for expressive ballots, and enable transparent delegate accountability.
