Module 1 › Chapter 1.1 › What is a Blockchain?
Core definition
A distributed, immutable ledger recording transactions across a network of nodes, so no single participant controls the history.
Analogy
Shared notebook
Pages are blocks; bound pages are permanent; identical copies live with many participants.
Common misconception
Blockchain is not synonymous with complete anonymity. Most public chains are pseudonymous; participant identifiers are visible even if real names are not.
Key Takeaway
Distribution plus cryptographic linking yields a ledger that is tamper-evident and verifiable by participants.
Structure metaphor
Example: Supply-chain notebook
Each shipment entry includes origin, timestamp, and handler. Multiple parties verify the notebook pages to ensure provenance and authenticity.
Why it matters
Removing a central auditor reduces single points of failure and enables transparent verification across organizations.
