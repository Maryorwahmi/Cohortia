Mitigating the 'God Object' Risk

Strategies for a focused and effective mediator.

Best Practices

Keeping the Mediator Focused

  • Delegate Logic: The mediator should coordinate, not perform, complex business logic. Delegate specific actions back to the Colleague objects.
  • Break Down: If a mediator becomes too large, consider decomposing the system into smaller, more focused mediators.
  • Use Events for Simpler Cases: For very simple, decoupled notifications, an event-driven approach might be a lighter alternative.
Key Takeaway

A well-designed mediator focuses solely on coordination, delegating specific tasks to colleagues to maintain loose coupling and prevent over-complexity.

Other Considerations

Performance and Safety

  • Performance Overhead: The extra layer of indirection can introduce a slight performance cost, but typically negligible for application-level interactions.
  • Dynamic Colleagues: Ensure robust mechanisms for adding or removing colleagues dynamically.
  • Circular Dependencies: Carefully define communication flow to avoid infinite loops between mediator and colleagues.
Practical Advice

Prioritize maintainability and flexibility over micro-optimizations, and always design for clear, unidirectional communication flows.
