Best Practice
How the principles fit together
Use ISP to create role-specific interfaces, LSP to keep base types minimal and substitutable, and DIP to decouple high-level modules by depending on those interfaces. This reduces runtime surprises and makes testing and changing implementations straightforward.
ISP → smaller, clearer interfaces
Prevents unnecessary method implementations.
LSP → safe inheritance
Ensure substitutability by keeping base types honest.
DIP → inject abstractions
High-level logic depends on interfaces, not concrete classes.
Scenario comparison
If you see NotSupportedExceptions, large interfaces, and many new expressions in business classes, you likely have combined ISP, LSP, and DIP violations. Fixing one often helps the others: splitting interfaces reduces false inheritance, and injecting abstractions removes the need to fake implementations.
Check your understanding
Can you point to a class in your codebase that constructs its own dependencies and also implements methods that throw NotSupportedException? That class is a candidate for these refactors.
