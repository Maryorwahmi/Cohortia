OLTP vs OLAP
Compare design goals, workloads, and examples
OLTP — transactional systems
  • Optimized for concurrent inserts, updates, and short reads.
  • Focus on ACID transactions and low latency.
  • Example: order entry, inventory updates, user sessions.
When to use: operational systems with many small writes.
OLAP — analytical warehouses
  • Optimized for large scans, aggregations, and complex joins.
  • Focus on historical, time-variant data, and fast reporting.
  • Example: sales trend reports, cohort analysis, business intelligence.
When to use: reporting, analytics, and long-running queries.
Misconception
A single database cannot usually excel at both high-throughput transactions and large-scale analytics simultaneously without architectural separation.
Key Takeaway

Match storage to workload: don't run heavy aggregations on production OLTP without replicas or an analytical store.
