Process Flow

When a Datanode fails

Detection: Namenode notices the missing Datanode via heartbeat absence.
Assessment: Namenode identifies which block replicas are now under-replicated.
Recovery: Namenode commands healthy Datanodes to copy replicas until the desired factor is reached.
Operator note
Re-replication consumes network and disk resources, so monitor cluster load during recovery and ensure enough spare capacity to rebuild replicas promptly.
Example scenario
If one Datanode holding a replica of a 128 MB block fails, the Namenode will see the block now has fewer than three replicas and will schedule another Datanode to copy one of the remaining replicas so the replication factor is restored.
Pitfall
Relying solely on replication without alerts can delay detection of multiple simultaneous failures that could reduce redundancy below safe levels.
Key Takeaway

HDFS self-heals by re-replicating from healthy copies, but operational planning and monitoring are required to keep the cluster resilient during failures.
