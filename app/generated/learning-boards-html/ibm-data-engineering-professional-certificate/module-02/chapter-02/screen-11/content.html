Module 02 › Chapter 2.2 › HAVING Clause
Definition

HAVING: filter by aggregates

Use HAVING when your filter depends on aggregate results produced by GROUP BY.

Examples
-- Departments with average salary > 70000
SELECT DepartmentID, AVG(Salary) AS AverageDepartmentSalary
FROM Employees
GROUP BY DepartmentID
HAVING AVG(Salary) > 70000;

-- Departments with more than 1 employee
SELECT DepartmentID, COUNT(*) AS NumberOfEmployees
FROM Employees
GROUP BY DepartmentID
HAVING COUNT(*) > 1;
Guidance
Use WHERE to reduce rows first, then GROUP BY, then HAVING to filter the grouped summaries. This order ensures correctness and efficiency.
Concrete example
Find departments with average salary above 70000, then order by average salary and limit to top results for a report.
Common misconception
Trying to write HAVING conditions inside WHERE or expecting WHERE to see aggregate values—this will not work.
Key Takeaway

HAVING filters group-level summaries; use it after GROUP BY to restrict aggregated results.
