Module 02 › Chapter 2.2 › Logical Operators
Process

Combine conditions safely

Build filters step-by-step and use parentheses to make precedence explicit so results match your intent.

Examples
-- Hired in 2022 AND Salary > 70000
SELECT FirstName, LastName, HireDate, Salary
FROM Employees
WHERE HireDate BETWEEN '2022-01-01' AND '2022-12-31' AND Salary > 70000;

-- Named Alice OR earning less than 70000
SELECT FirstName, LastName, Salary
FROM Employees
WHERE FirstName = 'Alice' OR Salary < 70000;

-- NOT hired in 2022
SELECT FirstName, LastName, HireDate
FROM Employees
WHERE NOT (HireDate BETWEEN '2022-01-01' AND '2022-12-31');
Guidance
When combining OR with AND, always add parentheses to group OR terms if you want them evaluated together. Test each sub-condition independently before combining.
Concrete example
To get Sales or Marketing staff who earn >50k, write (Department='Sales' OR Department='Marketing') AND Salary > 50000.
Common mistake
Omitting parentheses can include unintended rows, e.g., returning all Sales employees plus only some Marketing employees.
Key Takeaway

Make precedence explicit with parentheses to ensure your combined conditions yield the intended rows.
