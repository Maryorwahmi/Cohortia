Module 8 › Chapter 8.2 › Regions & AZs
What they are
A Region is a geographical area made of multiple Availability Zones. AZs are isolated data centers with redundant infrastructure connected by low-latency links.
Why it matters
  • Latency is proportional to distance; choose Regions near your users to minimize round-trip time.
  • Distribute across AZs to tolerate a single data center failure without losing the whole service.
  • AZs share low-latency connectivity, which supports synchronous replication and failover patterns.
Concrete example
An e-commerce site with customers in Europe deploys the storefront frontend and a replica of its database in an EU Region and spreads web servers across two AZs to avoid a single-AZ outage.
Key Takeaway

Regions determine geographic reach; AZs provide fault isolation inside a Region. Choose regions for reach, AZs for resilience.

Common mistake
Assuming AZ spread removes cross-continent latency. It doesn't—use regional placement to reduce long-haul delays.
