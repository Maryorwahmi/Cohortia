Module 8 › Chapter 8.1 › Network Performance Metrics
Metric definitions
  • Latency: time for a packet roundtrip or one-way delay, impacts interactivity and API responsiveness.
  • Throughput: sustained data transfer rate, important for bulk transfers and downloads.
  • Jitter: variation in packet timing, critical for audio and video streams.
  • Packet loss: percent of packets dropped, causes retransmissions and reduced effective throughput.
Example scenarios
Video conferencing: prioritize low latency and low jitter even if throughput is moderate.
Nightly backup: prioritize high throughput; latency is less critical.
Key Takeaway

Measure the metric that maps to user experience and test under realistic conditions.

Visual mnemonic
Imagine a postal analogy: latency is the delivery time for a single letter, throughput is how many letters fit on a truck, jitter is an unreliable courier who delivers at irregular intervals, and packet loss is letters lost in transit. This helps decide whether you need faster trucks, more trucks, or more reliable routing.
Common misconception
Upgrading an instance or bandwidth may increase throughput without reducing latency or jitter. Always confirm which metric your change affects with measurements.
