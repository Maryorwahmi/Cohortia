GENEVE Protocol: The Key to Transparency

How GWLB preserves original packet information for inspection.

1. Original Packet

Client sends standard IP packet.

2. GWLB Encapsulates

Packet wrapped in GENEVE (UDP 6081) header.

3. Appliance Decapsulates & Inspects

Appliance sees original packet, applies rules.

4. Appliance Re-encapsulates

Original packet (potentially modified) is re-wrapped.

5. GWLB Decapsulates & Forwards

GWLB sends original packet to its final destination.

Key Takeaway

GENEVE is fundamental to GWLB, enabling transparent inspection by preserving original packet headers throughout the service chaining process.

Why GENEVE Matters

Preserving Packet Integrity

  • Full Context: Appliances receive the complete, unmodified original packet, including source/destination IPs and all headers. This is crucial for accurate policy enforcement and logging.
  • No NAT Issues: Avoids the complexities and limitations of Network Address Translation (NAT) that often arise with traditional 'bump-in-the-wire' deployments.
  • Simplified Appliance Logic: Virtual appliances don't need to be aware of the GWLB or perform complex routing; they simply process the original packet.
  • Seamless Integration: The client and target application remain unaware that traffic has passed through an inspection device.
Analogy

Mail in an Envelope

Think of GENEVE like putting an important letter (your original IP packet) inside a special, transparent envelope (the GENEVE header). You send this envelope to a security office (your virtual appliance). The office opens the envelope, reads the original letter, perhaps stamps it 'approved,' then puts it back in the *same* envelope and sends it back. The post office (GWLB) then removes the envelope and delivers the original letter to its recipient, who never knew it was inspected. The original letter's content and addressing remain untouched.
