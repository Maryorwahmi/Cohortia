Associations & Propagations

Two actions define how attachments and route tables share route information and decide forwarding behavior.

Step 1
Create attachments for your VPCs, VPNs, or Direct Connect Gateways.
Step 2
Associate attachments to TGW route tables to choose the routing context for incoming traffic.
Step 3
Enable propagation so that attachment CIDRs or learned routes are advertised into chosen route tables.
Example scenario
App attachments associated with Route Table A. Shared-services attachment propagates its CIDR into Route Table A so apps reach shared services without extra static routes.
Common mistake
Enabling broad propagation without careful associations can expose internal networks across attachments unintentionally.
Pro tip
Group attachments by trust level and create separate TGW route tables for inspection vs. production traffic to minimize blast radius.
