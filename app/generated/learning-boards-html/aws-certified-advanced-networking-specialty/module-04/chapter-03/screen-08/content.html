BGP role in TGW
BGP exchanges dynamic routes between TGW and on-premises via VPN or Direct Connect attachments.
Concrete example
Your on-prem router advertises 192.168.200.0/24 to the TGW over BGP. If propagation is enabled into TGW-RT-Hybrid, that prefix will appear pointing at the VPN attachment.
Misconception
BGP learned routes are not global across all TGW route tables unless propagated into them; propagation targets control visibility.
Operational check
Verify BGP adjacency state and that the Amazon Side ASN does not conflict with your on-prem ASN to prevent session failures.
Key Takeaway

Use BGP for dynamic hybrid routing, but control propagation and check ASN settings to keep route visibility intentional and safe.

Quick troubleshooting steps
  1. Confirm BGP session state is established on both sides.
  2. Inspect advertised prefixes from on-prem to ensure expected subnets are present.
  3. Check TGW Propagations to see where those prefixes were placed.
Scenario
If on-premises reports it cannot reach a VPC subnet, verify TGW advertised that VPC CIDR to the on-prem router and that the on-prem router accepted the prefix in its BGP table.
