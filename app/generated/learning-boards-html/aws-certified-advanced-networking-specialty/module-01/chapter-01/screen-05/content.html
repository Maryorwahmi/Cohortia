Module 1 › Chapter 1.1 › Subnet Design
Process

Design subnets per availability zone

Step 1 — Map requirements
Decide which services require public access and which must remain private. Record latency and AZ redundancy requirements.
Step 2 — Allocate per-AZ CIDRs
Split the VPC CIDR into per-AZ subnets sized for expected host counts and growth.
Step 3 — Apply routing and boundaries
Assign route tables for public and private subnets and attach NAT Gateways or egress patterns as required.
Example subnet map
VPC: 10.30.0.0/16
Public-AZ1:  10.30.1.0/24  (Load balancer)
Public-AZ2:  10.30.2.0/24  (Bastion)
Private-App-AZ1: 10.30.10.0/24
Private-App-AZ2: 10.30.11.0/24
Private-DB-AZ1: 10.30.20.0/24
Private-DB-AZ2: 10.30.21.0/24
Misconception

Smaller subnets always increase security. In practice, subnets that are too small waste time on addressing and reduce usable IPs because AWS reserves addresses; size subnets for realistic growth.

Key Takeaway

Design subnets per AZ, choose sizes for growth, and separate public and private responsibilities to simplify routing and security.
