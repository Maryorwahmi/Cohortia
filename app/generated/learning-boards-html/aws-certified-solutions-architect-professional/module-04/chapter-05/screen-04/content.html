Core Concept

Why large-data moves differ

Scale changes the decision points: throughput, validation, security, and cost become primary design constraints.

Bandwidth limits
Network constraints can extend timelines; measure effective throughput and include overhead.
Integrity & validation
Checksums and validation reduce the risk of silent corruption at scale.
Cost & schedule trade-offs
Network vs. device shipment costs, and the opportunity cost of delayed availability, must be compared.
Key Takeaway

Measure effective throughput, run a pilot, and choose network or physical transfer based on measured constraints, not assumptions.

Concrete example
If a migration team has a 50 Mbps upload and needs 75 TB in two weeks, the network-only transfer is impractical; a Snowball Edge device sized for 80 TB may be the correct choice because it removes bandwidth constraints.
Misconception
Assuming standard copy tools are sufficient for massive moves is risky. Tools optimized for scale like DataSync, and physical devices, address throughput and integrity differently than rsync or ad-hoc scripts.
Check your understanding
  • Identify your bandwidth and estimate realistic throughput.
  • Plan checksum validation before and after transfer.
  • Decide if physical shipment or network transfer meets the timeline and budget.
Why it matters
At scale, a wrong choice wastes weeks and thousands of dollars. Use measurement and pilots to avoid guessing.
