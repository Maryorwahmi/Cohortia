Module 11 › Chapter 11.1 › Regions & Availability Zones
Definition

A Region is a geographic area containing multiple AZs. An AZ is one or more discrete data centers with redundant systems and independent failure domains.

Why it matters
  • AZ separation preserves availability during localized failures.
  • Close AZs enable low-latency replication and synchronous services.
  • Regions provide fault isolation and data residency control.
Key Takeaway: Place stateful systems across AZs and pick Regions that balance latency, compliance, and service availability.
Concrete example — multi-AZ database
Host the primary database in AZ-A and a synchronous standby in AZ-B within the same Region. Use cross-AZ replication and load balancing for stateless application servers to survive a single AZ failure without data loss.
Misconception
AZs are not identical to Regions. You cannot assume AZs are immune to regional disasters, so critical resilience requires cross-region planning for extreme events.
Pro tip
Design stateless services to scale across AZs and put durable state in multi-AZ services or across Regions when required for disaster recovery.
