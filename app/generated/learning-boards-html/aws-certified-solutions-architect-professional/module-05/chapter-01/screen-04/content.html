Module 5 › Chapter 5.1 › BC vs DR
Definition and scope
Clarify expectations: who must work, what systems must run, and which data must be preserved.
Business Continuity
Covers people, facilities, supply chain and mission-critical processes beyond IT.
Disaster Recovery
Subset of BC focused on restoring IT infrastructure and data so business functions can resume.
Analogy: Business continuity is the full fire drill and communications plan; disaster recovery is the plan to restore servers, databases, and connectivity after the fire. Both are needed and must be coordinated.
Key Takeaway

Treat DR as the IT arm of BC. Define priorities with business stakeholders so recovery work restores what actually matters first.

Practical example
A retail company may need tills to accept payments and a customer service desk to operate. The BC plan ensures staff and premises are available; the DR plan restores the payment processing backend and database replication so tills function again. If engineers only restore backups but staff cannot access systems, the BC goal is not met.
Common misconception: DR is not only backups. Backups are essential, but DR requires orchestration, provisioning templates, and runbooks to turn backups into running services in the recovery window.
Check your understanding
  • Is personnel relocation part of DR or BC?
  • What would you restore first for an order service: database or static site?
