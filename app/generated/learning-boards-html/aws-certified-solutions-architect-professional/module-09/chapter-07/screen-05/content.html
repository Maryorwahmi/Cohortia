Core Concept › Rolling Updates
How it works

New instances or containers are launched in small batches, receive traffic, and then old ones are terminated as the rollout proceeds.

Pros
  • Simple to implement and requires no duplicate production environment.
  • Lower infrastructure cost compared to Blue/Green.
Cons
  • Partial user impact if the new version is faulty.
  • Rollback can be complex and slower than Blue/Green.
Concrete example

An ECS service updates 10 tasks, replacing 2 at a time. If a new image introduces latency, 2 tasks at a time will show the issue until the rollout is paused or rolled back.

Common mistake

Assuming that a partial rollback is as simple as switching traffic — in reality you must coordinate replacing newer tasks with older images across all availability zones.

Key Takeaway

Use rolling updates for low-risk, frequent changes but ensure strong monitoring and a tested rollback process.
