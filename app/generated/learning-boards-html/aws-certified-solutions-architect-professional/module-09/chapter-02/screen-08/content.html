Step, Simple, and Scheduled Scaling
Behavioral differences and recommended combinations
Step Scaling
Adjust capacity in steps based on alarm thresholds. Good for tiered response where small breaches cause small adds and big breaches cause larger adds.
Simple Scaling
Waits for previous action to finish before triggering again. Easier to reason about but slower to react.
Scheduled Scaling
Pre-scale for predictable events, like promotions or scheduled releases. Combine with reactive policies for surprises.
Concrete scenario
For a ticket release: scheduled scaling raises capacity an hour before release; target tracking responds to request spikes during the release; step policies can add larger steps if latency climbs rapidly.
Mistake to avoid
Short cooldowns or aggressive thresholds lead to flapping—oscillating adds and removes that waste cost and destabilize the system.
