Module 9 › Chapter 9.3 › Asynchronous Communication
Definition

Send messages without blocking

Asynchronous communication allows a producer to emit messages and resume work while consumers process them later. This reduces coupling and isolates failures.

Concrete example

A checkout service publishes an "OrderPlaced" message and returns success to the user. Inventory and billing consume the message independently and retry on failure.

Common misconception

Asynchronous does not mean unreliable. Use durable queues, retries, DLQs, and idempotent consumers to ensure correctness.

Key Takeaway

Decouple timing: let producers move on and handle retries and errors in the consumer domain to increase resilience.

Pattern map
When you decouple with messages, consider four implementation concerns: delivery guarantees, ordering, durability, and consumer idempotency. This card set helps you reason about tradeoffs.
Delivery
At-least-once or exactly-once affects whether consumers must deduplicate.
Ordering
Use FIFO queues or Kinesis shards when exact sequence matters.
Durability
Persist messages until consumers confirm processing; DLQs capture failures.
Idempotency
Design consumers to handle duplicate deliveries safely when needed.
