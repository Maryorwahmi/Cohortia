Module 9 › Chapter 9.6 › Resilience — failure is inevitable
Definition & mindset
Design choices that contain faults and enable recovery.
• Failures are normal; design for detection and containment.
• Bound blast radius by decoupling, timeouts, and isolation.
• Redundancy without isolation can amplify failures.
• Measure resilience with controlled failure injection and metrics.
Key Takeaway

Resilience is proactive design: isolate, detect, and recover to keep essential functions running.

Analogy: ship bulkheads
Compartments keep a breach from sinking the vessel. Bulkheads in software isolate failures so a single fault does not collapse the service.
Example: thumbnail pipeline
Timeouts on metadata calls, retries to S3, and circuit breakers around metadata service prevent a single slowdown from failing all thumbnails.
Common mistake
Relying only on retries without idempotency and backoff can create duplicates and overload downstream services.
