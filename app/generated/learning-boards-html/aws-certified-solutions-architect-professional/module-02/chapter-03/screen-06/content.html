Module 2 › Chapter 2.3 › Geolocation vs Geoproximity
Geolocation routing
Directs users based on their geographic region. Best for localized content or legal compliance.
Pros
  • Predictable country/continent routing
  • Useful for content localization
Cons
  • IP-to-location mapping can be imperfect
  • Not a substitute for formal data residency controls
Geoproximity routing
Considers user and resource locations and allows a bias to shift traffic toward favored resources.
Pros
  • Fine control over traffic distribution by distance
  • Bias lets you prefer a specific region
Cons
  • Bias can unintentionally overload a region if not monitored
Concrete example
Prefer us-east by adding a positive bias even though eu-west is closer for some users; monitor latency and adjust bias if latency increases.
Common mistake
Using geolocation to enforce legal residency without validating registrar and data controls — geolocation alone is not a legal boundary.
Key Takeaway

Choose geolocation for user-based delivery and geoproximity with bias when you need to nudge traffic toward preferred regions, and test thoroughly.
