Module 6 › Chapter 6.1 › Why multi-account
Definition and purpose

A multi-account strategy divides workloads and responsibilities across accounts to contain risk and simplify governance.

Isolation
Limits the blast radius of security incidents or failures.
Governance
Centralized rules can be applied while teams retain account-level control.
Billing & Ownership
Clear cost centers and ownership for auditing and chargeback.
Concrete example
Production account: holds live services and strict SCPs Development account: sandbox for engineers Security account: collects CloudTrail and GuardDuty findings
Common misconception

Some teams fear too many accounts add overhead. The real risk is poorly planned accounts. Use automation and templates for baselines so additional accounts do not increase operational cost significantly.

Key Takeaway

Design accounts to map to operational or compliance boundaries; automate baselines to avoid manual drift.
