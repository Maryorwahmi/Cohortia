Definition › Encryption fundamentals
What is encryption at rest?
  • Protects stored bits on disks, object stores, and backups.
  • Depends on key control to prevent unauthorized decryption.
  • Often implemented by the storage service with KMS integration.
What is encryption in transit?
  • Protects data while moving over networks using TLS.
  • Prevents eavesdropping and tampering between endpoints.
  • Use ACM and load balancers or enforce TLS in client libraries.
Concrete example
A web app uploads reports to S3. Configure TLS for the upload endpoint and store the objects with SSE-KMS using a customer-managed key. This ensures safe transit and auditable key control for stored data.
Key Takeaway

Treat transit and rest as complementary protections and always verify both in your deployment tests.

Common mistake
Relying only on default service encryption without owning or auditing keys may fail compliance checks.
