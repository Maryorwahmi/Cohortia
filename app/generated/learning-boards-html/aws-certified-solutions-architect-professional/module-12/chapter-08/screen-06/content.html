Comparison › Graviton vs x86

When Graviton helps

  • • CPU-bound, parallel workloads often get better throughput per watt.
  • • Managed runtimes and modern toolchains usually support ARM without change.
  • • Lower energy per unit of work reduces emissions and cost when scaled.
Key Takeaway

Run benchmarks and compatibility tests. Efficiency gains must be measured in real workload terms to count as sustainability wins.

When x86 remains preferable

  • • Legacy binaries or closed-source dependencies that lack ARM builds.
  • • Specialized workloads tied to specific x86-optimized libraries.
  • • Migration costs or risk that outweigh measured efficiency benefits.
Concrete example

An analytics job on many cores ran 15% faster on M6g in benchmark tests, reducing total runtime and energy per job. But a legacy imaging pipeline required library changes and needed more validation before migrating.
