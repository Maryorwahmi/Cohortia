Definition
Service Control Policies
SCPs centrally limit the maximum permissions for accounts and OUs. They prevent actions before they occur.
Claim
An SCP defines what is allowed at most for an account or OU. It cannot grant additional permissions.
Concrete example
Attach an SCP to an OU that denies creating buckets in eu-west-2 to enforce data residency rules for all accounts in that OU.
Misuse to avoid
Do not rely on SCPs to grant access. Avoid overly broad denies that block legitimate administrative tasks; always validate in a sandbox.
Key Takeaway

Use SCPs as preventive guardrails and test them thoroughly. They shape the maximum permissions available across accounts.

Comparison: SCPs vs IAM
SCP
Prevents actions at the account or OU level. Does not grant permissions.
IAM Policy
Grants or denies permissions to identities. Effective permissions are intersection of IAM and SCP boundaries.
Design question
Which operations should be prevented organization-wide and which should be controlled per-role? Use that to split SCPs and IAM policies.
