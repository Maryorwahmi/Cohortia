Definition
Data Governance
Residency, encryption, access control, and lifecycle policies that protect data and satisfy regulations.
Residency
Use SCPs to deny resource creation outside permitted regions and register where data may be stored.
Encryption
KMS for managed keys; CloudHSM when you need FIPS 140-2 Level 3 validated HSMs and exclusive control.
Access Control
Lake Formation centralizes fine-grained permissions to avoid complex S3 policy sprawl.
Lifecycle
S3 Lifecycle Policies automate transitions and expirations to meet retention and cost needs.
Concrete policy example
Financial transactions: retain for seven years. Lifecycle action: first 365 days in S3 Standard, then transition to Glacier Deep Archive. Encryption: objects encrypted with a KMS CMK, and for the most sensitive keys use CloudHSM-backed key material.
Misconception
Encryption alone solves all compliance needs—false. You also need residency controls, key custody, access policies, and retention records.
Key Takeaway

Treat governance as four coordinated pillars and automate policies to keep the data lifecycle auditable.
