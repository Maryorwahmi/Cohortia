Module 1 › Chapter 1.3 › Why multi-account IAM is hard
Core Concept

Credential sprawl and cost

When identities are created per account, administrators repeat onboarding and offboarding tasks and struggle to keep permissions consistent.

Impact
Higher risk of stale or over-permissioned credentials, and increased audit burden.
Common mistake
Believing per-account users are inherently safer; inconsistent policies often make them less safe.
Key Takeaway

Inventory identities across accounts and centralize human access to reduce duplication and audit complexity.

Concrete example
A developer needs DevAccount-001 and ProdAccount-001 access. Without centralization they manage two different credentials or an ad-hoc set of cross-account role assumptions to switch contexts.
Centralization allows a single portal login and a permission set that grants the appropriate access in each account.
