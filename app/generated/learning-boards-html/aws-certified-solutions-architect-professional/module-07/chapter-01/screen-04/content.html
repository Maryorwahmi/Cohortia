Module 7 › Chapter 7.1 › FinOps
Definition

FinOps is cultural practice

Bring finance, operations, and engineering together to make trade-offs between speed, cost and quality with measurable accountability.

Architect responsibility

Design systems that are observable and cost-attributable so teams can make decisions quickly.

Concrete example

Compare managed database versus self-managed cluster considering hourly cost, operational effort and predictable spend.

Common misconception

Not just tooling—tools help, but process, ownership, and tagging make FinOps effective.

Value Flow
A simple governance loop to practice:
  1. Visibility: Instrument and collect cost data.
  2. Control: Apply budgets and tag policies.
  3. Reporting: Use CUR and dashboards for chargeback.
  4. Optimize: Make design changes and measure impact.
Key Takeaway

FinOps requires both organizational practice and tool-driven data; architects enable both by designing for measurement.
