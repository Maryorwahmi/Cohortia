Definition

SMART criteria and characteristics

Apply these checks to every requirement to make it implementation-ready and testable.

  • S
    Specific — avoid vague adjectives; define exact targets.
  • M
    Measurable — produce acceptance criteria and metrics.
  • A
    Achievable — feasible with available resources.
  • R
    Relevant — aligns to business goals and user value.
  • T
    Time-bound — set deadlines for non-functional targets when needed.
Example
Bad: "The application should be fast." Good: "The dashboard shall load within 2 seconds for 95% of sessions."
Key Takeaway

SMART plus verifiability and traceability turn wishes into work items you can implement and test.

Common misconceptions
Many teams assume that a signed requirement is final. In reality, validation is iterative — especially in agile contexts. Another mistake is treating subjective language like "intuitive" as acceptable. Replace it with measurable outcomes or usability tests.
Unambiguous
One interpretation only; avoid terms that mean different things to different stakeholders.
Traceable
Assign an ID and link requirement to design and tests in a traceability matrix.
Complete
Include acceptance criteria, preconditions, and success metrics where relevant.
Verifiable
If you cannot write a clear test for it, sharpen the requirement.
Quick exercise

Take one vague requirement from your project and rewrite it to include a measurable target and acceptance criteria. Consider feasibility before finalizing.
