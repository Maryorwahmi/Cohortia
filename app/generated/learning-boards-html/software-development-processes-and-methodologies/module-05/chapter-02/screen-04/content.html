Module 5 › Chapter 5.2 › What is Requirements Elicitation?
Definition

Requirements elicitation is the deliberate practice of engaging stakeholders through interviews, workshops, observation, surveys, and prototyping to uncover needs that guide design and development.

• Elicitation is iterative — expect to revisit requirements.
• Techniques reveal different kinds of information: interviews reveal motives, observation reveals actual steps.
• Validation through prototypes confirms assumptions early.
Concrete Example
A product manager runs a 15-minute interview with a power user who says they need faster reporting. Observing the user shows manual CSV exports and spreadsheets are the true bottleneck. The team prototypes a single click export and validates the improvement before specifying acceptance criteria.
Common mistake
Assuming a verbal requirement is complete without requesting examples or observing the user's environment.
Practical checklist
  • • Prepare open-ended questions and examples to prompt specifics.
  • • Use short prototyping to test assumptions quickly.
  • • Combine interviews with observation when possible.
  • • Record concrete scenarios and acceptance criteria.
Key Takeaway

Treat elicitation as iterative and mixed-method. Use concrete examples and prototypes to validate requirements early.
