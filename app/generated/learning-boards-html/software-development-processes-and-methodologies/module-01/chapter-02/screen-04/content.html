Module 1 › Chapter 1.2 › The Software Crisis
Definition and Symptoms
  • Projects missed delivery dates and exceeded budgets despite effort.
  • Products shipped with frequent, serious defects and poor usability.
  • Systems became hard to maintain; small changes caused regressions.
  • Stakeholder expectations and communications were inconsistent.
Common misconception

Believing the failures were only technical ignores human processes: lack of requirements, roles, and coordination were central contributors.

Key takeaway
Complexity without process produces unpredictability. Formal practices restore control.
A timeline of change
Early informal era
Small teams, minimal documentation, workable for small tasks.
Growth in complexity
Larger systems exposed coordination and estimation challenges.
Crisis symptoms
Missed deadlines, unstable releases, and maintainability problems.
Emergence of software engineering
Adoption of process, roles, and testing to manage complexity.
