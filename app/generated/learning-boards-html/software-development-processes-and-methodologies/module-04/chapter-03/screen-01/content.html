Introduction to Kanban

A practical guide to Kanban's philosophy, its four principles, and the six practices that help teams visualize and improve flow.

16:9 HD Classroom
What you will understand
How Kanban visualizes workflow and why WIP limits improve delivery.
Why it matters
Teams reduce delays and handoff friction by seeing work and enforcing limits.
Who benefits
Support, operations, maintenance, content teams, and anywhere priorities change often.
How we'll practice
Design a Kanban board with WIP limits and write explicit policies for key columns.
Explore Key Topics
Principles
Start with what you do now, evolve incrementally, respect roles, and encourage leadership.
Practices
Visualize workflow, set WIP limits, manage flow, and make policies explicit.
When to use Kanban
Best where work arrives unpredictably or continuous delivery is required.
Visual metaphor
Backlog
Ideas waiting to be pulled
In Development (WIP 3)
Limited work reduces context switching
Testing
Watch for pileups as bottlenecks
Done
Completed and ready to deliver
Traffic metaphor: lanes are stages, cars are work items; WIP limits prevent jams.
Key Takeaway

Kanban brings visibility and focus. Start small, measure lead and cycle time, and evolve your process experimentally.

Quick example
A content team uses: Ideas | Writing (WIP 2) | Editing (WIP 1) | Review | Publishing (WIP 1) | Done. Writers can only move finished drafts when editing has capacity, forcing the team to resolve the editing bottleneck together.
