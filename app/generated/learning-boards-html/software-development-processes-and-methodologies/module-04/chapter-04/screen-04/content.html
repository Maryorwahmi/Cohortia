Module 5 › Chapter 4.4 › Shared Agile Principles
Definition

Both frameworks prioritize making work visible, delivering incrementally, and continuously improving processes based on feedback and measurements.

Transparency
Boards and metrics surface work and bottlenecks to enable inspection.
Feedback loops
Frequent reviews and retrospectives create learning moments.
Incremental delivery
Small, testable increments reduce risk and shorten learning cycles.
Key Takeaway

Focus first on shared principles. The framework is the toolbox you use to enact them.

Concrete example
A team uses a visible board and metrics. Under Scrum they measure sprint outcomes and retrospective actions. Under Kanban they measure lead time and run flow experiments. Both teams hold short reviews to inspect and adapt.
Common misconception
Kanban is not 'no process' — it enforces flow discipline through WIP limits and metrics. Treat it as a disciplined approach to managing work.
