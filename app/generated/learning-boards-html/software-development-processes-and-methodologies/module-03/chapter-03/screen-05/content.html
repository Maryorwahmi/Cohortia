Module 3 › Chapter 3.3 › Four core principles
Definition

Start with what you do now

This principle keeps adoption low-friction. Map your current stages and work items, then introduce visual cards and policies without reassigning roles.
Concrete example
Convert your existing open tasks from email or spreadsheet into cards on a board, preserving who owns each task.
Misconception
Not changing roles does not mean no process change. Kanban asks you to add visibility and limits, not ignore improvement.

Agree to pursue incremental, evolutionary change

Small, measurable experiments reduce resistance and reveal real effects. Use metrics like lead time to judge success.
Experiment idea
Apply a temporary WIP limit for one week and compare lead time before and after.
Why it matters
Incremental change lets you keep what works and discard what doesn't based on data, not opinion.
Key Takeaway

Adopt Kanban without upheaval by mapping current work and running small, measurable experiments to improve flow.
