Module 3 › Chapter 3.3 › Kanban versus Scrum
Comparison

Key differences

Structure
Scrum prescribes roles and events. Kanban starts with current roles and adds flow rules.
Cadence
Scrum uses fixed-length sprints; Kanban flows continuously and pulls work when capacity exists.

Practical example

Imagine urgent bug reports arriving at random. In Kanban, the bug waits in the backlog until a slot opens in development or testing. This prevents overload and keeps teams focused on finishing work. In Scrum, the team may need to interrupt a sprint, triage the bug, or defer it to the next sprint, adding context switches.
Misconception
Kanban is not unstructured. It relies on explicit policies, WIP discipline, and clear metrics.
Pro tip
If your team already has ceremonies and stable roles, start Kanban by visualizing current work and adding a WIP limit to one column.
Key Takeaway

Kanban complements existing roles and focuses on flow; Scrum creates a structured cadence with prescribed roles and events.
