Module 3 › Chapter 3.3 › Explicit Policies

Why policies matter

Policies set the conditions that must be true before work moves. They reduce ambiguity and rework.
Example policy
'Ready for Test' requires a passing unit test, required documentation, and an assigned reviewer.
Misconception
Policies are not bureaucracy if they prevent rework and speed delivery; keep them concise and practical.

How to use policies

Write policies visibly on the board, apply them consistently, and treat them as experiment parameters you can adjust.
Try this
Make one policy for 'Definition of Ready' and run it for two sprints or two weeks, then measure changes in rework and lead time.
Key Takeaway

Explicit, minimal policies increase predictability and make experiments measurable.
