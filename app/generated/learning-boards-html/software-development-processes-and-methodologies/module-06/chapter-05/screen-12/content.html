Module 6 › Chapter 6.5 › Choosing Which Actions Matter Most
Criteria
Impact
How much user or process pain does this relieve?
Effort & Time
How long and who is needed to deliver?
Measurability
Can we measure success with a metric?
Ownership
Is there a clear owner and review date?
Example decision
Action A: Automate config parity check — High impact, moderate effort, measurable by mismatched-deploy rate, owner: DevOps.
Action B: One-off training for support — Moderate impact, low effort, measurable by ticket handle time but less likely to prevent recurrence.
From idea to backlog

Convert prioritized items into backlog entries with: description, acceptance criteria, owner, due date, and a success metric. Review during sprint planning.

SMART Action (example)
Automate config-check in CI to block mismatched deploys. Owner: DevOps. Due: end of next sprint. Success: zero mismatched deploys and reduced deploy time.
Pitfall
Recording many actions without owners or review dates turns retrospectives into a backlog of good intentions.
Key Takeaway

Prioritize actions that are measurable and owned, then track them like any other backlog item so the team delivers real improvement.
