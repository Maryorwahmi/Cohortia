Experiment Design

Hypothesis → Measurement → Decision

Define a clear hypothesis, choose a metric, set a small scope, and decide the success threshold before you start.

Example experiment
Hypothesis: Automating config-parity will reduce mismatched deploys and lower incident MTTR. Metric: mismatched deploys per 100 deploys, incident MTTR. Scope: initial deploys for 10% of releases during a two-week window.
Analysis
Compare control and experiment groups. If metrics improve and no negative side-effects appear, plan rollout. If not, refine the hypothesis and try again.
Documentation
Record experiment design and results in the team knowledge base so future teams can learn from success and mistakes.
Quick experiment checklist
  • State hypothesis and metric.
  • Limit scope to reduce risk.
  • Define success criteria and review date.
  • Document results and next steps.
Misconception
If an experiment fails, it still produced learning. Treat negative results as evidence, not failure.
Key Takeaway

Small, measurable experiments reduce risk and create reliable evidence for process and product changes.
