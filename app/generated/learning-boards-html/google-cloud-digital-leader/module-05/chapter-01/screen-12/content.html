Strengthening Security with MFA and Incident Response

Critical practices for protecting accounts and managing security events.

Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

Requires users to provide two or more verification factors (e.g., password + phone code) to gain access, significantly enhancing security against credential theft.

Incident Response Plan

A predefined set of procedures and actions to detect, respond to, and recover from security incidents, minimizing their impact and ensuring business continuity.

MFA Example

When logging into your Google Cloud console, after entering your password, you receive a prompt on your phone to approve the login, adding a second layer of verification.

Common Mistake

Lacking a tested incident response plan. Without one, organizations often react chaotically during a breach, leading to prolonged downtime and greater damage.

Key Takeaway

Implementing MFA for all accounts and having a well-defined, tested incident response plan are critical for proactive account protection and effective breach management.
