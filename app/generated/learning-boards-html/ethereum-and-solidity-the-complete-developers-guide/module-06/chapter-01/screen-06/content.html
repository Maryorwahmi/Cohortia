Module 6 › Chapter 6.1 › Gas Costs and Test Strategy
Gas as a test constraint
Treat gas like a functional requirement: measure and assert acceptable budgets during tests.
Regression checks
Record gas usage for critical paths and assert no unexpected increases after refactor.
Realistic state
Measure gas under realistic balances and storage to capture real-world cost behavior.
Acceptable budgets
Define and document acceptable gas windows for common operations like transfers and swaps.
Key Takeaway

Include gas assertions alongside functional checks so users can afford to use your contract after deployment.

Concrete example
Measure gas consumed by transfer and compare between implementations. If gas increases beyond documented budget after a change, fail the test until optimized.
Before: baseline gas for transfer = X
After refactor: transfer gas = Y; assert Y ≤ X + tolerance
If Y is much larger, prioritize optimization or alternative design.
Misconception: gas is secondary to correctness — false; high gas can make correct code unusable in practice.
