Module 6 › Chapter 6.6 › Front-running
What is front-running?
Front-running is a mempool-based attack where an observer submits a competing transaction with a higher fee to get it mined first, gaining an advantage from the original transaction.
Example — DEX trade
A large buy order is visible to bots. Attacker buys first at lower price, then the victim’s order fills at a higher price, letting the attacker profit.
Example — Auction
Seeing a high bid before settlement enables an attacker to submit an even higher bid just before closing.
Mitigation overview
  • Commit-Reveal: hide details by committing a hash, then reveal later.
  • Batching/Delaying: group actions or delay execution to remove single-transaction exposure.
  • Trusted relayer/sequencer: centralize ordering with trade-offs.
  • Slippage limits: cap acceptable price movement for trades.
Misconception: a single mitigation fits all cases. Choose based on whether secrecy, fairness, or decentralization is your primary requirement.
