Timelock essentials
The timelock holds execution rights and enforces a minimum delay. It separates approval from execution to protect the community.
Roles
Proposers schedule operations. Executors trigger them after the delay. Admins can grant or revoke roles; reduce admin keys after setup.
Min delay
Choose a delay long enough to allow response but short enough to avoid blocking routine operations.
Common pitfalls
Leaving deployer as admin or using an insufficient delay undermines the timelock's protective purpose.
Process flow (queue → execute)
When a Governor proposal passes, it is queued in the timelock. The timelock records the operation and the earliest execution time is computed by adding minDelay. After the period, anyone with executor rights can call execute and the operation runs. This separation gives the community time to react.
Example
minDelay = 1 hour. A passed proposal to transfer funds is queued; there is one hour for users to respond before execution.
Key Takeaway
A timelock is a fail-safe: configure roles and a realistic delay, and remove centralized admin privileges after governance is in place.
