Module 07 › Chapter 7.4 › Governance Mechanisms
Compare Voting Systems
Understand strengths and failure modes so you can pick a scheme that matches your DAO's goals.
Token-weighted voting
Simple and gas-efficient. Risk: wealth concentration may lead to plutocracy.
Quadratic voting
Reduces large-holder advantage by making extra votes costlier. Risk: complexity and potential for vote-purchase attacks if tokens are fungible with economic value.
Conviction voting
Votes accumulate strength over time, rewarding persistent support. Risk: lower responsiveness and higher parameter complexity.
Delegated voting
Improves participation by delegating to trusted representatives. Risk: representative centralization and delegation capture.
Scenario
A DAO votes on a grant to a small research team. Token-weighted voting favors large backers. Quadratic voting gives small, enthusiastic contributors more influence; conviction voting could let sustained support win over a short opposition surge; delegated voting could concentrate review to trusted delegates who evaluate the grant thoroughly.
Design tip
Combine mechanisms or tune parameters: you might use token-weighted voting with a higher quorum and allow delegation to reputable delegates for technical proposals.
Key Takeaway
Mechanisms trade simplicity for fairness and responsiveness. Choose intentionally and test conservatively.
