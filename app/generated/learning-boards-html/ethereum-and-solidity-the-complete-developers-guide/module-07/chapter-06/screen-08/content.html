Module 7 › Chapter 7.6 › Bridge Types
Centralized

Single-operator custody

Fast and simple, but introduces a single point of failure. Users must trust the operator to hold funds and execute transfers honestly.

Risk
Operator compromise or malice
Federated / Multi‑sig

Group custody with quorum

A set of known entities jointly sign transfers. This reduces single-party risk but relies on a majority behaving honestly.

Risk
Collusion or majority compromise
Decentralized / Trustless
Relies on cryptographic proofs, light clients, or rollup security to verify state across chains. Typically more secure but technically complex and slower to finality in some designs.
Practical note
Map a bridge to a category to reason about where attacks will target it: custody systems, validator collusion, or proof verification paths.
