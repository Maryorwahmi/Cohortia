Module 7 › Chapter 7.6 › Trustless Bridges
How they work

Proofs, light clients, and incentives

Trustless bridges provide verifiable evidence of events across chains, either by embedding a light client, using cryptographic proofs, or relying on the security of a base layer. These approaches remove operator-based custody but demand rigorous cryptographic correctness.

- Tradeoff: stronger guarantees vs higher development complexity and possibly greater costs.
- Verify assumptions: finality model, proof validity, and what happens on chain reorgs.
- Audits should include proof verification logic and light client implementations.
User implication
Trustless bridges reduce who you must trust, but they may be more conservative about finality and can require waiting for stronger confirmations.
Developer implication
Expect deeper cryptographic design, careful testing for reorgs, and attention to gas and proof sizes when implementing trustless verification.
