Comparison

Transparency vs Privacy

Public blockchains are transparent and auditable. Private and consortium networks restrict access and are often used when confidentiality and governance matter more than full openness.

Public Blockchain
Anyone can read transactions; addresses are pseudonymous. Best for open auditability and decentralized trust.
Private Blockchain
Access controlled by an organization, offering privacy and higher throughput but less decentralization.
Consortium Blockchain
Managed by a group of organizations, a middle ground between openness and permissioned governance.
Concrete Example
A public record shows that address A transferred ownership to address B at a timestamp. To map those addresses to companies or people you typically need off-chain records, which means privacy depends on design as much as on block visibility.
Design Question
Decide whether your system prioritizes public verifiability or confidentiality, then choose a network model and privacy controls that match that priority.
Misconception
Assuming pseudonymous addresses guarantee privacy. Transaction graph analysis can reveal identities without careful design.
