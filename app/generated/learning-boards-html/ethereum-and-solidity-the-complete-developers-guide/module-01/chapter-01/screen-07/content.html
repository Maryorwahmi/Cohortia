Comparison

Public • Private • Consortium

Each network type answers who can read, who can write, and who decides consensus. Your application’s needs determine the best fit.

Public
Permissionless, transparent, and decentralized. Anyone can join and verify state.
Private
Permissioned by a single authority; offers privacy and speed for internal systems.
Consortium
Permissioned and jointly governed by several organizations for shared, private workflows.
Concrete Scenario
A group of suppliers use a consortium ledger to share verified shipping records among partners while keeping data hidden from competitors outside the group.
Trade-offs
  • Public: highest decentralization, lower privacy.
  • Private: high privacy and performance, lower decentralization.
  • Consortium: balanced control among trusted parties.
Misconception
Permissioned networks are not automatically trust-free; participants still require governance and auditing to ensure integrity.
