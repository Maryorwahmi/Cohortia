Definition

What is a blockchain?

Blockchain is a distributed, append-only ledger: grouped records form blocks, and blocks are cryptographically linked to make a tamper-evident chain.

Analogy: Village ledger
Every villager keeps a copy of the same ledger. A new transaction is broadcast and appended by consensus. If someone tampers with their copy, the mismatch is visible.
Concrete Example
A product’s journey is recorded at manufacture, shipping, and delivery. Each step is an appended record that auditors can verify later.
Key Takeaway
Append-only distributed copies plus cryptographic linking creates practical, tamper-evident history for applications that need auditability.
Common Mistake
Believing that a shared ledger alone solves governance and privacy concerns — it provides integrity, not automatic privacy or access control.
