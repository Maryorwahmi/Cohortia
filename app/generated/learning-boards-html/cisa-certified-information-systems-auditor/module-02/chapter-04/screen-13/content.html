Assessment: Policy Enforcement Concern
Identifying Governance Breakdowns

Let's consider an assessment scenario to solidify our understanding. The CISO of 'SecureBank Inc.' discovers that a critical security policy requiring multi-factor authentication, or MFA, for all remote access is not being consistently applied across different departments. Some departments have implemented it, while others have not. From an information security governance perspective, what is the most immediate concern for an auditor? Let's analyze the options.

Option A, 'The CISO lacks technical expertise,' is unlikely to be the primary concern here; the issue is with policy application, not technical knowledge. Option B, 'The policy itself is poorly written,' is possible, but the core problem is the inconsistent application, not necessarily the policy's wording. Option C, 'There is a breakdown in policy enforcement and potentially a lack of management commitment to security,' directly addresses the observed inconsistency. When a critical policy isn't universally applied, it signals a failure in the governance process to ensure effective implementation and oversight, suggesting a potential lack of management commitment. This is a significant red flag for an auditor. Option D, 'The organization has not invested enough in security tools,' might be a contributing factor, but the immediate concern is the failure to use existing policies and tools consistently. Therefore, the most immediate and critical concern for an auditor is the breakdown in policy enforcement and the potential lack of management commitment, as it indicates a fundamental weakness in the organization's governance structure and its ability to protect itself.

Key Takeaway

Inconsistent application of security policies, like MFA, points to critical breakdowns in policy enforcement and potential lack of management commitment, which is a primary concern for auditors.

Audit Concern Analysis
Correct Answer: C

Breakdown in policy enforcement & lack of management commitment.

Why Not A/B/D?

Focus is on application, not CISO skill, policy wording, or tool investment alone.
