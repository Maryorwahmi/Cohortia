Common Mistakes in Security Governance
Avoiding Enforcement Breakdowns and Authority Issues

When auditing information security governance, auditors often identify recurring mistakes that organizations make, which can significantly undermine their security posture. One of the most critical and frequently observed issues is a breakdown in policy enforcement. For example, an organization might have a well-documented policy requiring multi-factor authentication (MFA) for all remote access, but if this policy is not consistently applied across all departments or systems, it creates a significant vulnerability. This inconsistency suggests a lack of robust management commitment to security, as a critical control is not universally implemented. Auditors will flag this as a major concern because it leaves the organization exposed to risks that the policy was intended to mitigate.

Another common mistake relates to the authority and positioning of the CISO. If the CISO is buried too deep within the organizational hierarchy, perhaps reporting through several layers of management, their ability to influence strategic decisions, secure adequate resources, and enforce security mandates across the enterprise can be severely hampered. This lack of authority can lead to security initiatives being deprioritized or overlooked. Effective governance requires that security has a voice at the appropriate levels of management, and that policies, once established, are rigorously enforced throughout the organization. Recognizing and addressing these common mistakes is crucial for building a mature and effective information security program.

Key Takeaway

Common mistakes include inconsistent policy enforcement and insufficient CISO authority, both of which create significant vulnerabilities and hinder effective security governance.

Critical Audit Findings
Policy Enforcement Gaps

Inconsistent application of security policies across departments or systems.

Limited CISO Authority

CISO's reporting structure hinders strategic influence and enforcement capabilities.

Lack of Management Commitment

Inconsistent application signals weak organizational commitment to security.
