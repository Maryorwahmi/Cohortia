Assessment: HIPAA Access Control
Verifying PHI Protection

Let's examine another assessment scenario, this time focusing on compliance. An IT auditor is assessing a healthcare provider's adherence to HIPAA, specifically the Security Rule concerning access control. Which of the following would be the most relevant audit step? Consider the options.

Option A, 'Reviewing the organization's marketing materials for patient testimonials,' is irrelevant to access control verification. Option B, 'Examining system access logs to confirm that access to Protected Health Information (PHI) is limited to authorized personnel based on job role,' directly addresses the core requirement of HIPAA's Security Rule regarding access control. This step verifies that the principle of 'minimum necessary' access and role-based access control is being implemented. Option C, 'Interviewing the CEO about their personal health insurance coverage,' is irrelevant to the organization's PHI access controls. Option D, 'Assessing the physical security of the organization's public waiting areas,' pertains to physical security but not directly to system access controls for PHI. Therefore, the most relevant audit step to verify adherence to HIPAA's Security Rule regarding access control is examining system access logs to confirm that access to PHI is limited to authorized personnel based on their job role. This directly validates the implementation of critical security controls designed to protect sensitive patient data.

Key Takeaway

Verifying HIPAA compliance for access control involves examining system logs to ensure PHI access is restricted to authorized personnel based on job roles.

HIPAA Access Control Audit
Most Relevant Audit Step: B

Examine system access logs for PHI to confirm role-based, minimum-necessary access.

Why Other Options Are Less Relevant

Marketing materials, CEO interviews, and physical waiting area security do not directly verify system access controls for PHI.
