Core Concept

The Foundation of IT Audit: Planning

Effective IT auditing begins with meticulous planning, setting the direction, scope, and objectives for the entire engagement. This phase is crucial for ensuring the audit is focused, efficient, and addresses significant risks.

  • Sets Direction: Defines what the audit will cover and why.
  • Ensures Efficiency: Prevents unfocused efforts and wasted resources.
  • Aligns with Goals: Connects audit objectives to the organization's strategic aims.
  • Addresses Risks: Focuses on the most critical threats to information assets.
Key Takeaway

Without proper planning, an IT audit risks being unfocused, inefficient, and failing to address the most significant organizational risks.

ISACA Standards

Guidance for CISAs

ISACA's Performance Standards provide the framework for robust audit planning:

  • 1201 – Engagement Planning: Mandates developing a comprehensive plan.
  • 1202 – Risk Assessment in Planning: Requires understanding the unique risk profile.
  • Business Context: Auditors must understand the organization's operations and strategic goals.
  • IT Environment: A thorough grasp of the specific IT infrastructure and systems is essential.
Practical Example

Cloud Migration Audit

When an organization migrates to the cloud, the audit plan must be specifically tailored to address unique cloud-related risks like data sovereignty, shared responsibility models, and vendor lock-in, rather than using a generic checklist.
