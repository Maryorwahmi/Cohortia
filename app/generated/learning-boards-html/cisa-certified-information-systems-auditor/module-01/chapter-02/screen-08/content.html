Performance Standard Focus
Sufficient and Reliable Evidence

Collecting adequate and trustworthy evidence is fundamental to supporting audit findings and conclusions, as guided by Standard 1203.

Sufficiency

Refers to the quantity of evidence. Auditors must gather enough information to support their conclusions, avoiding reliance on isolated data points.

Reliability

Refers to the quality and trustworthiness of evidence. Evidence obtained directly, from independent sources, or through controlled processes is generally more reliable.

Practical Application
Auditing Firewall Effectiveness

To audit a firewall's effectiveness, an auditor should not rely solely on a manager's statement. Instead, they should gather sufficient evidence by reviewing firewall logs for a representative period, examining the configuration rules, and perhaps performing controlled tests. The evidence is reliable if it comes directly from system logs and configurations, rather than hearsay. This ensures findings are well-supported.

Key Takeaway

The validity of audit findings hinges on collecting evidence that is both sufficient in quantity and reliable in quality.
