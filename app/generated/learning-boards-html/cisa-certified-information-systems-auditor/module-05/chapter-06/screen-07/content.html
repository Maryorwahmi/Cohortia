From Incident to Disaster: The Escalation Path

An incident often triggers the IR plan first, and if it escalates, it may then activate the BCP and DRP in a sequential manner.

Step 1

Incident Occurs

A security event or operational failure is detected.

Step 2

IR Plan Activated

Immediate actions to contain, eradicate, and recover from the specific incident.

Step 3

Escalation to BCP/DRP

If IR cannot contain the incident, BCP and DRP are invoked based on impact.

Example: Ransomware Attack

  • •**IR:** Team works to contain the spread, isolate affected systems, and eradicate malware.
  • •**Escalation Trigger:** If containment is prolonged and critical systems remain unavailable beyond RTO.
  • •**BCP Activation:** Ensures business continuity (e.g., manual processing) while systems are down.
  • •**DRP Invocation:** Restores encrypted systems from clean backups at a recovery site.
Key Takeaway

A clear, predefined escalation path ensures a coordinated and timely transition from immediate incident response to broader business continuity and disaster recovery efforts.
