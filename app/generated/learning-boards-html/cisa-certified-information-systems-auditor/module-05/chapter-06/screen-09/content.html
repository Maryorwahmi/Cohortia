IR Lifecycle

Containment, Eradication, and Recovery

Taking decisive action to stop, remove, and restore systems after an incident.

1. Containment

Limit the incident's scope and prevent further damage. Isolate affected systems, block malicious traffic, or suspend accounts.

2. Eradication

Remove the root cause of the incident. Clean infected systems, delete malware, patch vulnerabilities, and reconfigure security.

3. Recovery

Restore affected systems and data to normal, secure operations. This includes restoring from backups and rigorous testing.

Scenario Example

A ransomware attack encrypts critical servers. Containment involves isolating the affected network segment. Eradication means removing the malware and identifying the entry point. Recovery requires restoring data from clean backups and patching the vulnerability.

Key Takeaway

Effective IR requires a systematic approach to contain, eradicate, and recover, ensuring systems are not only restored but also secured against future threats.

The Action Phase

These phases are about active intervention to mitigate harm and restore integrity. They demand quick thinking, technical expertise, and adherence to predefined procedures.
