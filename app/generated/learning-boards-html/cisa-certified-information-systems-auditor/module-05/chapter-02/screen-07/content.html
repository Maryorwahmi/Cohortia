Critical Metric

Defining MTD: The Absolute Limit of Unavailability

Understanding the maximum time a process can be down before unacceptable consequences.

What is Maximum Tolerable Downtime (MTD)?

MTD, also known as Maximum Acceptable Outage (MAO), is the absolute maximum amount of time a business process or system can be unavailable before the organization experiences unacceptable consequences. Exceeding this threshold can lead to severe damage.

  • •Absolute Limit: Represents the critical threshold beyond which recovery is no longer viable or acceptable.
  • •Business-Driven: Determined by business owners based on potential impacts (financial, reputational, legal).
  • •Consequence-Focused: Directly linked to the severity of disruption impacts.
Key Takeaway

MTD sets the ultimate boundary for recovery, defining the point at which disruption becomes intolerable for the business.

Example: Online Transaction System

Scenario: A bank's online transaction system experiences an outage.

MTD: Set at 2 hours due to regulatory fines and potential customer exodus.

Consequence: Downtime exceeding 2 hours leads to unacceptable financial and reputational damage.
