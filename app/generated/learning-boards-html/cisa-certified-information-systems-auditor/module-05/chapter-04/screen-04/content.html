DRP: The IT Recovery Blueprint

Understanding its Purpose and Criticality in Business Resilience

DRP's Core Function

  • •Details steps to restore IT infrastructure, systems, and data.
  • •Technological counterpart to the Business Continuity Plan (BCP).
  • •Ensures critical IT services are brought back online within RTOs and RPOs.
  • •Often managed by IT teams, but critical for overall BCDR strategy.

Auditor's Critical Role

  • •Verify DRP is actionable, not just a document.
  • •Assess alignment with BIA-defined RTOs and RPOs.
  • •Identify risks from outdated, incomplete, or untested plans.

Why it Matters: The Risk of Failure

A DRP that fails to account for critical dependencies, lacks detailed procedures, or is based on outdated technology poses a significant risk to the organization's ability to recover. This can lead to extended downtime, data loss, and severe financial and reputational damage.

Concrete Example: Database Recovery

Imagine a DRP that meticulously outlines server restoration but omits granular steps for database recovery. If a disaster strikes, even with servers online, the business cannot function without its core data, leading to prolonged operational paralysis. This highlights the need for highly granular procedures.

Key Takeaway

A DRP must be a living, actionable plan, meticulously detailed and regularly updated, to ensure effective IT recovery and business resilience.
