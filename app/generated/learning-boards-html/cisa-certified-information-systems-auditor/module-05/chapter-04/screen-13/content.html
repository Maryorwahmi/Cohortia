Auditor's Role

Evaluating the DRP: Auditor's Responsibilities

Ensuring Technical Adequacy, Completeness, and Testability

Key Audit Areas

  • •Review DRP Documentation for clarity and alignment.
  • •Conduct Interviews with IT Personnel to gauge understanding.
  • •Verify Recovery Site Readiness (physical or cloud).
  • •Assess Backup and Restoration Procedures, including restore tests.
  • •Review Network and Security Configurations for recovery.
  • •Perform Dependency Analysis to identify overlooked links.

Common Mistake: Overlooking Dependencies

A frequent audit finding is a DRP that fails to adequately address interdependencies between systems, applications, or external vendors. A seemingly minor overlooked dependency can bring the entire recovery process to a halt.

Integration with BCP

Auditors must verify that the DRP is fully integrated with the broader BCP. IT recovery efforts must directly support overall business continuity objectives, ensuring a seamless transition from IT restoration to business function resumption.

Key Takeaway

The auditor's role is to provide independent assurance that the DRP is technically adequate, complete, testable, and fully integrated with the BCP to ensure organizational resilience.
