Data Resilience

Incremental and Differential Backups

Optimizing Backup Efficiency and Restoration Complexity

Incremental Backups

  • •Copies data changed since the *last* full or incremental backup.
  • •Faster backup, less storage.
  • •Restoration requires last full + ALL subsequent incrementals.
  • •High restoration complexity.

Differential Backups

  • •Copies data changed since the *last full* backup.
  • •Faster than full, more storage than incremental.
  • •Restoration requires last full + LAST differential.
  • •Moderate restoration complexity.

Example: Restoration Scenarios

To restore to Thursday with incremental backups (Full Mon, Inc Tue, Inc Wed, Inc Thu), you need Mon Full + Tue Inc + Wed Inc + Thu Inc. With differential backups (Full Mon, Diff Tue, Diff Wed, Diff Thu), you only need Mon Full + Thu Diff. This illustrates the difference in restoration steps.

Auditor's Focus: Restoration Test

Auditors must verify that restoration procedures for both incremental and differential backups are well-documented and regularly tested. A common mistake is assuming backups are good without ever attempting a full restoration, only to find issues during a real disaster.

Key Takeaway

Incremental backups are efficient but complex to restore; differential backups offer a balance. Auditors must ensure restoration complexity is managed and tested to meet RTOs.
