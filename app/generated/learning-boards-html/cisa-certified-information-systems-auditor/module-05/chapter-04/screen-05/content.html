DRP Structure

Components of a Comprehensive DRP (Part 1)

Establishing Foundation, Activation, Roles, and Asset Inventory

1. Introduction and Scope

Defines the DRP's purpose, scope, and objectives, ensuring alignment with the BCP. This sets the overall direction for recovery efforts.

2. Activation and Notification

Procedures for declaring an IT disaster, escalating to the IT disaster recovery team, and notifying relevant stakeholders. Timely activation is crucial.

3. Roles and Responsibilities

Clearly defined roles for IT disaster recovery team members, including contact information and specific tasks during recovery. Avoids confusion and ensures accountability.

4. Inventory of Critical IT Assets

A detailed list of servers, network devices, applications, databases, and other IT components, along with their configurations and dependencies. This acts as a critical roadmap for restoration.

Common Mistake: Outdated Inventory

A frequent pitfall is an outdated asset inventory. If the DRP lists systems that no longer exist or has incorrect configurations, recovery efforts will be significantly delayed or fail entirely. Regular updates are essential.

Key Takeaway

A DRP's foundation relies on clear scope, precise activation, defined roles, and an accurate, up-to-date inventory of critical IT assets.
