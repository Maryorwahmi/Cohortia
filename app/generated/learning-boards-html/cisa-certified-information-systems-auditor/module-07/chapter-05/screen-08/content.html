Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) Fundamentals

Strengthening access security with multiple verification factors.

Why MFA is Critical

  • •**Enhanced Security:** Significantly harder for attackers to gain access.
  • •**Protects Against Credential Theft:** Mitigates risks from stolen passwords.
  • •**Compliance Requirements:** Often mandated by regulations and industry standards.
  • •**Reduces Phishing Success:** Adds a layer of defense against social engineering.
Key Takeaway

MFA requires two or more distinct verification factors (know, have, are) to significantly enhance security beyond a single password.

Three Categories of MFA Factors

Something You Know

(e.g., Password, PIN)

Something You Have

(e.g., Token, Smartphone, Smart Card)

Something You Are

(e.g., Fingerprint, Facial Scan, Biometric)

Example MFA Combination

A user enters their password (something you know) and then approves a login request via a push notification on their registered smartphone (something you have). This combination provides a strong defense against credential theft.
