Privileged Access Management (PAM) Overview

Securing high-value accounts with elevated permissions.

Why PAM is Essential

  • •**High-Value Targets:** Privileged accounts offer extensive control over critical systems.
  • •**Mitigating Insider Threats:** Controls access and monitors activities of trusted users.
  • •**Preventing Lateral Movement:** Limits attacker's ability to escalate privileges.
  • •**Compliance Mandates:** Often required by regulatory frameworks.
Key Takeaway

PAM secures high-value privileged accounts through vaulting, session management, JIT access, least privilege, and comprehensive audit trails, mitigating risks from elevated permissions.

Core Components of PAM Solutions

1. Vaulting

Securely storing privileged credentials in a hardened, isolated vault.

2. Session Management

Isolating, monitoring, and recording privileged sessions, often via a jump server.

3. Just-in-Time (JIT) Access

Granting privileged access only when needed and for a limited duration.

4. Least Privilege

Ensuring privileged accounts have only the minimum necessary permissions.
