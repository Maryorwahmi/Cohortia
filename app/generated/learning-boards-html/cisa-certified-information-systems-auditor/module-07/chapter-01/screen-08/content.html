Digital Signatures

Combining Cryptography for Verifiable Identity and Integrity

Sender's Process: Creating a Digital Signature

1

Hash Document: The sender computes a hash value of the original document.

2

Encrypt Hash: The sender encrypts this hash value using their own private key, creating the digital signature.

3

Attach & Send: The digital signature is attached to the document and sent to the recipient.

Concrete Example

A CEO digitally signs a contract. The contract's hash is encrypted with the CEO's private key, proving the CEO's approval and ensuring the contract's content remains unchanged.

Recipient's Process: Verifying a Digital Signature

1

Compute New Hash: The recipient computes a hash value of the received document.

2

Decrypt Signature: The recipient uses the sender's public key to decrypt the attached digital signature, revealing the sender's original hash.

3

Compare Hashes: If the two hash values match, the document is authentic, untampered, and the sender cannot deny sending it.

Key Takeaway

Digital signatures provide strong assurance of authenticity and integrity, crucial for establishing trust in digital transactions and communications.
