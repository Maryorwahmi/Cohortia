Key Management Audit Checklist

  • 1
    Key Generation: Are keys generated using strong, certified random number generators?
  • 2
    Key Storage: Are keys stored in Hardware Security Modules (HSMs) or secure key vaults?
  • 3
    Key Rotation: Is there a policy for regular key rotation, and is it enforced (e.g., annually)?
  • 4
    Access Control: Who has access to the KMS, and are multi-factor authentication requirements in place?
  • 5
    Backup & Recovery: How are keys backed up, is the backup encrypted, and is the recovery process tested?
  • 6
    Key Destruction: Are secure procedures in place for destroying keys when no longer needed?

Scenario: Data-at-Rest Encryption

An organization encrypts sensitive customer data using AES-256. As an auditor, you would investigate the following aspects of their key management:

Inquiry: Key Storage

Are AES keys stored in a Hardware Security Module (HSM) or a secure key vault, and what are the controls around it?

Inquiry: Key Rotation Policy

What is the policy for key rotation (e.g., annual rotation), and is it consistently enforced across all data sets?

Inquiry: Access & Destruction

Who has authorized access to the KMS, and what procedures ensure secure key destruction?

Key Takeaway

The effectiveness of encryption hinges on robust key management processes throughout the entire key lifecycle.
