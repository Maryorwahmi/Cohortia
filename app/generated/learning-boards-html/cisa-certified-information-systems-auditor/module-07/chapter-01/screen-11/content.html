Common Cryptographic Weaknesses

Weak Key Management

Keys hardcoded, stored insecurely, or not rotated regularly, making them vulnerable to compromise.

Outdated/Weak Algorithms

Using algorithms like MD5 or DES, or insufficient key lengths, which are susceptible to attacks.

Lack of Regular Rotation

Cryptographic keys not periodically changed, increasing the risk of long-term compromise.

Improper Protocol Implementation

Even strong algorithms can be undermined by flaws in how they are integrated into systems or protocols.

Why These Mistakes Matter

  • Data Exposure: Compromised keys lead directly to unauthorized access to encrypted data.
  • Integrity Loss: Weak hashing allows undetected tampering with data and digital signatures.
  • Reputation Damage: Security breaches due to crypto flaws erode customer trust and organizational standing.
Key Takeaway

Auditors must look beyond algorithm choice to assess the entire cryptographic implementation lifecycle, especially key management and adherence to current standards.
