What are Corrective Controls?

These controls are designed to correct errors or irregularities and recover from them once they have been detected. They aim to restore the system to a secure and operational state.

  • Focus on remediation and recovery after an incident.
  • Minimize the impact of detected issues.
  • Essential for business continuity and disaster recovery.
Misconception

Corrective controls are not a substitute for preventive or detective measures; they are the last line of defense to mitigate damage and restore operations.

Key Takeaway

Robust corrective controls are vital for organizational resilience, enabling a swift and effective response to security incidents and system failures.

Examples of Corrective Controls

Backup & Recovery Procedures

Regularly backing up data and system configurations with tested procedures for restoration.

Incident Response Plans

Documented procedures for responding to security incidents, including containment, eradication, and recovery.

Patch Management

Systematic process for identifying, testing, and applying software patches to address known vulnerabilities.
