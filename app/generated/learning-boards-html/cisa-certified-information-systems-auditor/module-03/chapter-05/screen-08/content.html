Cutover Strategies: Managing the Transition to a New System

Organizations choose one of several cutover strategies to transition from an old system to a new one, each with distinct risk and resource implications.

High Risk / Fast

Big Bang

The old system is replaced entirely by the new system at a single point in time. High risk but can be faster.

  • High risk of disruption
  • Potentially faster deployment
Moderate Risk / Extended

Phased Implementation

The new system is introduced in stages, by module or by location. Spreads risk but extends timeline.

  • Risk is distributed
  • Longer implementation period
Low Risk / High Cost

Parallel Operation

Both old and new systems run simultaneously for a period. Lowest risk but most expensive.

  • Continuous fallback
  • High resource demands
Controlled Rollout / Refinement

Pilot Implementation

New system deployed to a small group of users or a single department first. Allows for learning.

  • Refinement before wider rollout
  • Can delay full deployment
Key Takeaway

The choice of cutover strategy significantly impacts risk, cost, and timeline; auditors must ensure risks are understood and mitigated for the chosen approach.
