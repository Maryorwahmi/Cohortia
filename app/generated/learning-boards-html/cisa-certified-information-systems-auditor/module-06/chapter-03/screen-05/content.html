Fundamental Principle

Least Privilege: Minimizing Access Rights

Granting only the essential permissions for legitimate functions.

Minimal Access: Users, programs, or processes receive only the permissions absolutely required for their tasks.

Damage Limitation: If a compromised entity has limited privileges, the scope of potential harm is significantly reduced.

Auditor's Focus: Reviewing access control matrices and user roles to identify and mitigate excessive permissions.

Key Takeaway

Implementing Least Privilege is a fundamental control that drastically reduces the attack surface and potential impact of security breaches.

Concrete Example

A web application that displays public product information should only have 'read' access to the product database. Granting it 'write' or 'delete' access would violate Least Privilege, creating a vulnerability if the application were compromised.

Common Misconception

It's often easier for developers to grant broad permissions to avoid access issues during development. However, this 'over-privileging' often carries over to production, creating significant security risks that are difficult to unwind later.

Why it Matters

Adhering to Least Privilege is crucial for preventing privilege escalation, lateral movement by attackers, and insider threats, thereby strengthening the overall security posture of an organization.
