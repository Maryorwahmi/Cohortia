Modern Paradigm

Zero Trust: Never Trust, Always Verify

Assuming threats exist both inside and outside the network perimeter.

No Implicit Trust: Assumes no user or device is inherently trustworthy, regardless of location.

Continuous Verification: Every access request is strictly authenticated and authorized, even from within the network.

Auditor's Focus: Verifying strict identity and access controls across all resources, regardless of network position.

Key Takeaway

Zero Trust enhances security by eliminating implicit trust and enforcing verification for every access attempt, crucial for modern distributed environments.

Concrete Example

An employee logs into the corporate network. With Zero Trust, when they try to access a sensitive document on a file server, they are prompted for multi-factor authentication again, even though they are already 'inside' the network.

Contrast with Perimeter Security

Traditional perimeter security focuses on keeping threats out, assuming internal networks are safe. Zero Trust challenges this by treating all access attempts, internal or external, as potentially malicious until verified.

Why it Matters

Zero Trust is essential for protecting against sophisticated threats like insider attacks and advanced persistent threats (APTs) that can bypass perimeter defenses, providing granular control over access to critical resources.
