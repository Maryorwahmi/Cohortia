Deconstructing Risk: Threats, Vulnerabilities, and Impacts

The Interplay of Potential Harm

The Risk Equation Explained

Information security risk is fundamentally the potential for loss or harm, often expressed as the product of these three elements:

  • Threat: A potential cause of an unwanted incident.
  • Vulnerability: A weakness that can be exploited by a threat.
  • Impact: The magnitude of harm resulting from exploitation.
Key Takeaway

Effective risk management requires a clear understanding of how threats exploit vulnerabilities to cause adverse impacts, which together define the overall risk.

Risk Components in Action

Threat: Malware Attack

A malicious software program designed to disrupt, damage, or gain unauthorized access to a computer system.

Vulnerability: Unpatched Software

A known security flaw in an operating system or application that has not been fixed by applying updates.

Impact: Data Breach & Financial Loss

Unauthorized access to sensitive data, leading to regulatory fines, legal costs, and loss of customer trust.
