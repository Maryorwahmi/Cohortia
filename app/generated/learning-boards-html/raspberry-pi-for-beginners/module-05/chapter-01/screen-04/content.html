Definition

Digital and analog signals

Understand whether a sensor outputs discrete states or a continuous voltage value, because that determines wiring and code.

Digital example: PIR

Outputs HIGH when motion is detected. Can be read by a GPIO or handled with an event handler in gpiozero.

Timing-sensitive example: DHT22

A single-wire protocol that requires precise timing and a DHT-aware library to decode temperature and humidity readings reliably.

Analog example: LDR

Resistance changes with light; use a voltage divider and an MCP3008 ADC to measure the voltage and convert to a numeric value.

Key Takeaway

Always identify whether the sensor is digital, analog, or timing-sensitive and choose GPIO, ADC, or a library accordingly.

Concrete Example
Terminal-style note
PIR outputs: LOW then HIGH when motion arrives. DHT22: sequence of timed pulses decoded by a DHT library. LDR: voltage spans 0 to 3.3V; ADC returns raw counts to map.
Common misconception

Assuming a sensor that looks like a digital switch will always give stable readings. In practice some devices are noisy or need pull resistors or timing-aware drivers.

Why it matters: picking the wrong input method can produce sporadic data, false triggers, or hardware damage. When in doubt, consult the sensor's output type and plan for retries and calibration.
