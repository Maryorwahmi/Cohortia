Module 5 › Chapter 5.4 › What is an actuator?
Definition

An actuator converts electrical signals into physical motion or switching. Examples used here are servos, DC motors, and relays.

Servo
Precise angle control, usually 0–180 degrees, requires PWM signal and power.
DC motor
Continuous rotation and speed control using a motor driver and PWM for speed.
Relay
An electrically operated switch for controlling higher-voltage loads from a low-voltage signal.
Key Takeaway

Pick the actuator that matches motion type and power needs, and always plan an interface that supplies motor power separately from the Pi's logic pins.

Concrete example
A small pan-tilt camera: servos control angles for pan and tilt while a relay could switch a separate camera heater on and off. The Pi sends PWM to the servos and toggles the relay with a safe low-voltage signal.
Common misconception
Never assume a GPIO can supply motor current. GPIO provides a control signal only; motors require separate power and often a driver with protection.
