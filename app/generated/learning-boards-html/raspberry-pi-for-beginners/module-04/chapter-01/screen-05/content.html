LED basics and orientation
An LED is a diode: current flows one way and it drops a small forward voltage.
Anode and cathode
Long leg = anode (positive), short leg = cathode (ground). Place accordingly on the breadboard.
Forward voltage
Typical red LED forward drop ~1.8 volts; this determines the resistor voltage.
Concrete example
With a GPIO at 3.3 volts and a red LED at 1.8 volts, the resistor sees 1.5 volts and limits current to protect the LED and pin.
Quick checks
  • Is the long leg toward the positive node?
  • Is a resistor present in series?
  • Are both legs seated firmly in different rows of the breadboard?
Common mistake
Placing both LED legs in the same row so they short; always use separate rows so current passes through the LED and resistor.
Analogy: the LED is a one-way valve for current — it only allows flow in its forward direction.
