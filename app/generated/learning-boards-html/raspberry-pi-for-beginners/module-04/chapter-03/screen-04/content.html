Module 4 › Chapter 4.3 › What is PWM?
Core Concept

Pulse Width Modulation

PWM switches a digital pin on and off rapidly. Duty cycle determines how much of each period is on, which sets average power.
Duty cycle
Percentage of time the signal is high during a cycle, e.g. 50% gives half power on average.
Frequency
How many on/off cycles per second; choose a frequency higher than the device's perception threshold.
Key Takeaway

PWM changes time at full voltage to vary average power; it does not change the voltage itself.

Concrete example
An LED at 50% duty cycle is on half the time and off half the time per cycle. Because switching is faster than the eye update rate, it looks half-bright.
Misconception
PWM changes time, not voltage. If you think the voltage drops, you'll misdiagnose dimming issues.
Pro tip
Use higher frequency for LEDs to avoid flicker; motors may respond differently due to inertia.
Check your understanding
If a PWM signal has a 25% duty cycle, what average power would the load receive? Answer: 25%.
