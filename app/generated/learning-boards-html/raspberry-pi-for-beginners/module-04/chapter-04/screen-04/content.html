Definition

Digital GPIO vs Analog Signals

Digital inputs report two states. Analog sensors output a voltage that varies with the measured quantity. An ADC bridges that gap.

Quick facts
  • 10-bit ADC → 1024 discrete steps.
  • Reference voltage sets full-scale mapping (commonly 3.3V).
  • Mixing 5V sensors with a 3.3V ADC risks damage.
Key Takeaway

An ADC is required to convert continuous voltages into numbers the Pi can process; resolution and reference determine precision.

Analogy

Analogy: measuring water level with steps

Imagine a tank with a continuous water level. An ADC is like a set of numbered steps; each step represents a small range of water heights. A 10-bit ADC gives you 1,024 steps to approximate the exact level.

Concrete example
Potentiometer wiper between 0 and 3.3V. ADC reading 512 corresponds to about 1.65 volts.
Common mistake
Expecting a Pi GPIO to return a voltage value. It returns high or low unless an ADC is used.
