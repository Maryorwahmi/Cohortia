Module 2 › Chapter 1.5 › Power and Ground Pins
Definition

5V, 3.3V and GND explained

Rail identification and safe usage for powering peripherals and providing a reference for measurements.

5V pins
Two pins provide a 5 volt supply for devices that explicitly require 5 volts; do not feed 5V into GPIO pins.
3.3V pins
Two pins provide a 3.3 volt supply and are the correct source for logic devices that expect 3.3V signals.
Ground pins
Eight grounds give multiple convenient return points; always share ground with external power supplies.
Concrete example
If a sensor requires 3.3 volts and draws a few milliamps, connect its VCC to a 3.3V pin, ground to any GND pin, and its data line to a GPIO. If you power a 5V relay, use the 5V rail or an external 5V supply and control the relay via a transistor so the Pi’s GPIO does not supply the relay current directly.
Warning
Never connect 5V directly to a GPIO pin. That will likely damage the SoC.
Key Takeaway

Power rails and grounds are not interchangeable with signal pins. Use the correct rail and external drivers for heavy loads.
