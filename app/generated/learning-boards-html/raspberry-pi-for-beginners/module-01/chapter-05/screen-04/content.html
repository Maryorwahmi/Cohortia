Core Concept

Header Categories

A quick definition of each category and why it’s handled differently in wiring and code.

Power pins
Two 5V and two 3.3V pins provide supply rails—measure them and avoid driving GPIO with these voltages.
Ground pins
Eight ground pins give a common reference for sensors and peripherals; always share ground with external circuits.
GPIO lines
Twenty‑six lines configurable as inputs or outputs in software for sensors, LEDs, or switches.
Special‑function pins
I2C, SPI, UART and PWM pins provide hardware support for common communication and timing tasks.
Concrete example
If you connect an I2C temperature sensor, you will use SDA and SCL on pins 3 and 5 (GPIO 2 and 3). Those pins include onboard pull‑ups to 3.3 volts, so the bus is ready for typical I2C sensors. For an LED use a GPIO line and a resistor, not a 5 volt pin. For a heavy load like a motor, use a transistor or motor driver powered from the 5V rail, while the GPIO controls the driver’s input.
Common misconception
Assuming any header pin is interchangeable. Some pins are power, others are signal lines with different rules and current limits.
Key takeaway
Treat rails and signals differently: power for supply, GPIO for control, special pins for hardware buses.
