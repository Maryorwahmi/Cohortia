Module 1 › Chapter 1.3 › microSD Cards
Definition
The microSD stores the operating system, applications, and user data.
What to look for
  • Capacity: 16 GB minimum; 32–64 GB recommended for projects and logs.
  • Performance class: choose A1 or A2 for better random reads/writes used by OS.
  • Brand and reliability: prefer known brands to reduce corruption risk.
  • Test the card with a short benchmark or small-file copy before installing the OS.
Example
Copy many small files; if transfers stall or fail, the card may be unreliable under OS load.
Common Mistake
Choosing a card solely by high sequential MB/s ratings and ignoring A1/A2 class.
Illustration: Performance focus
The operating system uses many small reads and writes. A1/A2 ratings are designed to handle this workload better than cards optimized for large-file sequential transfers.
Analogy: Treat the microSD like your system's hard drive; a slow drive makes the whole computer feel sluggish.
Key Takeaway

A reliable, A1/A2-rated card of adequate capacity prevents slow boots and corrupted files.
