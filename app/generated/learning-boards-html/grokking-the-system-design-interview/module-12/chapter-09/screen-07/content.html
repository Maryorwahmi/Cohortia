Definition
Admissible Heuristic
Never overestimates the true remaining cost; preserves A* optimality.
Use straight-line distance as a baseline heuristic on maps.
Convert distance to time using a safe maximum speed to remain admissible.
A weak heuristic approaches zero and behaves like Dijkstra’s algorithm.
Concrete example

Straight-line distance of 5 km with max speed 100 km/h converts to 3 minutes and is a lower bound on travel time, so it is admissible.

Misconception

Tuning heuristics for speed by overestimating can break optimality; document any approximation you accept.

Heuristic tuning cards
Conservative: global max speed → admissible, safe but may be weak
Regional tuning: adjust per area but validate admissibility at boundaries
Aggressive approximation: not admissible — faster but may be incorrect
