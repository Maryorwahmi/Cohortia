Module 12 › Chapter 12.4 › Caching & Hot Paths
When to cache

Cache read-heavy content metadata, manifests, and recent user progress to reduce tail latency and origin pressure.

Best practices
  • Set short TTLs for mutable data and invalidate on writes for critical fields.
  • Monitor cache hit ratios and replica lag for read replicas.
  • Design idempotent writes to tolerate occasional stale reads.
Concrete example

Keep top watched metadata and manifests in Redis with 60-second TTLs and invalidate entries when a new release or availability flag changes.

Pitfall

Relying on caches without invalidation leads to stale entitlement checks. Ensure caches are conservative for control-plane data.

Key Takeaway

Caching and replicas dramatically reduce read latency; combine them with monitoring and invalidation to preserve correctness.
