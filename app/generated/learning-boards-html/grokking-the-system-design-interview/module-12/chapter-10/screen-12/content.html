Module 12 › Chapter 12.10 › Consistency & Freshness
Definitions

Freshness: how recent the data is. Consistency: whether different views agree on the same data. They are related but distinct concerns for location systems.

Practical strategies
  • Short TTLs and automatic expiry to bound staleness.
  • Timestamped writes and idempotent consumers to reconcile out-of-order updates.
  • Partition by entity for ordered processing.
  • Separate real-time store (low-latency) from durable store (canonical history).
Example: dual-store pattern
A courier's current position is read from Redis for the UI. Every position is also appended to a durable event store asynchronously for analytics and audit. Timestamps ensure consumers choose the latest update.
Common misconception

Eventual consistency is not chaos. With monotonic timestamps and idempotent processing, updates converge predictably while preserving low latency.

Key takeaway
Assign clear SLAs: fast store for UI freshness, durable store for accuracy, and use timestamps to reconcile both.
