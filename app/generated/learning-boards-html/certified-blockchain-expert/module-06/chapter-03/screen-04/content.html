Module 06 › Chapter 6.3 › What is a Rollup?
Definition

Rollup mechanics

Execute off-chain, bundle many transactions, compute a new state root, and post a proof or optimistic batch plus data commitment to Layer 1.

Data availability: posting transaction data or a commitment on L1 so others can reconstruct state.
Integrity: L1 verifies a proof or processes a fraud proof to maintain correctness.
Throughput: Many off-chain transactions are compressed into one L1 verification step.
Key Takeaway

Rollups increase throughput by off-loading execution while keeping L1 as the trust root through commitments and proofs.

Analogy
Think of a rollup like a batch courier: many letters packaged into a single parcel with a seal. L1 checks the seal rather than every letter.
  • What it proves: the correctness of the new state root after applying the batch.
  • Why it matters: reduces on-chain computation and gas while preserving safety.
  • Misconception to avoid: off-chain does not mean unprotected; commitments preserve security.
Concrete example
Hundreds of transfers are applied off-chain, producing a single new state root and a proof. L1 validates that root or accepts a fraud proof within a challenge window.
