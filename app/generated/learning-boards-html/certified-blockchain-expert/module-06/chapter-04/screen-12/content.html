Design checklist
Follow these steps to conceptually design shard count, assignment, and a cross-shard flow.
Step 1 — Number of shards
Target TPS 10,000 / prototype TPS 100 = 100 shards required under ideal distribution.
Step 2 — Assignment mechanism
Example: assign transactions by hashing the sender address prefix to a shard ID, or route by transaction type to specific shards.
Step 3 — Cross-shard transfer (Alice on Shard A → Bob on Shard B)
Conceptual flow:
1. Alice submits transaction to Shard A; Shard A validates and locks or burns Alice's tokens.
2. Shard A creates a signed cross-shard message and posts it to a canonical message pool or beacon coordination layer.
3. Shard B sees the message, verifies the signature and proof of lock, then credits Bob.
4. Shard B emits a receipt; Shard A awaits the receipt to finalize and release the locked state.
5. If receipts fail to appear, dispute or recovery procedures revert or compensate according to the protocol.
Step 4 — Security challenge
Challenge: a shard attack if validators are weak or predictable. Mitigation: random validator sampling and a beacon chain that rotates validators across shards to raise the cost of capture.
Reflection
List your assumptions about perfect distribution, message finality, and validator honesty. Those assumptions determine whether your shard count and protocols preserve safety in real conditions.
