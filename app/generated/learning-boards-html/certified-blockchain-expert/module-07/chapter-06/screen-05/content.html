Module 7 › Chapter 7.6 › What Is a Blockchain Oracle?
Definition and role
An oracle retrieves external data, optionally validates or signs it, and publishes a result that smart contracts can read and act on. It transforms raw off-chain inputs into deterministic on-chain values.
  • Query an API, sensor, or human source.
  • Format and (optionally) aggregate results to reduce manipulation risk.
  • Deliver a signed or aggregated result in a transaction that a contract can verify or read.
Concrete use case
A stablecoin contract needs a USD price oracle to keep peg logic accurate. The oracle provides frequent, aggregated price snapshots the contract trusts for minting and burning decisions.
Quick comparison
Single-server oracle: simple but single point of failure.
Decentralized oracle network: higher reliability and tamper resistance.
Key Takeaway
An oracle moves trust to its design: pick an oracle architecture that matches your application's tolerance for risk, latency, and cost.
