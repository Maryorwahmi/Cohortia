Decentralization, Censorship Resistance, User Ownership

Clear definitions make it easier to design systems that meet the goals you choose.

Decentralization
Distribution of control and data across participants so no single operator can unilaterally change the system.
Censorship Resistance
Difficulty for any one entity to block, alter, or remove on-chain data because of replication and consensus.
User Ownership
Users hold keys and assets directly, and may hold governance rights via tokens, giving them direct control instead of platform custody.
Example: In a DeFi swap, smart contracts enforce rules (decentralized), the ledger records the swap publicly (censorship-resistant), and you keep custody of funds in your wallet (user ownership). Misconception check: decentralization does not automatically equal improved privacy or perfect governance.
