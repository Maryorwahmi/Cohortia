Smart Contracts: definition and limits

On-chain code that executes deterministically when transactions call it. It stores state and enforces application rules.

Benefits
  • Transparency and auditability of rules
  • Deterministic enforcement without intermediaries
  • Native custody and token interactions
Risks & limits
  • Immutability makes bugs costly
  • On-chain fixes may require governance or upgrades
  • Contract errors can lead to permanent asset loss
Concrete example
A swap contract executes token transfers automatically. If pricing logic is wrong, swaps could drain funds without a central operator to reverse them.
Pro tip
Design upgradeability and governance carefully, and practice thorough testing and auditing before deployment since immutability reduces remediation options.
