Best Practice
Key management and recovery

Protect private keys with hardware, redundancy, and clear recovery UX to prevent permanent loss or impersonation.

Hardware keys
Keep private keys in secure hardware to reduce theft risk.
Multi‑signature
Distribute signing authority to avoid single points of compromise.
Recovery protocols
Embed recovery methods into the DID approach to allow safe and auditable recovery paths.
Example strategy
A user stores a primary key in hardware, configures a multi‑sig arrangement with a recovery quorum, and registers a recovery policy in the DID method. This reduces single-point failures while providing a usable recovery path.
Misconception
Treating recovery as optional. Without recovery planning, key loss can be irreversible in decentralized systems.
Key Takeaway

Make key security and recovery a project requirement, combining hardware, redundancy, and method‑level recovery to protect users.
