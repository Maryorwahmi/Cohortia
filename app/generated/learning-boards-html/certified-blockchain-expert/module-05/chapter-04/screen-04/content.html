Core Concept
Centralized IAM and its limits
Central identity stores create single points of failure and fragment user profiles across services.
Problem bullets
  • Single point of failure: database breaches expose many users.
  • Identity sprawl: users must create separate identities across services.
  • Provider‑controlled recovery: resets centralize trust and attack surfaces.
Common mistake
Assuming centralization is always easier to secure. It may be administratively easier, but it concentrates risk.
Key Takeaway

Centralized IAM trades user control for operational simplicity, but at the cost of increased breach impact and fragmented user identity.

Analogy card
Think of centralized IAM as a single bank vault holding many customers' valuables. If the vault is breached or mismanaged, many customers suffer at once. Decentralized identity distributes custody back to individuals.
Why it matters
Design decisions about where identity lives affect compliance, breach response, and user privacy. Understanding centralized limitations helps you evaluate DID alternatives.
