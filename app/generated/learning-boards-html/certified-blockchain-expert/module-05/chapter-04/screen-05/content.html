Definition
Self‑Sovereign Identity

A model where people hold and control their identifiers and credentials, disclosing only what is necessary.

User Control
Individuals manage keys and consent to disclosures.
Privacy
Minimal disclosure reduces unnecessary data sharing.
Portability & Persistence
Credentials move across services and remain under owner control.
Example
A graduate receives a Verifiable Credential from a university. Later she reveals only the necessary claim to an employer, not her full profile. The credential is cryptographically signed so the employer can verify its authenticity without contacting the university directly.
Misconception
SSI does not remove the need for trusted issuers; it changes where trust anchors live and who controls the data shared in transactions.
Key Takeaway

SSI returns control to individuals while relying on verifiable, issuer‑signed credentials to preserve trust.
