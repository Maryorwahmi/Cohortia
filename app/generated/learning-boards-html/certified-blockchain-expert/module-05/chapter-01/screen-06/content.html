Module 5 › Chapter 5.1 › 51% Attack
Process Flow
How a 51% attack unfolds
1
Acquire majority control
Attacker accumulates >50% of mining power or stake, often via hardware purchase or staking concentration.
2
Create private fork
Attacker mines or signs blocks privately to build an alternate history that omits or reverses transactions.
3
Broadcast fork
Once the private fork outruns the public chain, the attacker releases it. Honest nodes adopt the longer chain, effecting a reorganization.
Limitations
Attackers cannot forge signatures or create coins from nothing; they can reorder or reverse transactions within the consensus model’s constraints.
Example scenario
On a small proof-of-work network, a group rents large amounts of hash power and mines a private fork to reverse a payment made earlier. Because the chain is short and hash power concentrated, the private fork overtakes the public chain, making the payment appear never to have happened.
Defender signals
  • High concentration of mining or staking power in a few entities.
  • Low total participation or low cost to acquire resources.
  • Sudden shifts in block times or unexpected reorgs.
Key Takeaway

A 51% attack is dangerous because it allows reordering and reversal under control, but it requires majority control which is costly on large, distributed networks.

Pro tip
Monitor concentration metrics and reorg history to detect rising risk early.
