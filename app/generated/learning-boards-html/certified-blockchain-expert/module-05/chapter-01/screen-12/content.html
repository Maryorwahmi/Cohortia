Module 5 › Chapter 5.1 › Operational Security
Human factor failures
Keys, contracts, and social attacks
Cryptography secures data integrity and authenticity when keys are safe and contracts are correct. Human mistakes and insecure practices remain primary failure points.
Key custody
Use hardware wallets and multi-signature controls for significant holdings. Avoid hot keys for large-value access.
Smart contract risk
Audit contracts, apply least privilege, and design upgrade paths to correct issues safely.
Concrete examples
  • Reused or backed-up private keys that are stolen via phishing.
  • Unaudited contracts that allow unauthorized withdrawals.
  • Centralized custody in exchanges that becomes a single point of failure.
Practical defense
Combine technical controls with operational processes: hardware wallets, multi-sig, audits, and staff training reduce human-risk exposure.
Key Takeaway

Protect keys and verify code. Cryptography secures data only when operational practices prevent key loss and code errors.
