GDPR & Immutability
Balancing the right to erasure with cryptographic permanence.
Definition
Immutability means once data is committed to the ledger it cannot be altered or deleted. GDPR requires erasure and rectification for personal data in many cases.
  • Conflict: permanence vs. erasure rights.
  • Consequence: avoid storing raw PII directly on-chain.
  • Recommended: store commitments or encrypted pointers on-chain.
Concrete example
A user's email is kept off‑chain in an encrypted database. A SHA‑256 hash of that email is committed on chain as proof of existence. When the user requests erasure, the off‑chain record is deleted; the on‑chain hash remains but cannot be reversed to reveal the email.
Common mistake
Believing a hash is the same as removing PII; hashes can still be privacy risks if weak inputs or linkage exist. Use salted hashes and encryption, and limit what is ever committed on-chain.
Key Takeaway

Design for erasure by keeping mutable personal data off-chain and using cryptographic commitments on-chain for integrity and auditability.
