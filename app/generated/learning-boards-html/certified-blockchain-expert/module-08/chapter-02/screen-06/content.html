What makes a token a security

A token is likely a security if it represents an investment with an expectation of profit derived from the efforts of others, or if it confers ownership-like rights such as dividends or governance tied to financial returns.

Examples
Fractional ownership of property paying rental income or tokens that deliver dividend streams.
Regulatory consequence
Registration, disclosure, and investor protections; transfer restrictions and accredited investor checks are common.
Technical opportunity
Smart contracts can embed compliance — automated dividend distribution and transfer gating to meet regulatory rules.
Concrete scenario

A token representing rental income from a building will most likely be a security. The issuer must plan for investor accreditation, disclosure schedules, and smart contract controls that restrict transfers to compliant parties.

Key Takeaway

If a token offers profit expectations or income rights, treat it as a security: design legal, disclosure, and technical controls accordingly.

Check: Does the token promise income or rely on a team's effort to increase value?
Pro tip: Use on-chain gating and identity oracles to align transfers with accreditation requirements.
