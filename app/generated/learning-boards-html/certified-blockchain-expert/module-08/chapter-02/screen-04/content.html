Module 8 › Chapter 8.2 › Payment Tokens
Definition & Regulatory Focus

Payment tokens are meant to transfer value and to be used for purchases or value storage. They are usually regulated under virtual currency frameworks and heavily scrutinized for AML/KYC compliance.

Real example
Bitcoin is the canonical payment token often treated as a virtual currency by regulators.
Why it matters
Exchanges and custodians must implement KYC, transaction monitoring, and reporting obligations that affect product architecture and compliance costs.
Common mistake
Assuming payment tokens are lightly regulated; in reality, anti-money-laundering rules are strict and can constrain product choices.
Concrete example & design prompts
Imagine integrating Bitcoin as payout currency. You must design for on-ramp/off-ramp compliance, transaction screening, and robust custody. For a wallet, include AML controls and clear user disclosures.
Key Takeaway

Payment tokens shift the compliance burden to service providers; design for KYC, monitoring, and clear user flows that show payment usage rather than speculative investment.

Check: Does your product enable immediate spending, or is it marketed for investment?
Pro tip: Document user flows that demonstrate functional use to support payment characterization.
