Module 8 › Chapter 8.3 › Why immutability creates a legal problem
Claim

Immutability prevents deletion

When personal data is placed on a public ledger it cannot be removed, which directly conflicts with GDPR's Right to Erasure and with accountability expectations for controllers.

Immutability
Distribution
Common misconception

Pseudonymous addresses guarantee privacy. In reality, address linkage and off-chain data can re-identify users, meaning apparent pseudonymity may not satisfy GDPR anonymization requirements.

Why it matters: misjudging identifiability exposes projects to compliance risk and enforcement action.
Concrete example

Public dApp storing user profiles

A dApp that writes names and emails to a public ledger cannot remove those entries on user request; even if a later transaction marks the profile as inactive, the original PII remains in the chain history.

Result: the platform cannot demonstrate erasure, creating regulatory exposure.
Key Takeaway

Avoid placing personal identifiers on immutable public ledgers; treat the chain as a proof layer rather than a PII store.
