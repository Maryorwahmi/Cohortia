Module 8 › Chapter 8.3 › Private vs Public Chains
Public Chain

Maximizes decentralization

Open participation, strong immutability, and global replication. This increases the difficulty of assigning controller responsibility and removes practical deletion options.

Use when censorship resistance and open verifiability are primary requirements.
Permissioned Chain

Increases operational control

Access is restricted, nodes and jurisdictions can be controlled, and governance can assign data controller roles—making compliance steps more feasible.

Use when regulatory accountability and controlled visibility outweigh the need for open decentralization.
Practical note

Permissioned ledgers reduce some compliance risks but require strong governance to ensure purge or update processes meet legal standards. Neither model automatically satisfies GDPR.
