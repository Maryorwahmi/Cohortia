Mainstream Adoption & Interoperability
Secure cross-chain flows are the backbone of many future scenarios.
Why it matters

Interoperability enables users to move assets, credentials, and state across networks, unlocking liquidity and composability that power real-world apps.

Security checklist
  • Prefer verifiable proofs over full-trust bridges.
  • Account for differing finality and reorg risk between chains.
  • Design for recovery and dispute resolution.
Common mistake

Assuming a single interoperability layer will be sufficient across all use cases; varied trust models will coexist and must be matched to risk profiles.

Interoperability Process
Step 1
Define trust model: custodial, federated, or trustless proofs.
Step 2
Select technical primitives: message relays, Merkle proofs, or light clients.
Step 3
Implement reconciliation and dispute resolution with clear fallbacks.
Example

A game NFT is minted on Chain A, a Merkle inclusion proof and ownership signature allow it to be recognized as collateral on Chain B without moving the original token.

Key Takeaway

Interoperability must be matched to security goals; proofs and minimal trust reduce systemic risk.
