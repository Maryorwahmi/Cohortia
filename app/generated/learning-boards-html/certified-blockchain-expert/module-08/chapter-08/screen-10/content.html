Security Future

Quantum Computing & Post-Quantum Cryptography

Preparing blockchains for a cryptographic paradigm shift.
Definition & Risk

Post-quantum cryptography comprises algorithms believed to resist attacks by quantum computers. The main risk is that widely used signature schemes could be broken in the future, exposing historical transactions and keys.

Threat

Long-term key compromise and transaction forgery if quantum attacks succeed.

Mitigation

Hybrid signatures, key rotation, and quantum-resistant key storage.

Practical Step

Identify long-lived assets now and plan migration timelines.

Concrete Example

A custodial wallet holding retirement funds should use hybrid signatures: transactions require both a classical ECDSA signature and a post-quantum signature. During the migration window the chain accepts both, allowing safe rotation without losing access.

Misconception: Quantum is either tomorrow or never. Plan with multi-year timelines and staged upgrades.
Action

Audit key lifetimes and adopt hybrid signing in next major release.

Key Takeaway

Quantum resistance is an engineering challenge you prepare for today; migration should be measured and auditable.
