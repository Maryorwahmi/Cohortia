Definition

DIDs and Verifiable Credentials

A privacy-focused identity model where issuers sign claims, holders store them, and verifiers check anchors on-chain.

DID
A decentralized identifier controlled by the holder; recorded as an anchor or registry entry for verification.
Verifiable Credential
A tamper-evident attestation issued to a holder and presented to verifiers with selective disclosure.
Key Takeaway

SSI gives control to users while using blockchain as an immutable anchor for verification and revocation without exposing private data.

Example flow
Issuer → Holder → Verifier
Issuer signs a credential and gives it to the holder. The holder stores it in a wallet and presents a cryptographic proof to a verifier. The verifier checks the issuer DID and revocation anchor on-chain.
Privacy note
Selective disclosure and zero-knowledge techniques can reveal only what is necessary, avoiding wholesale exposure of personal data.
Design challenge: how do you enable credential revocation without exposing user relationships or reintroducing a central revocation authority?
