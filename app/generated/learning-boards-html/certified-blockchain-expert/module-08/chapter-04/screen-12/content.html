Architecture

Mapping blockchain flows to tax responsibilities

For businesses, transactions generate accounting entries that affect corporate income and possibly VAT/GST. Payroll in crypto requires valuation at payment time and employer reporting. DAOs may lack clear reporting structures, so identify legal actors who control or receive value.

Corporate income
Record revenue, cost of goods sold, and expenses in fiat-equivalent values at relevant timestamps.
VAT / GST
Services billed in crypto may trigger indirect tax; retain invoices and rate calculations.
Payroll
Treat crypto wages as ordinary income for employees; compute withholding based on FMV at payment.
DAOs
Clarify who is economically benefiting and who reports taxes; consider using legal wrappers to reduce ambiguity.
Practical step
Create an internal policy mapping each blockchain flow to an accounting code and required supporting document to streamline returns preparation.
Common mistake
Assuming decentralization removes tax obligations; in practice responsibility attaches to identifiable persons or entities.
