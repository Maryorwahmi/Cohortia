Module 2 › Chapter 2.4 › Miner Economics
Revenue vs cost
Miners balance capital expenditure on hardware and ongoing electricity costs against expected rewards. Profitability determines who mines and how much hash power the network sustains.
Incentive: Block reward + transaction fees align miner interest with chain stability.
Attack costs
A 51 percent attack requires investing in a majority of hash rate and paying operational costs. If attacking damages coin value, the attacker's gains can be wiped out by market loss.
Misconception: an attacker can easily profit by double-spending. In large PoW networks the capital and running costs usually make this impractical.
Scenario: a miner weighs buying ASICs; expected reward stream must exceed amortized cost to justify investment.
Policy note: issuance schedules and halving events shift economic incentives and can influence miner participation.
Reflection: when could short-term profit tempt an attack despite long-term harm to the network?
