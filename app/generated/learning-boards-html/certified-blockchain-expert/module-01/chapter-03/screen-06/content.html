Module 1 › Chapter 1.3 › Types of DLT
Public DLT (Permissionless)
Anyone can join, read, write, and validate. Offers maximal decentralization and censorship resistance but can be slower and resource intensive.
Example: Bitcoin, Ethereum
Private DLT (Permissioned by one org)
Controlled by a single organization. Fast and private but loses decentralization; often functions like a distributed database within one entity.
Use case: internal enterprise workflows
Permissioned / Consortium
A group of organizations jointly manage the ledger. Permission is required to join and validate, giving a middle ground between openness and control.
Example: Hyperledger Fabric, Corda
When to pick public
When censorship resistance and broad decentralization are essential.
When to pick private
When privacy and transaction speed within a single organization matter more than decentralization.
When to pick permissioned
When multiple known organizations need to collaborate without a single trusted authority.
