Centralized Ledger vs Blockchain

Directly compare trust models, failure modes, and verification.
Centralized ledger
Single authority validates and stores records. Fast and simple but creates a single point of failure and potential censorship.
Blockchain ledger
Many nodes share and validate the ledger; changes require consensus. More resilient and auditable but operationally complex.
Concrete example
Editing a local text file is easy to hide. In a shared, append-only ledger, other participants detect an inconsistent history because copies disagree or consensus rejects the change.
Decision question
Does your application require tamper-evidence and distributed control more than simplicity and centralized performance?
Visual: trade-offs
Pros (Centralized)
  • Lower coordination overhead
  • Predictable performance
  • Centralized control
Pros (Blockchain)
  • Redundancy and resilience
  • Tamper-evidence and auditability
  • Reduced single-point risk
Key Takeaway

Choose a ledger model by weighing trust distribution versus operational complexity and performance needs.
