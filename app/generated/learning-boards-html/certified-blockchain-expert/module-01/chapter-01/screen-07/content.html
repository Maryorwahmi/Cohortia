Nodes, Copies, and Decentralization

How the network distributes responsibility and why that matters.
Node roles
Full nodes store the complete history; light nodes rely on others for verification. Validators or miners propose and commit blocks under specific protocol rules.
Resilience example
If a node goes offline, copies on other nodes keep the ledger available and consistent for new transactions.
Common misconception
More nodes does not automatically equal more decentralization; governance and control over client implementations matter.

Key Takeaway

Decentralization distributes authority and reduces single points of failure, but true decentralization depends on governance and incentives.
