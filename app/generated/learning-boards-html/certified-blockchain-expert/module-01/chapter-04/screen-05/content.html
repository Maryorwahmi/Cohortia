Definition

Full Nodes: The Backbone of Validation

Full nodes store the full ledger and independently verify every rule and transaction. They provide the authoritative local view of the chain.

What they check
  • Block headers and proof-of-work validity where applicable
  • Transaction format, signatures, and unspent inputs
  • Linkage to the previous block and chain selection rules
Practical note
Syncing a new full node requires downloading and verifying history, which can take hours or days depending on bandwidth and hardware.
Example: Detecting invalid chain
If an attacker broadcasts a block that spends inputs twice or violates script rules, a full node will reject that block and not extend its local chain with it.
Key takeaway
Full nodes preserve trustlessness; they are the network's referees and reduce reliance on third-party data providers.
