Architecture

BFT vs Leader-based ordering

BFT protocols
Offer deterministic finality and tolerate some faulty nodes; communication overhead increases with validator count.
Leader-based (Raft-like)
Provide simple ordering with high throughput when validator sets are stable; require operational controls for leader selection and failover.
Practical question

How many validators will participate and how often will membership change? The answer guides whether BFT communication costs are acceptable.

Example
A payments consortium with a dozen known banks may choose a BFT variant to guarantee finality and tolerate a few faulty nodes. A logistics network with a smaller set of trusted validators might choose a leader-based ordering for throughput and simpler operations.
Misconception
Consensus is not the only determinant of performance; architecture, transaction validation, and batching strategies also shape throughput and latency.
Key Takeaway

Pick consensus based on validator count, failure assumptions, and governance controls; consensus is an operational choice as much as a technical one.
