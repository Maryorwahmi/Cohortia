Firewall: purpose and scope
Enforces policy, reduces exposure, and controls traffic flows across boundaries.
Definition
A system that inspects network traffic and allows or blocks flows based on rules.
Why it matters
  • Reduces attack surface by blocking unsolicited inbound traffic.
  • Enables policy enforcement for services and users.
  • Forms part of a layered defense strategy.
Common misconception
A firewall alone is not enough; it should be combined with monitoring and segmentation.
Illustrative example
Allow HTTP/HTTPS to a web server, block administrative ports from the internet, permit specific internal SSH only from a management IP.
Analogy
A firewall is like a guarded checkpoint that checks each packet’s "papers" before letting it through.
Key Takeaway
Firewalls enforce boundary policies, but they must be tuned and paired with detection and segmentation.
