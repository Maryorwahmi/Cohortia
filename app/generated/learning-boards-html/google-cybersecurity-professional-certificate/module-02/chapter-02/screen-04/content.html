Core Concept

The highway and packets metaphor

Packets are the vehicles that carry headers for routing and payloads containing data. Different devices see different parts and act accordingly—this shapes where you place security controls.

Packet anatomy
  • Header: addresses and protocol info used for forwarding.
  • Payload: the user data that needs confidentiality or integrity protections.
Example
A web request uses TCP to ensure ordered delivery; routers route by IP, switches forward by MAC, and application content is carried in the payload.
Key points
  • Place encryption around the payload when confidentiality is required.
  • Use layer thinking to decide what a device can inspect or enforce.
  • A misconception: switches do not automatically provide IP-layer filtering without extra configuration.
Key Takeaway

Understanding packet structure and device visibility is the foundation for placing effective controls and detecting misuse.
