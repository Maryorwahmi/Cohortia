Definition
Phishing and the Human Element
Attackers manipulate people to bypass technical controls.

Phishing messages mimic trusted senders to harvest credentials or deliver malware. They often use urgency, authority, or curiosity to coerce actions.

Example
An employee receives a message that looks like it's from the CEO asking to update payroll via a link. The link goes to a fake login page.
Defense Layers
  • Filtering and DMARC/SPF checks to reduce delivery of phishing emails.
  • User training and simulated phishing drills to build recognition skills.
  • Easy reporting workflows so suspected messages are analyzed quickly.
Key Takeaway

People are targeted directly; reduce risk with training, reporting, and layered technical controls.

Scenario Card
Fake CEO email requests payroll link. One employee clicks and enters credentials.
Immediate Actions
Reset compromised credentials, review logs for suspicious access, and run phishing campaign to raise awareness.
Process Fix
Create a verification policy for executive requests that affects payroll or finance.
Misconception
Users are not the problem; often they lack the tools or processes to report suspicious requests quickly.
