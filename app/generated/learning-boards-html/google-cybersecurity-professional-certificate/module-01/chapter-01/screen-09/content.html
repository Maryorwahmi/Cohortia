Definition
DoS & DDoS
Resource exhaustion designed to deny service to legitimate users.

DoS floods a target from a single source; DDoS uses many sources. The effect is the same: availability is compromised.

Mitigations
  • Autoscaling and redundant infrastructure
  • Traffic filtering and scrubbing services
  • Rate limiting and upstream protections
Key Takeaway

Design for capacity and rapid detection so availability can be preserved under attack.

Concrete Example
When an e-commerce server becomes unreachable due to an influx of fake traffic, customers cannot buy and revenue is lost.
Immediate Steps
Enable DDoS protection, divert traffic through scrubbing, and activate incident response for communication and recovery.
Planning
Capacity testing, redundancy, and contractual DDoS protection reduce downtime risk.
Misconception
Treating every traffic spike as an attack leads to unnecessary disruption; combine traffic analysis with other signals.
