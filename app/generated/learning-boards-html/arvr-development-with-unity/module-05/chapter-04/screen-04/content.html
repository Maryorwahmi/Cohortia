Core Concept
Why Frame Rate and Latency Matter
Stable FPS and low latency preserve immersion and prevent motion sickness.
Definition
Frame rate measures frames per second. Latency measures time between input and visible response. Both must be low and stable in VR.
Concrete example
If your scene drops from 90 to 45 FPS during a heavy physics update, users can feel judder and discomfort. Fixing the heavy update or distributing work across frames stabilizes the experience.
Common misconception
Higher resolution always improves comfort. In VR, sacrificing stability for higher detail often increases motion sickness risk.
Key Takeaway

Aim for steady frame times and low input-to-photon latency; measure stability with the Profiler rather than assuming it from visuals.

Visualization
Frame time plot
Imagine a frame-time graph showing small, flat bars at target time. Spikes correspond to stutters and are what you must remove.
Reflection
Which systems in your project produce occasional spikes: heavy shaders, large textures, or physics? Start profiling those.
