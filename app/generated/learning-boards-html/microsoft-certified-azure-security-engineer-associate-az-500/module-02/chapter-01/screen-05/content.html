Key Distinction

User Risk vs. Sign-in Risk

Understanding the two primary categories of identity threats.

User Risk

The likelihood that a user account has been compromised. Focuses on the integrity of the user's identity over time.

  • •Leaked credentials found on the dark web.
  • •Unusual patterns of activity associated with a specific user.
  • •Account showing signs of persistent compromise.
Example

An employee's password is found in a data breach dump online, indicating their account may be compromised.

Sign-in Risk

The likelihood that a particular sign-in attempt is not legitimate. Evaluates the context of a single sign-in event.

  • •Impossible travel scenarios (e.g., rapid geographic changes).
  • •Sign-ins from unfamiliar locations or IP addresses.
  • •Attempts from infected devices or legacy authentication.
Example

A user signs in from New York, then 10 minutes later from Tokyo, an impossible physical travel scenario.

Key Takeaway

User risk focuses on account compromise, while sign-in risk focuses on the legitimacy of a specific access attempt.
