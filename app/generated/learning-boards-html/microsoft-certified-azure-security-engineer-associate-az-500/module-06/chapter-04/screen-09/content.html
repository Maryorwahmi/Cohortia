Core Principle

Why Data Retention Matters

Data retention defines how long specific types of data must be kept and when it should be securely deleted. It's essential for compliance with regulatory requirements (e.g., GDPR, HIPAA, SOX) and for minimizing risks.

  • Regulatory Compliance: Adhere to legal mandates for data storage.
  • Risk Mitigation: Reduce exposure from holding unnecessary data.
  • Cost Management: Optimize storage costs by deleting old data.
  • Operational Efficiency: Ensure data is available when needed for audits.
Analogy

Think of it like a library's archiving policy: some books are kept forever, others for a few years, and some are discarded after a short period to make space and manage resources.

Key Takeaway

Proper data retention balances legal compliance, operational needs, and cost efficiency by ensuring data is kept only for its necessary lifespan.

The Retention Balance
Compliance & Audit

Meet legal and regulatory requirements.

Risk & Cost

Minimize attack surface and storage expenses.

Effective data retention policies strike a balance between legal obligations to keep data and the security/cost benefits of deleting it when no longer needed.
