Response Phase

Containment: Stopping the Spread

Limiting Damage and Preventing Further Compromise

Key Containment Actions

  • Isolate Compromised VMs: Prevent lateral movement by segmenting affected machines.
  • Block Malicious IPs: Use Azure Firewall or NSGs to cut off communication with attacker infrastructure.
  • Revoke Compromised Credentials: Immediately invalidate stolen user or service principal access.
  • Disable Accounts: Temporarily suspend user accounts suspected of compromise.
Common Mistake

Delaying containment actions, allowing attackers more time to escalate privileges, exfiltrate data, or compromise additional systems.

Why Containment is Critical

Containment is the immediate priority after detection. It's about limiting the 'blast radius' of an attack. Without swift containment, a small incident can quickly escalate into a major breach, leading to greater data loss, operational disruption, and recovery costs.

Minimizes damage and reduces recovery effort

Example: VM Isolation

A virtual machine is found to be infected with ransomware. A containment action would be to apply a Network Security Group (NSG) rule that blocks all inbound and outbound traffic to that VM, except for specific management access, preventing the ransomware from spreading.

Key Takeaway

Effective containment is the immediate priority in incident response, crucial for stopping the attack's spread and limiting its overall impact.
