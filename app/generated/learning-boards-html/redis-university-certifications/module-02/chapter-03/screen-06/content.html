Internal structures

Redis uses a hash for member-to-score lookups and a skip list to maintain order. This explains the complexity of common operations.
Hash table
O(1) average lookup to find if a member exists and to get its score.
Skip list
O(log N) operations for insertion, deletion, and range queries; it maintains sorted order by score.
When a score changes, Redis updates both structures: hash for the score and skip list to reposition the member. This dual structure is why some operations are constant time and others logarithmic. It also explains why ZINCRBY is preferred for concurrent increments: it performs an atomic update and repositioning without your application performing read-modify-write cycles.

Practical impact

  • Expect O(log N) for range queries and updates that change order.
  • Use ZINCRBY to avoid race conditions and extra round trips.
  • Large leaderboards scale well due to logarithmic reordering cost.
Common Mistake

Assuming all ZSET operations are O(1) leads to surprising slowdowns when many range operations are issued at high frequency.
