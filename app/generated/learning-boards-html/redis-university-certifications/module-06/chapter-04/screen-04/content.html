Key metrics defined
What to collect and why each metric matters
Throughput (RPS)
Operations per second; useful for capacity planning and comparing command types.
Latency (avg, P50, P99)
Average shows common case; percentiles reveal tail behavior important for user experience.
Latency distribution
Look for long tails that indicate occasional expensive operations or network spikes.
Example
A benchmark shows 120k RPS, avg latency 0.6ms, P99 12ms. The P99 points to operations that occasionally block or hit network issues and require further investigation.
Interpretation bullets
  • High RPS with high P99: many requests succeed quickly but some are slow — check blocking commands and persistence spikes.
  • Low RPS with low latency: possibly limited by client concurrency or network RTT.
  • Latency grows with value size: larger payloads increase network and serialization time.
Key Takeaway

Collect both RPS and percentile latency. Investigate tail latency before optimizing averages.
