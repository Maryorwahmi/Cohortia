Module 4 › Chapter 4.3 › What is a WAP?
Definition & Responsibilities
  • Acts as a bridge between wireless radio and wired Ethernet.
  • Broadcasts SSID so clients can discover the network.
  • Handles authentication, association, and basic QoS.
  • Manages radio characteristics like channel and power.
Analogy
Think of a WAP as a gate agent: it verifies credentials, directs traffic, and keeps operations smooth during busy times.
Concrete example
A laptop sends a web request over Wi‑Fi. The AP receives the radio signal, converts it to a wired Ethernet frame, and forwards it to the switch and onward to the internet gateway.
Key takeaway
WAPs are active network devices, not passive radios. They enforce policies and connect users to the wired network.
Common mistake
Assuming an AP only broadcasts signal. In fact it authenticates and manages client sessions and can affect performance.
Pro tip
Name SSIDs clearly and set strong encryption from the first deployment to avoid insecure defaults.
