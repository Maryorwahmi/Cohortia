SSID and Joining Process
SSID is broadcast by an AP so clients can discover the network. After selecting an SSID, a client associates and then authenticates. These steps negotiate capabilities and confirm access rights.
Example — Home network
You see "MyHomeOffice_WIFI" on your laptop, enter the passphrase, and the AP authenticates you using WPA2‑PSK. Your device receives an IP and can access the LAN.
QoS and prioritization
APs can mark traffic and prioritize voice or streaming packets so those flows remain smooth during congestion. This is especially important for VoIP and video conferencing.
Misconception
SSID equals VLAN. In fact SSID is a name; mapping to a VLAN is a separate configuration on the AP or controller.
Key takeaway
Use clear SSIDs and enable strong encryption. For businesses prefer per‑user 802.1X rather than shared keys.
