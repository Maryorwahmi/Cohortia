Module 4 › Chapter 4.3 › Wi‑Fi Standards
Standards overview
Each generation improved speed, efficiency, or multi‑user handling. Choose based on device capability and environment density.
802.11b / g
2.4 GHz only; early consumer speeds; prone to interference.
802.11a
Introduced 5 GHz for higher throughput but shorter range.
802.11n (Wi‑Fi 4)
Added MIMO and dual‑band operation for higher aggregate throughput.
802.11ac (Wi‑Fi 5)
Enhanced 5 GHz performance with wider channels and more spatial streams.
802.11ax (Wi‑Fi 6)
Improves efficiency in dense networks using OFDMA and MU‑MIMO.
Concrete decision guide
  1. If many clients share the airspace, prefer Wi‑Fi 6 APs for efficiency.
  2. If devices need long range and compatibility, consider 2.4 GHz support too.
  3. For high throughput on short hops, prioritize 5 GHz capable APs and clients.
Misconception
Newer standards automatically improve range. In reality range depends on band, antenna design, and environment; newer standards primarily improve throughput and efficiency.
Key Takeaway

Match AP standard to client capability and network density for best results.
