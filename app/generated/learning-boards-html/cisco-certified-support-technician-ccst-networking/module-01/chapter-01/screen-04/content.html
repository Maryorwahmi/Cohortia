Module 1 › Chapter 1.1 › What is a Computer Network?
Definition
A collection of interconnected devices that communicate and share resources.
Why networks matter: share printers, centralize storage, support collaboration, and provide internet access. These are primary drivers for efficiency and cost savings.
Concrete example
A small office uses a file server so three staff members can edit the same documents without emailing copies. The server sits on the LAN and the router connects that LAN to the internet for external access.
Common misconception
Believing "network = internet." Local networks provide many services independently of the external link; always test local connectivity first.
Key Takeaway

Networks let devices share resources and communicate; design choices affect performance, cost, and troubleshooting steps.

Analogy
Neighborhood postal system: houses create letters, roads carry them, and sorting centers route them. This maps directly to end devices, media, and intermediary devices.
Senders & receivers: laptops, phones, printers.
Roads: Ethernet, fiber, wireless.
Sorters: switches, routers, firewalls, WAPs.
Outcome: Faster collaboration and reduced hardware duplication.
