Module 1 › Chapter 1.2 › Physical vs Logical
Definition

Physical and logical topology

Physical topology is the wiring and device placement; logical topology is the pattern of data flow determined by protocols. Both maps explain different failure modes and performance characteristics.

Physical
Wires, ports, switches
Used to locate hardware faults and verify cable runs.
Logical
Data flow and protocol behavior
Used to trace ARP, routing, VLAN and broadcast behavior.
Common misconception

Believing physical and logical topology always match. Protocols and VLANs can create logical flows that differ significantly from wiring.

Concrete example

A small office is wired as a star to a central switch. The network uses a protocol that organizes traffic into a logical ring for orderly access. If users cannot reach a shared server, a physical check might show the switch uplink is up, while a logical check could reveal a misconfigured VLAN that isolates the server. Both checks together pinpoint the real cause.

Check your understanding
  • When would a physical map be sufficient to solve a problem?
  • When must you inspect VLANs or routing to diagnose the issue?
  • How can logical topology hide a cable fault?
Key Takeaway

Use physical maps to find hardware failures and logical maps to understand traffic rules; both are necessary for effective troubleshooting.
