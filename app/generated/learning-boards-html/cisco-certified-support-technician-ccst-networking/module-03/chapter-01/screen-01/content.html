Introduction to IPv4 Addressing

Learn why IPv4 addresses matter, how they’re structured, and the practical checks technicians use every day.

In this lesson
Read dotted-decimal IPv4, convert to binary, use masks to find network and host, and check addresses on your machine.
Coming up
Structure
32 bits, four octets
Subnetting
Mask divides network & host
Tools
ipconfig and ip a examples
Visual concept
IPv4 = 32 bits
Example: 192.168.1.10 becomes four octets: 192 | 168 | 1 | 10
192   = 11000000
168   = 10101000
1     = 00000001
10    = 00001010
Why this matters
Devices need unique addresses to deliver packets reliably. Misreading masks or assigning reserved addresses causes connectivity failures you’ll diagnose as a technician.
Key Takeaway

IPv4 addresses are binary numbers expressed in readable octets; the subnet mask identifies which bits belong to the network.
