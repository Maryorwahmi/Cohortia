Module 7 › Chapter 7.4 › Idempotency & Outcomes
Concept explained

Tasks should report results that match your intent. Use failed_when to mark a task as failed only when your criteria are met, and changed_when to avoid false positives for change detection.

Concrete example
- name: Check if a specific service is running (and fail if not)
  ansible.builtin.command: systemctl is-active my_critical_service
  register: service_status
  failed_when: service_status.rc != 0 or 'inactive' in service_status.stdout
  changed_when: false
Key takeaway
Be explicit: translate raw output into business-level success or failure.
Common mistake
Trusting module return codes without checking stdout/stderr can produce misleading reports.
Why it matters
  • Accurate reporting prevents unnecessary follow-up tasks and alerts.
  • Clear failure semantics make incident response faster and less confusing.
  • Well-annotated checks document non-obvious success criteria for future maintainers.
Analogy
Think of failed_when as setting the rules of a test: the machine reports its raw score, and you decide what constitutes a pass or fail for your environment.
Try this
Review a check task in your playbooks and ask: does the module's default success map to your intent? If not, add failed_when or changed_when.
