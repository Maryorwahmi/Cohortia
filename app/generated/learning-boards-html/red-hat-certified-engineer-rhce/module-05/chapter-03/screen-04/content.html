Module 5 › Chapter 5.3 › What is Network Bonding?
Definition

Network bonding is a kernel driver that groups physical NICs into a single logical interface. The master holds the IP configuration, while slave devices are configured without IPs and act as members of the group.

Example: bond0 with enp0s8 and enp0s9. Assign 192.168.2.10/24 to bond0. If enp0s8 fails, traffic continues on enp0s9.
Common Misconception
Slaves should not retain IP addresses. If they do, they were not properly enslaved and the master will not carry traffic correctly.
Key Takeaway

Bonding centralizes IP on the master and uses kernels drivers to provide redundancy and, when allowed, bandwidth aggregation.

Annotated Diagram

IP: 192.168.2.10/24
