Module 6 › Chapter 6.4 › Access Control Importance
What access control is

Access control restricts which addresses can call sensitive functions or change critical state. In smart contracts this is implemented by patterns and modifiers.

Common consequences of weak rules
  • Unauthorized transfers or privilege escalation leading to theft.
  • Irreversible contract state changes that cannot be rolled back.
  • Loss of trust and costly migrations to replace flawed contracts.
Real-world analogy

Treat administrative keys like vault keys: multiple safeguards and separation of duties reduce single-point risk.

Misconception to avoid

Do not assume msg.sender represents the end-user in every context. When contracts call contracts, the caller is the intermediate contract address, changing authorization checks' behavior.

Check your understanding
Example
A DEX owner key controls fee and listing changes—if leaked, an attacker could change fee recipients or upgrade logic.
Key takeaway
Plan roles and controls before deployment and test authorization boundaries in staging.
