Module 6 › Chapter 6.6 › Oracle Problem
Definition
What the oracle problem is
Smart contracts cannot directly access external data. Oracles provide that bridge but bring trust and timeliness assumptions.
Analogy
A contract is sealed in a bubble; the oracle is the messenger reporting outside facts. If the messenger lies, the contract acts on falsehoods.
Why it matters
  • Incorrect inputs lead to incorrect on-chain state and potential financial loss.
  • Frequency and source of updates change exploitability.
  • Design choices determine how hard it is to manipulate data.
Key Takeaway

Oracles are necessary but not neutral; treat their feeds as threat surfaces, not as absolute truth.

Concrete example
Single-owner price oracle
A contract that accepts a price set by its oracle owner will immediately use any updated value. If the owner is malicious or compromised, on-chain logic executes incorrectly, enabling theft or incorrect liquidations.
Common misconception
Thinking that putting a feed on-chain removes all trust issues. In truth, the trust shifts to the data provider and the update method.
