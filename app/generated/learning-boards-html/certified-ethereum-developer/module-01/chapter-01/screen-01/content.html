Overview

Why this chapter matters

Blockchains let participants verify history without trusting a single authority. That shift enables new kinds of digital ownership, resilient systems, and programmable money.

Ledger = History
A ledger records each transaction in sequence and copies exist across nodes.
Hashes link blocks
Each block stores a fingerprint of the previous block, making tampering evident.
Decentralized control
Control and verification are shared, reducing single points of failure.
Key Takeaway

A blockchain pairs distributed copies of a ledger with cryptographic linking so participants can verify the history without trusting a single operator.

Visual Concept
