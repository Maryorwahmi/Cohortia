Module 1 › Chapter 1.1 › System Comparisons
Centralized

Single authority model

Owner sets rules and holds data. Failure of the owner affects all users.

Advantage: Simpler governance and often higher throughput.
Risk: Single point of failure and censorship.
Distributed

Copies for availability

Data is replicated to increase uptime but decision power may remain centralized.

Advantage: Better availability and resilience against hardware failures.
Trade-off: Control and policy can remain concentrated.
Decentralized

Shared control and copies

Participants share validation and governance; rules are enforced by consensus rather than a single party.

Advantage: Reduced censorship and single-point vulnerabilities.
Trade-off: Increased coordination complexity and possible throughput limits.
Scenario
For a corporate payroll choose centralized control for privacy and regulatory compliance. For a public currency prefer decentralization to avoid single-entity control and censorship. Each choice reflects trade-offs between trust, governance, and practicality.
