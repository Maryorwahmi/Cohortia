Introduction to Decentralized Applications

Understand what makes an application decentralized, its components, and the practical trade-offs developers face.

In this lesson: definition • architecture • trade-offs
Open source
transparent code
Decentralized

Incentivized
tokens & rewards

Why this matters

When backend rules and state live on a blockchain, users hold persistent control of tokens and records. Systems gain resistance to censorship and tampering, but must manage costs and UX differences compared with centralized apps.

Key Takeaway

DApps trade centralized control for transparent, blockchain-backed logic and ownership, introducing new engineering and UX trade-offs.

Quick example
A tipping DApp uses a smart contract to transfer ETH and IPFS to store profile images.
What to watch
Gas fees and user wallet flows often define whether a DApp will be practical for users.
