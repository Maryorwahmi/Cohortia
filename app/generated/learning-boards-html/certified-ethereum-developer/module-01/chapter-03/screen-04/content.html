Module 1 › Chapter 1.3 › What is a Wallet?
Definition

Wallet = Key Manager

A wallet generates, stores, and uses private keys to sign transactions. It may be software or hardware, custodial or non‑custodial.

• Public key/address receives funds. • Private key signs transactions. • Seed phrase restores keys if backed up.
Analogy
Think of a wallet as a set of car keys. The keys open and drive the car, but the car itself exists separately. Lose the keys and you lose access.
Key Takeaway

Do not share private keys or seed phrases. Back them up offline to avoid permanent loss.

Concrete Example
MetaMask Flow
MetaMask generates a seed phrase. You store it offline. MetaMask then signs transactions locally with your private key when you approve them.
Custodial Example
An exchange holds your private keys. This is easier but requires trusting the exchange for security and recovery.
Common Mistake
Treating a wallet like a bank account UI instead of recognizing it as the keeper of keys. That mindset increases risk of accidental exposure.
