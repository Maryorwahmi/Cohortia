Module 3 › Chapter 3.4 › Reentrancy — What it is and why it matters
Definition & core idea
An external call can re-enter your contract and run logic that assumes earlier state changes have occurred. This can lead to repeated withdrawals or inconsistent state.
Misconception: Only complex contracts are vulnerable. Small withdraw patterns are at risk too.
Concrete scenario: A malicious contract receives Ether and its fallback function calls withdraw repeatedly to drain the balance.
Why it matters: Funds can be drained irreversibly on-chain; tests must reproduce the attack path.
Key Takeaway

Assume external calls may call back into your contract; design state updates so that a reentrant call cannot observe stale state.

Analogy & guided test
Analogy: think of handing a key to a stranger mid-transaction. If you then change the lock after they act, the sequence allowed them extra access. Guided test: deploy a simple vulnerable withdraw contract and an attacker contract that calls withdraw from its fallback. Observe the victim contract balance fall faster than expected.
Check your understanding
  • What function orders allow reentrancy?
  • How could a fallback function be used to re-enter?
  • Which tests would reproduce the attack?
