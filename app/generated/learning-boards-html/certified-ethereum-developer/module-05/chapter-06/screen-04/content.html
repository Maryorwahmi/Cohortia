Module 6 › Chapter 5.6 › On-chain Limits
Why large files don't belong on-chain
Storing bytes in smart contracts consumes gas, slows transactions, and bloats the chain.
  • Cost: every on-chain byte increases gas and user fees.
  • Performance: blockchains are not optimized for binary serving.
  • Maintainability: updating large on-chain data is expensive and rare.
Common misconception
Storing files on-chain equals permanence. It does, but at a prohibitive cost; permanence can be achieved more cheaply with IPFS plus an on-chain CID reference.
Analogy
Think of the blockchain as a ledger: it records the receipt and the pointer to a warehouse, but not every physical item inside the warehouse. IPFS is the distributed warehouse where large items are stored.
Concrete example
An NFT contract stores ipfs://CID in tokenURI. The image bytes live on IPFS; the chain stores a small string that proves the image contents.
Key Takeaway

Store references on-chain and assets on IPFS to balance permanence and cost.
