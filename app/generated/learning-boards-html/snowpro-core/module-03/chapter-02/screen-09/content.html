Module 3 › Chapter 3.2 › Micro-partitions and pruning
What are micro-partitions?
Snowflake automatically stores table data in consecutive micro-partitions. Each micro-partition carries metadata such as min/max per column. This metadata is cheap to consult and powers pruning.
Why it matters
When your filters align with micro-partition ranges, Snowflake can skip scanning entire micro-partitions and avoid reading bytes.
Pruning example
If sale_date column's micro-partition ranges do not overlap your requested date range, Snowflake prunes those partitions and does not read them. This is the core reason why filtering on date often reduces scanned bytes drastically.
Misconception
Micro-partitions are not user-managed; they are Snowflake-managed units. Clustering only adjusts how rows with similar values are co-located across micro-partitions.
Key Takeaway
Design queries to filter on columns with strong micro-partition correlation to allow pruning to skip work.
