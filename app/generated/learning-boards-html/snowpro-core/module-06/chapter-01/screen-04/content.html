Core Concept
What the Exam Blueprint Is and Why It Matters
A strategic document that lists domains, sub-topics, and approximate weightings to guide study focus.
Definition
An official guide detailing each domain, its scope, and how much it contributes to the score.
Practical Use
Prioritize study time by weighting, annotate with confidence ratings, and plan targeted practice.
Step-by-step example
  1. Download the official blueprint and list the eight domains.
  2. Under each domain, write the sub-topics mentioned in the guide.
  3. Rate your confidence for each sub-topic: Strong, Moderate, or Weak.
  4. Allocate weekly hours to each domain proportional to its weighting and your weakness.
Concrete example
If Virtual Warehouses is 15–20% and you are weak, schedule two focused practice sessions and a timed quiz in the same week. If Semi-Structured Data is 5–10% and you are strong, review key functions lightly.
Key Takeaway

Treat the blueprint as a dynamic study map: annotate, rate, allocate, practice, and reallocate as you improve.

Common mistake
Studying every topic equally rather than allocating effort by weight and weakness.
