Core Concept › What is a Resource Monitor?
Definition and purpose
A monitor tracks credits and enforces quota-based actions to limit compute spend. It is used to prevent unexpected billing and to formalize budgets.
Why it matters
Transforms passive metering into active controls that stop runaway queries or forgotten warehouses.
Common misconception
Monitors do not automatically reduce storage costs; they primarily control compute via warehouse suspension and account-level limits.
Concrete example
A daily monitor with CREDIT_QUOTA = 5 will notify at intermediate thresholds and suspend the warehouse when the quota is reached, stopping further compute credits for that period.
Analogy
Treat the monitor like a prepaid data cap. You set how many credits are allowed for a period, and the system enforces that cap so you don’t exceed your planned spend.
Key Takeaway

A resource monitor links measured credit usage to automated actions, making budgets enforceable rather than advisory.
