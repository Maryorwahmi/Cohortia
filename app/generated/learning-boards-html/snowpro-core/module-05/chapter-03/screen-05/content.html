Module 05 › Chapter 5.3 › How Synchronization Works
Incremental flow
Changes on the primary are captured and transferred as deltas, updating replicas efficiently without full copies each time.
Monitoring
Use SHOW REPLICATION DATABASES to view replication status and identify lag. Schedule checks in your runbook to avoid promoting a stale replica.
Misconception
Expecting zero latency. Replication is fast and incremental, but lag exists and should inform your RPO.
Check your understanding
When would you accept a few minutes of lag to gain geographic read locality? When would you require near-zero lag for transactional workloads?
Example scenario
On the primary: INSERT rows into my_source_table. After replication and SHOW REPLICATION DATABASES shows the replica is current, SELECT from the replica to confirm visibility. Writes to the replica will fail until a failover promotes it.
Practical note
Include replication status checks in automation that triggers failover to ensure you promote an up-to-date replica.
Key takeaway

Replication is continuous and incremental; monitor status and plan for lag in your recovery objectives.
