Module 5 › Chapter 5.6 › RTO & RPO
What they are
Clear, measurable recovery and data-loss objectives driven by business impact.
Definition: RTO is allowable downtime. RPO is allowable data loss window.
Example: Public checkout: RTO = minutes, RPO = seconds. Reporting DB: RTO = hours, RPO = hours.
Misconception: Technology limits should not set targets; business cost should.
Map to design
Choose synchronous or auto-failover for low RTO/RPO; use async replication or less frequent backups for higher tolerances.
Verification
Run tests to measure actual RTO and observe replication lag to verify RPO.
Concrete scenario
An online store assigns RTO = 5 minutes and RPO = 10 seconds for the checkout DB. This requires minimal replication lag and automated failover capabilities to meet the target.
Key Takeaway

Let business impact set RTO/RPO. Use these targets to select replication, failover modes, and backup cadence.

Common mistake
Assuming the highest-availability option is always required. Cost and complexity should match business value.
