Module 5 › Chapter 5.3 › Disaster Recovery vs HA
Definition

HA versus DR

High availability keeps systems running in-region; disaster recovery prepares for cross-region restoration after catastrophic failure.

HA: Focus on redundancy and failover inside the same region to reduce minute-to-minute downtime.
DR: Focus on cross-region recovery, acceptable data loss, and re-establishing operations in a different region.
Why it matters
  • Planning DR helps you meet business continuity targets and compliance obligations.
  • Confusing HA for DR can lead to failed recovery when a whole region goes down.
Concrete example

An e-commerce application uses availability zones and a single-region replica for HA. If the region loses all capacity, zone-level HA provides no cross-region failover. A geo-restore or Auto-Failover Group is needed to recover services in another region and restore customer-facing operations.

Common mistake

Assuming HA equals DR. Many teams discover this only during an actual outage when cross-region recovery steps are missing.

Pro tip

Document cross-service dependencies and include network configuration and DNS in DR drills to avoid partial recoveries.

Key Takeaway

Treat HA and DR as complementary: HA reduces local downtime, DR recovers from regional outages.
