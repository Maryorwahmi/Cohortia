Readability Rules

Condensing IPv6 Addresses: Omitting Leading Zeros

Making Long Addresses More Manageable

Rule 1: Leading Zeros

Any leading zeros in a 16-bit segment can be removed. A segment of all zeros can be represented as a single '0'.

Common Mistake

Do not remove trailing zeros or zeros in the middle of a hexadecimal block, as this changes the address value.

Key Takeaway

Omitting leading zeros is the first step to simplifying IPv6 addresses, improving clarity without altering the address itself.

Omitting Leading Zeros Example

2001:0db8:85a3:0000:0000:8a2e:0370:7334
Applies Rule
2001:db8:85a3:0:0:8a2e:370:7334

Notice how '0db8' becomes 'db8', '0000' becomes '0', and '0370' becomes '370'.
