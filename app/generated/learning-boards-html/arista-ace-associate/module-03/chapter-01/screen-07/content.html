Flooding, Broadcast, and Unknown Multicast

When Switches Send Frames Everywhere

If a destination MAC address is not found in the switch's table, the switch floods the frame out all active interfaces (except the ingress port). This ensures the frame reaches its destination and allows the switch to learn its MAC address from the response.

Types of Flooded Traffic
  • Unknown Unicast: Destination MAC not in table.
  • Broadcast: Destination MAC FF:FF:FF:FF:FF:FF (for all devices).
  • Unknown Multicast: Multicast group not learned on specific ports.
Key Takeaway

Flooding is a necessary mechanism for discovery and broadcast communication, but it can lead to network congestion if not managed properly.

Misconception

Flooding is not always a sign of a problem; it's a normal and necessary behavior for initial device discovery and broadcast communication. However, excessive or continuous flooding can indicate a network issue like a loop.
