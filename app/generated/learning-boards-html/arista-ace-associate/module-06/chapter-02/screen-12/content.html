Process Step 3: Formulate Hypothesis

Propose a Root Cause

Based on gathered information, develop a plausible explanation for the problem.

  • •
    Logical Deduction: Connect symptoms to potential causes.
  • •
    Narrow Scope: Focus on the most likely culprits first.
Process Step 4: Test Hypothesis

Confirm or Deny

Use advanced tools to validate your hypothesis:

  • •
    tcpdump: Inspect packet flow for specific traffic.
  • •
    monitor session: For sustained, in-depth traffic analysis.
  • •
    event-monitor: Track real-time system events.
OSI Model Framework

Always remember the OSI model: start at the physical layer and work your way up. Is the cable good? Is the interface up? Is IP addressing correct? Is routing working? Is the application listening?

Common Mistake

Changing multiple variables at once during testing. This makes it impossible to isolate the true cause or effect of any single change.

Key Takeaway

Formulate clear hypotheses and test them systematically with targeted tools, refining your approach until the root cause is confirmed.

Example: Testing Hypothesis
# Hypothesis: Firewall is blocking port 8080
tcpdump interface Ethernet1 host 10.0.0.10 and port 8080

# If packets are sent but no response, hypothesis confirmed.
