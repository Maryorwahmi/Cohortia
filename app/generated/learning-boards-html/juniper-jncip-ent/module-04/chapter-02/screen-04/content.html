Core Concept

The Rendezvous Point (RP) in PIM-SM

A central meeting point for multicast sources and receivers

RP's Core Function

The Rendezvous Point (RP) acts as a central, well-known router in a PIM-SM domain. It's where multicast sources register their presence and where receivers send their initial join requests for a specific multicast group.

Shared Tree (*,G) Establishment

Initially, multicast traffic flows over a shared distribution tree, denoted as (*,G). This tree is rooted at the RP, meaning all traffic for that group first travels to the RP before being forwarded to interested receivers. The asterisk signifies 'any source'.

Switch to Shortest-Path Tree (S,G)

For more optimal forwarding, a receiver's Designated Router (DR) may eventually switch to a shortest-path tree (SPT) directly from the source. This is an (S,G) tree, where 'S' is the specific source, bypassing the RP for that particular source-group pair.

The RP Discovery Challenge

A critical aspect of PIM-SM is how all routers in the domain discover the correct RP for each multicast group. Without a reliable discovery mechanism, sources cannot register and receivers cannot join, leading to multicast forwarding failures.

Key Takeaway

The RP is fundamental for PIM-SM, acting as a central hub for initial multicast tree setup. Its reliable discovery is paramount for functional multicast routing.

Common Misconception

The RP is not always in the data path. After initial setup, traffic often switches to a direct shortest-path tree (SPT) from the source to the receiver, bypassing the RP.
