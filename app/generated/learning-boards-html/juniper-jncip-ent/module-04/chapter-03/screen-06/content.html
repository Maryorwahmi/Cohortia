VRRP Mechanics

VRRP Operational Principles and Failover

How Master and Backup Routers Coordinate

The Failover Process

  1. 1.Master Responds: Master router responds to ARP for virtual IP/MAC.
  2. 2.Traffic Flow: All host traffic flows through the Master.
  3. 3.Backup Monitoring: Backup routers monitor Master via VRRP advertisements.
  4. 4.Master Failure: If Master fails, advertisements stop.
  5. 5.Backup Takes Over: A Backup router becomes Master, taking virtual IP/MAC.
  6. 6.Seamless Transition: Hosts experience minimal disruption.
Key Takeaway

VRRP's coordinated failover, driven by advertisements and priority, ensures that network services remain uninterrupted even when a primary gateway fails.

Priority and Preemption

Priority Mechanism

Routers are assigned a priority (0-255). Higher priority means a more preferred Master. The router with the highest priority becomes Master.

Preemption

When enabled, a higher-priority router will take over as Master once it recovers from a failure, even if a lower-priority router is currently Master.

Common Misconception

A common mistake is assuming a backup router will always relinquish the master role. If preemption is disabled on the higher-priority router, it won't automatically take back the master role after recovery.
