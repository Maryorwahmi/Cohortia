PIM Dense Mode (PIM-DM)

Initial Flooding Followed by Pruning Uninterested Branches

Operational Model

Uses a "flood and prune" mechanism, initially sending traffic everywhere and then stopping it where not needed.

Suitability

Best for networks where multicast receivers are densely distributed and bandwidth is not a major concern.

Resulting Tree

Creates a Shortest-Path Tree (SPT) rooted at the source, delivering traffic along the most direct paths.

Key Takeaway

PIM-DM is simple but can be inefficient in sparse networks due to its initial flooding behavior.

PIM-DM Flood and Prune Process

1

Source Sends Traffic

Multicast traffic originates.

2

Routers Flood Traffic

Initially, traffic is sent out all interfaces (except incoming).

3

Uninterested Routers Prune

Routers without local receivers send 'prune' messages upstream.

4

SPT is Formed

A shortest-path tree is established from source to receivers.
