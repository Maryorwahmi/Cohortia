Core Concept

Virtual Private Networks (VPNs)

Securely Extending Private Networks Over Public Infrastructure

What is a VPN?

A VPN creates a secure, encrypted 'tunnel' over an untrusted public network, such as the internet. It allows remote users or networks to connect as if they were directly on a private network.

  • Confidentiality: Data is encrypted, preventing eavesdropping.
  • Integrity: Ensures data has not been tampered with in transit.
  • Authentication: Verifies the identity of communicating parties.
  • Anti-Replay: Protects against malicious retransmission of packets.

The Role of IPSec

IPSec (Internet Protocol Security) is a suite of protocols that provides cryptographic security services at the IP layer. It's the underlying technology that enables VPNs to offer strong security guarantees.

Analogy: Think of IPSec as the armored vehicle that carries your sensitive data safely through the dangerous 'highway' of the internet, while the VPN is the secure route itself.

Key Takeaway

VPNs use IPSec to create secure, encrypted tunnels over public networks, ensuring data confidentiality, integrity, authentication, and anti-replay protection.
