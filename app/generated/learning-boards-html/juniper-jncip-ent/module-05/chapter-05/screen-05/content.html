Strict-Priority (SP) Scheduling

Prioritizing critical traffic above all else

How Strict-Priority Works

Strict-Priority (SP) is a scheduling algorithm where traffic in an SP queue is always transmitted before any traffic from lower-priority queues. This ensures that delay-sensitive applications receive immediate service.

Mechanism

The scheduler checks SP queues first. If packets are present, they are sent. Only when SP queues are empty does it move to lower-priority queues.

Best Use Case

Ideal for real-time, delay-sensitive traffic like VoIP, video conferencing, and network control protocols.

The Importance of Policing SP Traffic

While SP ensures priority, it can also starve lower-priority queues if not managed carefully. Policing limits the maximum rate of SP traffic, preventing it from consuming all available bandwidth.

  • •Without policing, a burst of SP traffic can completely monopolize the link.
  • •Policing ensures SP traffic adheres to its allocated bandwidth, allowing other queues to be serviced.
  • •It's a critical safeguard to maintain overall network fairness and prevent unintended service degradation.
Concrete Example

A company uses SP for VoIP calls. During a busy period, if a large file transfer is also marked as SP (mistakenly or maliciously), it could degrade voice quality by consuming all bandwidth, unless a policer limits its rate.

Key Takeaway

Strict-Priority offers absolute prioritization but must be combined with policing to prevent lower-priority traffic starvation and ensure overall network health.

Common Mistake

Not policing Strict-Priority traffic. This can lead to SP queues consuming all available bandwidth, effectively starving all other traffic classes and causing widespread performance issues.
