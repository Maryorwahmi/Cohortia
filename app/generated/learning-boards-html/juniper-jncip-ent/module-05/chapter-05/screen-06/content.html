Weighted Round Robin (WRR) Scheduling

Fair bandwidth distribution based on assigned weights

How WRR Allocates Bandwidth

Weighted Round Robin (WRR) assigns a weight to each output queue. The scheduler then cycles through these queues, transmitting a number of bytes from each queue proportional to its weight, ensuring fair sharing.

Weight Assignment

Each queue receives a numerical weight, defining its relative share of bandwidth compared to other queues.

Cyclical Transmission

The scheduler repeatedly visits each queue, sending a 'slice' of data based on its weight, then moving to the next.

Benefits of WRR

WRR provides a balanced approach to bandwidth management, preventing starvation and offering predictable performance for different traffic classes.

  • •Prevents starvation: Ensures all queues receive some bandwidth, unlike unpoliced SP.
  • •Fair sharing: Distributes bandwidth proportionally, allowing for differentiated service without absolute dominance.
  • •Predictable performance: Traffic classes receive a consistent share of bandwidth over time.
Concrete Example

If Queue A (critical data) has a weight of 4 and Queue B (best-effort) has a weight of 1, Queue A will transmit 4 times as many bytes as Queue B in each cycle, effectively getting 80% of the bandwidth while Queue B gets 20%.

Key Takeaway

WRR uses weights to proportionally distribute bandwidth among queues, offering a fair and starvation-free scheduling mechanism for diverse traffic types.

Comparison to SP

Unlike SP, which can starve lower-priority queues, WRR guarantees a minimum share of bandwidth to all configured queues, making it more suitable for general data traffic where fairness is key.
