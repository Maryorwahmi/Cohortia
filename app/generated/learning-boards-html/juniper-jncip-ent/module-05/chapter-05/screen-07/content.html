Weighted Fair Queuing (WFQ) Scheduling

Achieving granular fairness among individual traffic flows

WFQ: Fairness at the Flow Level

Weighted Fair Queuing (WFQ) is an advanced scheduling algorithm that not only assigns weights to queues but also strives to provide fair bandwidth distribution among individual traffic flows *within* each queue.

Flow-Aware

WFQ identifies and manages individual traffic flows, ensuring each gets a fair share of the queue's allocated bandwidth.

Granular Control

Prevents a single 'greedy' flow from monopolizing the bandwidth allocated to its queue, benefiting other flows within that same queue.

WFQ vs. WRR: A Key Distinction

While WRR ensures fairness among queues, WFQ extends this fairness to the individual flows within those queues, offering a more refined level of traffic management.

  • •WRR: Fair distribution *between* queues based on weights.
  • •WFQ: Fair distribution *between* queues AND *among flows within* each queue.
  • •Ideal for environments requiring fine-grained fairness, such as multi-tenant networks or shared internet access.
Concrete Example

In a shared office network, if multiple users are downloading files (different flows) within the 'best-effort' queue, WFQ ensures that no single user's download monopolizes the queue's bandwidth, providing a more equitable experience for everyone.

Key Takeaway

WFQ provides granular fairness by distributing bandwidth not just among queues, but also among individual flows within those queues, preventing single flows from dominating.

When to Use WFQ

Choose WFQ when you need to ensure fair bandwidth allocation for multiple users or applications sharing the same forwarding class, especially when some flows might be more aggressive than others.
