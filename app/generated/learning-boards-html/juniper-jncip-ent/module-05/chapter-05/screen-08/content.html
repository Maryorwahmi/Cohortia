Traffic Shaping: Controlling Transmission Rates

Smoothing out traffic bursts to maintain consistent rates

What is Traffic Shaping?

Traffic shaping is a QoS mechanism that buffers excess traffic and releases it at a consistent, configured rate. This smooths out traffic bursts, ensuring a predictable output flow.

Mechanism

Excess packets are held in a buffer until bandwidth becomes available, then transmitted at the defined `shaping-rate`.

Key Parameters

Configured with a `shaping-rate` (maximum transmission rate) and `burst-size` (amount of data that can be sent above the rate for a short period).

Shaping vs. Policing

It's important to differentiate shaping from policing. While both control traffic rates, their actions on excess traffic are fundamentally different.

  • •Policing: *Drops* excess traffic immediately when it exceeds the configured rate.
  • •Shaping: *Buffers* excess traffic and *delays* its transmission until bandwidth is available.
  • •Shaping is preferred when packet loss is unacceptable, but increased latency is tolerable.
Concrete Example

A service provider guarantees a customer 50 Mbps bandwidth. Traffic shaping ensures that even if the customer bursts to 100 Mbps, the excess is buffered and sent at 50 Mbps, preventing drops and adhering to the SLA.

Key Takeaway

Traffic shaping buffers and smooths out traffic bursts to a consistent rate, preventing drops and ensuring compliance with SLAs, though it can introduce latency.

Potential Pitfall

Excessive buffering due to shaping can lead to increased end-to-end latency, which can negatively impact real-time applications like VoIP or video conferencing.
