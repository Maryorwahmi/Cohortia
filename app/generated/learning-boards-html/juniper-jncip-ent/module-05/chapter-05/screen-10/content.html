Weighted Random Early Detection (WRED)

Prioritizing drops based on packet loss priority

WRED: Differentiated Congestion Avoidance

Weighted Random Early Detection (WRED) extends RED by applying different drop probabilities based on a packet's loss priority. This allows for preferential treatment of critical traffic during congestion.

Loss Priority

Derived from DSCP or CoS values, indicating how important a packet is to preserve during congestion.

Mechanism

As queue fills, low-priority traffic starts dropping earlier and more aggressively than high-priority traffic.

Configuring Drop Profiles

Juniper implements WRED through `drop-profiles`, which define queue fill levels and associated drop probabilities for different loss priorities.

  • •`fill-level`: The percentage of queue fullness.
  • •`drop-probability`: The likelihood of a packet being dropped at a given `fill-level`.
  • •Multiple drop profiles can be mapped to a single scheduler based on loss priority.
Concrete Example

You might configure a drop profile for 'low' loss priority to start dropping packets at 50% queue fullness, while a 'high' loss priority profile only starts dropping at 80% fullness, preserving critical traffic.

Key Takeaway

WRED provides granular congestion avoidance by applying loss priority-based drop probabilities, ensuring critical traffic is protected during network congestion.

Why it's Important

WRED is crucial for maintaining the quality of experience for high-priority applications in congested networks, preventing their degradation while gracefully shedding less important traffic.
