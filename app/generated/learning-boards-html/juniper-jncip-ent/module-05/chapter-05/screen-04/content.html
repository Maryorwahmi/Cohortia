QoS Congestion Management: The Role of Output Queues

How Juniper devices handle traffic when interfaces are busy

Understanding Output Queues

Output queues are buffers on network interfaces where packets are temporarily held when the incoming traffic rate exceeds the interface's transmission capacity. Each queue is typically associated with a specific forwarding class, allowing for differentiated treatment of traffic.

Purpose

Prevent immediate packet drops during bursts of traffic by providing temporary storage.

Mapping

Packets are directed to specific queues based on their forwarding class, determined by classification and marking.

The Scheduler's Role

The scheduler is the core mechanism that determines which packets from which output queue are transmitted next. It acts as a 'traffic cop,' enforcing QoS policies by prioritizing different traffic types based on configured algorithms.

  • •Ensures fair allocation of bandwidth among queues.
  • •Prevents lower-priority traffic from being completely starved.
  • •Manages latency by controlling queue depth and transmission order.
Concrete Example

Imagine a highway with multiple toll booths (queues) for different vehicle types (forwarding classes). The toll booth operator (scheduler) decides which lane's vehicles get to pass next, ensuring emergency vehicles (high priority) go first, while others are managed fairly.

Key Takeaway

Output queues are essential buffers, and the scheduler is the traffic controller that ensures QoS policies are enforced during congestion, preventing packet loss and maintaining performance.

Common Misconception

Some believe queues eliminate congestion. They don't; they manage it. If traffic consistently exceeds capacity, queues will eventually fill, leading to drops. QoS aims to manage *how* and *when* drops occur.
