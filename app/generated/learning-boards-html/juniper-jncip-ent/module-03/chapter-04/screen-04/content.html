Core Concept

BGP Communities: Tags for Policy

Simplifying BGP policy application and enabling advanced traffic engineering.

What is a Community?

A 32-bit number attached to BGP routes, acting as a flexible tag for policy signaling. It allows BGP speakers to group routes and apply common policies.

AS:VALUE Format

Often represented as AS:VALUE (e.g., 65000:100), where the first 16 bits are the ASN and the last 16 bits are a custom value.

Optional Transitive Attribute

Communities are propagated to other Autonomous Systems by default, making them ideal for signaling policy across AS boundaries.

Community-Based Policy Flow

1. Tag Routes

Attach communities to specific BGP routes.

2. Propagate Tags

Communities travel with routes across BGP peers.

3. Apply Policies

BGP speakers match tags to apply specific routing actions.

Analogy

Think of BGP communities like shipping labels on packages. Each label (community) indicates how the package (route) should be handled, routed, or delivered by different carriers (BGP routers).

Key Takeaway

BGP communities provide a scalable way to apply policies by grouping routes with common tags, simplifying complex BGP configurations.
