Influencing BGP Path Selection with `then` Statements

Modifying Local Preference, MED, and AS_Path

Action Definition

`then` statements dictate the actions to be taken on a route once it matches the `from` conditions of a policy term.

Flow Control Actions

Actions like `accept` and `reject` control whether a route is processed further or discarded. `next-term` and `next-policy` allow continued evaluation.

Key Takeaway

`then` statements are the core mechanism for traffic engineering, allowing you to modify BGP attributes and control route propagation to meet specific network objectives.

Common `then` Actions
`local-preference `Influences outbound path selection within the AS. Higher value is preferred.
`metric ` (MED)Influences inbound path selection from external ASes. Lower value is preferred.
`as-path prepend `Adds AS numbers to AS_Path, making the path appear longer and less preferred.
`community add `Tags routes with BGP community values for further policy matching or signaling.
Pro-Tip

Carefully consider the order of `then` actions, as some may override others. Always test policy changes in a lab environment before deploying to production.
