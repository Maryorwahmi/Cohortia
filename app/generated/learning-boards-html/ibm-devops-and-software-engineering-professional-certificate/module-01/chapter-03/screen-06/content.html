Scrum: roles, artifacts, and Sprints
A predictable cycle for delivering increments and inspecting outcomes.
Product Owner
Maximizes product value and orders the Product Backlog.
Scrum Master
Servant-leader who facilitates Scrum practices and removes impediments.
Development Team
Cross-functional professionals delivering a potentially releasable Increment.
Artifacts
Product Backlog · Sprint Backlog · Increment
Artifacts create transparency about what will be built and what is done.
Sprints
Time-boxed 1–4 week cycles
Sprints focus the team on a Sprint Goal and a plan for delivering value.
Example
For the watchlist feature, the Product Owner orders backlog items, the team commits to a Sprint Backlog, and after the Sprint the Increment is reviewed with stakeholders.
Common Mistake
Treating the Scrum Master as a project manager who assigns tasks undermines self-organization. The Scrum Master protects the team’s ability to self-manage.
Key Takeaway

Scrum provides structure with roles and artifacts, while preserving team autonomy and frequent inspection.
