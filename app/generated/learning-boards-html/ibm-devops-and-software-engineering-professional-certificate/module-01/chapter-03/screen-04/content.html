Agile: a working definition
Iterative, collaborative, and focused on delivering working software frequently.
  • Iterative cycles reduce risk by delivering small increments and getting feedback.
  • Collaboration emphasizes people and interactions over rigid processes.
  • Working software becomes the primary measure of progress, not extensive documentation.
Concrete Example
A team ships a minimal watchlist so customers can save titles, then refines the feature from real usage data.
Common Mistake
Assuming Agile means no planning. Agile replaces long plans with continuous, lightweight planning and checkpoints.
Key Takeaway

Agile reduces risk by shortening the feedback loop between idea and user validation.

Why it emerged
Rigid, documentation-heavy processes made it slow to react to user or market changes. Agile arose to support learning, adaptation, and faster delivery.
Problem
Long delivery cycles produced outdated products.
Agile response
Short cycles, frequent releases, regular feedback.
Reflection
Think of one project where a small release and early feedback could have changed the outcome. How would you re-scope the first sprint?
