Module 6 › Chapter 6.1 › Why container orchestration?
Definition

Container orchestration

Automated management of container deployment, scaling, networking, and lifecycle across multiple hosts.

Automation: start, stop, and restart failed containers without manual intervention.
Scaling: add or remove replicas based on load or policy to meet demand.
Networking: service discovery and load balancing to route traffic between services and Pods.
Example
A microservices app scales its front ends during peak traffic and reschedules backend Pods if a node fails, preserving user experience.
Common mistake
Assuming orchestration only schedules containers. It also maintains networking and desired state.
Key Takeaway

Orchestration makes large-scale container management practical, reliable, and observable.

Analogy

Kubernetes as conductor

Kubernetes coordinates containers like a conductor coordinates musicians: it ensures correct timing, recovery when something fails, and balanced output across the ensemble.

Scenario
If a node crashes, orchestration will reschedule the affected Pods to healthy nodes to keep the service running.
Why this matters
Automation reduces human error and speeds recovery, which directly improves service reliability and developer productivity.
Reflection: When you next see multiple failing services, ask whether automation could have prevented the outage and how orchestration would limit the blast radius of changes.
