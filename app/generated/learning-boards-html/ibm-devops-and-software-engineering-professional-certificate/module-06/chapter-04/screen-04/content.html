Module 6 › Chapter 6.4 › Why Pods are Ephemeral
Definition

A Pod's writable layer is not a durable store. Pods are created and destroyed for scaling, updates, and failure recovery. Relying on local container filesystem for important data risks data loss.

Why it matters
  • Databases and log stores need data to survive Pod deletion.
  • Ephemeral storage complicates backups and restores unless externalized.
  • Design choices affect scheduling and availability.
Analogy

Think of a Pod as a temporary workstation. Anything left on the desk is gone when the worker leaves unless you file it into a cabinet that stays behind.

Scenario & misconception

Example: a Pod running a database that writes /var/lib/data inside the container. Deleting that Pod removes the writable layer and the data. A frequent misconception is assuming restarting the Pod preserves files; only cluster-backed storage or host-mounted directories can provide durability.

Check your understanding
If you delete a Pod and a new Pod is scheduled on a different node, will container-local files persist? No — they are lost unless backed by persistent storage.
Key Takeaway
Treat Pods as ephemeral; plan storage so data outlives the Pod when durability is required.
