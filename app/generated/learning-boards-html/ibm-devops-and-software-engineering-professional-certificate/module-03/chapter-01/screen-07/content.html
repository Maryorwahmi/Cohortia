Resource pooling and rapid elasticity
Shared pools improve utilization; elasticity adjusts capacity to demand.
Example scenario
An online store auto-scales web servers during a sale and releases them after traffic drops, minimizing idle capacity and cost.
Design caution
Elasticity requires metrics and well-tuned policies. Cold-start time and scaling limits affect user experience during sudden spikes.
Operational risk
Multi-tenancy can cause noisy neighbor effects; consider isolation or dedicated resources for critical workloads.
Key Takeaway

Elasticity and pooling enable lower cost and rapid scale, but teams must design policies and account for startup delays and isolation needs.

Scaling policy starter
  1. Pick a metric (CPU, request latency, queue length).
  2. Set threshold and cooldown to avoid flapping.
  3. Set min and max capacity to control cost.
