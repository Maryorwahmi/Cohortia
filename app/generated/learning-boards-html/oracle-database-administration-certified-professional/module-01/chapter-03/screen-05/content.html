Process Flow
Online Redo Log Cycle
Log writer writes sequentially to a group. When full, it switches to the next group. Archived copies allow point-in-time recovery.
1
LGWR writes redo entries to the current group as transactions commit.
2
When the group fills, LGWR switches to the next group and the filled files become candidates for archival.
3
ARCH copies filled members to archive logs if ARCHIVELOG mode is enabled.
Concrete example
Two groups, each with two members on separate disks, ensure a single disk failure won't lose an entire group.
Common mistake
Placing all redo members on one array creates a single point of failure despite having multiple members.
Quick checks
Use the V$LOGFILE and V$LOG views to list groups and members and confirm multiplexing and sizes.
Always keep at least two groups. Multiplex each member on different disks when possible.
If ARCHIVELOG is required for your recovery SLA, ensure archive destinations are healthy and monitored.
Remember: losing all redo logs prevents opening the database without restoring from backups and archives, risking data loss.
