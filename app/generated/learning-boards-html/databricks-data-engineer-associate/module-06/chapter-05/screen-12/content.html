Module 6 › Chapter 6.5 › Centralized Monitoring
From logs to alerts
Centralize audit and diagnostic logs so security teams can correlate events and detect incidents.
Ingestion Pattern
  1. Deliver logs to Log Analytics or SIEM with consistent schema.
  2. Normalize user, resource, and timestamp fields for correlation.
  3. Store logs in access-controlled, encrypted storage for retention.
Detection Examples
  • Spike in failed logins followed by data export events.
  • Unusual cluster creation by a service principal outside business hours.
  • Repeated secrets access failures indicating potential misconfiguration.
Pitfall
Giving broad read access to logs exposes sensitive metadata — restrict log access and audit who queries logs.
Incident Playbook (brief)
  1. Contain: revoke suspicious tokens or disable a compromised cluster.
  2. Collect: preserve relevant audit and diagnostic logs for the time window.
  3. Recover: restore minimal privileges and rotate any exposed secrets or keys.
  4. Review: perform a post-incident access and process review to close gaps.
Key Takeaway

Centralized logging and practiced incident response turn records into quick, auditable actions when problems occur.
