Core Concept

Data Warehouse

A system designed for stable, structured historical data with schema-on-write and transactional guarantees.

Strength: predictable performance for BI and reporting
Trade-off: less flexible for unstructured or fast-evolving sources
Common mistake: modeling every source upfront and losing agility
Key Takeaway

Use warehouses for governed, repeatable reporting where consistency and performance are essential.

Concrete Example
Monthly finance ledger

A ledger table must never admit partial writes or duplicates. ACID transactions ensure nightly batch loads and concurrent reads do not produce inconsistent reports.

Why it matters
  • Business users rely on stable aggregates and SLAs for reports.
  • Strict schemas reduce downstream fix-up work and surprises.
Misconception

A warehouse is not always the best place for experimental or semi-structured data; forcing all data through schema-on-write can slow teams down.
