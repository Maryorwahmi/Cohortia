Module 5 › Chapter 5.1 › Transforms and the 3D coordinate system
Axes & meaning
  • X — right (positive to the right)
  • Y — up (positive upwards)
  • Z — out toward the viewer (positive out of the screen)
Remember: Godot uses a right-handed system; rotate around axes to change orientation.
Concrete example
Place a cube at Vector3(0,0.5,-3). Rotate its parent 90 degrees around Y, then move the child along its local X and observe the world direction changes.
Common misconceptions
Treating Z as just layering like 2D or assuming local and global transforms are interchangeable. Local transforms are always relative to the parent and will change if the parent rotates.
Key Takeaway

Master axes and local vs global behavior now; it prevents costly rework in level layout and animation.

Try this
Rotate the parent 45 degrees and move a child along its local Z to see world displacement differ from local intent.
Pro tip
When precise world-aligned movement is needed, modify the global_transform or calculate world offsets in code.
