Module 5 › Chapter 5.1 › Initial Full Synchronization
Process Flow

Snapshot, Transfer, Buffer, Replay

1
Create RDB snapshot
Master writes a background RDB file of its current dataset to disk.
2
Send snapshot to replica
The master transfers the snapshot stream to the replica over the replication connection.
3
Buffer writes
While the snapshot is in transit, the master buffers new write commands to keep ordering.
4
Replay buffered commands
After loading the snapshot, the replica receives and applies buffered commands to reach parity.
Example
Adding a replica to a dataset with large keys will cause an RDB snapshot and transfer. Disk speed and network bandwidth directly affect sync time and buffer size.
Operational hint
Monitor master CPU, disk IO, and buffer growth during initial syncs. Consider scheduling replica additions during low traffic windows for large datasets.
Check after sync
Use INFO replication on both master and replica to confirm role, master link status, and replication offsets. A small offset difference is expected; large offsets indicate lag.
