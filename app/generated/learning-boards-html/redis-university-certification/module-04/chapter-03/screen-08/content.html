Module 4 › Chapter 4.3 › Time-To-Live (TTL) and Expiry
What TTL does

TTL causes Redis to automatically delete keys after a set time, preventing permanent stale entries and bounding memory usage.

r.setex("cache:product:prod-001", 30, json.dumps(product_data))  # expires after 30 seconds
Design trade-offs
  • Short TTL: fresher data, more misses.
  • Long TTL: higher hit rate, risk of stale responses.
  • Combine TTL with invalidation for critical updates.
Practical tips
  • Namespace keys clearly to make invalidation easy.
  • Use short negative-cache TTLs for non-existent keys to prevent penetration.
  • Consider slight TTL jitter or probabilistic early expiry to smooth cache rebuilds.
Key Takeaway

TTL is a simple lever to balance freshness and hit rate; choose values based on data change frequency and acceptable staleness.
