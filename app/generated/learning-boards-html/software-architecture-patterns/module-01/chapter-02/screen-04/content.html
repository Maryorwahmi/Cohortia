Core Concept
Requirements vs Constraints
Clear definitions avoid designing in the dark.
Functional Requirements
Define the actions the system must support, such as "users can request a ride" or "users can make payments." These determine features and endpoints.
Non-functional Requirements
Describe qualities like response time, throughput, security, and maintainability. They drive architecture tactics such as caching or redundancy.
Constraints
Limits like budgets, mandated languages, or regulatory rules. Treat as non-negotiable input to design choices.
Key Takeaway

Capture both what you must build and what limits you must obey. Without both, the architecture will miss critical targets.

Concrete Example
E-commerce checkout
Functional: users can make payments. NFR: payment completes within 2 seconds. Constraint: must comply with PCI. These together imply dedicated payment services, encryption, and monitoring.
Common mistake
Assuming NFRs later leads to rework; record and prioritize early.
Pro tip
Ask "what happens if" a driver fails to surface impact and urgency during elicitation.
