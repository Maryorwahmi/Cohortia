Pitfall
Shiny New Object Syndrome
Adopting new tech for novelty rather than fit increases risk. Require evidence and operational plans before committing.
Controlled experimentation
Allow timeboxed spikes to validate new tools against prioritized NFRs, but don’t promote them to production without evidence.
Questions to ask
  • What measurable benefit does this provide?
  • Can our team operate and support it?
  • What is the migration and ongoing cost?
Example
A new framework promised developer productivity but required custom deployment tools and increased debugging time, which harmed delivery velocity.
Key Takeaway

Innovation is necessary but should be evidence-driven and limited until operational readiness is proven.
