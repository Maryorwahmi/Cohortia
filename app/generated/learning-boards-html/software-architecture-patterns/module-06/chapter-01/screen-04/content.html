Definition
Architectural Decision
A choice about system structure, component interaction, or deployment that significantly affects quality attributes and future evolution.
Why it matters
These decisions determine long-term costs, team shape, and risk. Poor choices produce technical debt and can slow product delivery for years.
Concrete example
Selecting microservices increases independent deployability but adds operational burden like service discovery, distributed tracing, and network failures to manage.
Common misconception
Architecture is only about code. It also shapes teams, costs, and compliance responsibilities.
Key Takeaway

Treat architectural choices as documented commitments and evaluate them against the most important quality attributes.

Annotated Decision Card
Decision
Choose primary application architecture: monolith or microservices.
Rationale: team size, operational maturity, time-to-market constraints.
Alternatives
  • Monolith — simpler operations, faster initial development
  • Microservices — independent scaling, but higher operational cost
Impact
Monolith may delay scaling certain features; microservices require investment in observability and deployment tooling.
