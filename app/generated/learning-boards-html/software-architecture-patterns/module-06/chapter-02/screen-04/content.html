Module 06 › Chapter 6.2 › What is an ADR?
Definition

An ADR records the reason behind one architectural choice in a compact, searchable form. It focuses on trade-offs and decisions, not implementation details.

Why it helps
Prevents knowledge loss, supports onboarding, and documents justification for auditors or future architects.
Common misconception
ADRs are not full design documents. Keep them focused on decisions and trade-offs.
Concrete Example
ADR 001: Choosing a Messaging Queue
  • Context: High-throughput event handling with ordered delivery for some topics.
  • Decision: Use a managed Kafka cluster for ordering guarantees and throughput.
  • Consequences: Operational overhead and learning curve; strong throughput and partitioning.
Key Takeaway

An ADR should let a future reader understand the why without asking the original authors.
