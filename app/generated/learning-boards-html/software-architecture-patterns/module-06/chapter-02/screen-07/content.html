Module 06 › Chapter 6.2 › How to Write ADRs
Step-by-step
  1. Title, Status, Date — identifier and state.
  2. Context — concise problem and NFRs, link to tickets.
  3. Decision — a clear, specific statement.
  4. Alternatives Considered — short notes on rejected options.
  5. Rationale — explicit trade-offs tied to NFRs.
  6. Consequences — benefits, risks, and action items.
Concrete writing tips
  • Prefer one decision per ADR to keep records focused and searchable.
  • Reference requirement IDs for traceability rather than copying specs into Context.
  • Keep alternatives short but meaningful — show you considered real options.
  • Include measurable consequences where possible: e.g., estimated latency, cost delta.
Check your ADR

If a colleague can summarize the why and the required follow-ups without questions, the ADR is likely clear enough.
