Core Concept
Decision Matrix
A structured table for comparing alternatives against criteria to support objective discussion.
Definition
List alternatives, define measurable criteria, assign scores and weights, then compute totals to compare.
Why it helps
  • Reduces subjective bias by forcing explicit criteria and scores.
  • Creates a documented rationale for decisions and trade-offs.
  • Highlights which criteria drive a choice, enabling focused discussion.
Common misconception
Treating the highest score as the absolute answer without qualitative review.
Example snapshot
A simple textual matrix for messaging systems:
Criterion | Weight | Kafka (1-5) | RabbitMQ (1-5)
Scalability | 5 | 5 | 3
Durability  | 4 | 4 | 4
Ease of Use | 3 | 2 | 4
Cost        | 2 | 3 | 4
Team Expertise | 3 | 2 | 4
Totals (weighted) -> Kafka: 59  RabbitMQ: 63
Key Takeaway

The matrix clarifies why an alternative scores higher and surfaces assumptions you must validate before finalizing a decision.
