Module 4 › Chapter 4.2 › What MVC means
Definition
Model‑View‑Controller splits an application into three focused layers so each can evolve independently and be tested in isolation.
  • Model: data, business rules, validation, and persistence.
  • View: declarative rendering and input capture with minimal logic.
  • Controller: interprets input, updates Model, and selects View.
Analogy

Think of Model as the rules engine, View as the display, and Controller as the receptionist who receives requests and directs them to the right place.

Key Takeaway

MVC is about clear responsibilities: put domain logic in Models, presentation in Views, and orchestration in Controllers.

Concrete example
An e‑commerce Product Model enforces price and inventory rules. A Product View formats price and shows availability. A Controller handles "Add to Cart" by calling Model methods and then choosing the cart View.
Common misconception

A common error is putting business rules inside controllers or templates. That leads to duplication and harder tests. Prefer a rich Model and thin controllers that orchestrate, not compute domain rules.
