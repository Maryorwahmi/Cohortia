Module 6 › Chapter 5.5 › Why failures are expected
Definition
Failure as normal

Distributed systems combine many moving parts. Each can fail independently and transiently. Treating failures as expected lets you design protections such as timeouts, retries with backoff, and containment patterns like circuit breakers and bulkheads.

Concrete example
An auth service times out. Front-end services keep waiting and exhaust threads, causing user-facing endpoints to degrade. Containment prevents the problem from cascading.
Common misconception
Assuming only fixing the failing component is enough. Without containment, failures immediately affect other services while you investigate.
Key Takeaway

Design boundaries and limits to prevent a single failure from consuming system-wide resources.

Why it matters
Containment reduces mean time to recovery and limits user impact. It gives operators time to fix the root cause rather than fighting a growing pile of overloaded services.
Practical checklist
  • Identify critical external dependencies and their failure modes.
  • Add timeouts and retry policies with backoff.
  • Introduce containment patterns where repeated failures would cause resource exhaustion.
Reflection
Which single dependency in your system would cause the largest cascade if it slowed? Design one short containment you could add today.
