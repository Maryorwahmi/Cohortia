Warning Distributed Monolith — What to Avoid
Recognize and prevent designs that add cost without autonomy
What it looks like
  • Services are separate processes but share the same database schema.
  • Most interactions are synchronous, forming long call chains.
  • Deployments require coordinated changes across multiple services.
Example
A checkout flow requires several synchronous calls to other services and each change requires coordinated deploys, erasing the benefits of independent services.
How to prevent it
  1. Ensure each service owns its data and schema.
  2. Limit synchronous chains for user-facing flows.
  3. Invest in tracing and contract tests to find hidden coupling.
Key Takeaway

Separation without independence produces a distributed monolith. Design for data ownership and independent operations to realize microservice benefits.
