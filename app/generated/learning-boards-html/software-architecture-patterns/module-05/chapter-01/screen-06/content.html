Process Flow Principles of Microservices
A step-by-step look at the guiding principles
Step 1 — Autonomy
Services deploy and fail independently. This lets teams release without coordinating wide rollouts but requires robust failure isolation.
Step 2 — Specialization
Each service maps to a business capability, such as orders or payments, enabling focused design and ownership.
Step 3 — Loose coupling
Minimize shared state and tight synchronous chains. Favor asynchronous messaging for decoupling where appropriate.
Step 4 — Bounded Context
Define a clear model and data ownership per service so domain concepts remain consistent inside the boundary.
Step 5 — Polyglot choices
Choose the best language and storage per service when justified, but plan for the operational cost of diverse stacks.
Misconception
Autonomy does not mean zero coordination. Teams still need well-defined APIs, contract testing, and monitoring to avoid drift.
Key Takeaway

Adopt microservice principles deliberately: align services to business capabilities, keep boundaries clear, and plan for operational complexity when choosing heterogeneous technologies.
