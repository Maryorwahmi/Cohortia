Why ACID fails across microservices
Two-phase commit creates blocking, coupling, and runtime complexity
  • ACID expects a single transactional coordinator and shared locking semantics.
  • Two-phase commit (2PC) requires all participants to prepare and commit in lockstep, which can block resources during failures.
  • Network partitions or slow services make 2PC fragile and reduce system availability.
  • Attempting global ACID couples services around a coordinator and negates microservice independence.
Concrete example
An order placement involving Order, Payment, Inventory, and Shipping services would block if one participant in 2PC is slow or unavailable, delaying unrelated operations and holding locks.
Key Takeaway

Global ACID is often the wrong tool for distributed services. Prefer patterns that preserve service autonomy and accept eventual consistency when appropriate.

Common misconception
Believing that distributed transactions must mimic local ACID leads to overly complex architectures and reduced availability.
Why it matters
Choosing the wrong consistency model can make systems brittle; the goal is to balance correctness, availability, and independence of services.
