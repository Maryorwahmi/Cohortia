Orchestration-based Saga
A central orchestrator directs commands and coordinates compensations

The orchestrator receives a start request and issues commands to services in sequence, waiting for their responses before proceeding.

If a step fails, the orchestrator triggers compensation commands for earlier steps and updates the Saga state accordingly.

Pros: clearer control flow and easier monitoring. Cons: additional service to build, scale, and make highly available.

Operational notes
  • Make the orchestrator durable and horizontally scalable to avoid it becoming a single point of failure.
  • Keep orchestration logic explicit and versioned, so changes are auditable and testable.
  • Record Saga state to enable restarts and compensation decisions after crashes.
Common mistake

Building a simple orchestrator without thinking about high availability or state persistence can make the orchestrator the weakest link in the system.
