Collider types and behavior
Define where physics interactions happen; edit size and offset to polish gameplay.
BoxCollider2D
Efficient for platforms, walls, and blocky sprites.
CircleCollider2D
Great for rolling or round objects; cheap and smooth for collisions.
PolygonCollider2D
Fits complex outlines precisely but costs more CPU. Use only when needed.
EdgeCollider2D
Series of connected lines useful for terrains and one-sided collisions.
Practical tip
Make colliders slightly smaller than the sprite to reduce frustrating catches on corners. Test in play mode and adjust offsets as needed.
Key Takeaway

Choose the simplest collider that matches shape. Precision costs CPU; player experience often improves with slightly forgiving shapes.

Try this
Swap a PolygonCollider2D to a BoxCollider2D for a static crate sprite and observe CPU cost and collision feel.
