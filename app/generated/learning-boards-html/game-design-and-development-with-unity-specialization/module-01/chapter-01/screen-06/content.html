Module 1 › Chapter 1.1 › Dynamics
Definition

Dynamics are emergent play behaviors

They are the strategies and interactions players produce while using mechanics against systems and each other.

Emergence
Dynamics are context-dependent and often unpredictable until observed in playtests.
Example
Combining 'double jump' with 'wall slide' can create the dynamic of chained traversal that opens shortcuts or secret areas.
Designer test

Watch for player-invented strategies. If players find a dominant exploit, adjust mechanics or introduce counter-choices to rebalance dynamics.

Concrete dynamic scenarios
  • • Timing-based platforming sequences produced by precise jump mechanics.
  • • Flanking and rush tactics emerging from movement and unit production in a strategy game.
  • • Risk-taking behavior driven by resource scarcity and reward placement.
Misconception

Do not assume a mechanic automatically produces a desired dynamic. Dynamics depend on context, level design, and player skill. Use playtests to validate.

Check your design

Intentionally build a level where a mechanic has room to produce the intended dynamic and observe whether players actually do it.
