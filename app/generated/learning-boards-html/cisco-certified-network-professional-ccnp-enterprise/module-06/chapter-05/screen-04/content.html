Core Concept

Why Layer 2 and infrastructure services matter

Layer 2 problems can stop or misroute frames. Services depend on that forwarding to reach clients. Verify both layers.

Check physical and VLAN membership first: show vlan brief; show interfaces switchport.
Confirm service reachability: ping the DHCP/DNS/NTP server from the router or switch management plane.
Use protocol state commands: show spanning-tree active, show etherchannel summary, show ip dhcp binding.
Key Takeaway

Treat reachability and protocol state as equal steps: path first, then service details.

Concrete Example
A client on VLAN 20 cannot reach the DHCP server at 192.168.1.50. Verify the access port is in VLAN 20 and the router subinterface for VLAN 20 is up. If so, check for missing ip helper-address on the router subinterface. Fixing the helper address restores DHCP Offer/ACK exchange.
Common Mistake
Assuming a trunk is sufficient to carry all VLANs. A trunk may be operational but may not be allowing the needed VLANs or may have a native VLAN mismatch.
