Core Concept › PKI Hierarchies
Two-tier model
Offline Root CA signs online Issuing CAs that perform day-to-day issuance.
Characteristics
  • Root CA is air-gapped and used rarely.
  • Issuing CAs handle enrollment, renewal, and revocation.
  • Recovery is possible without re-issuing root unless root is compromised.
Example
Root CA offline in a secure room; issuing CA online in DMZ issuing server certificates to web servers.
Three-tier model
Adds an intermediate layer to separate functions and policies.
Benefits
  • Policy separation per intermediate (servers, users, devices).
  • Limits operational exposure by narrowing scope per CA.
  • Supports staged rollouts and delegated administration.
Misconception
More tiers always equal more security. Not always; complexity increases management and chain length.
Key Takeaway
Choose the hierarchy that balances risk reduction with operational ability to manage keys and certificates.
Check your understanding
If an issuing CA is breached, explain how the chosen hierarchy affects recovery steps and certificate re-issuance.
