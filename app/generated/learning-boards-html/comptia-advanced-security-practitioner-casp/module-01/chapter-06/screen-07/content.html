Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

Adding layers of verification for stronger identity assurance

What is MFA?

An authentication method requiring users to provide two or more distinct verification factors from different categories to gain access to a resource.

Factor Categories

  • Knowledge: Something you know (e.g., password, PIN).
  • Possession: Something you have (e.g., phone, hardware token).
  • Inherence: Something you are (e.g., fingerprint, face scan).

Key Takeaway

MFA is a critical security control that significantly reduces the risk of unauthorized access by requiring multiple, diverse forms of identity verification.

Practical Example

Logging into an application with your password (knowledge) and then approving the login via a push notification on your smartphone (possession).

Design Consideration

Architects must balance strong security with user convenience. Overly complex MFA can lead to user frustration and attempts to bypass the system, creating new vulnerabilities.
