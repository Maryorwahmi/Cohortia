Foundations of IAM Architecture

Managing identities and access across complex environments

What is IAM?

IAM is the framework for managing digital identities and controlling their access to resources. It ensures the right individuals and services have the right access, at the right time, for the right reasons.

Identity Lifecycle

  • Provisioning: Creating and granting initial access.
  • Management: Updating attributes and permissions.
  • De-provisioning: Revoking access and deleting identities.

Centralized vs. Decentralized

  • Centralized: Consistent policy enforcement across the enterprise.
  • Decentralized: Local identity management for specific apps/cloud services, often federated.

Key Takeaway

A robust IAM architecture is essential for balancing security, usability, and compliance in hybrid and multi-cloud environments, managing identities from creation to deletion.

Real-World Example

An employee joins a company. IAM provisions their account, grants access to necessary tools, and later de-provisions access when they leave. This ensures access is always appropriate and timely.

Common Misconception

IAM is not just about 'login and password'. It's a continuous process of verifying, authorizing, and auditing access across all digital entities, including devices and services, not just human users.
