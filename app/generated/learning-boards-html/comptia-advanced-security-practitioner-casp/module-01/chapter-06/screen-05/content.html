Fundamental Distinction

Authentication vs. Authorization

Knowing who you are versus what you can do

Authentication (AuthN)

  • Purpose: Verifies the identity of a user, device, or service.
  • Question: "Are you who you claim to be?"
  • Examples: Passwords, MFA, biometrics, digital certificates.

Authorization (AuthZ)

  • Purpose: Determines what an authenticated identity is permitted to do.
  • Question: "What are you allowed to access or perform?"
  • Examples: Role-Based Access Control (RBAC), Attribute-Based Access Control (ABAC), policies.

Key Takeaway

Authentication confirms identity, while authorization grants permissions. Both are distinct and essential for a secure access control system.

Scenario Example

You log into your banking app (authentication). The app then allows you to view your balance but not transfer funds without additional verification (authorization).

Why it Matters

A strong authentication mechanism is useless without granular authorization. An attacker who bypasses authorization can gain unauthorized access even with valid credentials.
