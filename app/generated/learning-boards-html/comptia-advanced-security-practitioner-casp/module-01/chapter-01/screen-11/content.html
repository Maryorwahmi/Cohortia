Frameworks in Practice: A Combined Approach

Leveraging Multiple Frameworks for Holistic Design

Step 1: Business Requirements (SABSA)

Start with SABSA to derive security requirements directly from business objectives and attributes. This ensures security is business-driven.

Example: Identifying 'regulatory compliance for customer data' as a core business attribute.

Step 2: Structured Design (TOGAF)

Use TOGAF's ADM to structure the design process, moving from vision to business, information systems, and technology architectures.

Example: Designing data encryption solutions within the 'Information Systems Architecture' phase.

Step 3: Risk Management & Reporting (NIST CSF)

Map implemented controls back to NIST CSF functions for ongoing risk management, assessment, and communication of cybersecurity posture.

Example: Reporting on 'Protect' function effectiveness using metrics derived from implemented controls.
Key Takeaway

A combined framework approach allows architects to leverage the specific strengths of each, resulting in a more robust, business-aligned, and manageable security architecture.
