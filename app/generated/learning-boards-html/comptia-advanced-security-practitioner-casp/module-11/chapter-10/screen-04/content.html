Module 11 › Chapter 11.10 › What is CSPM?
Definition

CSPM: Continuous posture and compliance

CSPM ingests cloud configuration via APIs, compares settings to benchmarks and regulations, and surfaces misconfigurations for remediation and reporting.

Concrete Example
A CSPM flags an S3 bucket that is publicly accessible; it includes severity and recommended steps to remove public access.
Misconception
CSPM is not primarily runtime protection. It detects configuration issues; it may offer remediation but does not replace workload runtime defenses.
Why it matters
Automated posture checks reduce audit labor, help maintain compliance, and provide a single source of truth across accounts and subscriptions.
How it works
Ingest & Scan
Connect via read-only APIs to cloud accounts and continuously scan resources like storage, IAM, and security groups.
Map to Standards
Compare findings to CIS Benchmarks and regulatory frameworks to generate compliance reports.
Prioritize & Remediate
Use asset criticality and threat context to surface urgent issues and optionally trigger automated remediation workflows.
Key Takeaway

CSPM gives continuous visibility into configuration and compliance, but it should be integrated with remediation and incident workflows to be effective.
