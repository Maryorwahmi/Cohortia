Core Concept

Identity defines access

IAM makes explicit which identities may perform which actions. Use roles and policies that are auditable and scoped to resources.

Explanation
  • Cloud workloads and APIs expand the attack surface beyond network controls.
  • Grant actions explicitly and avoid wildcard permissions that increase risk.
  • Treat service identities as first-class, auditable entities with lifecycle management.
Concrete example
A developer role with s3:GetObject and s3:PutObject for a single development bucket prevents accidental or malicious access to production buckets.
Key Takeaway

Make identity the control point: limit, audit, and rotate access rather than relying on network isolation alone.

Layered definition and metaphor
Definition

IAM controls who can do what, where, and when across cloud resources. It is the primary mechanism for enforcing least privilege.

Analogy

Think of identity as a keycard that contains exactly the doors (resources) and times (conditions) a user can access, rather than a master key.

Common mistake

Relying solely on network controls and granting broad permissions with wildcards creates privilege escalation windows.
