Module 6 › Chapter 6.7 › Containment strategies
Short-term containment

Immediate actions to limit impact

  • Network segmentation: isolate affected hosts or VLANs to prevent lateral movement.
  • Host isolation: disconnect a compromised machine from the network.
  • Service shutdown: stop a compromised application to halt exploitation.
  • Account disablement: disable credentials used in the compromise.
Considerations
Assess business impact, evidence risk, and reversibility. Favor actions that are reversible and documented.
Long-term containment

Strategic changes to reduce recurrence

Re-architect segments, implement stronger controls, and deploy improved detection to remove the adversary’s ability to re-enter or evade detection.

Example
After isolating affected POS hosts, plan long-term segmentation between POS and general corporate networks and deploy enhanced EDR rules and monitoring.
Common mistake
Over-containment that unnecessarily halts operations, or under-containment that leaves lateral movement paths open. Document the trade-offs.
Key Takeaway

Containment must limit damage and preserve evidence while minimizing business disruption. Choose actions with clear reversibility and documented impact.
