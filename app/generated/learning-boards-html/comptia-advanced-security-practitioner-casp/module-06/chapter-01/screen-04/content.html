Module 6 › Chapter 6.1 › Defining Threat Intelligence
Definition

Threat intelligence converts data into insight by adding attribution, intent, and operational context so defenders can act with confidence.

What TI includes
  • Actor hypotheses and motivations
  • Tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs)
  • Impact assessment and mitigation guidance
Key Takeaway
Context turns an IoC into a decision — prioritize and respond based on actor and intent, not only observables.
Analogy & Concrete Example
Analogy: raw data are ingredients; IoCs are labeled ingredients; threat intelligence is the prepared meal with recipe and serving suggestion. Example: a list of malicious IPs becomes intelligence when analysts link them to a ransomware group conducting extortion campaigns against the financial sector and recommend immediate blocking plus additional logging on payment endpoints.
Common misconception
Treating IoCs as finished intelligence leads to misprioritization. Always ask: who, why, how, and what is the impact?
