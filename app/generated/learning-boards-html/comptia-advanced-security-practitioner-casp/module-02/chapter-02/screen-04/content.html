Module 2 › Chapter 2.2 › IAM: Purpose and advanced scope
Definition

Advanced IAM explained

IAM integrates authentication, authorization, directory services, and privileged controls to manage identities across hybrid environments.

  • • Central authentication via federation reduces repeated logins and centralizes policy.
  • • MFA strengthens identity assertions by demanding additional factors beyond a password.
  • • PAM isolates privileged access using vaults, proxied sessions, and rotation.
  • • Directory sync keeps canonical attributes consistent between on-prem and cloud.
Key Takeaway

Centralization improves manageability but increases the criticality of the authentication plane; protect it with layered controls, monitoring, and governance.

Analogy
IAM as a secure passport and customs system

Think of the IdP as passport control: it validates identity once. SSO is the passport enabling travel across services. MFA is extra identity verification at checkpoints. PAM is a special permit for administrators, recorded like a monitored special-ops entry.

Concrete example
User authenticates with their corporate IdP and accesses multiple business apps without re-authenticating.
Common mistake
Treating SSO as a security improvement by itself without enforcing MFA or monitoring on the SSO provider.
Key Takeaway

Plan for centralization benefits and centralization risks. Use MFA, segmentation, and auditing to mitigate concentrated risk.
