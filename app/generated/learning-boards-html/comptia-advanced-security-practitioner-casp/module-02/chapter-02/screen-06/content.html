Module 2 › Chapter 2.2 › SSO — benefits and risks
Benefits
  • • Improved user experience and reduced password resets.
  • • Centralized policy enforcement and simpler MFA deployment.
  • • Consolidated logs for threat detection and auditing.
Risks
  • • The IdP becomes a single point of failure and a high-value target.
  • • Misconfigured trust relationships can grant unintended access.
  • • SSO does not replace per-application authorization checks.
Concrete scenario
An enterprise enables SSO for dozens of cloud apps. Administrators must enforce MFA for the IdP and segment networks to prevent lateral movement in case an admin workstation is compromised.
Misconception
SSO is not an authorization solution. Applications still require role and permission enforcement after authentication.
Pro tip
Protections: require MFA for all administrative IdP accounts, monitor abnormal authentications, and test failover of the IdP to avoid downtime.
Key Takeaway

SSO centralizes and simplifies authentication but raises the importance of hardening the IdP and preserving per-application authorization.
