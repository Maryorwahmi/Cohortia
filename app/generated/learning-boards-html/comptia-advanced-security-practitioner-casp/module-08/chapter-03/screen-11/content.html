Module 08 › Chapter 8.3 › Post-Exploitation
Common post-exploitation activities
  • Persistence: set up controlled, temporary ways to regain access if needed for demonstration.
  • Privilege escalation: look for misconfigurations or credentials to elevate access.
  • Data gathering: collect configuration files, keys, or sensitive documents that show impact.
  • Lateral movement and pivoting: use the compromised host to reach internal systems.
Misconception
Aggressive persistence or data exfiltration without clear scope can cross legal boundaries. Limit actions to what demonstrates business impact and is authorized.
Scenario card
After obtaining a meterpreter session on a web server, an analyst retrieves a configuration file containing a domain service account. Using that account, the tester authenticates to another host and demonstrates the ability to access sensitive customer records. All steps are recorded, and persistence artifacts are removed during cleanup as per RoE.
Key Takeaway

Post-exploitation shows business risk, but must be scoped, documented, and reversible unless otherwise agreed.
