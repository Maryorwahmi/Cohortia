Module 8 › Chapter 8.6 › Pretexting & Baiting
Pretexting defined
A constructed story that gains trust or authority to gather details.
Concrete example: an attacker calls claiming to be from IT needing your username to reset an account. The caller uses internal terminology to sound authentic.
Why it succeeds: people trust apparent authority and want to resolve disruptions quickly.
Misconception: assuming verification by caller ID is sufficient; caller IDs can be spoofed.
Baiting and quid pro quo
Physical lures and exchange offers that trade help for access.
Baiting example
A labeled USB left in a parking lot. The safe simulation logs insertion events without running code.
Quid pro quo example
An offer to fix computers in exchange for credentials or remote access, often delivered by phone.
Key Takeaway
Treat unknown media and unsolicited offers as security incidents and report them.
