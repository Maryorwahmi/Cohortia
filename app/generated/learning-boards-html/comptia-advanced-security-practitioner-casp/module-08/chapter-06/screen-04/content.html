Module 8 › Chapter 8.6 › What is Social Engineering?
Definition and psychology
A human-focused attack that uses persuasion over code.
  • Exploits authority: requests that appear to come from IT or executives.
  • Exploits urgency: deadlines or threats that push quick action.
  • Exploits curiosity and reward: enticing offers or unusual attachments.
Example: Spear-phishing
A tailored email referencing a current vendor invoice sent to an accounts payable manager to trick them into approving payment or revealing credentials.
Why it works
Contextual details lower suspicion and increase trust.
Common mistake
Assuming tech alone will stop these attacks.
Defensive controls
Combine people-focused training with technical controls.
Multi-factor authentication reduces value of stolen passwords.
Email filtering and DMARC reduce spoofed messages.
Clear verification steps for requests protect routine procedures.
Report-phishing mechanisms enable rapid response and learning.
Key takeaway

Social engineering targets predictable human behavior; reduce risk by changing routines, adding verification, and combining training with technical controls.
