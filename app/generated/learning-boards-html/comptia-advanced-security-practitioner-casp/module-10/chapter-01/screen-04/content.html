Module 10 › Chapter 10.1 › Why secure SDLC matters
Core Concept

Shift-left and economics of fixes

Finding issues earlier reduces cost, shortens feedback loops, and improves confidence in releases.

Common misconception

A single pen test before release is sufficient. It is not; late testing finds issues that may require architecture or data-flow changes.

Concrete example

If an authorization boundary is missed in design, retrofitting fine-grained controls after deployment can require rewrites across services and APIs.

Key takeaway

Design decisions determine many security outcomes; invest in early review and threat modeling to avoid expensive rework.

Definition

Secure SDLC explained

A process that embeds security-related activities into requirements, design, development, testing, deployment, and maintenance so security becomes part of the product lifecycle.

Three measurable outputs
  • Fewer high-severity findings at release.
  • Shorter mean time to remediate vulnerabilities.
  • Traceable security requirements and verification steps.
Exercise

Identify one bug fixed late in your project and note which earlier SDLC activity could have prevented it: requirements abuse case, design threat model, or automated SAST check.
