Module 10 › Chapter 10.2 › What is Threat Modeling?
Definition & Purpose

Threat modeling helps teams identify what might be exploited, estimate likelihood and impact, and decide where to apply controls in the design phase.

Why it matters

Finding architectural flaws early reduces remediation cost and prevents insecure designs from being implemented.

Common misconception

Threat modeling is not a one-time checklist. It must be revisited when requirements or architecture change.

Key Takeaway

Make threat modeling part of design reviews so controls are chosen with architectural intent.

Practical seed: Data Flow Diagram
A DFD lists components, data stores and flows. Use it to ask attacker-focused STRIDE questions for each element.
Example

For a UserProfileService, a DFD flow from API Gateway to the service suggests testing for information disclosure and tampering of profile data.
