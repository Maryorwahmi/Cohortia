Threat Modeling and Secure Design Principles

Proactive techniques to find and mitigate threats during design, and the principles that make systems resilient.

In this lesson

Understand threat modeling methods, use DFDs to find attack paths, and apply secure design principles like least privilege and defense in depth.

Why it matters

Catching design flaws early reduces cost, avoids architectural rework, and strengthens an organization’s security posture.

Explore Key Topics
STRIDE categories and examples, DREAD risk ranking, and PASTA steps that connect business context to attack simulation.
Explore Key Topics
Coming up
STRIDE
A mnemonic for threat categories used with data flow diagrams to find attacker paths.
PASTA
A seven-step, risk-centric process that ties business objectives to simulated attacks and mitigations.
Secure Principles
Least privilege, defense in depth, secure defaults and separation of concerns guide architecture choices.
Visual Concept
Data Flow Diagram (DFD) Seed
Components: Web App, API Gateway, UserProfileService, Database. Use this as the starting point to ask STRIDE questions.
Quick example
If the API Gateway forwards user profile requests, ask how that flow could be tampered with or reveal data.
Practical tip
Start simple: draw a minimal DFD and iterate. Use STRIDE to populate threat lists, then use DREAD or PASTA to prioritize mitigations based on business impact.
