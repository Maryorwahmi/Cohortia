Module 4 › Chapter 4.3 › Securing Management Interfaces & RBAC
Architecture

Isolate and limit access

Treat the management plane as a separate trust zone. Enforce MFA, role separation, and jump-host controls. Forward logs to a central collector for analysis and alerting.
Dedicated management VLAN with ACLs
MFA on hypervisor and management console
RBAC splitting network, storage, and VM roles
Concrete control
Use a hardened jump host that administrators use for all hypervisor actions. Only that host can access the management VLAN. Record access and require just-in-time elevation when full admin rights are needed.
Misconception
Assuming strong passwords alone will protect management interfaces — use layered controls: MFA, RBAC, and network isolation.
Key Takeaway

Design the management plane as an isolated, audited zone with minimal access and strict role separation to reduce single-point-of-failure compromises.
