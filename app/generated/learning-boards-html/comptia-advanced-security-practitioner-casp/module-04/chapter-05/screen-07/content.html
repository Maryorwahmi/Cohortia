Module 4 › Chapter 4.5 › Multi-Factor Authentication
What MFA provides
A second verification factor dramatically reduces account takeover risk even if passwords are compromised.
Common MFA factors
Virtual authenticator apps, hardware tokens, SMS (less preferred), and biometrics in supported platforms.
Service accounts
Prefer roles and temporary credentials for non-human actors; MFA is primarily for interactive human sign-in.
Implementation notes
  • Require MFA for all administrative and privileged accounts.
  • Use secure enrollment and recovery to prevent social-engineered bypasses.
  • Log and alert repeated second-factor failures.
Key takeaway
MFA is not optional for critical access; design processes and logging to keep it effective and auditable.
