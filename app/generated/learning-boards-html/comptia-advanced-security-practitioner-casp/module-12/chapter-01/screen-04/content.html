Module 12 › Chapter 12.1 › What is BCP?
Definition

A holistic management process

Identify threats and ensure the organization can continue critical functions across people, processes, technology, and facilities. The focus is on business outcomes, not only system restoration.

People
Roles, alternate staffing, communications
Processes
Manual workarounds and procedural steps
Technology
Systems, backups, and redundancy
Facilities
Alternate sites, power, and connectivity
Concrete Example

If the CRM and telephony fail, a BCP might specify an alternate call center location, temporary order-taking spreadsheets, and secure ways to reconcile transactions later. The plan prioritizes customer-facing continuity rather than immediate full system restoration.

Misconception

BCP is not the same as a list of IT restore steps. If you restore systems but business processes cannot function, recovery has failed.

Dependency Map

A simple table of a critical function with dependencies helps prioritize recovery activities and align DRP details to business needs.

Critical Function: Order Processing
Dependencies: payment gateway, order database, customer service staff, alternate communications channel.
Recovery Goal Example
Maintain order taking with manual entry for up to 24 hours while IT restores systems to meet a data reconciliation process.
Key Takeaway

Design BCP around business outcomes and dependencies, not only technology restoration steps.
