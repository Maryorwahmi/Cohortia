Module 12 › Chapter 12.1 › BCP vs DRP
BCP (Business Continuity)

Business outcomes and continuity

Defines which business functions must continue, acceptable downtime, manual workarounds, alternate sites, and stakeholder communication strategies.

Focus: People, processes, facilities, and business-level priorities.
Example: Keep order processing active via alternate call center or manual forms.
Mapping exercise

List a critical function, its tolerable downtime, and the people and facilities required. That list becomes the anchor for DRP mapping.

Try this

Write one sentence tying a technical recovery objective to a business outcome, for example: restore customer orders within four hours so shipping is not delayed.

DRP (Disaster Recovery)

Technical recovery procedures

Specifies system restore steps, backup retrieval, failover configurations, and networking tasks required to recover IT services that support the business.

Focus: Systems, data, applications, and infrastructure.
Example: Restore CRM database from the most recent backup and reconfigure telephony failover.
Common mistake

Focusing only on DRP tasks without verifying they recover the business outcomes required by the BCP can lead to incomplete recovery.

Key Takeaway

Align DRP steps to the business goals set by BCP; every technical action should map to a recovery objective.
