Module 12 › Chapter 12.10 › Cyber Resilience
Definition

Cyber resilience explained

The ability to continue delivering intended outcomes despite cyberattacks by combining anticipation, protection, detection, response, recovery, and adaptation.

  • Anticipation: identifying likely attack paths before they are exploited.
  • Detection: rapid discovery of active attacks.
  • Recovery: restore systems securely and verify integrity.
Example Scenario
Ransomware recovery with integrity checks
  1. Quarantine affected segments and failover to recovery environment.
  2. Validate backup snapshots against known-good checksums and malware scans.
  3. Rotate credentials and apply secure baselines before rejoining network.
Key Takeaway

A resilient DR plan treats recovery as a secure operation, not just data restoration.

Common Mistake

Restore-first, verify-later

Restoring systems without scanning or baseline checks can reintroduce threats. Verification must be baked into runbooks.

Practical Checklist
  • Scan restored images for malware and compare checksums.
  • Verify configuration against secure baseline policies.
  • Monitor restored hosts closely for anomalous behavior for a defined hold period.
Misconception check: Verification adds time, but it prevents faster re-failures and larger impact later.
