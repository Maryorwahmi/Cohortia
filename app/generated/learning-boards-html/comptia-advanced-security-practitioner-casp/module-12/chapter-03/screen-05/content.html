Definition

Classifying disasters by impact

Each disaster type changes priorities: speed might be vital for accidental outages, while evidence preservation is critical for intentional attacks.

Natural
Physical damage, transport needs, and regional outages.
Accidental
Hardware failure, power loss, human error; usually fast recovery with backups.
Intentional
Attacks like ransomware require forensics and careful restore verification.
Practical branching
Your DRP should include alternate playbooks that specify which steps to run automatically and which require manual authorization based on the disaster type. For example, automated restores may be fine after a hardware failure but not after suspected data compromise.
Concrete scenario
A ransomware detection means you must preserve logs and backup snapshots, run integrity checks on backups, and coordinate with incident response before a full restore to avoid reintroducing the threat.
Common mistake
Rushing a restore without verification after an attack can lead to a re-compromise of restored systems.
