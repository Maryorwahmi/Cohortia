Module 12 › Chapter 12.9 › Evidence & Chain of Custody
Definition
Evidence preservation: capture images and logs in a forensically sound way and document every handling step to maintain chain of custody.
Five-step preservation procedure
  1. Identify compromised assets and scope of impact.
  2. Create forensically sound disk/instance images and store immutable copies.
  3. Export system, network, and application logs to secure storage.
  4. Document who handled evidence, when, where, and why (chain of custody).
  5. Obtain IR Lead sign-off before any destructive action or restoration.
Example runbook checkpoint
DRP Checkpoint: For each compromised VM, the DRP Lead must confirm with IR Lead that a forensic snapshot has been taken and stored in immutable storage, and the evidence transfer is logged with personnel details and timestamps before any restoration proceeds.
Common mistake
Restoring systems immediately without imaging them first, destroying volatile evidence and breaking chain of custody.
