Core Concept
What is Supply Chain Resilience?
Definition, practical meaning, and why it matters for BCP/DRP

Supply chain resilience is your capacity to anticipate, prepare for, and respond to supplier disruptions so critical services remain available. It treats vendors as parts of your operational topology rather than external helpers.

  • Inventory vendors that support critical functions identified in your BIA.
  • Measure how each vendor's failure affects your RTO and RPO targets.
  • Translate vendor assurances into contractual and technical controls.
Example
A CRM SaaS outage prevents order processing even though your database is available; vendor availability becomes a continuity requirement.
Common mistake
Assuming that a vendor's certification automatically meets your RTO/RPO needs is risky; certifications do not guarantee recovery metrics.
Key Takeaway

Resilience is measured by the impact a vendor failure would have on your recovery objectives and by your ability to enforce compensating controls.

Mapping metaphor
Imagine your operations as a set of linked services. Each vendor is a link. Break a link and the services downstream will feel the effect. Your job is to identify the strongest links you depend on and make them redundant or controllable.
Try this
List three vendors whose failure would stop revenue. For each, identify if contracts define RTO/RPO and whether you have an alternate.
Why it matters
Without vendor-aligned recovery objectives, your internal BCP can fail despite flawless internal controls.
