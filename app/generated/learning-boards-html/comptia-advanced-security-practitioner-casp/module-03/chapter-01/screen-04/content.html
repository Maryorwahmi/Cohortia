Core Concept

Why segmentation is essential

Segmentation creates many smaller trust zones, reducing the impact of a single compromise and enabling least-privilege network access.

Concrete example
Place payment systems in a dedicated VLAN, route through a firewall, and allow only necessary application ports from specific management hosts.
Common misconception
VLANs by themselves are not security controls. Without ACLs or firewall rules at routing points, VLAN separation can be bypassed once routing exists.
Why it matters
Limiting lateral movement is the fastest way to reduce breach impact and protect high-value assets like payment systems or control networks.
Key Takeaway

Segmentation works only when paired with enforcement and verification; design for minimal allowed flows and prove them in a lab.

Segmentation patterns
Perimeter + internal VLANs
Traditional model: perimeter firewall plus VLANs separating user, server, and DMZ zones. Add ACLs at routing points.
PVLANs for host isolation
Use PVLANs to limit peer-to-peer communication inside a VLAN while permitting egress via promiscuous ports.
VRF and microsegmentation
VRF separates routing state; microsegmentation enforces policies at the workload level for strongest containment.
