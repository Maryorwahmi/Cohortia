VRF: Virtual routing instances

A VRF creates an independent routing table. Traffic in one VRF cannot reach another unless explicitly routed or firewalled.

Common use case
Multi-tenant environments where tenants require complete routing separation and may use overlapping IP ranges.
Configuration elements
  • ip vrf <VRF_NAME>
  • route-distinguisher and route-target import/export
  • assign interfaces or VRF-aware subinterfaces
Design notes
  • Map services that must cross VRFs and plan explicit firewalls or route policies.
  • Document route-targets and route distinguishers for each VRF to avoid accidental imports.
  • Monitor routing tables separately and ensure control-plane protections for route distribution protocols.
Key Takeaway

VRF gives isolation at the routing layer and is the best choice when separate routing instances or overlapping addressing are required.
