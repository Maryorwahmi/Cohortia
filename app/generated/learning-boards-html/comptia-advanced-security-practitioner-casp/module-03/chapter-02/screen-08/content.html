Module 3 › Chapter 3.2 › Mobile Device Management
Definition

Mobile Device Management

MDM gives admins device-level control: configuration, policy enforcement, inventory, and remote actions like wipe and lock.

Capabilities
  • Enforce password/PIN complexity and auto-lock timeouts.
  • Require full-disk or file encryption.
  • Deploy and revoke apps, and perform remote wipe or lock.
Concrete Policy Example
Corporate Device Security Policy (MDM)

1. Device Enrollment: All corporate-issued devices must enroll in the MDM.
2. Password/PIN: Minimum 6-digit PIN; auto-lock after 5 minutes; lockout after 10 failed attempts.
3. Encryption: Full device encryption enforced.
4. App Control: Only approved app stores; unknown sources prohibited.
5. Remote Actions: Admins may lock or wipe devices for loss/theft.
Considerations & Mistakes
Misconception
Assuming MDM is always appropriate for personal devices. In BYOD, device-level control can breach privacy; consider app-level controls instead.
Operational Note
Define enrollment and removal processes, and ensure users know what data can be wiped in a remote action.
Key Takeaway

MDM is ideal for corporate-owned devices to enforce device-wide security; balance enforcement with privacy expectations in BYOD policies.
