Module 3 › Chapter 3.2 › Designing Guest Networks
Process Flow

VLAN segmentation, captive portal, and controls

A secure guest network separates users with VLANs and firewall rules, then applies captive portals and rate limits for control.

Step 1: VLAN and SSID mapping
Map the guest SSID to a dedicated VLAN that carries only guest traffic and has no route to internal subnets.
Step 2: Firewall ACLs
Create explicit deny rules from guest VLAN to internal ranges while allowing outbound Internet access. Log and monitor flows.
Step 3: Captive Portal & Limits
Use a captive portal for terms or credentials, and enforce rate limiting to prevent abuse and resource exhaustion.
Key Takeaway

Guest SSID must be paired with VLAN and firewall isolation; SSID separation alone is insufficient to secure internal resources.

Example Scenario
Network mapping
Guest SSID → VLAN 200 → Subnet 10.200.0.0/24; firewall denies 10.200.0.0/24 → 10.0.0.0/16 but allows 0.0.0.0/0 to Internet.
Common Mistake
Relying solely on separate SSIDs without implementing Layer 3 rules or VLAN tagging, which leaves guests able to reach internal hosts if switching is misconfigured.
Operational Note
Log guest sessions, set timeouts, and periodically audit ACLs to ensure isolation remains effective as the network changes.
