Module 9 › Chapter 9.6 › What is DLP and why it matters
Definition
Data Loss Prevention

A set of tools and processes that detect and prevent unauthorized exfiltration or exposure of sensitive data through inspection and enforcement.

Concrete Example
A payroll spreadsheet with bank account numbers is detected by pattern matching or fingerprinting and prevented from being emailed externally; the event is logged and an alert generated for review.
Common misconception
DLP is not a foolproof silver bullet. It needs classification, tuning, and operational processes to be effective.
How it works — high level
Content inspection
Pattern, keyword, fingerprint, or ML-based detection applied to files and traffic.
Policy engine
Rules define what to protect, where it can go, and what action to take on violations.
Enforcement
Block, encrypt, quarantine, or alert based on policy and context.
Response
Incident handling, forensics, and policy tuning after events are investigated.
Key Takeaway

DLP is an enforcement layer that depends on accurate detection and operational processes to stop sensitive data from leaving controlled boundaries.
