Interpreting and Prioritizing Findings
A practical rubric to turn alerts into prioritized work items.
Triage dimensions
  • Severity — technical impact of the flaw.
  • Exploitability — how easily an attacker can reach it.
  • Business impact — data exposure, downtime or reputation risk.
Example prioritization
A critical SQL injection in a public API with proof-of-concept exploit is high priority. A low-confidence information leak on an internal endpoint may be scheduled for later triage.
Practical steps
  1. Reproduce the finding and record request/response evidence.
  2. Score severity, exploitability and business impact to prioritize.
  3. Create a ticket with reproducer and expected fix; retest after remediation.
Common mistakes
Ignoring retests, relying only on a tool's confidence score, or treating every alert as equally urgent.
Key Takeaway

Prioritization reduces noise and focuses fixes on the most exploitable, high-impact vulnerabilities; always require evidence and retesting.
