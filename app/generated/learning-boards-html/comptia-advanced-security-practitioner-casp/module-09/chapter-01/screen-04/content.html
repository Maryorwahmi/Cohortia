Module 9 › Chapter 9.1 › What is the SSDLC?
SSDLC — Definition & Phases
An SDLC that integrates security activities at each phase so risks are identified and mitigated early.
Requirements
Define security and compliance needs, data sensitivity, and abuse cases.
Design
Threat modeling and secure design patterns such as least privilege.
Development
Secure coding practices, dependency hygiene, and code review.
Testing & Maintenance
SAST/DAST/IAST, penetration testing, monitoring, and patching.
Explanation & Example
  • Definition: SSDLC means planning and implementing security tasks through requirements to maintenance so controls are deliberate.
  • Why it matters: Fixing a vulnerability in production costs far more than addressing it during design or coding.
  • Concrete example: For login, require hashed credentials, rate limiting, and session controls as part of requirements.
  • Misconception: SSDLC is not just an extra validation step at the end — it is an ongoing engineering practice.
Key Takeaway

Plan security early. Convert controls into requirements and verify them through design and testing.

Common Mistake

Treating SSDLC as a one-time project results in gaps and a false sense of security.
