Traditional arrays vs SDS

A side-by-side comparison of architectural differences and operational implications.

Traditional arrays
  • Proprietary controllers and firmware.
  • Fixed scaling limits; upgrades often require costly appliances.
  • Predictable, appliance-level SLAs but potential vendor lock‑in.
Software‑Defined Storage
  • Distributed software on commodity servers with horizontal scaling.
  • Programmable APIs and automation; flexible growth.
  • Requires validated hardware and robust network design to meet SLAs.
Concrete scenario
A media company choosing between a SAN and SDS must weigh predictable appliance performance for a known workload against the agility and lower capex of SDS for rapid scaling and varied workloads.
Key Takeaway

SDS offers flexibility and scale but demands deliberate planning to achieve consistent performance and availability.
