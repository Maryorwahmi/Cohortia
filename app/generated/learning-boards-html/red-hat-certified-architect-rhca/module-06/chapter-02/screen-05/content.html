Module 6 › Chapter 6.2 › Ceph OSD
What an OSD does
Store objects, replicate data to peers, monitor health, and participate in rebalancing and recovery.
Design considerations
  • Map one OSD per disk or partition for predictable performance.
  • Use SSD/NVMe for WAL/DB to accelerate metadata and small writes.
  • Plan OSD count to balance capacity, throughput, and recovery parallelism.
Common mistake
Under-provisioning network bandwidth causes slow recovery and may impact client performance during rebuilds.
Pro tip
Monitor OSD I/O and recovery backlog; adjust replication speed limits or add OSDs to avoid sustained overloads.
Concrete example
A cluster with HDD-backed OSDs and NVMe for DB/WAL will typically show faster small-write performance than HDD-only OSDs because the write path leverages the NVMe for commit latency reduction while sending bulk data to the HDDs asynchronously.
Key Takeaway

OSD hardware choices and count directly affect throughput, latency, and how quickly the cluster self-heals after failures.
