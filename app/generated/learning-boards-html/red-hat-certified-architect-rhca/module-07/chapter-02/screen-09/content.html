Module 7 › Chapter 7.2 › Bridged vs NAT (libvirt) networks
Bridged

Direct LAN presence

Guests receive IPs from the physical DHCP and behave like full network hosts. Easier for services and monitoring that expect routable addresses.

  • Pros: direct access, simpler routing, better for production services.
  • Cons: needs host security hardening and firewall configuration.
NAT (libvirt)

Isolated private networks

Guests live on a private range managed by the host with outbound NAT. Good for isolated labs or when external exposure is undesired.

  • Pros: quick setup, adds a layer of isolation, simpler firewall implications.
  • Cons: inbound access requires port forwarding and can complicate service discovery.
Example decision

If you host production services reachable by other datacenter hosts, prefer bridged networking. If you spin up isolated test environments or containers of services that do not need inbound access, NAT can speed provisioning.
