Module 5 › Chapter 5.2 › Provider vs Tenant
Provider networks
Direct mapping to physical networks. Use for external access, floating IPs, and services needing predictable performance. Simpler to troubleshoot and avoids encapsulation overhead.
When to choose
High-bandwidth endpoints, external-facing services, and where NIC mapping is required.
Caveat
Limited segmentation count compared to overlays and requires physical VLAN planning.
Self-service (tenant) networks
Virtual networks managed by tenants, typically using overlays such as VXLAN. Provide strong isolation and flexible tenant topologies at scale but introduce encapsulation overhead and underlay requirements.
When to choose
Large multi-tenant clouds needing many isolated segments and tenant-managed networks.
Caveat
Requires underlay MTU and bandwidth tuning to handle encapsulation overhead efficiently.
Concrete example & takeaway
Use a provider network mapped to a physical NIC for floating IPs and public services. Use VXLAN-based tenant networks for internal tenant isolation when you need many segments. Ensure the underlay supports MTU and bandwidth for VXLAN encapsulation. Avoid the misconception that overlays are inherently slow; with proper underlay design they provide scale without unacceptable overhead.
