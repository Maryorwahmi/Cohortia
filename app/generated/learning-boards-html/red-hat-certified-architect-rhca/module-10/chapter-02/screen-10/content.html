Definition

Saga Pattern

A Saga is a sequence of local transactions where each step publishes events that trigger the next step. If a step fails, compensating actions undo prior steps.

Two styles
  • Choreography: services react to events and progress the workflow.
  • Orchestration: a coordinator commands each participant to act.
Concrete example
Payment charges card, Order creates record, Inventory reserves stock. If reservation fails, Inventory emits failure and a compensating refund is issued.
Key Takeaway

Use Sagas to maintain business correctness across services without two-phase commits, but design compensations and test failure paths thoroughly.

Pitfall
Assuming compensations are simple. They are often as hard as the forward actions and must be idempotent and safe to run multiple times.
