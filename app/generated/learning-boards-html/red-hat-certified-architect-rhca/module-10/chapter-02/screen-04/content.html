Definition

Bounded Context

A bounded context is a logical boundary containing a consistent domain model. It becomes a candidate microservice owned by a single team.

Why it matters
Boundaries reduce coupling, enable independent scaling and deployment, and let teams choose the right technology per domain.
Concrete example
In an e-commerce system, Product Catalog, Order Management, and Payment Processing are separate bounded contexts and can become services with different teams and datastores.
Common mistake
Avoid creating many small services that still depend synchronously on each other. That becomes a distributed monolith and costs you the benefits of microservices.
Analogy
Monolith
All capabilities live in one codebase. Changes affect many teams.
Bounded Contexts
Clear business areas with explicit models and contracts between them.
Key Takeaway

Design services around business capabilities and ensure contracts and data ownership are explicit to avoid coupling.
