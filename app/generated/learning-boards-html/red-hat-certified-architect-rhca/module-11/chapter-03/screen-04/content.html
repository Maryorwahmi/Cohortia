Module 11 › Chapter 11.3 › Why accurate time matters
Definition

Consistent timestamps and security

Timestamps let you sequence events across machines and validate time-sensitive protocols. When clocks disagree, both operational and security features break.

Concrete example
If a client’s clock is minutes ahead of a Kerberos KDC, authentication fails because tickets appear not yet valid or expired.
Common misconception
Small drift is harmless. Even second-level drift can confuse log ordering and certificate validation in distributed systems.
Why it matters
  • Log correlation: consistent timestamps let you rebuild event sequences across nodes.
  • Security: Kerberos and other auth methods reject requests beyond acceptable clock skew.
  • Certificates: validity periods depend on accurate system clocks to accept or reject certs.
Key Takeaway

Make time part of your security and observability plan. Verify sources and monitor offsets to keep systems trustworthy.
