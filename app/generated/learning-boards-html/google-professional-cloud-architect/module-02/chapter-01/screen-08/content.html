SLA, SLO, and SLI: Defining Service Levels

Formalizing expectations for performance and availability

SLA (Agreement)

A formal, legally binding agreement between a service provider and a customer, outlining expected service levels (e.g., 99.99% uptime).

SLO (Objective)

Internal targets for a service's performance and availability, often more stringent than the SLA, used to guide engineering efforts and design.

SLI (Indicator)

Actual metrics used to measure performance against the SLOs, such as error rate, latency, or uptime percentage. These are raw data points.

Example: GCP Compute Engine

GCP offers a SLA of 99.99% monthly uptime for multi-zone Compute Engine instances. An internal team might set an SLO of 99.995% uptime, measured by an SLI of successful VM pings every minute.

Key Takeaway

The SLA, SLO, and SLI framework provides a structured approach to defining, targeting, and measuring the reliability and performance of cloud services, aligning business and engineering goals.
