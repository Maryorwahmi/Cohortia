How SCPs work
SCPs are attached to the root, OUs, or accounts. They define a boundary: even if an account policy Allows an action, an SCP Deny will block it. They do not grant permissions by themselves.
Use case
Prevent creating resources in restricted regions or deleting audit logs across many accounts.
Design tip
Treat SCPs as organizational policy: keep them minimal, well-documented, and owned by central security or platform teams.
Hierarchy example
Organization Root
› OU: Production (SCP: deny deletion of CloudTrail logs)
› OU: Development (SCP: deny s3:CreateBucket in us-west-2)
Testing note: apply a conservative SCP to a test OU first, then expand its scope. Remember, SCP denies are evaluated with the same deny-first rule — they are part of policy evaluation and can cause unexpected denies if not coordinated with account-level policies.
