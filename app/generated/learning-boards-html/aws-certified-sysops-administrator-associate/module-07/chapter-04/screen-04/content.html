RTO and RPO — The Foundations of DR

Clear definitions let you compare strategies objectively.

Definitions
Recovery Time Objective (RTO)
Maximum acceptable downtime after a failure. Measured in seconds, minutes, or hours.
Recovery Point Objective (RPO)
Maximum acceptable data loss, expressed as the age of the most recent recoverable data.
Why it matters
These two numbers determine how much infrastructure and replication you need and help prioritize DR spend.
Key Takeaway

Ask stakeholders for specific RTO and RPO values before proposing a DR strategy; these narrow choices quickly.

Concrete example & common misconception
Example
A database with RTO 30 minutes and RPO 5 minutes pushes you toward continuous replication and a warm standby or active-active model to meet both targets.
Misconception
Low RPO does not guarantee low RTO. You can have frequent backups (low RPO) but still take hours to provision infrastructure (high RTO).
Check your understanding
Pick a non-critical analytics job and propose acceptable RTO/RPO. If they are hours and a day, Backup and Restore is likely sufficient.
