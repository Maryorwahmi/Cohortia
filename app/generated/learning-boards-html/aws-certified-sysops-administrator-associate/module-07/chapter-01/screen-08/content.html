Process Flow › RPO & RTO
Define RPO and RTO
Start with business impact, not technology. Convert impact tolerances into time windows for data loss and downtime.
  1. Identify business processes and acceptable loss/downtime.
  2. Translate those limits into RPO and RTO values with stakeholders.
  3. Design backups, replication, and automation to meet those values.
Operational consequences
Lower RPO: more frequent backups or continuous replication. Lower RTO: faster automation, standby capacity, and tested runbooks.
Concrete example
If an e-commerce sale demands at most 5 minutes of order loss, plan replication and backup frequency accordingly and test the failover path to ensure recovery within the allowed downtime.
Misconception
RPO and RTO are technical knobs; they are business-defined targets that drive technical design.
