Module 8 › Chapter 7.7 › Mapping RPO and RTO to DR Strategy
Mapping process
  1. Inventory critical applications and dependencies with stakeholders.
  2. Assign RPO and RTO values per application and justify them.
  3. Match to a DR strategy based on tolerance and cost constraints.
  4. Design runbooks and automation needed to meet targets.
  5. Schedule tests that validate the end‑to‑end recovery time.
Example
A payment processing service with RTO of minutes should avoid cold restores and prefer Warm Standby or Active/Active architectures.
Strategy quick guide
Backup & Restore
Lower cost; longer RTO. Use for noncritical workloads.
Pilot Light
Minimal resources in DR region; data prepared. Faster than cold restore.
Warm Standby
Scaled-down environment ready to scale up. Good balance of cost and recovery speed.
Active/Active
Full deployments in multiple regions. Low RTO/RPO, higher complexity and cost.
Misconception: The cheapest option rarely meets business availability needs. Always map strategy to the SLA.
