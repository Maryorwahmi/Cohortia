Module 8 › Chapter 8.4 › RDS Storage Types
Storage comparison
gp3
Baseline 3,000 IOPS, throughput separable from size. Cost-effective and flexible for most workloads.
gp2
IOPS scale with volume size. May force larger volumes for performance.
io1 / io2
Provisioned IOPS for sustained low-latency workloads; high cost. Use only when gp3 cannot meet needs.
Magnetic
Cheapest and slowest; avoid for production.
Concrete example

Your workload requires 5,000 IOPS and 200 GiB of storage. With gp3 you provision 200 GiB plus 5,000 IOPS. With gp2 you'd need to size the volume much larger to achieve similar IOPS, increasing storage cost unnecessarily. If the workload instead requires sustained sub-millisecond I/O consistency, consider io1/io2 after validating gp3 cannot match latency.

Key Takeaway

Start with gp3 for most workloads. Reserve io1/io2 for sustained high IOPS where latency guarantees are essential.

Misconception: choosing io1/io2 because of occasional spikes is often wasteful; test gp3 provisioning first.
