Module 2 › Chapter 1.4 › What is a VPC?
Definition

A VPC is a logically isolated virtual network defined by a CIDR block. It provides address space, subnets, routing, and integration points for connectivity.

Why it matters

Correct CIDR sizing and AZ subnet placement prevent address exhaustion and support high availability.

Example
Use 10.0.0.0/16 split into /24 subnets across AZs for servers and databases.
Misconception
VPC is not a physical network; it is a logical boundary with managed routing and controls.
Visual: Address partitioning
VPC CIDR: 10.0.0.0/16
/24 subnet — AZ-a — app servers
/24 subnet — AZ-b — databases (private)
/24 subnet — AZ-c — NAT & public gateways
Key Takeaway

Choose CIDR and subnet sizes with future growth and AZ redundancy in mind to avoid disruptive renumbering.
