Core Concept

AND, OR, NOT

Combine multiple boolean expressions to narrow or broaden result sets. Remember operator precedence and prefer parentheses for clarity.

AND — all must be true
Use when every specified condition is required for the row to qualify.
OR — any may be true
Use when one of multiple alternatives should include the row.
NOT — negate a condition
Use NOT to invert a condition, often improving readability compared to inequality operators.
Key Takeaway

Always test sub-expressions and use parentheses when mixing AND and OR to avoid logic bugs.

Example queries
-- Find employees in department 90 with salary > 10000
SELECT employee_id, first_name, salary, department_id
FROM employees
WHERE department_id = 90 AND salary > 10000;

-- Alternative with OR and parentheses
WHERE (department_id = 90 AND salary > 10000) OR department_id = 60;
Misconception
Assuming AND and OR evaluate left-to-right; AND actually has higher precedence than OR.
Pro tip
Use parentheses to document your intent and make queries easier to audit.
