Module 1 › Chapter 1.1 › What is a Database?
Definition

An organized collection of structured information stored electronically, managed to ensure integrity, availability, and secure multi-user access.

Structure
Predictable formats enable reliable queries.
Management
DBMS enforces rules and handles concurrency and backups.
Shared Use
Multiple clients can safely read and write.
Key Takeaway

Databases are managed systems, not simple file stores. They guarantee consistent results for many users.

Real-world example
An e-commerce app uses a database to keep product data, orders, and inventory synchronized across web, mobile, and point-of-sale systems. Inventory counts must update atomically when orders are placed.
Common misconception
Files are enough for storage. In reality, they lack integrity rules and safe concurrency controls.
Pro tip
When modeling data, think about the operations you need: reporting, updates, or transactions. That drives structure choices.
Why it matters

Reliable databases let teams build features without re-implementing storage, consistency, or security, and they scale to support many users and large volumes of data.
