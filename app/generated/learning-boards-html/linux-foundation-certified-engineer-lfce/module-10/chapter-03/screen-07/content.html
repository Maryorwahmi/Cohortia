Comparison

si / so — swap-in vs swap-out

Swap-in (si)
Pages read from swap into RAM per second — indicates data returning to memory.
Swap-out (so)
Pages written to swap from RAM per second — indicates active eviction to disk.
Concrete scenario
If vmstat shows constant so and increased bi/bo when a batch job runs, the job is forcing pages to disk and slowing overall throughput — a classic thrashing pattern.
Misconception
Any swap usage equals thrashing. In reality, brief swap use is normal; watch for sustained activity.
Action
Locate heavy processes, lower swappiness temporarily, and consider RAM addition or tuning app memory.
Key Takeaway

Sustained si/so are the clearest indicator of thrashing; correlate with bi/bo and performance impact before acting.
