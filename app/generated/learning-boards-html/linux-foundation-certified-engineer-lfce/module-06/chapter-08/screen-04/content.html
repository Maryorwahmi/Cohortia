Module 7 › Chapter 6.8 › Scanning vs Pen Testing
Definitions and practical distinctions

Understand what each method promises, and what it cannot guarantee.

Vulnerability Scanning
  • Automated matching of system state to vulnerability signatures.
  • Non-intrusive, repeatable, suitable for scheduled checks.
  • Best for broad coverage and patch tracking.
Penetration Testing
  • Manual and automated techniques to attempt exploitation.
  • Intrusive and goal-oriented: demonstrates real impact.
  • Used for high assurance and attack-path analysis.
Concrete example
A weekly GVM scan flags outdated OpenSSH. A pen test attempts to exploit that outdated version chain to gain shell access and shows whether an exploit leads to privilege escalation.
Common mistake
Assuming scan results are complete. Scanners detect known patterns; they cannot always find chained or logic vulnerabilities a human tester might discover.
Key Takeaway

Use scans for routine hygiene and pen tests for validating exploitability and impact on critical systems.

Quick contrast
Scope
Scans: broad and regular. Pen tests: focused and time-bound.
Intrusiveness
Scans are generally non-intrusive; pen tests may intentionally disrupt to confirm exploitability.
Outcome
Scans: lists to prioritize. Pen tests: proof of concept and mitigation guidance.
