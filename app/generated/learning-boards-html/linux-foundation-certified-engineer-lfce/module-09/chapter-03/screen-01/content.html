Automating System Tasks with cron and systemd Timers

Learn when to keep using cron and when to prefer systemd timers, plus how to author, enable, and verify scheduled jobs safely.

Estimated time: 15 minutes
In this lesson
cron syntax, crontab types, and output handling.
Coming up
systemd .service and .timer unit examples and verification.
Practice
Hands-on cleanup script and timer creation steps.
Why this matters

Reliable scheduling keeps backups, cleanups, and monitoring tasks running without manual intervention. Choosing the right scheduler affects observability and reliability.

Quick orientation

We will begin with crontab basics, show common mistakes, and then transition to systemd timers with unit examples and verification commands. The hands-on cleanup task ties both concepts into a reproducible workflow.

Concept cards
cron
Daemon-based scheduler; uses crontab entries and minimal environment.
systemd timers
Timer and service units work together; support persistence and journal logging.
Explore Key Topics
Crontab syntax
Fields: minute, hour, day-of-month, month, day-of-week. Use absolute paths.
Unit files
A .service describes execution; a .timer schedules it with OnCalendar.
Safety & logging
Run with least privilege, capture stdout and stderr to logs or journal.
