Core Concept
Log centralization
Why shipping logs off-host is critical to forensic readiness
Ship • Protect • Correlate
Benefits of centralization
  • Prevents attackers from erasing local evidence.
  • Makes it simple to correlate events across multiple systems.
  • Allows long-term retention and controlled access for legal needs.
Design for secure transport and restricted access to the log repository.
Practical considerations
  1. Choose what to centralize: journal entries, auth logs, application logs, and audit exports.
  2. Protect logs from tampering and ensure retention policies meet investigation needs.
  3. Use central logs to build cross-host timelines for distributed incidents.
Key Takeaway

Centralize logs to retain evidence and enable correlation, but secure the log store and transport thoroughly.

Concrete Example
Forward journal and auth logs to a remote syslog server so that even if local files are cleared, a copy remains for investigation.
Misconception
Assuming centralized logging replaces auditd; they are complementary—auditd is security-focused and tamper-resistant on the host.
Check
Do you have immutable or write-once storage for logs that could be used in legal proceedings?
