Core Concept
Indicators of Compromise
Observable signs that a Linux system may be breached
Definition • Examples • Misconceptions
Definition & common examples
  • Unexpected user accounts or recent changes to /etc/passwd or /etc/shadow.
  • Unknown processes or binaries running with elevated privileges.
  • Services listening on high-numbered ports not part of baseline.
  • Repeated failed authentications concentrated in time.
Analogy
Think of IOCs as unfamiliar footprints around a locked door — one footprint might be a mistake, many suggest a break-in.
Investigative checks
Use these commands to confirm suspicious signals and collect evidence:
ps aux — review running processes and command-line args
lsof -i — identify open network sockets
last / lastb — view recent logins and failed attempts
Key Takeaway

Look for correlated anomalies across users, files, processes, and network activity before escalating.

Example
A new user entry plus a process listening on port 31337 suggests a backdoor; investigate process executable and audit logs to attribute creation.
Common Mistake
Treating a single failed login as a breach; instead correlate with patterns and other evidence.
