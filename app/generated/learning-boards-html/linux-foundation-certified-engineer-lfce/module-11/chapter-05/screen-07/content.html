iostat - device metrics explained
Use extended stats to measure throughput, latency, and device utilization.
%util
Shows the percentage of time the device was busy. Persistent near-100% indicates saturation.
await
Average time for I/O requests. High await means requests are taking long to complete.
avgqu-sz
Average queue length. High values mean requests are waiting and the device is a bottleneck.
Concrete example — dd write
# Large sequential write to test device
dd if=/dev/zero of=tempfile bs=1M count=1000 oflag=direct
# Then run: iostat -x 1 5 and watch %util, await, avgqu-sz
Key Takeaway

Match high %util with increased await and avgqu-sz to confirm a true device bottleneck before planning mitigations.

Common mistake
Relying on %util alone without checking latency measures can hide intermittent queuing and latency problems.
Pro tip
Run iostat during the suspected incident and during a controlled test to compare normal versus stressed behavior.
