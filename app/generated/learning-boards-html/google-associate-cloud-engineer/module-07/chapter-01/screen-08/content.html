Core Principle

How IAM Policies are Inherited

Permissions granted at a higher level in the resource hierarchy are automatically inherited by all child resources.

Key Aspects of Inheritance

  • Downward Flow: Policies flow from Organization to Folders, then to Projects, and finally to Resources.
  • Additive: Permissions are additive; a child resource inherits all policies from its parents.
  • Caution: Overly broad permissions at higher levels can create security risks.
Key Takeaway

Leverage policy inheritance for scalable management, but always apply the principle of least privilege by granting permissions at the lowest necessary level.

Example Scenario:

If a user is granted 'Viewer' role at the Folder level, they can view all projects and resources within that folder, even if no specific policy is set on the individual project.
