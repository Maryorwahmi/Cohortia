Core Concept
Streams are typed pipes
A stream formats values to characters and moves them between program and I/O devices. It maintains state and supports manipulators.
Standard streams
std::cout, std::cin, std::cerr, std::clog are pre-connected objects you use directly.
Buffering matters
Buffered streams delay writes for efficiency; unbuffered streams write immediately.
State and flags
Streams have error flags and format state that persist until changed or reset.
Key Takeaway

Treat streams as objects with state and buffering semantics. Choose the appropriate stream when immediacy or buffering matters.

Analogy & example
Analogy: A stream is like a water pipe with a valve. Data flows through, and manipulators adjust the shape of the water, such as precision or width. Example: Using the insertion operator with std::cout converts an integer to characters and writes them into the stream buffer for later flushing to the console.
Common misconception
People often think writing always appears immediately. In reality, buffering may delay output. Use std::cerr for immediate error messages or flush when necessary.
Check your understanding
Why would you choose std::clog instead of std::cerr for routine debug information? Consider performance and crash scenarios.
