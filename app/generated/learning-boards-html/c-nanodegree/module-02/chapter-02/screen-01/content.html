Black-box analogy
Imagine a sealed device with three input controls: read, write, and configure. The outer code can only use those controls; it cannot reach inside to flip wires or change sensors directly. That sealed area is the private implementation.
Why this helps: reduces bugs, isolates change, enforces invariants.
Public knobs expose safe operations; internals remain private.
