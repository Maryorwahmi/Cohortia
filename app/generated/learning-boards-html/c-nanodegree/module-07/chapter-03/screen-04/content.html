Module 7 › Chapter 7.3 › Condition Variable
What it does

A condition variable lets a thread wait for a boolean condition to become true without busy‑polling. It works with a mutex: wait() atomically releases the lock and puts the thread to sleep; notify wakes a waiter to re-acquire the lock and continue.

Mechanics
  • wait(unique_lock, predicate) releases lock and sleeps atomically.
  • notify_one wakes a single waiter; notify_all wakes all waiting threads.
  • Predicate re-checks the condition to survive spurious wakeups.
Common mistake
Calling notify while holding the mutex can delay the woken thread; unlock before notifying when possible.
Key Takeaway

Use condition variables to block threads efficiently and hand off work only when real work exists.

Analogy: waiting at a bakery
Imagine a baker who wakes a single customer when the next loaf is ready. Customers don’t stand and stare; they wait outside until the baker calls them. notify_one is that call; the predicate is the customer checking that the loaf is indeed available.
Why it matters for pipelines
  • Reduces CPU usage compared to polling.
  • Improves latency by waking exactly when needed.
  • Makes shutdown predictable with a finished flag and notify_all.
