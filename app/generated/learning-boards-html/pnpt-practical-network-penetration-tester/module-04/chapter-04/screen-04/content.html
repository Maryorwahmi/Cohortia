Core Concept

Understanding Persistence and Lateral Movement

Securing long-term access and expanding network control

Persistence

  • Maintain access to a compromised system or network.
  • Ensures continued control even if initial access is detected.
  • Crucial for long-term engagement and simulating real-world threats.

Lateral Movement

  • Move from one compromised system to another.
  • Expands control deeper into the network.
  • Aims to reach high-value targets like Domain Controllers.
Key Takeaway

Persistence and lateral movement are interdependent phases that allow attackers to maintain control and expand their reach within a compromised network, simulating a full attack lifecycle.

Common Misconception

Many believe that achieving Domain Admin privileges is the final step. However, without persistence and lateral movement, this access can be fleeting. Real-world attacks focus on establishing resilient access and exploring the network thoroughly.

Real-World Scenario

Imagine compromising a single workstation. Without persistence, a simple reboot or antivirus scan could erase your access. With lateral movement, you could use that initial access to pivot to a server, then to a Domain Controller, gaining control over the entire network, even if the workstation is cleaned.
