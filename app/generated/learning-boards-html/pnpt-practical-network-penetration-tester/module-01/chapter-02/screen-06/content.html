Defining the Scope: In-Scope vs. Out-of-Scope

Precisely identifying what systems and activities are permitted and forbidden during a penetration test.

In-Scope Assets

Specific systems, networks, applications, and IP addresses authorized for testing.

Out-of-Scope Assets

Systems or activities explicitly forbidden from testing to prevent damage or legal issues.

Permitted Activities

Clear list of authorized test types (e.g., vulnerability scanning, web app attacks).

The Scope Boundary

Clearly defined in-scope and out-of-scope elements prevent unintended consequences and legal liabilities.

In-Scope

Targeted systems, specific IPs.

Out-of-Scope

Fragile legacy systems, DoS attacks.

Key Takeaway

Always ensure the RoE clearly defines both in-scope and out-of-scope assets and activities to avoid legal and operational risks.
