Core Concept

Front-running and Transaction Ordering

Understand how mempool visibility creates economic attack opportunities and mitigation patterns.

Why it matters
  • Pending transactions are visible; bots can react and submit priority transactions.
  • Order-sensitive logic like auctions and large trades is exposed to manipulation.
  • Mitigation changes protocol design rather than relying solely on gas economics.
Practical patterns
Commit-reveal schemes hide intent until after commitments are recorded. Batching or oracle-driven finalization reduces the window of opportunistic ordering. Where appropriate, use time-locked or aggregated settlement to reduce immediate ordering effects.
Example: commit a bid hash, reveal later to prevent frontrunning the bid.
Common mistake: assuming higher gas price deters attackers; it only raises cost.
Key Takeaway

If your protocol’s outcome depends on transaction order, adopt design patterns like commit-reveal or batching to limit economic exploits.
