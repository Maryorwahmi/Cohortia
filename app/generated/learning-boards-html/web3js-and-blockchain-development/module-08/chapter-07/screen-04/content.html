Core Concept
L1 scalability & interoperability
Monolithic L1s process every transaction on-chain which limits throughput and increases gas when demand rises.
• Execution, storage, and settlement all happen on the same layer, creating a throughput ceiling.
• High demand leads to gas-price auctioning and expensive transactions for users.
• Multiple independent chains fragment liquidity and require bridges for asset movement.
Example
A popular DEX during a market swing imposes high fees which deter small traders and reduces overall volume.
Key Takeaway

Scaling and cross-chain tools are responses to structural limits in L1 design that affect cost and accessibility.

Common misconception
Treating gas spikes as purely economic ignores the underlying capacity limits. Layer 2s address structural throughput constraints.
Why it matters
User experience, cost, and the practical feasibility of high-frequency applications depend directly on whether you can reduce per-action gas and latency.
