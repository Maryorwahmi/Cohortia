Module 2 › Chapter 2.2 › Signing & Broadcasting
Sign then broadcast
Wallets sign locally with the private key and produce v, r, s. The signed, serialized transaction is then sent to a node to enter the mempool.
Step
Construct transaction fields, compute the hash, sign with private key to obtain signature components, serialize and broadcast.
Example
Confirming a MetaMask send: MetaMask signs locally and sends the signed transaction to an Ethereum node which adds it to the mempool.
Misconception
Signing does not equal finality. Signed transactions wait in mempool until mined into a block.
Why network matters
Nodes relay transactions and miners/validators select from mempools. Network congestion and gas price determine how quickly your signed transaction is included.
Key Takeaway

Signing proves authorization; broadcasting publishes it to the network. Inclusion timing depends on fees and mempool conditions.
