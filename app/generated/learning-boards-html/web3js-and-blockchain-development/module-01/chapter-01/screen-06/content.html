Module 1 › Chapter 1.1 › Centralized vs Distributed
Centralized
Single authority holds data and enforces rules.
  • Low coordination overhead
  • Faster single-writer updates
  • Single point of failure and censorship risk
Distributed Ledger
Many participants share and verify the same ledger copy.
  • Redundancy and resilience
  • Transparency and verifiability
  • Higher coordination and storage costs
Concrete example
A bank's database can be modified by the bank; a public blockchain's ledger requires network consensus and shared verification to accept changes.
Misconception
Decentralization doesn't mean no governance. It shifts decision-making and requires mechanisms to coordinate upgrades and rules among participants.
Key Takeaway

Choose centralized architectures for efficiency and control; choose distributed ledgers when verifiability and resistance to central control matter.
