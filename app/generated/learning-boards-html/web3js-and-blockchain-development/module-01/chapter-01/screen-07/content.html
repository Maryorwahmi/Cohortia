Module 1 › Chapter 1.1 › Decentralization Explained
Definition

What decentralization does

It distributes copies, decision power, and verification so the system does not depend on one operator.

Resilience
Multiple nodes mean no single outage stops the ledger.
Distributed trust
Participants rely on rules and cryptography rather than a central admin.
Censorship resistance
No single actor can unilaterally censor transactions across a well-distributed network.
Concrete scenario
If one node is taken offline by an outage, other nodes still host the ledger and process transactions following consensus rules.
Misconception
Decentralized doesn't automatically mean private or ungoverned. Privacy and governance are separate design choices layered on top of decentralization.
Key Takeaway

Decentralization shifts trust from operators to protocol rules and many independent participants, improving resilience and reducing censorship risk.
