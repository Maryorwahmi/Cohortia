Definition

DLT and blockchain

DLT replicates and synchronizes a database across participants. Blockchain is a DLT variant that groups records into cryptographically linked blocks to create an append-only chain.

Permission models
DLT can be permissioned (known participants) or permissionless (open participation); blockchain implementations vary too.
Choice matters
Trade-offs include throughput, privacy, governance, and openness.
Concrete example
An enterprise DLT might replicate the ledger among partner companies and restrict access, optimizing for performance. A public blockchain opens participation and prioritizes verifiability and censorship resistance.
Misconception
Not all DLTs use block chaining or offer the same transparency; evaluate each system's architecture and guarantees.
Key Takeaway

Treat blockchain as a specific DLT style with cryptographic chaining; inspect permission and consensus choices to understand guarantees.
