Definition

Smart Contract

A deployed program that executes when called and enforces on-chain rules and state changes.

Properties
Immutable bytecode, on-chain storage, addressable, and callable by transactions.
Example
An escrow contract that releases funds when a deliverable is confirmed.
Risk
Bugs are permanent; thorough testing and audits reduce the chance of costly errors.
Analogy & Practice

Smart contracts as dApp backends

A front-end interacts with a contract via transactions or calls, and the contract enforces the rules reliably on-chain.

Concrete scenario
A marketplace contract that transfers NFT ownership and emits events for indexing when a sale completes.
Misconception
Contracts do not guarantee correctness beyond their code. They automate rules, but the rules must be correct and secure.
Key takeaway

Treat contracts as immutable backends: plan carefully, write tests, and keep heavy computation off-chain when possible.
