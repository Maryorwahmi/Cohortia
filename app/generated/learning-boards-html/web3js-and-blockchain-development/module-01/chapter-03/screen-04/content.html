Module 1 › Chapter 1.3 › Evolution Timeline
Web1 → Web2 → Web3
Web1 — Read-only
Static pages and content publishing by site owners; users consume content and technical barriers limit who can publish.
Web2 — Read-write
Interactive platforms enable user-generated content and social features but centralize data and control in platform operators.
Web3 — Read-write-own
Users hold identity and assets via wallets; blockchains and decentralized storage provide shared, censorship-resistant primitives.
Why the layers matter
  • Blockchains store verifiable state and transactions, not large binary files.
  • Smart contracts automate rules without a trusted intermediary.
  • Decentralized storage holds heavy content while blockchains reference it via hashes.
Key takeaway
Treat Web3 as layered design: choose the correct layer for data, logic, and identity to avoid performance or cost mistakes.
