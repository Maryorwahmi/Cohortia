Digital signatures: sign and verify
How signatures prove who authorized a transaction
Signing flow
  1. Create transaction data and compute its hash or digest.
  2. Use your private key to sign that digest, producing a signature value.
  3. Broadcast the transaction with the signature attached.
  4. Nodes use your public key to verify the signature and the digest matches the transaction content.
Example: signing a transfer of 2 tokens to address X. The signature ties your private key to that exact transfer, so it cannot be changed later without invalidating the signature.
Why this matters
Signatures let decentralized networks accept transactions without a trusted intermediary because anyone can verify the signer's authority.
Misconception
Signatures do not encrypt the transaction. The transaction remains visible; the signature only vouches for origin and integrity.
Key Takeaway
A valid signature proves the signer authorized the exact data and that the data has not changed.
