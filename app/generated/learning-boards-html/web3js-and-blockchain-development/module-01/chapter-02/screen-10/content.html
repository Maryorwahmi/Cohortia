Core Concept
Non-repudiation and transaction verification
Why signatures mean a signer cannot deny a transaction
Why signatures provide evidence
Signatures are derived from a private key and the message; verification with the public key confirms both origin and content. This creates durable evidence that a particular key holder authorized the exact message at the time of signing.
Example: A signed transfer that moves assets from Alice to Bob can be presented later as proof that Alice authorized that transfer. Nodes use verification to accept and record such transactions.
Caveat
If the private key was stolen, signatures made by the thief are indistinguishable from the owner's. Non-repudiation assumes proper key custody.
Practical mitigation
Use secure wallets, offline key storage, and monitoring so you can respond quickly to suspected compromise.
Key Takeaway
Signatures are strong evidence of authorization, but they depend on private key security in practice.
