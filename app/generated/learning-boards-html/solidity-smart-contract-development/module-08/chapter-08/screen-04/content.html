Module 8 › Chapter 8.8 › IPFS Fundamentals
Definition
IPFS: content addressing and immutability

IPFS assigns a CID to content. That CID is a verifiable fingerprint of the files you uploaded. If anything changes, the fingerprint changes too.

Analogy

Think of the CID as the SHA fingerprint of a release artifact. The fingerprint guarantees integrity, like a signed release tarball.

Common misconception

IPFS does not guarantee permanence by itself; pinning is required to keep content hosted on reliable nodes.

Key Takeaway

Use IPFS for integrity and censorship resistance, and use pinning services to maintain availability for production dApps.

Example
After running your build, upload and pin the dist folder. Pinning yields a CID you can share. A user who loads the CID gets exactly the files you uploaded; any later change will produce a different CID.
How to ensure availability
  • Pin the CID with a service like Pinata to guarantee at least one stable host.
  • Consider running your own IPFS node as a long‑term pinning strategy for critical releases.
  • Update any name records (such as ENS) whenever you publish a new CID.
