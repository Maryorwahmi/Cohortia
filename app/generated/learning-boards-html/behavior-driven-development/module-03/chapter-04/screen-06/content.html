Step Design

Writing Concise and Unambiguous Steps

Each Step, a Single Logical Action

Principles of Effective Steps

  • •

    Single Action/State: Each step should represent one distinct logical operation or condition.

  • •

    Unambiguous: The meaning of the step should be clear and open to only one interpretation.

  • •

    Concise: Avoid overly long or complex sentences; get straight to the point.

Why it Matters

Clear steps improve readability for business users and simplify the creation of robust, reusable automation code.

Key Takeaway

Focus on making each Gherkin step a clear, single, and unambiguous statement of behavior to enhance understanding and maintainability.

Example: Complex vs. Refactored

Anti-Pattern: Overly Complex Step

When I log in, update my profile, and then view my dashboard

Combines multiple distinct actions.

Best Practice: Refactored Steps

When I log in with valid credentials
And I update my personal details
Then I should see my dashboard

Each step describes a single, clear action.

Common Misconception

Believing that combining actions into one step saves time. It often leads to brittle scenarios and harder-to-maintain automation.
