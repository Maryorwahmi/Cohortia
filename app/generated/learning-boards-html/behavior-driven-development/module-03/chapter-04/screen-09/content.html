Core Principle

Declarative vs. Imperative Gherkin

Describing What, Not How

Understanding the Distinction

  • •

    Declarative: Describes what the system should do or its state. Focuses on observable behavior.

  • •

    Imperative: Describes how the system achieves a result. Leaks implementation details.

Why it Matters

Declarative Gherkin is more readable for business stakeholders, more resilient to technical changes, and better reflects business intent.

Key Takeaway

Always strive for declarative Gherkin steps that focus on observable behavior, keeping implementation details out of your feature files.

Example: Declarative vs. Imperative

Best Practice: Declarative

Then the order status should be 'Confirmed'

Describes the desired outcome.

Anti-Pattern: Imperative

Then the `order_status` column in the `orders` table should be 'Confirmed'

Leaks database implementation details.

Common Misconception

Thinking that including technical details makes Gherkin more precise. It actually makes it less readable and more brittle.
