One `When` Per Scenario

Each scenario should ideally have only one primary When step to ensure it tests a single, focused behavior.

Clarity: Makes the scenario's purpose immediately clear.

Maintainability: Easier to debug and update when changes occur.

Avoid Over-testing: Prevents scenarios from becoming too complex and covering too many behaviors.

Key Takeaway

If a scenario requires multiple actions, consider combining them into a single, higher-level When or splitting the scenario.

Refining `When` Steps

Problematic
When I search for "Milk"
And I click "Add to Cart"

Two distinct actions.

Improved
When I add "Milk" to my cart

Single, high-level action.

Refining When steps ensures each scenario is focused, making it easier to understand and test.
