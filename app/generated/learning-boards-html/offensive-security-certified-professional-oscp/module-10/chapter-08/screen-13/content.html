Risks & Best Practices

Common Mistakes and Risks in EDR Evasion

Navigating the pitfalls of advanced bypass techniques.

Incorrect Syscall Numbers/Offsets

Using wrong values can lead to system crashes (BSOD) or immediate detection by the EDR.

Consequence

System instability or immediate termination of the malicious process.

Leaving Traces

Actions like loading clean DLLs, modifying memory, or injecting assembly can be detected by EDRs.

Consequence

EDR detects the evasion attempt itself, even if the final payload bypasses a hook.

Version Dependency

Syscall numbers and DLL layouts change across Windows versions/patches, reducing reliability.

Consequence

Payloads may fail or cause errors on target systems with different OS builds.

Ethical Implications

These powerful techniques must only be used in authorized penetration testing scenarios.

Consequence

Misuse can lead to severe legal and professional repercussions.

Key Takeaway

EDR evasion is a high-risk endeavor requiring meticulous planning, deep technical understanding, and strict adherence to ethical guidelines to avoid detection or system instability.
