IDS/IPS Evasion

Traffic Fragmentation

Traffic fragmentation involves splitting network packets into smaller pieces. This can make it harder for signature-based IDS/IPS to reassemble and detect malicious patterns that are spread across multiple fragments.

# Nmap fragmentation example
nmap -f -sS -p 80 192.168.1.10

# Hping3 fragmentation example
hping3 -c 1 -d 120 -p 80 --data-string "GET /evil HTTP/1.1\r
\r
" --frag 192.168.1.10
  • •`-f` (Nmap): Fragments IP packets into 8-byte chunks.
  • •`--frag` (Hping3): Specifies fragmentation.
Key Takeaway

Traffic fragmentation can be an effective technique to bypass signature-based IDS/IPS by breaking up malicious payloads, but it must be used carefully to avoid network instability.

How Fragmentation Evades Detection
Original PacketMalicious Signature Intact
Fragment 1Part of Signature
Fragment 2Another Part
Fragment 3Remaining Part

By splitting the payload, the IDS/IPS may fail to reassemble the full signature, allowing the traffic to pass.

Common Mistake

Over-fragmentation can lead to network instability or packets being dropped by firewalls/routers, making your attack unreliable or easily detectable by network anomalies.
