Key Concepts: Covert Channels & Tools

Advanced Evasion and Port Redirection Techniques

DNS Tunneling

Encapsulating non-DNS traffic within DNS queries and responses to establish a covert channel, often used to bypass egress filtering.

ICMP Tunneling

Encapsulating data within ICMP (ping) echo requests and replies to create a covert channel in highly restricted environments.

Traffic Fragmentation

Splitting network packets into smaller pieces to evade IDS/IPS that might struggle to reassemble and detect signatures across fragments.

Chisel

A fast, single-binary TCP/UDP tunnel over HTTP, secured with SSH, useful for port forwarding and reverse shells.

Socat

A versatile command-line utility for establishing bidirectional data streams, often used for complex network relays and port forwarding.

Custom Protocols & Encryption

Using unique communication methods and strong encryption to render C2 traffic unintelligible to signature-based IDS/IPS.

Summary Takeaway

DNS and ICMP tunneling offer stealthy covert channels, while traffic fragmentation and custom protocols provide IDS/IPS evasion. Tools like Chisel and Socat are indispensable for flexible port forwarding and network manipulation in advanced penetration testing scenarios.
