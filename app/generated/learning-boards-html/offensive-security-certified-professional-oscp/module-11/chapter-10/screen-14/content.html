Best Practices for Stealthy & Reliable Scripts

Avoiding common errors and enhancing operational security in post-exploitation.

Robust Error Handling

Implement try-catch (PowerShell), if-else (Bash), or try-except (Python) to make scripts resilient to unexpected conditions and prevent crashes.

Prioritize Stealth

Minimize disk writes, use in-memory execution (e.g., PowerShell IEX), and avoid obvious filenames or variable names to evade detection.

Modularity & Readability

Break down complex tasks into smaller functions or separate scripts. This improves maintainability, reusability, and makes debugging easier.

Controlled Logging

Implement your own temporary logging within scripts to track actions, then securely delete or exfiltrate these logs to avoid leaving forensic evidence.

Common Pitfall

Writing noisy scripts that generate many errors or unnecessary output can quickly alert defenders. Always redirect errors to /dev/null or handle them silently.

Key Takeaway

Adhering to best practices in error handling, stealth, modularity, and logging is crucial for developing reliable and undetectable post-exploitation scripts.
