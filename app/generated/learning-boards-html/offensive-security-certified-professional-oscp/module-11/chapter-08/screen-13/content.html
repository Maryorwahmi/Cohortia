Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Incomplete Clearing: Leaving partial logs or forensic artifacts behind.
  • Suspicious Gaps: Creating obvious breaks or deletions in log sequences.
  • Forgetting Timestamps: Not altering file timestamps, revealing true activity times.
  • Neglecting Secure Deletion: Relying on simple deletion, allowing data recovery.
  • Over-Aggressive Clearing: Deleting all logs, which is a major red flag.

Ethical Imperatives

  • Explicit Authorization: Always obtain written permission before performing anti-forensics.
  • Scope Limitations: Understand and adhere to the agreed-upon scope of the penetration test.
  • No Irreversible Harm: Avoid actions that could permanently damage systems or data.
  • Demonstrate, Don't Destroy: The goal is to show capability, not to cause actual harm or disruption.

Critical Warning

Improper anti-forensics can destroy critical system logs, hinder legitimate troubleshooting, or even render a system unusable. Always proceed with extreme caution.

Key Takeaway

Responsible anti-forensics demands meticulous attention to detail, avoiding common mistakes, and strict adherence to ethical guidelines and explicit authorization.
