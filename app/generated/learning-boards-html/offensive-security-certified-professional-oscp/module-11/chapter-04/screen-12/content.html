Operational Security

Common Mistakes and Mitigation

Avoiding Detection During Lateral Movement

Common Attacker Mistakes

  • Being Too Noisy: Brute-forcing credentials, triggering account lockouts.
  • Not Cleaning Artifacts: Leaving behind tools, dumps, or logs.
  • Incorrect Privileges: Attempting techniques without sufficient access.
  • Lack of Stealth: Using easily detectable methods or tools.
  • Ignoring Scope: Interacting with unauthorized hosts.

Best Practices for Attackers

  • Prioritize Stealth: Use known hashes (PtH) over brute-forcing.
  • Clean Up: Remove all traces of your activity.
  • Verify Privileges: Ensure you have the necessary access for a technique.
  • Stay Within Scope: Strictly adhere to authorized targets.
Key Takeaway

Effective lateral movement requires stealth, efficiency, and strict adherence to operational security to avoid detection and legal consequences.

Defensive Mitigation Strategies

  • Strong Password Policies: Complex, unique passwords.
  • Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA): Adds an extra layer of security.
  • Endpoint Detection & Response (EDR): Detects suspicious process activity.
  • Network Segmentation: Limits the blast radius of a compromise.
  • Least Privilege: Users and services only have necessary permissions.
  • Log Monitoring: Detects unusual login attempts, process creations, and network connections.
Safety Note

Lateral movement can quickly spread compromise. Always ensure explicit authorization for *every* host you interact with. Unauthorized actions have severe legal and operational consequences.
