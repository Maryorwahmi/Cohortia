Core Concept

Understanding Rootkits

Concealing presence at the kernel and user levels.

What is a Rootkit?

  • Concealment: Hides processes, files, network connections, and system data.
  • Privileged Access: Enables continued, often privileged, access to a system.
  • Evasion: Designed to evade detection by standard OS utilities.
  • Collection of Tools: Not a single piece of software, but a suite of components.
Key Takeaway

Rootkits are advanced tools that provide deep concealment by manipulating OS functions, making them extremely difficult to detect and remove.

Types of Rootkits

Kernel-mode Rootkits
  • Modify OS kernel functions directly.
  • Highest level of stealth and control.
  • High risk of system instability.
User-mode Rootkits
  • Modify libraries or application behavior.
  • Less powerful than kernel-mode.
  • Easier to implement and less risky.

Detection Tools

Specialized tools like chkrootkit and rkhunter are designed to detect the presence of rootkits by looking for common indicators of compromise and kernel modifications.
