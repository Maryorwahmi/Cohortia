Introducing 'Potato' Attacks

Leveraging NTLM and Impersonation for SYSTEM access

What are 'Potato' Attacks?

A family of privilege escalation techniques that exploit the SeImpersonatePrivilege (or SeAssignPrimaryTokenPrivilege) and weaknesses in the Windows NTLM authentication process.

  • Target: Low-privileged service accounts (e.g., `LocalService`, `NetworkService`).
  • Mechanism: Trick a local service into authenticating to an attacker-controlled NTLM listener.
  • Outcome: Obtain a `SYSTEM` token to launch processes with highest privileges.
Common Misconception

'Potato' attacks do not steal NTLM hashes for remote relay; they leverage *local* NTLM reflection/relay.

Key Components of a 'Potato' Attack

Service Account

SeImpersonatePrivilege

  • Allows impersonation
  • Often held by services

NTLM Reflection/Relay

  • Local authentication abuse
  • No hash leaves machine
SYSTEM Privileges
Key Takeaway

'Potato' attacks exploit SeImpersonatePrivilege and NTLM reflection to gain SYSTEM privileges from service accounts.
