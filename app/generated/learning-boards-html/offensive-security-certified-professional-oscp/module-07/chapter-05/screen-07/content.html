Vulnerability Identification

Spotting Vulnerable Cron Jobs

What to Look For in Crontab Entries

Why These Factors?

These three elements directly influence the potential for privilege escalation. A job running as root, executing a modifiable script, and running frequently is a prime target.

Common Pitfall

Only focusing on the 'root' user. Even if a job runs as a non-root user, if its script is writable by another low-privileged user, it could still be part of a larger attack chain.

Key Takeaway

A truly vulnerable cron job combines privileged execution, a modifiable script/environment, and often, high frequency.

Key Indicators for Exploitation

1. User Executing the Job

Prioritize jobs running as root or other privileged users. This determines the level of access you can gain.

2. Script/Command Being Executed

Examine the full path. Look for writable scripts or commands called without full paths (e.g., rm instead of /bin/rm).

3. Frequency of Execution

More frequent jobs (e.g., every minute) allow for quicker exploitation and testing of payloads.
