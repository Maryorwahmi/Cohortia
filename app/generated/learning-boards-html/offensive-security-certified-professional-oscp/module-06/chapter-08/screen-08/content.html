Physical Security Threat

Tailgating: The Art of Following Through

Gaining unauthorized physical access by exploiting human politeness

What is Tailgating?

An unauthorized person follows an authorized person into a restricted area, often by exploiting politeness or creating a distraction. Also known as piggybacking.

Common Methods

  • Holding a door open for someone.
  • Creating a distraction (e.g., dropping items).
  • Impersonating a delivery person or contractor.
  • Leveraging perceived authority (e.g., wearing a fake uniform).

Example: The 'Struggling' Delivery

An attacker, dressed as a delivery person and carrying a large, cumbersome box, approaches a secured entrance. An employee, seeing them struggle, kindly holds the door open, allowing the attacker to bypass the access control system and enter the building.

Exploiting Social Norms

Common Mistake

Underestimating the human factor in physical security. Technical controls are ineffective if employees don't follow protocols or challenge unknown individuals.

Key Takeaway

Physical security requires active human vigilance; employees must be empowered to challenge unknown individuals, even if it feels uncomfortable.
