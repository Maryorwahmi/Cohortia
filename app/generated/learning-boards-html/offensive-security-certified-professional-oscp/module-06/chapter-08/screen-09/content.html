Psychological Triggers

The Science of Influence: Part 1

Leveraging authority, scarcity, and urgency in social engineering

1. Authority

People are more likely to comply with requests from perceived authority figures (e.g., CEO, IT administrator). Attackers impersonate these roles.

2. Scarcity/Urgency

Creating a sense of limited availability or immediate need pressures victims into hasty decisions, bypassing rational thought.

Example: 'Urgent CEO Request'

An employee receives an email seemingly from the CEO, demanding an immediate wire transfer for a 'confidential acquisition.' The email emphasizes urgency and the CEO's authority, pressuring the employee to act without verification.

Exploiting Power Dynamics

Common Mistake

Assuming that logical thinking will always override psychological pressure. Under stress or perceived authority, individuals can make irrational decisions.

Key Takeaway

Recognizing authority, scarcity, and urgency as manipulation tactics is crucial for resisting social engineering attempts.

Pro-Tip: Question Everything

Develop a habit of questioning requests, especially those that create urgency or come from high-ranking individuals, and always verify through a secondary channel.
