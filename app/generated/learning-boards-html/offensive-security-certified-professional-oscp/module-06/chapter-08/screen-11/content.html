Defense Strategy

Countermeasures: Awareness & MFA

Building human and technical resilience against social engineering

Security Awareness Programs

Go beyond basic phishing training to include scenarios for pretexting, baiting, and physical security. Regular, interactive training is key.

Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

A critical technical control that requires more than one method of authentication. Even if credentials are stolen, MFA prevents impersonation.

Example: Simulated Phishing

An organization conducts a simulated phishing campaign. Employees who click the malicious link are immediately directed to a training module, reinforcing lessons on identifying and reporting suspicious emails.

Reinforcing Vigilance

Common Mistake

Treating security awareness as a one-off event. Training must be continuous, updated regularly, and include practical exercises to be effective against evolving threats.

Key Takeaway

A robust defense against social engineering combines ongoing human education with essential technical controls like MFA.

Pro-Tip: Phishing-Resistant MFA

Implement FIDO2/WebAuthn hardware security keys. These are phishing-resistant as they cryptographically bind authentication to the legitimate website's origin, preventing real-time phishing proxy attacks.
