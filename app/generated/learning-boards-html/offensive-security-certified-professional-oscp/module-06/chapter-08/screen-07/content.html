Indirect Attack

Water Holing: Compromising Trusted Sites

Targeting specific user groups through their frequented websites

What is Water Holing?

A highly targeted attack where an attacker compromises a website frequently visited by a specific group of users and plants malware there.

How it Works

  • Identify target group's common online destinations.
  • Compromise a legitimate website they trust.
  • Inject malicious code or malware.
  • Infect targets when they visit the compromised site.

Example: Industry Forum Compromise

A cybersecurity firm's employees frequently visit a popular industry forum. An attacker compromises this forum, injecting a zero-day exploit. When employees visit the forum, their systems are silently infected, granting the attacker a foothold.

Exploiting Trust in Familiar Sites

Common Mistake

Believing that only unknown or suspicious websites pose a threat. Legitimate, trusted sites can be compromised and used as attack vectors.

Key Takeaway

Water holing leverages trust in familiar websites; robust endpoint protection and network monitoring are essential for defense.

Pro-Tip: Browser Security

Keep browsers and plugins updated, use ad-blockers, and consider browser isolation technologies to mitigate risks from compromised websites.
