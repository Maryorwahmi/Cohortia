Psychological Triggers

The Science of Influence: Part 2

Leveraging liking, social proof, commitment, and reciprocity

3. Liking

People are more likely to agree to requests from individuals they like or find appealing. Attackers build rapport to gain trust.

4. Social Proof

People look to the actions of others to determine their own. Attackers claim 'everyone else is doing it' to encourage compliance.

5. Commitment/Consistency

Once someone commits to a small request, they are more likely to agree to larger, subsequent requests to maintain consistency.

6. Reciprocity

People feel obligated to return favors. Attackers offer a small 'help' before asking for a significant favor.

Example: The 'Helpful' Colleague

A new 'colleague' (attacker) offers to help an employee with a complex task. After gaining trust and a small favor, the 'colleague' then asks for 'temporary' access to a system, leveraging liking and reciprocity.

Exploiting Social Bonds
Key Takeaway

Awareness of these subtle social dynamics is crucial, as they can bypass rational defenses and lead to compliance.
