Advanced Tactics

Baiting and Quid Pro Quo

Tempting victims with lures and exchanges

Baiting Defined

Involves tempting victims with a physical lure (e.g., a malicious USB drive) to trigger an attack when interacted with. Exploits curiosity.

Quid Pro Quo Defined

An attacker offers a service or benefit (e.g., 'free tech assistance') in exchange for information or access, exploiting helpfulness or desire for convenience.

Example: The 'Lost' USB Drive

An attacker leaves a USB drive labeled 'Confidential Q4 Financials' in a company parking lot. An employee finds it, plugs it into their work computer, and unknowingly executes malware, establishing a reverse shell.

Curiosity as a Weapon

Example: 'Free' Tech Support

An attacker calls random employees, claiming to be from 'IT Support,' offering to fix a 'critical system error.' They then ask for login credentials to 'diagnose' the issue, leveraging the desire for convenience and perceived authority.

Exploiting Helpfulness
Key Takeaway

Be wary of unsolicited physical items or offers of 'help' that require sensitive information; they are common social engineering lures.
