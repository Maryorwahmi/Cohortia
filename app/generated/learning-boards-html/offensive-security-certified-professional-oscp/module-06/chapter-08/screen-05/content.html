Pretexting: The Fabricated Scenario

Crafting believable stories to extract sensitive information

What is Pretexting?

An attacker creates a fabricated scenario (a 'pretext') to engage a target and extract confidential information or prompt an action.

Key Characteristics

  • Well-researched and plausible story.
  • Often involves impersonating a trusted entity (e.g., IT support, HR).
  • Aims to create a sense of urgency or importance.

Example: The Urgent IT Call

An attacker calls an employee, claiming to be from IT support, stating there's an urgent system issue requiring their login credentials immediately to prevent data loss. The attacker might even know the employee's name and department.

Exploiting Authority & Urgency

Common Mistake

Failing to independently verify the identity of the caller or sender. Always use official, known contact channels to confirm requests for sensitive information.

Key Takeaway

Pretexting thrives on trust and urgency; always verify identities through official channels before sharing sensitive data.

Pro-Tip: Verification Protocol

Establish a 'call-back' policy: if someone requests sensitive information, hang up and call them back on a publicly listed or internally verified phone number, not one they provide.
