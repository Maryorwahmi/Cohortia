Impersonation: The Next Step

Leveraging Stolen Credentials for Deeper Access

What is Impersonation?

Impersonation is the act of assuming the identity of another person, typically by using stolen credentials, to gain unauthorized access or deceive others. It transforms harvested data into active access.

Why it Matters

Impersonation provides a critical foothold for lateral movement within a network. It bypasses perimeter defenses and allows an attacker to operate from within, often with elevated privileges.

Beyond Simple Access

It's not just about logging in; it's about accessing sensitive documents, internal communications, and potentially discovering even more credentials, expanding the attack surface significantly.

Concrete Example

An attacker harvests an employee's Microsoft 365 credentials. By impersonating the employee, they gain access to their email, OneDrive, and SharePoint, potentially discovering sensitive company data or further attack vectors.

Key Takeaway

Impersonation is the powerful follow-up to credential harvesting. It converts stolen data into active, unauthorized access, enabling deeper penetration and lateral movement within a target environment.
