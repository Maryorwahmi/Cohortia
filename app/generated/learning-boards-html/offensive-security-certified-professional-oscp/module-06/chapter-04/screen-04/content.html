Core Concept

What is Phishing?

Phishing is a social engineering technique where attackers impersonate a trustworthy entity to deceptively acquire sensitive information like usernames, passwords, and credit card details through electronic communication.

  • Often takes the form of email, but can also be SMS (smishing) or voice calls (vishing).
  • Relies on human psychology, exploiting trust, urgency, fear, or curiosity.
  • A consistently effective initial access vector for cybercriminals.
Common Misconception

Phishing is not just about email. It encompasses any deceptive electronic communication designed to trick users, including text messages (smishing) and voice calls (vishing).

How it Works: The Psychological Angle
Exploits Trust

Masquerades as a known entity.

Creates Urgency

Demands immediate action.

Induces Fear

Threatens negative consequences.

Piques Curiosity

Offers intriguing but false information.

Key Takeaway

Phishing exploits human psychology to trick victims into revealing sensitive data, making it a persistent and effective initial access method for attackers.
