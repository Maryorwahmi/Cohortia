Common Attacker Mistakes

  • Generic Payloads: Using easily detectable malware or scripts that are quickly flagged by security tools.
  • Unsophisticated Phishing: Employing generic phishing templates that lack convincing pretexts and are easily recognized by users.
  • Lack of Reconnaissance: Failing to research the target's security stack, leading to ineffective evasion attempts.
  • Failure to Adapt: Not evolving attack methods when initial attempts are detected or blocked.
Key Takeaway

Successful evasion requires deep reconnaissance and continuous adaptation, while ethical penetration testing demands strict adherence to scope and non-destructive methods.

Safety Notes for Penetration Testers

  • Non-Destructive Testing: Always ensure your evasion techniques are non-destructive and do not cause actual damage.
  • Adhere to Scope: Strictly operate within the agreed-upon scope of the engagement to avoid legal and ethical issues.
  • No Persistent Threats: Do not create persistent backdoors or leave behind malicious artifacts.
  • Clear Communication: Document and communicate all findings and methods clearly to the client.

Example: Smart Evasion vs. Generic

A generic phishing email with a known malware attachment is easily blocked. A targeted spear-phishing email with a custom, obfuscated payload delivered via a trusted cloud service, after reconnaissance of the target's security stack, is far more likely to bypass defenses.
