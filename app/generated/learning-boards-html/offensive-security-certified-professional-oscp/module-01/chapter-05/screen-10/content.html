Port Management

Understanding Network Port Classifications

Well-known, Registered, and Dynamic Ports

Key Takeaway

Ports are logical endpoints categorized into well-known, registered, and dynamic ranges, guiding the identification of services and potential vulnerabilities.

Why it Matters for Pentesting

Knowing port categories helps you quickly infer the likely service running on a port, even if it's not a standard well-known port, aiding in targeted scanning.

Pro Tip

Always perform service version detection on open ports, as services can run on non-standard ports, and the port number alone isn't definitive proof of the service.

Network Port Categories

Well-known Ports (0-1023)

Reserved for common, universally recognized services. Examples: HTTP (80), HTTPS (443), SSH (22), FTP (20, 21), DNS (53).

Registered Ports (1024-49151)

Assigned by IANA to specific applications or services. Often used by custom applications or less common protocols.

Dynamic/Private Ports (49152-65535)

Used for client-side connections when initiating communication. These are typically ephemeral and short-lived.
