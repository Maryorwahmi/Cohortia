Interpreting Nmap Results and Lab Safety

Understanding Attack Vectors and Maintaining a Secure Lab

Interpreting Nmap Output

  • Reveals open ports (e.g., 21/FTP, 22/SSH, 80/HTTP, 3306/MySQL).
  • Identifies running services and their versions.
  • Each open port/service is a potential attack vector.
  • Guides your next steps in vulnerability research and exploitation.

Critical Lab Safety Measures

  • Network Isolation: Keep VMs on Host-Only/Internal networks.
  • No External Access: Never connect vulnerable VMs to untrusted networks.
  • Snapshots: Take snapshots before exploits to easily revert to a clean state.
  • Controlled Environment: Ensure all activities remain within your isolated lab.

Example: FTP Service

If `nmap` reveals an open FTP port (21) running an old version, you might research known vulnerabilities for that specific version. This could lead to finding exploits for anonymous login, directory traversal, or even remote code execution.

Key Takeaway

Nmap results are your roadmap for exploitation, but always pair analysis with rigorous safety practices like network isolation and VM snapshots.

Pro Tip

Name your snapshots clearly (e.g., "Metasploitable2 Clean") so you can easily revert to a known good state after destructive testing.
