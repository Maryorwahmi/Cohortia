Security Implications

High Impact on Domain Security

PtH and PtT attacks can lead to complete domain compromise, allowing attackers to impersonate any user, access any resource, and maintain long-term persistence. This highlights the critical importance of protecting credential artifacts.

Key Protection Targets

NTLM Hashes & Kerberos Tickets

Defensive efforts must focus on preventing the theft of NTLM hashes (especially `krbtgt` and service account hashes) and Kerberos tickets. Implementing strong credential hygiene is paramount.

Ethical & Legal Considerations

Authorized Use Only

These powerful techniques must only be used in authorized lab environments or during legitimate penetration tests with explicit permission. Misuse carries severe legal and ethical consequences.

Key Takeaway

Understanding PtH/PtT is vital for both offensive and defensive security, emphasizing the need for robust credential protection and responsible, authorized use of these powerful techniques.

Defensive Measures Against PtH/PtT
Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)Significantly reduces the risk of credential theft leading to compromise.
Least Privilege PrincipleLimit user and service account privileges to only what is necessary.
Regular `krbtgt` Key RotationPeriodically change the `krbtgt` account password to invalidate old Golden Tickets.
Endpoint Detection & Response (EDR)Monitor for suspicious activity indicative of PtH/PtT attacks.
