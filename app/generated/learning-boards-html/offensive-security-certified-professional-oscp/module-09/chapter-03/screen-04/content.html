Core Concept

Initial Access & Credential Dumping Overview

The foundational steps in an Active Directory penetration test

The Attack Flow

1

Reconnaissance & Enumeration: Gather information about the target environment.

2

Initial Access: Gain a first foothold on a domain-joined machine.

3

Credential Dumping: Extract sensitive authentication data from the compromised host.

4

Lateral Movement & Escalation: Use acquired credentials to move deeper or gain higher privileges.

Key Takeaway

Initial access and credential dumping are interdependent phases, with success in one directly enabling the next stage of compromise.

Why These Phases Matter

  • •Foundation for Further Attacks: Without initial access, advanced attacks like lateral movement or privilege escalation are impossible.
  • •High-Value Targets: Credentials are often the most valuable assets an attacker can obtain, unlocking access to critical systems.
  • •Real-World Relevance: These techniques are consistently observed in real-world breaches and advanced persistent threats (APTs).
  • •Defense in Depth: Understanding these attack vectors is crucial for designing effective defensive strategies and incident response plans.
Scenario Example

An attacker gains initial access to a workstation via a phishing email. They then dump credentials from that workstation to find a domain administrator's hash, enabling them to compromise the entire domain.
