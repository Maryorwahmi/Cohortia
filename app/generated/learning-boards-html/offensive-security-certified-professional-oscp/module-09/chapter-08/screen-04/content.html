Understanding Persistence and Defense Evasion

Maintaining access and operating stealthily after initial compromise.

Definition: Persistence

The ability for an attacker to maintain access to a compromised system or network, even after reboots, password changes, or other security measures.

Definition: Defense Evasion

Techniques used by attackers to avoid detection by security controls, monitoring systems, and incident responders.

Why it Matters: Sustained Control

Initial access is often temporary. Persistence ensures long-term control, allowing attackers to achieve their objectives without repeatedly compromising the target. This is crucial for thorough reconnaissance, data exfiltration, and further lateral movement.

Real-World Impact: Undetected Operations

Effective defense evasion allows attackers to operate within a network for extended periods, sometimes months or even years, without being discovered. This 'dwell time' enables them to gather vast amounts of sensitive information and cause significant damage before remediation.

Key Takeaway

Persistence and defense evasion are interdependent strategies for achieving and maintaining covert, long-term access in compromised environments.
