The SEH Overwrite Chain

This technique involves a precise sequence of overwrites and jumps to redirect execution.

  • •Overwrite SEH with a POP POP RET gadget address.
  • •POP POP RET adjusts ESP to point to nSEH.
  • •Overwrite nSEH with a short jump (e.g., \xeb\x06\x90\x90).
  • •Short jump lands on NOP sled, leading to shellcode.
Concrete Example

After a crash, the program attempts to execute the overwritten SEH pointer, which is now the POP POP RET gadget. This gadget then manipulates the stack to point to the attacker's short jump.

Key Takeaway

The POP POP RET gadget is fundamental in SEH overwrites, precisely positioning the stack pointer to execute a short jump into the attacker's shellcode.

SEH Overwrite Payload Structure

# Example payload structure for SEH overwrite
[Junk] # Fill buffer up to nSEH
+ [nSEH (short jump)] # e.g., eb069090 (jump over SEH)
+ [SEH (POP POP RET address)] # Address of a suitable gadget
+ [NOPs] # NOP sled for reliability
+ [Shellcode] # Your malicious payload

This structure ensures that when an exception occurs, execution is redirected through the gadget and into the shellcode.
