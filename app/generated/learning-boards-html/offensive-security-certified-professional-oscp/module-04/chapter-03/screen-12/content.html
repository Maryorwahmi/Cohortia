Padding: The Foundation of Precise Control

Understanding how filler bytes ensure EIP lands correctly.

  • •Padding consists of filler bytes (e.g., 'A's) sent before the EIP overwrite.
  • •Its role is to fill the buffer precisely up to the return address location.
  • •Incorrect padding leads to misaligned EIP overwrite and failed exploits.
  • •Bytes after EIP fill the stack, often visible in ESP.
Key Takeaway

Padding is critical for ensuring that the attacker-controlled EIP value lands at the exact memory address required to redirect program execution reliably.

Misconception

Padding is not just random filler; it's a precisely calculated amount of bytes to align the EIP overwrite correctly. Too much or too little will cause the exploit to fail.
