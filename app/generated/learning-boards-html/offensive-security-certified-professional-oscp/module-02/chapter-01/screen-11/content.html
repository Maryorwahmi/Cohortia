Systematic Documentation

Organizing Your Reconnaissance Findings

A common mistake beginners make is not systematically documenting their findings. Reconnaissance generates a vast amount of data, and without proper organization, you'll quickly get lost, making it difficult to recall specific details later in the engagement.

It's crucial to establish a consistent methodology for saving all output from your tools, taking detailed notes, and organizing this information logically. Consider using tools like CherryTree, Obsidian, OneNote, or even simple markdown files stored in a structured directory.

For each target, create a dedicated folder and within that, subfolders for different types of information, such as `osint`, `network_scans`, `web_app_recon`, or `credentials`. Within these subfolders, save the raw output from tools like `whois`, `dig`, `nmap`, and web crawlers. Add your own observations, hypotheses, and potential attack vectors.

This disciplined approach will save you immense time and frustration later in the engagement, especially when you need to refer back to a specific piece of information or correlate findings from different sources. Proper documentation is not just about tidiness; it's a critical component of effective penetration testing.
