Passive vs. Active

Passive Information Gathering

Observing from a Distance

Reconnaissance can be broadly categorized into two main types: passive and active. Let's first explore passive information gathering. This method involves collecting data about a target without directly interacting with their systems or network.

You're essentially observing from afar, leveraging publicly available information, and crucially, leaving no discernible trace on the target's logs or network infrastructure. Think of it like listening to conversations from across a room or reading public records and news articles.

The primary advantage of passive reconnaissance is its stealth; the target is highly unlikely to know they are being investigated. This allows you to gather intelligence without raising alarms or triggering security defenses.

However, the information obtained through passive means might sometimes be less precise, potentially outdated, or incomplete compared to direct interaction. Common sources for passive reconnaissance include general search engines like Google, social media platforms, financial reports, news archives, and public domain registration records. These sources provide a wealth of information that can be pieced together to form an initial profile of the target.
