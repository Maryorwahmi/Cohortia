Passive vs. Active

Active Information Gathering

Direct Interaction with the Target

In contrast to passive reconnaissance, active information gathering involves direct interaction with the target system. This means your actions will touch the target's infrastructure, potentially leaving traces that could alert them to your presence.

Examples range from simple network probes like sending `ping` requests to more intrusive activities such as port scanning, vulnerability probing, or interacting with web applications.

While active reconnaissance can yield more accurate, detailed, and up-to-date information than passive methods, it carries a significant risk of detection. Your activities might be logged by firewalls, detected by Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS), or simply appear as unusual traffic patterns in server logs, alerting the target to your investigation.

In a real-world penetration test, active reconnaissance should only be performed after careful planning, explicit authorization, and a thorough understanding of the potential consequences. Unauthorized scanning is often perceived as a hostile act and can have serious legal repercussions. For OSCP labs, you'll often be permitted to perform active scanning within controlled environments, but it's crucial to cultivate a mindset of caution, stealth, and respect for the target's environment, even when authorized.
