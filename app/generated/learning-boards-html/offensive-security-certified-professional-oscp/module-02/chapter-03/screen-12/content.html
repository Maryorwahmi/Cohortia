UDP Scan (-sU)

Used to scan for open UDP ports, which host services like DNS, SNMP, and DHCP.

Challenges
  • Slower and less reliable due to UDP's connectionless nature.
  • No response often means open/filtered, not definitively open.
Command: nmap -sU 192.168.1.100
PORT     STATE         SERVICE 53/udp   open|filtered domain 161/udp  open|filtered snmp

Specifying Ports (-p)

By default, Nmap scans the 1000 most common ports. The -p flag allows you to customize this.

Examples
  • -p 1-1024: Scan ports 1 to 1024.
  • -p 21,22,80,443: Scan specific ports.
  • -p-: Scan all 65535 ports (noisy, time-consuming).
Command: nmap -sS -p 21,80 192.168.1.100
PORT   STATE SERVICE 21/tcp open  ftp 80/tcp open  http
Key Takeaway

UDP scans are slower and less definitive, while the -p flag offers granular control over which ports Nmap targets.
