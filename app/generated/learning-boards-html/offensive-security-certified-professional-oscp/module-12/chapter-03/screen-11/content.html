Precision Over Speculation: Avoiding Common Reporting Pitfalls

Ensuring your impact statements are specific, factual, and evidence-backed.

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

Exaggeration

Claiming full domain compromise from a low-privilege shell without proof.

Vagueness

Using phrases like "This could be bad" or "Might lead to issues" instead of specific consequences.

Key Takeaway

Always tie your impact statements directly to your demonstrated Proof of Concept, ensuring they are specific, factual, and verifiable.

Best Practices for Impact Statements

Ensure your impact is specific, factual, and directly supported by evidence.

Specificity

Clearly state the exact consequences, e.g., "Unauthorized remote code execution with SYSTEM privileges."

Factual Basis

Only report what you have proven. If data wasn't exfiltrated, don't claim data theft.

Evidence-Backed

Support all claims with concrete evidence like screenshots, command outputs, or logs.

Example

A screenshot of cat /etc/shadow combined with successful hash cracking demonstrates information disclosure and potential account compromise.
