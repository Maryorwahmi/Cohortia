How integrity is protected
  • Hashing and checksums to detect unexpected changes.
  • Digital signatures and certificates to confirm origin.
  • Version control and change management to record authorized edits.
  • Immutable logs for reliable forensic data.
Misconception: encryption alone ensures integrity. Encryption hides data, but signatures or hashes are needed to detect modification.
Concrete example
Verifying a downloaded installer
Compare the publisher's provided checksum with the calculated checksum before running installers to avoid tampered or malicious packages.
Support checklist
  1. Preserve evidence and logs.
  2. Check hashes and signatures.
  3. Restore from trusted backups if integrity is broken.
Key Takeaway

Integrity means you can trust data; protect it with detection and controlled changes.
