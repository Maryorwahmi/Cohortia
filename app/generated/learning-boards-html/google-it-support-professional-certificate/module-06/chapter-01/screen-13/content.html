Module 6 › Chapter 6.1 › Social Engineering
Common techniques
  • Phishing: fraudulent bulk messages requesting credentials.
  • Spear phishing and whaling: targeted, personalized attacks.
  • Pretexting and baiting: fabricated scenarios or tempting offers.
  • Tailgating: physical access gained by following someone in.
Defense habits
Verify requests out-of-band, scrutinize sender domains, hover over links to inspect targets, and report suspicious messages promptly.
Concrete scenario
CEO impersonation for wire transfer
Treat such requests with suspicion: verify via a known phone number, consult finance policy, and document the verification before acting.
Common mistake
Acting immediately under pressure. Attackers use urgency to block verification—pause and verify.
Key Takeaway

Human verification steps and a culture of reporting are the most reliable defenses against social engineering.
