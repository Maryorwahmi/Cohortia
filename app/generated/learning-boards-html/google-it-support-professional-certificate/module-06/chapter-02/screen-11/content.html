Module 6 › Chapter 6.2 › Network Segmentation: VLANs & DMZs
Definition
Why segmentation matters
Divide networks so compromises are contained and security policies apply to distinct groups of devices.
VLANs
Logical network separation on switches to group devices and apply different policies.
DMZ
A segment for public-facing services isolated from the internal network.
Concrete example
Guest Wi‑Fi devices on VLAN 100, internal workstations on VLAN 10, and web servers in DMZ VLAN 200—firewall rules prevent VLAN 100 from reaching VLAN 10.
Verification & pitfalls
  • Label VLANs and document purpose so policies remain clear during changes.
  • Verify routing and access rules; logical separation requires correct firewalling between VLANs.
  • Remember segmentation reduces blast radius but does not replace strong authentication or monitoring.
Key Takeaway

Plan VLANs and DMZs to match function and trust levels—proper firewalling between them enforces real isolation.
