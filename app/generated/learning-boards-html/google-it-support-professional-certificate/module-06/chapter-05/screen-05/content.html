Policy Hierarchy
A top-down structure from general security goals to specific, verifiable procedures helps maintain consistency and accountability.
Top-level Security Policy
States the organization's commitment to protect information assets and assigns overall ownership.
Child Policies
Password Policy, Acceptable Use Policy, Data Handling Policy, Clean Desk, Remote Work.
Operational Procedures
Concrete steps and system settings that enforce policies, plus verification tasks for support staff.
Policy Map — Practical Template
Use this small template to make policies verifiable: Parent Policy → Child Policy → Owner → Verification Step.
Example 1
Parent: Security Policy. Child: Password Policy. Owner: IT Security. Verification: System requires 12+ character passwords and prevents reuse of last 5.
Example 2
Parent: Security Policy. Child: Remote Work Policy. Owner: IT Operations. Verification: Remote devices have enforced disk encryption and VPN configured.
Common Mistake
Not assigning owners or verification steps, which makes policies unenforced and ineffective.
Check Your Work
Map two policies from your workplace and identify one verification action you can perform in 10 minutes.
Key Takeaway

Link policies to owners and verification steps so support teams can measure and enforce security consistently.
