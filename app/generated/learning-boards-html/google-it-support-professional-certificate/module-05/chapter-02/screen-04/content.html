Module 5 › Chapter 5.2 › What is a Data Center?
Definition

Data center as a controlled facility

A facility engineered to keep servers and related systems running with high availability through managed power, cooling, and security.

Metaphor: Hospital operating room
Like an operating room, conditions are tightly controlled and backup systems are in place to protect critical processes.
Concrete example
A customer database hosted in a data center with redundant UPS and generator backup remains reachable during short power interruptions.
Common misconception
A data center is more than racks; it's a coordinated set of facility systems and operational practices designed for continuous availability.
Components Overview
Power Systems
UPS, generators, and PDUs provide layered power delivery for resilience.
Cooling
CRAC/CRAH units and aisle containment maintain optimal temperatures.
Security
Physical controls and monitoring prevent unauthorized access and detect hazards.
Operations
Asset tracking, change control, and capacity planning support reliable service delivery.
Key Takeaway

A data center is an engineered facility where power, cooling, security, and operations work together to ensure continuous IT service availability.
