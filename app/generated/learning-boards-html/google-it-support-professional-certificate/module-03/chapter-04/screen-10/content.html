Module 4 › Chapter 3.4 › tracert / traceroute — Find where the path breaks
Example output (illustrative)
1 <1 ms <1 ms <1 ms 192.168.1.1 2 10 ms 11 ms 12 ms 203.0.113.1 3 * * * Request timed out 4 * * * Request timed out
Interpretation
If initial hops succeed and later hops time out, the break is beyond your local network. Timeouts may be due to filtering or router behavior — validate with other tests.
Scenario
Tracert to a remote site shows responses through your ISP then consistent timeouts. This suggests a routing or filtering problem beyond the ISP's local network and is appropriate to report to the ISP with the tracert log.
Misconception
A single hop timeout is not definitive; upstream routers can drop traceroute probes while allowing other traffic. Look for consistent failures further along the path.
Key Takeaway

Tracert localizes failures along the path. Use it to gather targeted evidence for escalation when the break lies outside the local network.
