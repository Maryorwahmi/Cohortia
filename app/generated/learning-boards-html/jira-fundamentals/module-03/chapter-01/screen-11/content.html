Avoiding Workflow Complexity

The Dangers of Too Many Statuses and Transitions

Why Simplicity Matters
User Confusion

Too many options overwhelm users, leading to incorrect usage.

Increased Overhead

More statuses and transitions mean more configuration and maintenance.

Difficult Maintenance

Complex workflows are harder to adapt and troubleshoot.

Best Practice: Meaningful Stages

Each status should represent a truly distinct and meaningful stage in the issue's lifecycle, not just a minor sub-step. If a status doesn't change ownership or significantly alter the issue's state, reconsider its necessity.

Example: Over-Complication

Instead of 'In Progress - Coding', 'In Progress - Testing', 'In Progress - Documentation', aim for a single 'In Progress' status, with sub-tasks or checklists for granular steps.

Key Takeaway

Strive for simplicity and clarity in workflow design; complex workflows lead to user frustration and increased administrative burden.
