Audience First: Tailoring Dashboards for Impact

Design dashboards that cater to specific user needs and roles.

Why Audience Matters
  • •Relevance: Ensures data is meaningful to the viewer.
  • •Clarity: Avoids clutter by focusing on essential information.
  • •Actionability: Helps users make informed decisions quickly.
  • •Engagement: A well-designed dashboard is more likely to be used.
Key Takeaway

Effective dashboard design starts with understanding your audience's needs, ensuring the displayed information is relevant, clear, and actionable for their specific role.

Dashboard Examples by Audience

Executive Dashboard

Focus: High-level progress, risks, strategic overview.

Gadgets: Burndown Charts, Version Reports, Critical Blockers (Filter Results).

Team Dashboard

Focus: Current sprint work, individual assignments, immediate bottlenecks.

Gadgets: My Open Issues (Filter Results), Sprint Health, Two Dimensional Filter Statistics (Assignee vs. Status).

Common Mistake

Creating a generic dashboard for everyone. This often leads to information overload for some users and a lack of specific insights for others.
