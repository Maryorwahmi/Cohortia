Designing Effective Workflows

Crafting Purposeful Statuses and Transitions

Reflect Team Process

Workflows should accurately mirror how your team works, avoiding unnecessary steps that complicate the process.

Clear Purpose for Transitions

Each status and transition should have a distinct purpose, potentially with conditions or validators to enforce process integrity.

Key Takeaway

Design workflows that are streamlined and purposeful, reflecting your team's actual process to enhance efficiency and reduce confusion.

Workflow Elements

  • Statuses: Represent distinct stages (e.g., To Do, In Progress, In Review, Done).
  • Transitions: Pathways between statuses (e.g., 'Start Work', 'Send for Review').
  • Conditions: Rules that must be met to perform a transition (e.g., 'Assignee must be set').
  • Validators: Checks that ensure data integrity before a transition (e.g., 'All sub-tasks are complete').
Common Mistake

Overly complex workflows with too many statuses or unclear transitions lead to 'status bloat' and user frustration.

Example Scenario

A 'Ready for QA' status is added between 'In Progress' and 'In Review' to clearly define a handoff point, with a validator ensuring code is committed before moving.
