Issue Linking: Explicit Connections

Establishing Relationships Between Any Two Issues

Peer-to-Peer Connections

Issue linking connects any two issues, regardless of their type or hierarchy. This allows for flexible relationship mapping across your project.

Contextual Relationships

Use links to show dependencies, related work, or historical connections, providing crucial context for each issue. This helps teams understand the 'why' behind their tasks.

Improved Decision Making

By visualizing connections, project managers and team members can make more informed decisions, identify risks, and prioritize work effectively.

Concrete Example

Linking a Bug to its Feature

A 'Bug: Login button unresponsive' might be linked to the 'Story: Implement User Login'. This clearly shows which feature is affected by the bug, helping developers prioritize and understand the impact of their fix.

Key Takeaway

Issue linking is a versatile tool for mapping the intricate web of relationships within your project, providing essential context for every work item.

Misconception Alert

Not a Hierarchy

A common misconception is that linking creates a hierarchy. While it shows relationships, it doesn't inherently imply a parent-child structure like sub-tasks or Epics. Links are more about peer-to-peer connections.
