Chaining Attacks for Privilege Escalation

Combining techniques to achieve domain dominance and implementing effective mitigation strategies.

Attack Paths: Privilege escalation often involves chaining multiple attacks (e.g., Kerberoasting → PtH → Golden Ticket).

BloodHound: Tools like BloodHound help visualize and understand complex AD attack paths.

Mitigation: Enforce strong, unique passwords, audit AD configs, implement MFA, and segment networks.

Key Takeaway

Effective AD security requires understanding attack chaining and implementing a defense-in-depth strategy with continuous auditing and strong controls.

Real-world Connection

The Domino Effect of Compromise

In a real-world scenario, an attacker might first gain a foothold through a phishing email, compromising a low-privileged user. They then use Kerberoasting to get a service account hash, crack it, and use those credentials to access a development server. From there, they might dump local admin hashes with Mimikatz, use Pass-the-Hash to move to a more critical server, and eventually find a path to a Domain Controller to obtain the krbtgt hash for a Golden Ticket. This multi-step process highlights why defenders must secure every link in the chain.

Common Pitfall

Focusing solely on preventing initial access while neglecting lateral movement and privilege escalation defenses leaves an organization vulnerable to advanced persistent threats.
