What is Privilege Escalation?
Moving from Low-Level User to Root
Definition

Privilege escalation is the act of gaining higher-level access to a computer system than initially obtained. This typically means moving from a standard user account to an administrative or root account.

Why It Matters

Achieving root privileges is crucial for most post-exploitation activities, such as installing backdoors, accessing sensitive data, modifying system configurations, or pivoting to other systems.

The Ultimate Goal: Root

On Linux, the 'root' user has unrestricted access to all commands and files. Gaining root is the primary objective for full system control and deep compromise.

Key Takeaway

Privilege escalation is the essential step to convert limited initial access into complete control over a Linux system, enabling critical post-exploitation activities.
