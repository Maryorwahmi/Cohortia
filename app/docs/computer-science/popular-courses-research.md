# Computer Science Catalog: Popular Courses and Demand Signals

**Research snapshot:** October 2, 2026  
**Catalog reviewed:** `catalog-courses-by-subcategory.json`

## What the catalog contains

The JSON declares 338 course rows across 13 subcategories. There are 329 unique IDs because some programs appear in more than one category. Two unique entries, “Verify uncertain degrees” and “Decide on inclusion rules,” are editorial notes, not courses. After deduplication and removing those two notes, the catalog contains 327 course or certificate offerings.

The strongest demand signals below are from live Coursera program pages: cumulative enrollments, program ratings, review counts, and Coursera's own “Trending right now” label where shown. Counts are snapshots, not growth rates.

## Category Placement and Priorities

1. **Data Science, Machine Learning & AI:** Prioritize Google Data Analytics for reach; Machine Learning and Deep Learning for strong rating/review evidence; add the GenAI course as a timely topic with solid enrollment but less mature review volume.
2. **Operating Systems & System Administration:** Prioritize Google IT Support first because Coursera currently labels it trending; Google IT Automation with Python is a strong next step.
3. **Programming & Software Development Fundamentals:** Prioritize Python for Everybody as the broad beginner entry point.
4. **Web Development:** Prioritize Meta Front-End, IBM Full Stack, then Meta Back-End, depending on whether the learner wants front-end, broad full-stack, or Python backend.
5. **Cybersecurity:** Prioritize Google Cybersecurity for entry-level learners; the catalog's exam certifications such as Security+ are relevant next steps, but their exam providers do not expose comparable course-enrollment totals.
6. **Databases:** Meta Database Engineer has a positive but smaller public footprint than the programs above; treat it as a focused niche recommendation, not a mass-market leader.
7. **Cloud Computing & DevOps:** IBM Full Stack is a relevant cross-category option because its curriculum includes cloud-native tooling. I did not find comparable public course demand figures for the catalog's cloud certification exams, so don't rank those by assumed popularity.
8. **Computer Networks:** No standalone networking listing in the catalog sample checked had comparable public learner-volume evidence. Google IT Support includes networking, but it is better categorized primarily as IT support / systems administration.
9. **Mobile App Development:** No catalog match with comparable, verified current enrollment and review signals was identified in this pass.
10. **Software Engineering, Architecture & Design:** IBM Full Stack overlaps with this area, but the catalog's dedicated architecture/design titles did not have comparable live demand metrics verified.
11. **Game Development:** No directly comparable current enrollment/review evidence was verified for the listed game-development courses.
12. **Blockchain & Web3:** No directly comparable current enrollment/review evidence was verified for the listed programs.
13. **Hardware, Embedded & IoT:** No directly comparable current enrollment/review evidence was verified. Remove the two editorial reminder entries from this category during catalog cleanup.

The “no comparable evidence verified” categories are not necessarily unpopular. Many listed items are exams, self-paced provider courses, or offerings whose platform does not publish enrollment totals. They need a separate marketplace-specific check before ranking.

## What “Hot” and “Selling Fast” Can Mean Here

The most defensible current “hot” signals are Coursera's explicit “Trending right now” label on Google IT Support, the strong live enrollment/review totals above, and broader GenAI career relevance. Coursera's [2025 Learner Outcomes Report](https://www.coursera.org/explore/learner-outcomes), based on more than 52,000 learners across 179 countries, reports that 91% of learners achieved a positive career outcome after completing a course; among learners who completed GenAI content, 91% reported a positive career outcome. That is career-outcome evidence, not a measure of current course sales.

“Selling fast” cannot be verified from the public pages reviewed: they do not expose daily or weekly enrollment velocity, paid conversion, revenue, or remaining seats. Use “high-demand / high-enrollment” for the programs above, and reserve “selling fast” for a course where the provider publishes recent sales or enrollment growth data.

## Catalog Cleanup Notes

- Keep one canonical row per course ID; use a secondary category/tag field for cross-listed offerings. Nine IDs currently occur in two subcategories.
- Remove `verify-uncertain-degrees` and `decide-on-inclusion-rules`; these are research reminders, not courses.
- Add provider URLs and record a `checkedAt` date for future trend refreshes. The source catalog currently has no course URLs, which makes ongoing verification harder.
