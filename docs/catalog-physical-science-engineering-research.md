## 4. Cross-Category Overlap & Deduplication Notes

- **Data Science / Machine Learning**: Significant overlap with Physics (computational physics, quantum computing), Electrical Engineering (signal processing, ML), and Industrial Engineering (optimization, analytics). Keep physics-focused and engineering-specific courses here; move general ML/DS to Data Science category.
- **Mathematics**: Calculus, differential equations, linear algebra are prerequisites for most engineering programs. Keep applied/engineering-focused math here (e.g., "Engineering Mathematics"); move pure math to Math & Logic category.
- **Computer Science / Programming**: Programming for engineers (MATLAB, Python for scientific computing) is kept here; general CS courses belong in IT/Computer Science category.
- **Health / Medicine**: Biomedical engineering courses overlap with Health category. Keep device-design, instrumentation, and engineering-focused content here; move clinical/medical content to Health.
- **Business / Management**: Engineering management, project management, and operations research overlap with Business category. Keep technical/project management here; move MBA-level business strategy to Business.
- **Environmental Science**: Overlaps with Health and Business. Keep engineering-focused environmental content (water treatment, air pollution control, sustainable design) here; move policy/ecology-focused content to Environmental Science or Health.
- **Earth Sciences**: Geology, oceanography, and meteorology courses could overlap with Environmental Science. Keep geological and marine-focused content here; move climate policy and ecology to Environmental Science.
- **Aerospace**: Some courses overlap with Mechanical Engineering (fluid mechanics, thermodynamics) and Electrical Engineering (avionics, communications). Keep aircraft/spacecraft-specific content here.
- **Materials Science**: Overlaps with Chemistry and Mechanical Engineering. Keep materials characterization, processing, and advanced materials here; move general chemistry to Chemistry category.
- **Energy Systems**: Renewable energy courses appear in Electrical, Mechanical, Chemical, and Environmental subcategories. Keep discipline-specific applications here; move general energy policy to Environmental Science or Business.
- **FE/PE Exam Prep**: Appears in multiple engineering subcategories. Can be consolidated or kept in Industrial, Systems & General Engineering as the most cross-cutting location.

## 6. Open Questions (need 10 resolved)

1. **Delivery mode accuracy**: Many engineering programs require hands-on labs. Should we mark lab-heavy degrees as "Hybrid" rather than "Online" even if lectures are online? Need to verify each program's lab requirement.
2. **ABET accreditation**: Should we add an "accreditation" field to degrees (e.g., ABET, Washington Accord, local bodies) for engineering programs specifically?
3. **FE/PE exam prep**: Should FE/PE exam prep courses be consolidated into a single subcategory or kept within each engineering discipline? They appear in Civil, Electrical, Mechanical, Chemical, and General Engineering.
4. **Cross-category overlap resolution**: How to handle courses that clearly belong to multiple categories (e.g., "Renewable Energy Systems" appears in Electrical, Mechanical, Chemical, and Environmental)? Should we duplicate or tag with multiple subcategories?
5. **African university inclusion**: Should we prioritize African universities (e.g., University of Lagos, University of Nairobi, University of Cape Town) that may not have extensive online offerings, or focus on international platforms accessible to African students?
6. **PhD vs. Professional Doctorate**: Should we distinguish between research PhDs and professional doctorates (e.g., DEng, EngD) in engineering?
7. **Diploma vs. Certificate**: Should we include HND (Higher National Diploma) and other sub-bachelor diplomas common in Commonwealth countries (e.g., Nigeria, UK, Tanzania)?
8. **Engineering technology vs. Engineering**: Should we include Engineering Technology degrees (e.g., BS ET) which are more applied/practical, or stick to traditional engineering degrees?
9. **Software/tool-specific courses**: Many engineering courses rely on specific software (MATLAB, ANSYS, AutoCAD, Revit, ArcGIS). Should we include software-specific courses or focus on conceptual/theoretical content?
10. **Emerging fields**: How to handle emerging interdisciplinary fields (e.g., Quantum Engineering, Space Resources, Climate Engineering, AI for Science) that don't fit neatly into traditional subcategories?
