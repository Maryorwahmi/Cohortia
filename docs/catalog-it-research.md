## 5. Open Questions

1. **Overlap with Computer Science** – Many IT courses (programming, algorithms, software engineering) overlap with CS. Should fundamental programming courses be excluded from IT and kept in CS only?
2. **Cybersecurity overlap** – Cybersecurity is heavily represented in both CS and IT. Should it be a standalone subcategory in IT or consolidated under IT Security?
3. **Cloud computing overlap with Data Science** – Cloud certifications (AWS, Azure, GCP) appear in DS, CS, and IT. Should they be duplicated or split by primary focus (architecture vs. data engineering vs. infrastructure)?
4. **DevOps vs. SRE placement** – DevOps and SRE bridge development and operations. Should they be in IT, CS, or a separate category?
5. **Enterprise systems scope** – Salesforce, SAP, ServiceNow, and Workday courses are included. Should they be kept in IT or moved to Business?
6. **Certification vs. Professional Certificate** – Many IT certs (CompTIA, Cisco, Microsoft, AWS, Google) are industry certifications. Should they be tagged as `Certification` or `Professional Certificate` in the schema?
7. **ITIL / COBIT / ISACA tagging** – IT governance certs (ITIL, COBIT, CISA, CISM) are included. Should they be tagged as `Certification` or `Certificate`?
8. **Associate degrees** – Some IT support roles require only associate degrees. Should associate-level IT degrees be included or should the catalog focus on bachelor's and above?
9. **Vendor-specific vs. vendor-neutral balance** – CompTIA and Cisco dominate. Should we add more vendor-neutral infrastructure certs or broaden to niche vendors (Juniper, Arista, F5, Fortinet)?
10. **Hardware and IoT** – Arduino, Raspberry Pi, and embedded systems courses overlap with CS/engineering. Should they stay in IT or be moved to a future Engineering/Physical Sciences category?
